# T23: Gaussian-noise forcing routes: scope and counterexamples

**From:** L11, L20  
**Test:** `tests/test_theorems.py::test_t23_gaussian_ours30_fails_literal_total_signal_consistency`; `::test_t23_independent_non_gaussian_errors_can_leave_zero_readings_alone`; `::test_t23_elliptical_non_gaussian_scale_mixture_has_correct_covariance_and_zero_factor`; `::test_t23_fixed_n_strong_signal_consistency_does_not_select_gaussian`; the historical Gaussian/Student comparison is `::test_t23_only_gaussian_noise_leaves_a_zero_reading_alone`.
**Status (B05, 30 Oct, Codex):** Gaussian forcing is **not established by either stated route**. The current Gaussian ours30 fails literal L11 on a fixed weak-signal Gaussian world as N grows. Z admits non-Gaussian models, including independent-channel ones. Fixed-N amplitude consistency holds for a class containing the Gaussian. The additional spherical-symmetry-plus-independence characterization below is conditional, not a new premise or ruling.
**Source:** lemma list d4, row T23

## Statement

The historical claim was "by consistency given the flat push; by Z under ours28".
B05 establishes the following scope instead:

1. A zero reading's flat noise-volume factor is mass-independent for every radial
   error density in whitened reading space, not only the Gaussian. Even independent
   symmetric channels can have this property without being Gaussian.
2. With ours30's actual positive-misfit weights and Bartlett power, Gaussian world
   **and** Gaussian model can concentrate at a wrong mass while the total true push
   grows. Thus the tempered consistency route fails as stated.
3. For fixed N and fixed finite errors, increasing the true amplitudes gives
   concentration at the true mass for Gaussian noise and bounded Gaussian scale
   mixtures under the stated powers. This narrower consistency result does not
   select a unique noise model or prove unrestricted L11.

## Proof or support

### B05: the model, the factor and the quantifiers

**Standard mathematics:** integrated likelihood in an errors-in-variables model,
population criteria of M-estimators, and multiplicative autocorrelation for the Z
condition. The derivations and counterexamples below are Codex's. The transform
identity used in the independent-channel example is
[NIST DLMF 1.14.36](https://dlmf.nist.gov/1.14.E36), with inversion as in
[1.14.34](https://dlmf.nist.gov/1.14.E34).

Work with one noise pair, full candidate subspaces of dimension k, and the existing
flat volume in declared noise units. After whitening, let U_m have orthonormal
columns spanning the candidate plane. For a radial model density
$q(e)=\rho(\|e\|^2)$, integration along that plane gives

$$E_y(m)=\int_{\mathbb R^k}q(y-U_m b)\,db
=\Psi_k(K(m)),\qquad
\Psi_k(t)=\int_{\mathbb R^k}\rho(t+\|b\|^2)\,db,$$

where $K=\|y\|^2-H$ is the declared-metric residual. This fixes the convention used
in this calculation; it is not a choice about T36's parked measure question.
For a Gaussian scale mixture with size law P this becomes, up to a constant,

$$\Psi_k(t)=\int v^{-k/2}\exp[-t/(2v)]\,dP(v).$$

The covariance is the declared one when $E_P[v]=1$. In particular
$P=\tfrac12\delta_{1/2}+\tfrac12\delta_{3/2}$ gives a proper, non-Gaussian elliptical
model with that covariance; its marginal fourth moment in unit noise is 3.75,
against 3 for a Gaussian. Alternative factors in the fixed-N comparison below are
raised to the unchanged ours30 $g_i$ and η, computed from its geometric terms.
Both powers are constant in the hypothesized mass.

L11 is a total-signal statement and allows N to grow with each reading's signal
fixed. It is stronger than increasing amplitudes with N fixed. The archive
`docs/ours27/ours27_perturbation_matrix_2026-09-27.md` §4 tested two independent-channel
families (Student t5 and Laplace) at fixed signal as N grows; its own limitations
paragraph disclaims a proof that all non-Gaussian models fail. Its untempered
Gaussian expected-residual identity cannot be passed through data-dependent g
without a new argument.

### Z: what the zero-reading condition actually implies

For y=0 the radial factor is
$E_0(m)=\int\rho(\|b\|^2)db$, constant for every m. Thus the elliptical family,
including the covariance-correct mixture above, passes this Z check. For a zero
reading ours30 has g=1 and zero score and curvature, so tempering does not remove
this counterexample.

There is a stronger counterexample to the historical independence qualification.
Let φ be the standard-normal density and define an even function

$$h(x)=9|x|^{-5}\int_0^{|x|}t^4\varphi(t)dt-\varphi(x),\quad x\ne0,
\qquad h(0)=\tfrac45\varphi(0).$$

Monotonicity of φ on the positive half-line gives $h\ge(4/5)\varphi>0$.
Its positive-half Mellin transform is obtained by exchanging the two integrals:

$$M_h(s)=M_\varphi(s)R(s),\quad
R(s)=\frac{s+4}{5-s},\quad0<\operatorname{Re}s<5.$$

Since $R(s)R(1-s)=1$, the multiplicative-autocorrelation identity cited above gives

$$\int_0^\infty h(x)h(ax)dx
=\int_0^\infty\varphi(x)\varphi(ax)dx
=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi}\sqrt{1+a^2}},\quad a>0.$$

Inversion is justified on $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$: the normal Mellin factors have
exponential decay there and R is bounded. Also $\int_{\mathbb R}h=5/4$ and
$\int_{\mathbb R}x^2h(x)dx=7/2$. With $b=\sqrt{14/5}$,
$p(x)=(4b/5)h(bx)$ is therefore an even zero-mean unit-variance density.
It has $|x|^{-5}$ tails and is non-Gaussian. Nevertheless the **independent** error
model $p(e_F)p(e_a)$ has covariance I and the constant zero-reading factor

$$\int_{\mathbb R}p(t\sin\theta)p(t\cos\theta)dt
=\frac{16b}{25\sqrt{2\pi}}=0.427237195399971\ldots$$

for every angle, with the endpoints following by continuity. This proves that
independence plus the zero-line integral condition is still insufficient. The
Student t5 example excludes that particular product density, not every product.

**The valid conditional Gaussian characterization:** assume the *full* whitened
joint density is spherical and also factors into independent continuous positive
channel densities. Sphericity gives identical even marginals p and
$p(x)p(y)=p(\sqrt{x^2+y^2})p(0)$. Then
$F(t)=\log[p(\sqrt t)/p(0)]$ satisfies $F(s+t)=F(s)+F(t)$ for nonnegative s,t.
Continuity gives $F(t)=-ct$; integrability requires c>0 and unit variance fixes
$c=1/2$. This is the Herschel–Maxwell argument. The Z condition is a central line
integral, and does not itself supply the full spherical-density assumption.
No missing noise-independence or symmetry premise is inserted here.

### Literal L11 fails for the current Gaussian tempered law

Use actual 1D ours30, c=s=1 and declared covariance I. Write X=F,Y=a and κ=1/2.
Its positive-mass weight is exactly

$$g(X,Y)=\begin{cases}1,&XY\ge0,\\
\min\{1,\kappa/\min(X^2,Y^2)\},&XY<0.\end{cases}$$

Under a centered Gaussian world, channel-swap symmetry gives
$E_0[gX^2]=E_0[gY^2]$, while suppressing only negative products gives

$$B=E_0[gXY]
=4\int_{\sqrt\kappa}^\infty(t-\kappa/t)\varphi(t)^2dt
=\frac{e^{-\kappa}-\kappa E_1(\kappa)}{\pi}
=0.103973970639162\ldots>0.$$

Here $E_1(x)=\int_x^\infty e^{-t}dt/t$.

Thus the population weighted quadratic at zero signal peaks at π/4, mass 1.
The counterexample itself has a **fixed nonzero** signal: true mass 2,
$\theta_0=\arctan2$, and independent readings
$Z_i=(X_i,Y_i)\sim N(\mu,I)$ with $\mu=r(2,1)/\sqrt5$, $r=0.01$.
Both world and model are Gaussian, and the declared covariance is correct.
The total true acceleration energy is $Nr^2/5\to\infty$.

For the per-reading score
$f_\theta=g(X\sin\theta+Y\cos\theta)(X\cos\theta-Y\sin\theta)$,
$E_0 f_{\theta_0}=B\cos(2\theta_0)=-3B/5$.
The Gaussian density ratio $D_\mu=\exp(\mu^\top Z-r^2/2)$ satisfies
$E_0(D_\mu-1)^2=e^{r^2}-1$. Since $|f_\theta|\le\|Z\|^2/2$ and
$E_0\|Z\|^4=8$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

$$|E_\mu f_\theta-E_0 f_\theta|\le\sqrt2\sqrt{e^{r^2}-1},\qquad
E_\mu f_{\theta_0}<-0.048.$$

The true angle is consequently not a population maximizer. In addition
$E_\mu[gXY]\ge B-\sqrt{e^{r^2}-1}>0.09$, using $E_0(gXY)^2\le1$.
The population quadratic has a unique acute principal angle θ* different from θ0.

The empirical weighted quadratic converges uniformly to that quadratic by the
law of large numbers applied to its three coefficients. Its fitted curvature
$C_N/N$ tends to the positive eigenvalue gap C*. Its squared-score average
$J_N/N$ tends to $J_*>0$, finite because g≤1 and Gaussian fourth moments are finite.
Positivity follows from a nonzero score on an open set of observations.
Hence $\eta_N\to\min(1,C_*/J_*)>0$. Multiplication by this positive scalar preserves
the incorrect maximizer. The normalized law, and its median, concentrate at θ*:
outside any neighbourhood of θ* the continuous population criterion has a strict
gap, which produces exponential decay in N relative to a neighbourhood with positive
prior mass. Thus ours30 fails L11 in this allowed world. It is a failure of the
tempered candidate, without any claim that experimental errors must be Gaussian.

The assumption lost in carrying over the archive is that weighting would preserve
the expected-score identity. Here g is correlated with the sign of XY and creates
positive expected cross energy even at zero signal. A positive series power changes
the concentration, not this population optimum. This finding makes no change to g,
η, L11 or any other premise, and makes no claim about the new ours31 probe.

### What remains true: fixed-N amplitude consistency for a class

Hold N and finite errors e_i fixed, and let
$y_i(R)=R U_{\theta_0}a_i+e_i$, $\sum\|a_i\|^2>0$, with θ0 interior.
At the true angle $K_i(\theta_0)=\|P_\perp e_i\|^2$ stays bounded. Since
$Q_{+,i}\le K_i(\theta_0)$, each ours30 g_i is uniformly bounded away from zero.
The leading angular criterion is
$\tfrac12 R^2(\sum_i g_i\|a_i\|^2)\cos^2(\theta-\theta_0)$, plus O(R).
Its fitted curvature is at least cR²,
its best angle differs from θ0 by O(1/R), and each fitted score is O(R).
Therefore J=O(R²), and η is uniformly positive (or takes its fallback 1).

For a scale law supported in $[v_{\min},v_{\max}]\subset(0,\infty)$, the integrated
factor obeys
$\Psi_k(K)\le v_{\min}^{-k/2}\exp[-K/(2v_{\max})]$ and is bounded below for bounded K.
Outside any fixed angular neighbourhood of θ0, an informative reading has
$K_i\ge cR^2$, while inside a window of width 1/R all residuals are bounded.
A proper prior with continuous positive density at θ0 consequently gives a normalizer at least c/R
and outside probability at most $CR e^{-cR^2}\to0$. Its median converges to the true
mass. This applies to both the fixed Gaussian and the non-Gaussian two-size mixture
above, with the actual geometric ours30 g and η held as the powers of each factor.

This is a **fixed-N amplitude theorem**. It neither establishes unrestricted L11
for that mixture nor repairs the Gaussian counterexample with N increasing.
No model is selected by numerical performance. The named tests check the analytic
bounds, the alternative densities and zero factors, and both fixed-N limits with
nontrivial g and η. All alternative factors live in the test only; `met/` is unchanged.
