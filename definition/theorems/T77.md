## Standard mathematics

A constrained-risk inequality by the second-moment (chi-square) method (Duchi and Ruan 2020; Brown and
Low 1996): a procedure that does well at one point of the parameter space must do badly at points whose
likelihood ratio to it has a bounded second moment. T45's score and its conditional variance are
additive over independent readings (Moreira 2003); a confidence set is not a sufficient summary for
combining (Singh, Xie and Strawderman 2005 make the corresponding point for confidence distributions).

## Three things "a null result" can mean

1. **Exactly zero observed readings.** Z (L20): an algebraic identity.
2. **A block declared to carry no signal.** Its likelihood does not depend on the mass.
3. **A block whose true signal is zero, not disclosed.** Its readings are random noise. This is the
   case below, and it is the one an experimenter meets.

## Statement

A fixed informative result $X$ (another route, or a fixed block of ordinary readings) has a baseline point
$B$ that is not exactly right with probability one. Beside it comes a block of $N$ readings with signals
$r_i$, total energy $\Delta_N=\sum r_i^2$; $U_N$ is the combined point.

**(1) Two promises that cannot both be kept.** *Untouched on noise:* when the block is pure noise,
$U_N-B\to0$ in probability. *Consistent at the rate:* on every sequence of blocks with
$\Delta_N^2/(N+\Delta_N)\to\infty$, $U_N\to m$ in probability. For every procedure there is an identifying
sequence of blocks on which the second fails if the first holds. When $X$ is a fixed block of the same
kind of readings and $U_N$ the definition's point on the pooled series, the second promise is L11
itself. So a point that meets L11's rate is moved, by enough noise-only readings, away from the result of
some fixed informative block. No property of the procedure is used.

**(2) A weight beside the result does not escape it.** If the block reports a point and a weight $w_N$,
combined with $X$ by inverse weighting, then $w_N\to0$ on noise and consistency of the combination on
every identifying sequence are incompatible in the same way.

**(3) What can be had.** A weight that stays bounded and does not vanish on noise, and grows without
bound on identifying sequences, exists: with $G$ the block's 2×2 matrix of dot products,
$w=(\lambda_{\max}-\lambda_{\min})^2/\operatorname{tr}G$ tends in law to $2\chi^2_2$ on noise and is asymptotic to
$\Delta^2/(2N+\Delta)$ under signal. A control showing the weaker promise is attainable; not a rule.

**(4) What combining needs.** For independent blocks T45's signed score and its conditional variance
add, candidate by candidate, and the combined test is exact, zero-safe and free of order. The sets, or
the whole p-value curves, are not enough: two blocks whose matrices of dot products are $I$ and $3I$ have
score zero at every mass, so identical p-value curves (all 1) and identical sets at every level; beside
the same further reading $(3,-1)$, at $m=1$ in noise units, one gives $16/3$ (rejected at 95%) and the
other $16/5$ (kept).

**(5) What a long noise-only block does to the score set.** On one instrument, with a fixed informative
block beside $N$ noise-only readings, the signed score curve over the positive masses tends in law to
$Z_1\sin2\theta+Z_2\cos2\theta$ with $Z_1,Z_2$ independent standard normal, whatever the fixed block was. The
true mass stays covered at the stated level; the fixed block is forgotten. The 95% set is every positive
mass with probability $1-e^{-3.8415/2}=0.8535$. Simulated with one reading at signal 20: beside 400
noise-only readings the kept set is $\sqrt2$ times as wide and never everything; beside 4000 it is
everything in 0.10 of series; beside 40 000 in 0.72; beside $1.6\times10^6$ and $10^8$ in 0.854 and 0.853.

**(6) Past one instrument the curve alone is not enough; the curve with the score's variance is (E14-3;
harvested 23 Nov from `explore/e14-ours34-walls`, checked by Claude).** Forty readings on an instrument of
noise ratio 1 and forty on one of noise ratio 2, each block a force-only and an acceleration-only reading of
equal size in noise units, twenty times: both curves are the constant 20 at every mass. Beside the same
further reading ($F=5$, $a=1$, unit noise), T45's test of $m=1$ gives 3.7895 with the first block (kept at
95%) and 4.6753 with the second (rejected). The score is $-\tfrac12$ the slope of the curve on the full type
(any covariance, declared subspaces), so the curve carries the score; what it does not carry past one
instrument is the score's variance. On one instrument, in the instrument's own angle, that variance is
$E-K(\theta)$, the energy the candidate does explain, and $E$ is twice the curve's mean level: this is (4)'s
"the unnormalised curve is enough". The noise ratio enters only through the change of coordinate from that
angle to mass, which differs between instruments. So what adds across blocks and suffices for T45's test is
the pair: the curve and the variance curve, both zero for a zero reading.

**In one line:** a reading at signal $r$ is undone by about $r^4$ noise-only readings, in every procedure
that keeps L11's rate, because that is what the rate says: the square of the total signal energy
against the number of readings.

## Proof of (1), array form (re-derived)

Give every reading of the block squared signal $t=\Delta/N$ along the true line with an independent fair
sign. Against pure noise the block's likelihood ratio is $L=\prod_ie^{-t/2}\cosh(\sqrt t\,u_m^\top Y_i)$, and
$E_0L^2=\cosh(t)^N\le e^{\Delta^2/2N}$ (since $E\cosh(2\sqrt tZ)=e^{2t}$). The law of $X$ is the same under
both, so $L$ is the joint ratio. For the event $A_N=\{|U_N-B|>\epsilon\}$, with $\alpha_N$ its probability
on noise, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $P_{\rm mix}(A_N)\le\sqrt{\alpha_N}\,e^{\Delta^2/4N}$. Off $A_N$ and on
$\{|B-m|>3\epsilon\}$, which has a fixed probability $\delta>0$, $|U_N-m|>2\epsilon$. So
$P_{\rm mix}(|U_N-m|>2\epsilon)\ge\delta-\sqrt{\alpha_N}\,e^{\Delta^2/4N}$. Take
$\Delta_N=\sqrt N\,d_N$, $d_N^2=\min\{\log(1/\alpha_N),\sqrt N\}$: then $d_N\to\infty$,
$\Delta_N^2/(N+\Delta_N)\to\infty$, and the subtracted term is at most $\alpha_N^{1/4}\to0$. Some fixed
choice of signs does no better than the mixture, and those choices form one identifying sequence on
which the error probability stays near $\delta$.

(2) the same bound with $A_N=\{w_N>w_0\}$. (4) T45 §1; the example by direct computation ($S=4$, $C=2$
for the added reading; $C=1$ and $3$ for the blocks). (5) the noise block's matrix is
$NI+\sqrt N\,(\text{a symmetric Gaussian matrix})+o$, the fixed block is $O(1)$, and on a half-turn of
$2\theta$ the largest value of $(Z_1\sin2\theta+Z_2\cos2\theta)^2$ is $Z_1^2+Z_2^2\sim\chi^2_2$.

## Against the rows

- **"A 0/0 result enters combination with weight zero" [R-2026-09-29-3].** The ruling is about a 0/0
  result beside another determination (Can's words: "0/0 from the mass measurement and 1000kg from
  rho*volume, we should have the answer be around about 1000"). That is meaning 1, and it holds on both
  lines. Nothing on the pages asks the same of noise-only readings (meaning 3). So (1) is in conflict
  with no row: it is a consequence of L11's rate that noise-only readings do move a result, by any
  procedure, with a law and a point or with E13's curve and sets (T76 (4) is the same fact inside the
  weighted-score family, with the count $r^2$ where the squared score halves and (5)'s $r^4$ where the
  reading is gone). **Corrected 22 Nov, night (Can: "noise only reading having no influence was something
  you made up since the beginning"):** this entry first read the stronger promise into the ruling and
  asked which of two rows gives way. There is one row.
- **What the quoted width promises on noise [property N; G28].** (3) and (5): a true statement stays
  available; "no influence" does not.
- **What is carried, under E13 [`../../problems/O20.md`].** (4): whatever is carried from a block must
  determine its score and variance curves. A set, a p-value curve and a normalised law do not. On one
  instrument the unnormalised curve does: $K(\theta)=\operatorname{tr}G-u^\top Gu$ fixes the whole matrix $G$,
  its level included (the two blocks of (4) have the constant curves 1 and 3, checked with
  `met.evidence.Curve`). So on one instrument not normalising is exactly what keeps the state that
  combining needs; E12's "what normalising erases" is this constant (read, not checked). ~~Past one
  instrument, whether the curve is enough is not checked.~~ **23 Nov:** it is not, by (6); the curve and
  the score's variance curve are. Note what the example of (6) also shows: both blocks have a flat
  curve, which under T73 says nothing about the mass, and each still moves the verdict on the new
  reading, which alone rejects $m=1$ at 8.0. That is (1) seen on forty readings. Whether the pair is
  enough for T75's tests of other statistics is not checked.
