## Standard mathematics

- **Student's t** (Student 1908; Fisher 1925): a normal score over an independent chi-square estimate of
  its own scale. Among intervals valid whatever the scale, it is the shortest equivariant one (the pair
  mean and scatter is complete and sufficient; Lehmann–Scheffé).
- **The Birge ratio** (Birge 1932) and metrology's "larger of the internal and the external error": widen by
  the square root of observed over expected scatter when that exceeds 1. This is the plug-in form of the
  same thing, with the normal quantile in place of Student's.
- **Kac–Bernstein**: under the declared Gaussian the mean and the scatter are independent, so the scatter
  says nothing about the mean's error (T75 (2)).
- A sum over pairs with a symmetric kernel is a U-statistic (Hoeffding 1948): the pair weight of 18 Nov
  (`../../problems/O14.md`).

## Statement

Plain words. $Z$ is T45's normal score at a candidate mass, $K$ the curve there (T73), $M$ the number of
leftover directions of the non-zero readings ($\sum k_i$).

**(1) A weight on each reading's score is closed (E14-5).** In T76's family only the constant weight keeps
the mass where the curve is least (T81's point) inside the kept set. One reading ($F=20$, $a=2$) beside a
hundred ($F=0.2$, $a=2$), unit noise: the least is at mass 1; the weighted scores there are 3.003
($p=0.0027$) under $H/(1+H)$ and 12.6 under $H$. A reading and its equal parts have one curve and, under any
other weight, two scores.

**(2) The curve and the score's variance cannot carry a discount (E14-6).** Two readings at 5 and 6 with
$\sigma=1/\sqrt{98}$, and fifty honest readings around 5.5 with $\sigma\approx0.505$ whose scatter is exactly
its expected value, have the same curve and the same score variance at every mass (to $4\times10^{-5}$ at
$\sigma_a=10^{-4}$; equal in the limit). A rule that reads only those two keeps the same set on both, so it
cannot stay wide on the first as σ shrinks and narrow on the second as readings are added.

**(3) Under one unknown common scale the leftover gives an exact Student test (E14-7).** Suppose every
declared covariance is right up to one common factor $v>0$. At the true mass, given every reading's part
along the line,

$$t=Z\,\sqrt{\frac{M-1}{K-Z^2}}\ \sim\ \text{Student with }M-1\text{ degrees},$$

on the full type (any covariance, declared subspaces, mixed readings). Simulated with five readings of
mixed subspaces and correlated covariances: it keeps the true mass in 0.949, 0.952 and 0.951 of series at
$v=1$, 7 and 0.2, where the bell keeps it in 0.953, 0.551 and 1.000.

**(4) Joined with the bell it is "expected over observed, capped at 1, to the first power" (E14-7).** Keep a
mass when either test keeps it:

$$Z^2\ \le\ z^2\cdot\max\!\Big(1,\ \frac{t_{M-1}^2}{z^2}\cdot\frac{K-Z^2}{M-1}\Big).$$

"Expected" is the count $M-1$, "observed" the leftover at that mass less the score's share, and the factor
in front is Student's quantile over the normal one. On two direct scalar readings it is the mean
$\pm\max(1.386\,\sigma,\ 6.353\,|x_1-x_2|)$: 4.915 to 6.185 on 5.5 and 5.6, and 0 to 11.85 on 5 and 6, at
σ = 0.1 and every σ below. Under an unknown scale Student's interval is the unique shortest equivariant one.

**(5) What it costs where the declared σ is right (Claude).** On honest direct readings the joined test is
wider than the bell in 88%, 81%, 74% and 61% of series of 2, 3, 5 and 20 readings, by a median factor 5.1,
2.1, 1.5 and 1.14, and holds the true mass in 0.992, 0.985, 0.979 and 0.962. With two scalar readings the
declared σ decides the width only when the two differ by less than 0.22σ.

**(6) That cost is forced in the wider world (Claude).** A rule that keeps the bell while a pair's scatter is
below its expectation and uses Student above it holds the true mass in 0.966, 0.914, 0.875 at an inflation
of 1, 2, 4. So if the level is promised under an unknown inflation, pairs that look ordinary are widened.

**(7) The pair weight of 18 Nov is the plug-in member (Claude).** On direct readings it has the same
estimate of the inflation and the normal quantile in place of Student's: 5 and 6 give 4.52 to 6.48, and
5.5 and 5.6 keep the bell's 5.41 to 5.69. In 3D the two nearly coincide (4.926 to 6.074 against 4.935 to
6.065). It reads no count: it tells the two series of (2) apart from the readings themselves. Under the
declared σ it holds the truth in 0.957 to 0.964 and widens 32% to 46% of series by 1.1 to 1.4. Under a
common inflation it falls short: 0.83 and 0.73 for two readings at an inflation of 4 and 25, 0.93 for
twenty; the limit is $P(|t_{N-1}|\le1.96)$: 0.70, 0.81, 0.88, 0.935 for 2, 3, 5, 20 readings.

**(8) An exact reference law, exact indifference to a zero reading, continuity at a zero reading: two of
the three (E14's break B; 18 Nov, night).** Twenty exactly zero readings beside the 3D far pair change
neither rule. Twenty readings of size $10^{-9}$ return the joined test to the bell's 5.36 to 5.64 (its count
goes from 6 to 66) and leave the pair weight where it was. The joined test has the first two, the pair
weight the last two, and a count that takes zero readings in would have the first and the third.
Recording each reading as ten equal parts narrows both (to ± 0.24 and ± 0.22): both pool one scale over
all the readings.

## Against the rows

Plain words first, labels in brackets.

1. **σ is believed as given [L18; R-2026-11-17-1].** Where the declared σ is right the bell is exact at
   every level of disagreement, so a discount is not needed there and nothing there fixes its size:
   T78 (5), E14-7's own third case (any floor $c\,|x_1-x_2|$ keeps the level, $c$ unforced) and
   `O14.md` (23 Nov) are three statements of this. A forced size exists exactly when the level is also
   promised in a wider world, and (3)–(4) is that size for the smallest one that keeps everything declared
   except the overall size of the noise.
2. **The two scenes [R-2026-11-17-3].** The far pair is believed less than the near one under both rules
   (± 6.35 against ± 0.64; ± 0.98 against ± 0.14). Scene A, "Convincing", comes out as 5.55 ± 0.64 under
   (4), where the combined uncertainty is ± 0.14; by (6) that is the premise's price and not a fault of the
   probe. Whether it is acceptable is a question about the goal (`../bounds.md`).
3. **"Found from the information already available, in an exact way" [R-2026-11-17-3].** (3) is that, for
   one number, the common inflation. The pair weight finds the same number and is not exact.
4. **A reading with no information changes nothing [L20].** In the wider world a small reading is evidence
   that the noise is small, so the antecedent fails for every reading but an exactly zero one; (8).
5. **Same curve, same result [L17 as E14 reads it].** Holds for the point (T81) and the bell's set; by (2)
   it cannot hold for any discount. L17 as ruled speaks of the mass.
6. **Nothing borrowed between readings [L19].** One pooled scale is learned from all the readings' leftovers.
   It is a scale of the noise, not of the pushes; whether L19's reason reaches it is not sorted.
7. **Consistency [L11].** The joined set tends to the bell's as readings are added (0.962 at twenty; E14
   reports 0.953 at four hundred).

## What is not shown

- Uniqueness of (4) beyond the textbook case, and that joining by "either keeps it" is the only way to keep
  both worlds.
- What happens when each reading has its own inflation (E14's break D reports 0.86; not rerun), or when the
  declared ratios between instruments are wrong.
- An exact reference law for the pair weight (18 Nov found none that keeps Z and continuity).
- E14-4 (sets for two bodies from the carried state) is not checked.

## Sorts (Claude, provisional)

- **The discount is one factor on the result, not a weight on each reading.** Three arguments now: (1);
  the 18 Nov sort; `O14.md` (23 Nov), a law for each reading's own error gives a sharp middle, a tie, or
  two peaks.
- **Asked to be unique, the discount needs a wider world, and then it is (3)–(4).** R-2026-11-17-3 asks for
  a discount; where σ is right none is forced; a definition has one body. So either the level is promised
  under an unknown common inflation, or the discount's size is not part of what the premises fix. On this
  the direction of `ours34` has the forcing argument and the pair weight does not.
- **Which two of (8)** is not sorted. It turns on whether a zero or near-zero reading may count towards how
  large the noise is, which is a question about the goal.
