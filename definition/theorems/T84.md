## Standard mathematics

- **Consistency of the errors-in-variables slope needs only second moments of the errors** (Gleser 1981; the
  total-least-squares literature, Gallo 1982): the estimating equation is unbiased under the declared
  covariance.
- **Peeling** (a rate from a bound on the supremum of a centred process on shells; van de Geer 2000).
- **Doob's maximal inequality** and **von Bahr–Esseen** (1965): $E|\sum X_i|^q\le2\sum E|X_i|^q$ for independent
  mean-zero terms and $1\le q\le2$.
- **Heavy tails:** the largest of $N$ squared errors with tail index $\nu$ grows like $N^{2/\nu}$, so without a
  fourth moment one reading can outweigh the summed signal.

## Statement

Plain words. $D=\sum_ik_i$ is the number of noise components, $\Delta$ the total true signal energy in noise
units (B27's letters). "The least" is the mass at which the curve is least on the closed arc from zero to
infinite mass (T81), read as an angle $\phi=\arctan m$.

**(1) The curve's expected rise from the true mass needs only the declared covariance (E17-G1).** If each
reading's error has mean zero and exactly the declared covariance, then for every candidate mass
$E[K(m)-K(m_0)]$ equals the rise of the noiseless curve, whatever the errors' shape: skewed, discrete or
heavy-tailed. (Each reading's expected leftover is its noiseless leftover plus $k_i$, because the trace
of a projector is its rank.) Measured on thirty readings of the full type under skewed, two-point and
5-degree Student errors, 2,000 series each: equal within sampling error at four masses (the largest of the
twelve differences is 2.2 standard errors).

**(2) With fourth moments the least keeps L11's rate, on the whole arc (E17-G1).** Suppose the errors are
independent, have mean zero and the declared covariance, have fourth moments bounded by one constant
$M_4$ (2 for Gaussian), and the readings' effective covariances have condition numbers bounded by one
constant in the units in use. Then

$$|\widehat\phi-\phi_0|=O_p\!\Big(\frac{\sqrt{M_4D+\Delta}}{\Delta}\Big),$$

so the least converges whenever $\Delta^2/(D+\Delta)\to\infty$: B27's threshold, which B27 proved for
Gaussian errors on a declared compact interval of masses. Here it holds for the least taken over every
mass from zero to infinity. Measured on the full type (mixed dimensions, declared subspaces, correlated
covariances) with $\Delta=6D^{0.75}$: the error times $\Delta/\sqrt{D+\Delta}$ has median 0.69, 0.75, 0.79 under
Gaussian errors at 60, 240 and 720 readings, 0.63, 0.75, 1.05 under skewed ones and 0.81, 0.87, 0.66
under two-point ones.

**(3) What (2) settles on main.** The head of `../proof.md` listed as not written "errors that are not
Gaussian in the world", and as not known "whether any probe attains it on vector readings". With (2):
the point of T81 attains it on vector readings, and the errors need the declared covariance and fourth
moments, not the Gaussian shape. (B27's own attaining estimator was already the curve's least, on its
interval; ours30's and ours33's medians were what was not known.)

**(4) Instruments of unbounded ratio (E17-G2).** For scalar readings on instruments of any noise ratios
$s_i$, with $w_i=m_0s_i/(m_0^2+s_i^2)$ and $J=\sum_iw_i^2r_i^2$ (B27's weighted energy), the noiseless curve
rises by at least $\rho^2J$ at every mass on the whole arc, $\rho=|m-m_0|/\max(m,m_0)$. And the centred curve
is at most of order $\sqrt{\Delta+M_4D}$ uniformly over the whole arc, for any ratios (a layer-cake
representation of the monotone weights, then Doob's inequality on each layer). So on the whole arc

$$\frac{J}{\sqrt{M_4D+\Delta}}\to\infty$$

is enough for the least to converge.

**(5) On the whole arc B27's weighted condition is not enough.** One reading on an instrument of ratio 1
with signal energy $8\log N$ at a true mass of 1, beside $N$ noise-only readings on an instrument of
ratio $N$. B27's weighted condition $J^2/(W+J)$ grows (9.0, 12.2, 15.4 at $N$ = 100, 500, 2500), and the
least taken on the interval 0.2 to 5 is within 0.5 of the true mass in 0.86, 0.92, 0.95 of series. The
least taken on the whole arc is at a mass of $N/2$ or more in 0.24, 0.36, 0.48 of series. The far
instrument's readings barely turn with the mass near 1, so they do not count in $W$; near a mass of $N$
they do, and their noise alone, of size $\sqrt{2N}$, outweighs the one reading's $8\log N$.

Nothing on the premise list is contradicted: by L11 as restated, $\Delta/\sqrt D$ falls on this sequence
(3.67, 2.22, 1.25), so it is not an identifying one. What it shows is that the weighted form is a statement
about a declared interval of masses.

**(6) Without a fourth moment the threshold is higher (E17-G3).** Errors with the declared covariance
and tail index $\nu$ between 2 and 4 (a spherical Student law, standardised): the least converges when
$\Delta$ outgrows $D^{2/\nu}$, not $D^{1/2}$. Measured at a true mass of 2 with $\Delta=8N^{0.65}$, the share of
series whose least is 0.5 or more from the truth, at $N$ = 100, 400, 1600, 6400:

| errors | 100 | 400 | 1600 | 6400 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gaussian | 0.33 | 0.18 | 0.06 | 0.02 |
| Student, 5 degrees (a fourth moment) | 0.36 | 0.27 | 0.14 | 0.10 |
| Student, 2.5 degrees (none) | 0.40 | 0.43 | 0.52 | 0.58 |

With $\Delta=6N^{0.95}$, above $N^{0.8}$, the last row comes back (0.13, 0.05, 0.05, 0.03). Under the same
2.5-degree errors a point read from $\sum_i\log(1+K_i/4)$ converges at the lower growth (0.13, 0.03,
0.01 at 100, 400, 1600), and under Gaussian errors it does about as well as the least (0.36, 0.17, 0.08). The
branch notes that such a point needs the errors to be symmetric, where (1) does not; not checked.

## Proof (of (2); read and re-derived)

Write $Z(\phi)=K(\phi)-K(\phi_0)-G(\phi)$ with $G$ the noiseless rise; $E\,Z=0$ by (1). *Separation:* in each
reading's noise units the distance from the true mean to the candidate's mean space is at least
$\|\mu_i\|\,|\sin(\phi-\phi_0)|/\sqrt{\kappa_i}$, so $G(\phi)\ge\Delta\sin^2(\phi-\phi_0)/\kappa_0\ge4\Delta(\phi-\phi_0)^2/(\pi^2\kappa_0)$
on the whole arc. *Derivative:* $Z'=-2\sum\mu_i^\top P_i'\varepsilon_i-\sum\varepsilon_i^\top P_i'\varepsilon_i$ with
$\operatorname{tr}P_i'=0$, $\|P_i'\|\le\sqrt{\kappa_i}$ and $\|P_i'\|_F^2\le2k_i\kappa_i$, so
$\operatorname{Var}Z'(\phi)\le\kappa_0(8\Delta+4M_4D)=\sigma^2$ at every angle (the two terms are bounded
separately, so skewness is allowed). *Peeling:* $E\sup_{|\phi-\phi_0|\le r}|Z|\le2r\sigma$; if the least is in
the shell $2^jr_0\le|\phi-\phi_0|<2^{j+1}r_0$ then $|Z|$ is at least $G$ there; Markov on each shell and a
geometric sum give $P(|\widehat\phi-\phi_0|\ge r_0)\le2\pi^2\kappa_0\sigma/(\Delta r_0)$.

## Against the rows

1. **Consistency [L11; G25].** (2) and (3): the rate is attained by the curve's least on the full type
   with bounded condition numbers, on the whole arc. The bin stays measurement (17 Nov, provisional): the
   moment condition is a fact about errors and would be the same for any slope.
2. **The Gaussian model [L13; "the data need not be Gaussian", 27 Sept].** (1): for where the curve points
   on average, only the declared covariance is used, as the 27 Sept line says. (2) and (6): for the rate,
   the world's errors also need fourth moments. That is a condition on the world's errors that the pages
   did not carry.
3. **The point on the closed arc [T81].** (5): with instruments of very different ratios, noise-only
   readings on a far instrument can carry the least to the far end although a declared interval would
   hold it. A declared interval of masses is not an input of the output without a starting law
   (`../../problems/O20.md`), so on that line the condition is (4)'s, or the unweighted one.
4. **σ believed [L18].** Every result here takes the declared covariance as exact.

## What is not shown

- That (2)'s dependence on $M_4$ and on the condition number is sharp; the lower bounds are B27's, for
  Gaussian errors.
- The branch's compact-interval statement for errors that are not Gaussian (its E17-G2 (2)): read, not
  checked.
- Any condition for covariances of unbounded condition number beyond (4)'s scalar case; B27's two
  histories still stand.
- That a sequence with $\Delta$ between $D^{1/2}$ and $D^{2/\nu}$ defeats *every* point, not only the least.
