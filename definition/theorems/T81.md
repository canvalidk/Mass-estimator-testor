## Standard mathematics

- **Functional invariance of maximum likelihood** (Zehna 1966, *Ann. Math. Statist.* 37): the
  maximiser of the induced (profile) likelihood of $g(\theta)$ is $g(\hat\theta)$, for any $g$. T74's
  composite curve is the profile over the split, so (1) is this theorem.
- **Infimal convolution** (Rockafellar 1970, §5), as in T74.
- **The least itself** is the errors-in-variables (total least squares) best point: the first probe
  (`GOAL.md` §5, row 1), and on one instrument the larger eigenvector of T73 (4)'s 2×2 matrix.
- **Boundary estimates.** A likelihood maximised over a closed parameter set can peak on its boundary;
  nothing below is special to mass in that respect.

## Statement

Write $m^*(K)$ for the mass at which a curve $K$ (T73) is least on the closed arc $[0,\infty]$, when
there is exactly one such mass.

**(1) The least composes, under any way of composing.** For two bodies with curves $K_1,K_2$ and any
function $g$ of the two masses, the composite curve $K_g(M)=\inf_{g(m_1,m_2)=M}[K_1(m_1)+K_2(m_2)]$ is
least exactly at $M=g(m_1^*,m_2^*)$, with least value $K_{1,\min}+K_{2,\min}$. So under T74's composition
the least adds for lumping and adds in inverse for a common force, both exactly, at any noise. It does
the same for the product of the two masses, which no rung asks for: this is invariance, not a
property of L5 and L14.

**(2) Asked of the point, composition forces the least (E14-1).** Take the curves with one interior
least, together with everything T74's two compositions and a change of units make from them. Let $R$
give each such curve a positive mass, with

- (i) $R(K_1\,\square_+K_2)=R(K_1)+R(K_2)$ (the lump's point is the sum of the parts' points);
- (ii) $1/R(K_1\,\square_\parallel K_2)=1/R(K_1)+1/R(K_2)$ (the same for a common force);
- (iii) units: if every mass is rescaled by $\lambda$, so is $R$ (L3);
- (iv) if a sequence of curves becomes arbitrarily high everywhere outside every neighbourhood of one
  mass, relative to its least there, $R$ tends to that mass.

Then $R(K)=m^*(K)$ for every such curve. The swap and the indifference to a constant added to the curve
are not assumed; they follow.

**(3) On the data themselves the least does not add.** Bodies of mass 1 and 2 sharing each acceleration
reading, the lump's force reading being $F_1+F_2$ (the 28 Oct scene): the least of the lump's own curve
differs from the sum of the parts' leasts by 0.122%, 3.35% and 27.9% (median of 30 series, seed 7) at
strong, middle and weak signal. The median of ours29's law had 0.273%, 2.62% and 23.25% on 28 Oct.
Fitting the summed data is not the sum of the fits, for this point as for every other.

**(4) Where there is no single interior least.**

- (a) *The kinds* (E14-2): the flat curve, with or without a leftover (the all-zero experiment; a
  force-only beside an acceleration-only reading of equal size, where $K\equiv1$); the least at one end
  only ($F=10\pm1$, $a=0\pm1$: at infinite mass; the swap: at zero); the two ends equally low, with the
  curve highest inside (force against acceleration, equal in noise units); and two equal interior lows,
  which needs two noise ratios, since on one the curve has a single turn (T73 (4)).
- (b) *On one isotropic instrument the least is interior exactly when $\sum_i\mathbf F_i\!\cdot\mathbf a_i>0$.*
  Otherwise it is at zero or at infinite mass, whichever channel has less energy in noise units. This
  is the 29 Oct sign condition met again. At no signal it happens in half of all series. For one
  scalar reading whose true pair is $r$ noise units in each channel the share is $2\Phi(r)\Phi(-r)$:
  0.27, 0.044, 0.003 at $r=1,2,3$. By the same rule, simulated: five 3D readings, 0.15 at $r=1$ and 0.001
  at $r=2$; twenty, 0.02 at $r=1$.
- (c) *It is not continuous in the readings.* Force against acceleration with the two energies equal to
  one part in a million: the least is at infinite mass or at zero according to which is larger.

**(5) At a curve whose least is at an end, no positive value keeps (i) and (ii) (E14-2).** A body
measured at 3 ($F=3$, $a=1$, unit noise) under a common force with a body that did not move ($F=10$,
$a=0$): the composite curve has one interior least, at 3. If (ii) holds there, $1/3=1/3+1/R$, so the
second body's point is infinite. Lumping with a body that was not pushed gives zero the same way. On
the flat curve, (i) gives $R=2R$.

## Proof

(1) $K_1(m_1)+K_2(m_2)\ge K_{1,\min}+K_{2,\min}$ with equality only at $(m_1^*,m_2^*)$, which lies on the
level set $g=M$ only for $M=g(m_1^*,m_2^*)$; the arc is compact and the curves continuous on it.

(2) Let $m^*$ be the least of $K$ and $c(\epsilon)>0$ the lowest value of $K-K_{\min}$ outside
$(m^*-\epsilon,m^*+\epsilon)$. Compose $n$ bodies with curve $K$ at a common acceleration and divide the
mass by $n$: by (i) and (iii) $R$ is unchanged. If the mean of $n$ non-negative masses is at most
$m^*-\epsilon$, at least $n\epsilon/2m^*$ of them are below $m^*-\epsilon/2$, so the new curve is at
least $n\,\epsilon\,c(\epsilon/2)/2m^*$ above its least there. On the heavy side one mass must exceed
$m^*+\epsilon$, which costs $c(\epsilon)$, and no more can be shown: one body can carry the whole excess,
so the lowest point of that wall never exceeds $K(\infty)-K_{\min}$, which is finite at every finite σ (T73). Now compose $n$ of
these under a common force and multiply the mass by $n$: by (ii) and (iii) $R$ is again unchanged, and
the same count on the inverse masses raises the heavy side to at least
$n\,\epsilon\,c(\epsilon/2)/2(m^*+\epsilon)$ while the light side keeps its height. The least has stayed at
$m^*$ throughout, both walls grow without bound, so by (iv) $R\to m^*$; and $R$ never moved. The two
compositions are both used: each raises only one side.

(4b) On one instrument $K(\theta)=\tfrac12(A+B)+\tfrac12(B-A)\cos2\theta-C\sin2\theta$ with $A,B,C$ the
summed dot products of T73 (4); on $2\theta\in[0,\pi]$ it has an interior low exactly when $C>0$.
(5) and (4a), (4c): by computation on the stated readings.

## Against the rows

Plain words first, labels in brackets. A finding is against the row as it stands and does not rewrite it.

1. **"Composition is not a constraint on the point at finite noise" [L5, L14 as restated;
   R-2026-10-28-1].** The 28 Oct sort had two legs. *No functional of a law adds both ways* (Jensen: the
   mean against the harmonic mean). That leg is about laws, and on the curve it is gone: by (1) the
   least adds both ways, exactly. *On data nothing adds:* that leg stands, (3). The ruling's question
   was "are we revealing that nothing might have that property of additivity?"; for the composite
   computed from the parts, something has it.
2. **The first probe's record [`GOAL.md` §5 row 1; T73, "What it does not give"].** Of the three things
   that killed the best point: *it goes to an end* stands, and (4b) says exactly when; *the positive
   root can be the curve's highest point* is a fault of solving the quadratic, not of taking the least
   on the closed arc; *"a mode, so it does not compose"* is true of laws pushed forward and false under
   T74's composition. T73's sentence is corrected (23 Nov); a test written with it on 22 Nov already
   showed the composite bottoming where the parts do.
3. **A positive mass on every measured input [L2 as read on 29 Sept; T13; `type.md`].** By (4) and (5)
   the least is at zero or infinite mass on a set of inputs that is not small, and no positive value
   can stand in for it there without breaking (i) or (ii). T13's "$\widehat m\in(0,\infty)$" is a theorem
   about a proper starting law with the median, so on the E13 line it is not a premise.
4. **The point is a family [G6; L23].** On the law-and-point line units and the swap leave a family of
   location functionals. On the curve, with (i)–(iv), one member is left where the least is interior.
   The least also does not depend on the coordinate the curve is read in (L23's content).
5. **Zeros, units, the swap, same curve same mass, nothing borrowed [L20, L3, T5, L17, L19].** Met:
   exact zeros from any instrument leave it unchanged to the last bit; it is a function of the curve
   alone, and the curve is a sum over readings.
6. **Consistency [L11].** ~~Not checked here.~~ It is the total-least-squares estimate, whose rate is the
   literature's (B19). **24 Nov (T84):** it keeps L11's rate on the full type with bounded condition
   numbers, on the whole closed arc, for errors with the declared covariance and fourth moments; with
   instruments of very different ratios, noise-only readings on a far instrument can carry it to the far
   end (T84 (5)), and without fourth moments its threshold is higher (T84 (6)).

## What is not shown

- That both compositions are *needed* for (2). The proof uses both; no functional is exhibited that
  meets (i), (iii), (iv) and is not the least.
- (iv) is not on the premise list in this form. It is consistency asked of the point of a curve, and it
  is applied to curves that are computed composites, not curves of a series.
- Whether the end is inside the sets of T75 when the least is at an end. **24 Nov, for the score set
  (T45), on one input: it is not.** Two scalar readings with force against acceleration, (10, −3) and
  (10, −3.1) at unit noise: the least is at infinite mass (18.61), and the score test rejects that end
  (p = 1.6 × 10⁻⁵) and keeps only a sliver round the mass at which the curve is *highest* (0.305, where
  it is 218.6). From exploration E15 (its E15-3), recomputed on main's curve and score test:
  `../../tests/test_ours35_against_main.py`. So the point of (4) and the set kept by the score test are
  not yet one body on such inputs. E15's further claim, that the conditional likelihood-ratio test of
  T75 keeps the end, is not checked.

## Sorts (Claude, provisional)

- **Composition at the point returns as a requirement, for the composite computed from the parts.** Under
  F1 there is no mass behind what the definition returns, so "masses add" is a statement about returned
  masses. It was restated on 28 Oct because nothing could meet it; on the curve it is met, so that
  reason is gone (`TASK.md` §4: the premise was dropped for having no model). Then (2) makes the point
  the least wherever the least is interior. Clause (c), a new measurement of the composite, stays a
  matter of coverage, by (3).
- **On this line the point's range is the closed arc, zero and infinite mass included.** Forced by (5)
  from the sort above. It is also T36's own type (a mass is a line in the stacked space, and the two
  axes are lines) and the type T80's sets already have. Whether a returned zero or infinity is "a mass"
  in the dictionary's sense is a question about the goal (`../bounds.md`).
