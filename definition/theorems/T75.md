## Standard mathematics

Under a null hypothesis whose nuisance parameters have a sufficient statistic, the law of the data
given that statistic is free of them, so any statistic has a known conditional null law and its test is
exact (Neyman structure; Lehmann and Romano 2005, §4.3). In the weak-instrument literature this is
Moreira's conditional likelihood-ratio test (2003), which T45, T69 and B24 already use on one instrument;
the step here is that the conditioning works reading by reading on any declared covariance and subspace.
A conditional law sampled by simulation gives an exact test at the rank of the observed value (Dwass
1957; Barnard 1963). Conditioning also on the length of each leftover leaves only its direction, uniform
for every spherically symmetric error (Fisher 1935; Lehmann and Romano 2005, §15.2). That the sample
mean is independent of the differences only for Gaussian errors is Kac (1939) and Bernstein (1941);
that an ancillary is independent of a complete sufficient statistic is Basu (1955).

## Statement

At a candidate $m_0$ split each whitened reading, inside its span, into $U_i=P_i(m_0)z_i$ (along the
candidate's mean space) and $V_i=(I-P_i(m_0))z_i$ (the leftover), as in T45 §1.

**(1) Any statistic has an exact test of $m_0$.** If $m_0$ is the true mass and the errors have the declared
Gaussian laws, then given all the $U_i$ the $V_i$ are independent standard normal vectors in $k_i$
dimensions, for every value of the pushes. So for any function $T$ of the readings,
$p(m_0)=P\{T^*\ge T_{\rm obs}\mid(U_i)\}$ satisfies $P\{p(m_0)\le\alpha\}\le\alpha$ at the true mass, on the full
type, with no size law and nothing carried from one reading to another. The statistic in `met/` is the
drop of the weighted leftover from the candidate to its least,
$T=W(m_0)-\inf_mW(m)$, $W(m)=\sum_iw_iK_i(m)$, with weights $w_i=w(\|U_i\|^2)$, which the conditioning fixes.
$w\equiv1$ is the likelihood-ratio statistic of the declared Gaussian.

**(2) Under the declared Gaussian the gap between readings widens nothing.** For two scalar readings of
one mass the average is independent of the gap, so given any gap the average lies within
$1.96\,\sigma/\sqrt2$ of the mass with probability 0.95, and the test of (1) keeps the same width around a
far pair as around a near one (5.5 and 5.6: 5.55 ± 0.14; 5 and 6: 5.50 ± 0.14). This holds only for
Gaussian errors. So a width that grows with the disagreement is not a consequence of believing the
σ's under the Gaussian model; another premise has to supply it (`../../problems/O14.md`, 17 Nov and
22 Nov). On main since 17 Nov as a measurement (`tests/test_believed_less.py`); the name and the
"only" are E13's insight 2.

**(3) A weight that vanishes with $\|U_i\|$ makes the test blind to zero readings, and to more.** With
$w(0)=0$ an exactly zero reading has weight 0 and drops out: the p-value is the same number with or
without it. With $w\equiv1$ it does not drop out, because its conditional law still gives it $k_i$ random
leftover components: 150 zero readings take a rejected candidate (p = 0.04) to a kept one (p = 0.12),
and 1000 take it to p = 1.
The price of $w(0)=0$: at a candidate where a reading has nothing along the line the reading is
invisible, however large its leftover there. A single reading $(F,a)=(2,-1)$ at $\sigma=0.1$ has leftover
500 at $m=0.5$ and is kept there with p = 1.

**(4) The scale-free null.** Condition also on each $\|V_i\|$. If each reading's error, in its own noise
metric, has a law that depends only on its length (the declared Gaussian; the declared Gaussian times
any unknown size, one per reading, of any law), then given $U_i$ and $\|V_i\|$ the direction of $V_i$ is
uniform on the sphere, and the test that redraws only directions is exact for all of them. A zero
reading has no direction to redraw and drops out.

**(5) What each null does with disagreement, under the declared Gaussian.** Ten scalar readings of one
mass, coverage of the true mass at the 95% level by fifths of the leftover, least disagreement first
(3000 series, seeded; the last `MET_SLOW` test regenerates it):

| | 1st | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | 5th | all |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gaussian null | 0.950 | 0.957 | 0.947 | 0.942 | 0.957 | 0.950 |
| scale-free null | 0.848 | 0.957 | 0.972 | 0.985 | 0.998 | 0.952 |
| either keeps | 0.950 | 0.965 | 0.972 | 0.985 | 0.998 | 0.974 |

The Gaussian null is right at every level of disagreement (this is (2)). The scale-free null is right
on average only: it is too narrow when the readings agree and wider than needed when they do not.

**(6) The two joined ("the floor").** Keep a candidate when either test keeps it (the Gaussian null with
a zero-safe weight; the scale-free null unweighted). Because the scale-free test alone is exact for
every noise size, the join needs no correction for using two tests, is zero-safe, never keeps less than
the declared σ's allow, and keeps more when the readings disagree. On Can's scenes written as vector
readings (two readings, 2D or 3D): the near pair is kept as 5.55 ± 0.14 and the far pair as nearly everything
between the two readings, 5.5 ± 0.45 to 0.49, where the Gaussian null alone keeps 5.5 ± 0.14. With the
σ's shrunk fivefold and twenty-five-fold the far pair's kept set does not move (5.05 to 5.95). No σ is revised
and no number is handed over. By (5) it is a true statement at every level of disagreement and
conservative when they disagree (0.97 to 0.995 overall on the Gaussian worlds measured).

**(7) Where the scale-free null says nothing.** Its smallest p-value on $n$ scalar readings is $1/2^n$,
and on one instrument each sign pattern ties with its mirror image about the candidate line, so it is
$2/2^n$: 0.0625 at five readings, 0.031 at six. So on scalar readings of one instrument it keeps every
mass until there are six (E13's insight 8 says five; its fixed grid breaks the tie numerically). Two
scalar readings, near or far, are kept at every mass, by the join too. On vector readings two suffice.
On readings that agree better than their σ it collapses (ten readings within 0.01 at σ = 0.1: narrower
than 0.02; one exactly aligned vector reading: a single point), which is what the join repairs.
**24 Nov (T83 (2), from exploration E17):** the same count makes it jump beside a zero reading on scalar
readings. Five readings keep a candidate at 0.0625; an exactly zero sixth reading changes nothing; a
sixth reading a thousandth of a noise unit in size makes it 0.031 and the candidate is rejected. In
exact arithmetic that holds for any size above zero; `met/conditional_lr.py` treats differences below
$10^{-9}$ of the statistic as ties, so below about $10^{-4}$ it still returns 0.0625. On vector readings
it is steady.

## Proof

(1) At the true mass $z_i\sim N(\mu_i,I)$ with $\mu_i$ in the range of $P_i(m_0)$, so $U_i$ and $V_i$ are
independent Gaussian, $V_i\sim N(0,I-P_i)$, and $U_i$ is sufficient for the push (T45 §1). The Monte Carlo
p-value $(1+\#\{T^*_b\ge T_{\rm obs}\})/(B+1)$ is exact when the draws are independent of the data. The
infimum is computed by one fixed rule applied alike to the observed readings and to each draw, so the
statement holds for the computed statistic whatever its numerical accuracy. (2) Kac–Bernstein; for the
conditional statement, Basu. (3) a reading with $w_i=0$ enters neither $W$ nor the draws. (4) given the
size, $z_i-\mu_i$ is $N(0,vI)$: $U_i$ and $V_i$ are independent given $v$, and $V_i/\|V_i\|$ is uniform and
independent of $\|V_i\|$, $U_i$ and $v$. (6) a union of sets covers at least as often as either.

## What the check found against E13's text

- **The fixed grid.** E13 takes the infimum on a fixed grid (41 angles by default, 31 to 121 in its
  tables). The test stays exact but loses power when the grid is coarser than the curve: on five scalar
  readings near 2 at σ = 0.01, with 121 angles scaled to the instrument's noise ratio, its observed
  statistic is 0 at every candidate tried and every mass is kept. Here the infimum
  is a scan of 361 angles followed by golden section, applied alike to data and draws. E13's set
  endpoints for the scalar scenes are therefore grid-limited ([5.426, 5.68] against [5.415, 5.685]).
- **Six scalar readings, not five** (7).
- **"(0, ∞)" for $F=10\pm1$, $a=1\pm1$** is $[3.28,\infty)$: unbounded above only (T73 (1)).
- **The floor's coverage** on the Gaussian worlds here is 0.97 to 0.995, above E13's 96–98%.

## What it does not settle

- **Which statistic and which weight.** (1) holds for every statistic. For the score tests the weights
  are an exact family in which no member is best at both of two jobs (T76). Under F2 a family is not a
  definition: this is the open item, not a detail.
- **Random noise-only readings.** A zero reading drops out; a reading of pure noise does not, and enough
  of them erase a fixed informative reading from these sets, as from any procedure that keeps L11's
  rate (T77; T76 (4)).
- **Whether the join is forced.** (6) meets what R-2026-11-17-3 asks on vector readings, by (5) at the
  cost of saying less than the declared Gaussian allows. Nothing here derives it from a premise.
- **The level.** A set needs a level; what is defined without one is the function $m\mapsto p(m)$.
- **The domain.** On positive masses the likelihood-ratio set is never empty (opposed readings are kept
  at the two ends); on the whole line of slopes no positive mass is kept. L1 takes $m>0$ as a premise
  and asks for no gate, which is the first.
