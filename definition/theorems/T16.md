## Statement

$\lambda\sin2\theta_c$ at any centre $c$ equals one reading with $P=Q$, $D=2\lambda$, at the level of the law

**Scope (31 Oct, Claude, from B06's proof):** exact for the flat factor and for ours30's raw law ($\eta=1$) when the reading has $g=1$; under ours30 the coefficient is $g\lambda$, and a negative tilt is bounded below by $-\kappa/2$. Not an identity for a recomputed $\eta$.

## Proof or support

**Standard mathematics:** a quadratic form on the projective angle and multiplication
of a density by an exponential tilt. Work with one isotropic noise pair of ratio c
for the extra reading, and write $\theta_c=\arctan(m/c)$. In its noise units let
$P=\|x\|^2$, $Q=\|y\|^2$, $D=x\cdot y$. The projection formula of T36 gives

$$H(m)=P\sin^2\theta_c+Q\cos^2\theta_c+2D\sin\theta_c\cos\theta_c.$$

Thus $P=Q=A$ and $D=2\lambda$ imply
$H=A+2\lambda\sin2\theta_c$. The unweighted flat log-factor is $H/2$, so its
m-dependent contribution is exactly $\lambda\sin2\theta_c$. Adding it multiplies
any existing mass law by $\exp(\lambda\sin2\theta_c)$ and then renormalizes.
The existing law's starting centre $c_{\rm prior}$ can differ from the extra
reading's noise ratio c: its prior cancels from the before/after density ratio.

**Conditions needed for the current equation.** In ours30 the raw law means η=1
while retaining the reading's misfit weight. The added log-factor is therefore

$$\ell(m)=\tfrac12gA+g\lambda\sin2\theta_c,$$

and the actual raw tilt coefficient is **gλ**. The stated coefficient λ is obtained
when g=1 (or λ=0). In this equal-energy case $E=2A$ and
$\max_{m>0}H=A+\max(D,0)$, with the endpoints understood as limits, so

$$Q_+=A-\max(D,0),\qquad
g=\min\{1,\kappa/[A-\max(D,0)]\},\quad\kappa=k-\tfrac12.$$

For λ≥0, the 1D realization $x=y=\sqrt{2\lambda}$ has $A=D=2\lambda$, $Q_+=0$
and g=1, giving every positive tilt. For λ<0, Cauchy–Schwarz requires
$A\ge|D|=2|\lambda|$, and $Q_+=A$. Hence $|g\lambda|\le\kappa/2$.
For the minimal-energy 1D realization $x=-y=\sqrt{2|\lambda|}$, g=1 only when
$|\lambda|\le1/4$; larger negative tilts saturate at coefficient -1/4.
More generally the bound is $-\kappa/2$ for a declared k. The historical explanation
that a 1D reading always has g=1 applies to ours29's whole-line misfit, not to
ours30's positive-mass misfit. This is a condition missing from the Statement.

Finally η also enters the density ratio. Holding an externally fixed η would give
coefficient ηgλ. Under the defined calibrated law, adding a reading can change η
and hence every existing reading's exponent as well. Thus the simple multiplicative
tilt statement applies directly to the raw η=1 law with the g condition above;
it is not an identity for a generically recomputed calibrated η. No assertion about
T31's forcing argument is needed for this algebra.
