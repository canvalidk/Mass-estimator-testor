# T67: Pairwise profile comparisons can order score roots without changing regular local width

**From:** T54/T55's physical Gaussian profile and conditional score; T56's false-score-root obstruction; T65's separation of local evidence from global prior disagreement. The pairwise criterion below is an additional research construction, not a forced VD rule.
**Test:** `tests/test_pairwise_anchor.py`; `tests/test_pairwise_continuum.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). A bounded invariant pairwise penalty gives a proper law with the full Godambe Gaussian and composite-coverage limits in the stated regular repeated-block experiment, and the fixed-experiment amplification limit. Those results permit the unchanged full half-Cauchy or another proper prior with the stated local regularity. A separate compact-supplied-prior version repairs T65's degenerating-shape experiment. The full-prior T65 case, arbitrary identifying histories, finite-noise coverage and selection of this criterion remain open. No production equation, prior, physical input, premise or ruling is adopted.

## Standard mathematics and the object being constructed

Comparing candidates pairwise is standard in test-based estimation; see, for
example, [Baraud, *From robust tests to Bayes-like posterior distributions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.12011).
That paper is background for the comparison viewpoint, not a theorem
applied to this incidental-signal experiment. The displayed criterion and
its normalization, local limits and scope are established here directly.
Exponentiating it together with an estimating-function contrast defines a
quasi-posterior, rather than a likelihood justified by candidate-test
exactness. T55/T56 state the relevant distinction.

The proposed ordering penalty compares each candidate with all rivals
under the **original supplied joint prior**. It fits no mass, chooses no
score basin and estimates no distribution of experimental signals.
The prior measure, the positive-part operation, the denominator and its
unit offset, the exponential combination and the positive constant below
are additional research choices. Successful limiting behavior does not
select those choices or establish their authority under L19.

## 1. Finite-data definition

Use T54's full common-acceleration acquisition for K scalar body masses,
with independent events, declared positive-definite covariances and
each shared acceleration observed once. At event i choose a whitening
W_i and write

$$z_i=W_iY_i,\qquad X_i(m)=W_iC(m),\qquad
P_i(m)=X_i(m)[X_i(m)^TX_i(m)]^{-1}X_i(m)^T.$$

For a finite data set D define the profile, its score, conditional score
covariance and the usual exact-rank statistic by

$$L_D(m)=-\frac12\sum_{i\in D}\|(I-P_i(m))z_i\|^2,\qquad
S_D=\nabla L_D,\qquad T_D(m)^2=S_D(m)^TC_D(m)^+S_D(m).$$

The score lies in the range of C_D. At observed rank zero use the existing
T56 convention T_D^2=0. Mathematical assertions concern exact rank,
separately from numerical rank tolerances.

Let Pi be a supplied proper joint law on (0,infinity)^K. For a candidate
m and rival t put

$$V_D(m,t)=\sum_{i\in D}\|(P_i(t)-P_i(m))z_i\|^2,$$
$$A_D(m)=\int\frac{[L_D(t)-L_D(m)]_+}
{\sqrt{1+V_D(m,t)}}\,\Pi(dt),\qquad [x]_+=\max(x,0).$$

For a fixed explicit research constant kappa>0 the proposed law is

$$Q_D(dm)=Z_D^{-1}
\exp[-T_D(m)^2/2-\kappa A_D(m)]\Pi(dm).$$

The same original Pi supplies the rival average and the final starting
law. This is a prior-dependent kernel. T62's characterization for
Pi-independent kernels is not invoked as though it applied unchanged.
No posterior-only sequential updating claim is made.

Profile differences can be computed directly as
sum_i z_i^T[P_i(t)-P_i(m)]z_i/2. This avoids subtracting two large
candidate-independent residual constants. The construction uses no
unrestricted fitted maximum; maxima enter only the limit proofs below.

## 2. Properness, bounds and bookkeeping invariance

Let E_D=sum_i ||z_i||^2. Orthogonal projectors obey
-I <= P_i(t)-P_i(m) <= I, in the Loewner order. Consequently

$$0\le V_D(m,t)\le E_D,\qquad
-E_D/2\le L_D(m)\le0,\qquad 0\le A_D(m)\le E_D/2.$$

T56's stacked-map bound gives 0<=T_D(m)^2<=E_D. The complete kernel is
therefore between exp[-(1+kappa)E_D/2] and one. All displayed integrals
are finite and measurable: graph projectors are continuous in finite
candidates and bounded, while the pseudoinverse statistic is measurable.
The normalizer is finite and strictly positive for every proper Pi,
including an atomic one. Every divergent nonnegative prior moment remains
divergent at fixed finite data. Gaussian local limits below are shape and
quantile statements, not finite-law moment assertions.

An independent measured exact-zero event adds zero to every profile
contrast, V_D, score and conditional covariance. It changes no output.
Empty and entirely zero observed data give A=T^2=0 and return Pi.
This is whole-event neutrality, not an assertion that copied observations
are independent events or that zero-valued coordinates can be deleted.

A change of whitening conjugates each event's projectors by the same
orthogonal matrix and transforms its z_i accordingly. It preserves every
inner product above. Passive units/frame changes with the complete graph,
covariance and prior transformed consistently give the same result.
Independent whole-event sign reversals also change nothing. Smooth
invertible parameter re-expressions reindex the rival integral and push
Pi forward; the profile and pairwise norm are scalars, as is T_D^2 under
its stacked-map interpretation. No parameter metric or prior density
value is chosen in this operation.

Adding a data-only constant to L changes no contrast. For disjoint
independent event sets, profile contrasts, V_D, S_D and C_D add at common
candidates. Retaining those functions and the same original Pi therefore
permits associative merging followed by one evaluation of A_D. The final
nonlinear penalty generally does not add; this is not posterior-only
sequential closure. Joint prior dependence is used as supplied, not
reconstructed from marginal priors.

## 3. Full normalized limit in the regular repeated-block experiment

Use T55's experiment: a fixed finite block of full common-acceleration
Gaussian events, K scalar masses, fixed positive-definite event covariance
shapes and fixed accelerations, with at least one nonzero acceleration.
Repeat that block independently n times. The fitting operation still
profiles each event's acceleration separately; their equality across
blocks is a calibration-world condition, not an estimator input.
Fix an interior positive truth m0 and a proper Pi whose restriction to a
neighborhood of m0 has a positive continuous Lebesgue density and no
additional singular component there. Pi needs no global density,
compact support, moments or independence between body masses.

Let mhat_n be the global profile maximum, uniquely interior with probability
tending to one. At truth write A>0 for T55's block sensitivity and
B=A+G>0 for its score variability. For any fixed kappa>0,

$$\operatorname{TV}\left(
Q_n\{\sqrt n(m-\widehat m_n)\in\cdot\mid Y\},
N_K(0,A^{-1}BA^{-1})\right)\longrightarrow0$$

in sampling probability. The sampling center has the same limit,
sqrt(n)(mhat_n-m0) => N_K(0,A^-1 B A^-1). The law concentrates at m0,
its scalar median is consistent when K=1, and actual pushforward central
intervals for fixed smooth scalar composites with nonzero gradient at
truth have asymptotically nominal coverage. Sum and parallel sum are
included. These are correct-Gaussian-world asymptotic conclusions, not
finite-noise confidence guarantees.

### Uniform profile separation is available on the full mass domain

T55 compactifies the positive mass graph by
u=(m,1)/sqrt(1+||m||^2), in a compact spherical orthant. For one nonzero
true acceleration the true mean belongs to no other candidate plane,
including a boundary plane. Fixed finite covariance geometry preserves
this separation after whitening. The expected block profile is thus
uniquely maximized at m0 and has a positive gap outside every fixed
neighborhood of m0 on that compact closure. The finite menu of empirical
scatter matrices gives the corresponding uniform profile law of large
numbers. This controls remote masses as well as finite compact subsets;
no prior-tail truncation is needed.

Choose any fixed sufficiently small neighborhood U of truth. A smaller
fixed neighborhood V0 has Pi(V0)>0 and, with probability tending to one,

$$\inf_{t\in V0,\,m\notin U}[L_n(t)-L_n(m)]\ge c_U n$$

for some c_U>0. Also E_n=O_p(n). Section 2 consequently gives

$$\inf_{m\notin U}A_n(m)
\ge\frac{c_U n\,\Pi(V0)}{\sqrt{1+E_n}}
=\Omega_p(\sqrt n).$$

The notation means a positive lower bound of that order on events of
arbitrarily high probability, with constants allowed to depend on that
probability. A fixed rival neighborhood orders false roots and all
remote candidates without selecting a root in the construction.

### The penalty disappears uniformly on the local sampling scale

For each fixed H<infinity, Taylor expansion about the profile maximum gives

$$\sup_{\|h\|\le H}
[L_n(\widehat m_n)-L_n(\widehat m_n+h/\sqrt n)]=O_p(1).$$

T55's local observed negative Hessian is at least c n I on a fixed
neighborhood, on events of probability tending to one. Together with
the global separation just established, this implies that every rival t
improving on any such local candidate lies in
B(mhat_n,C_H/sqrt(n)), on events of arbitrarily high probability.
Indeed the rival's loss from the maximum cannot exceed the candidate's
O_p(1) loss; local strong concavity then bounds its squared distance by
O_p(1/n), and global separation excludes outside rivals.

The positive gain is no larger than that same O_p(1) loss, and the
denominator is at least one. The prior density is locally bounded, so

$$\sup_{\|h\|\le H} A_n(\widehat m_n+h/\sqrt n)
\le O_p(1)\Pi\{B(\widehat m_n,C_H/\sqrt n)\}
=O_p(n^{-K/2})=o_p(1).$$

No derivative of the nonsmooth positive-part operation is required.
In particular a penalty that orders distant roots at rate sqrt(n) adds
no asymptotic local curvature or displacement on this scale.

### Normalization and tails, not just a local expansion

T56 gives the score expansion and local covariance convergence, uniformly
on bounded h,

$$T_n(\widehat m_n+h/\sqrt n)^2
=h^TAB^{-1}Ah+o_p(1).$$

The preceding penalty bound leaves this quadratic unchanged. A ball of
radius 1/sqrt(n) around mhat_n supplies normalizing mass of order at least
c_p n^-K/2 on arbitrarily high probability events, using the positive
local prior density. Outside U, the score kernel is at most one and the
pairwise penalty bound gives normalized mass at most

$$O_p(n^{K/2})\exp[-c_p\sqrt n]\longrightarrow0.$$

Within U, the local Hessian and upper conditional-covariance bounds imply
T_n(m)^2>=c n||m-mhat_n||^2, as in T57. Since exp(-kappa A_n)<=1,
this controls rescaled local tails by Gaussian tails after division by
the preceding normalizer. The prior density is bounded on U and locally
continuous at truth. Local expansion, local tail control and global
separation therefore give the asserted full normalized TV limit.
The fitted-center sampling limit is T55's; the delta method and the
matching pushforward quantiles then prove composite coverage.

## 4. Fixed finite experiments under common signal amplification

Use finitely many full-protocol events with arbitrary fixed SPD covariances
and deterministic accelerations R a_i, some a_i nonzero, while R tends to
infinity. Keep the same prior regularity at the interior truth as above.
The profile compactification in T54 supplies a uniform global gap of
order R^2, local negative Hessian of order R^2 and a center of error
order R^-1. Total whitened energy is O_p(R^2).

The proof of section 3 now gives

$$\inf_{m\notin U}A_R(m)=\Omega_p(R),\qquad
\sup_{\|h\|\le H}A_R(\widehat m_R+h/R)=O_p(R^{-K})=o_p(1).$$

The first bound uses a fixed positive-prior true neighborhood and
V<=E_R. The second uses O_p(1) maximum local gain, local Hessian R^2
and improving-rival prior mass O_p(R^-K). A ball of radius 1/R supplies
normalizer of order at least R^-K. Outer mass is
O_p(R^K)exp(-c_p R), and the local score bound controls the remaining
rescaled tails. Hence the full law has the same centered R-scale Gaussian
TV limit as T54's fixed-experiment small-noise theorem, with the matching
sampling center and smooth-composite interval limits. The extra graph
noise term in T55's Godambe covariance is lower order in this regime.
This proves amplification as well as replication; no covariance-size
mixture or non-Gaussian claim is included.

## 5. What pairwise normalization establishes on T65's history

First make a separate, explicit experiment declaration: replace T65's
full half-Cauchy by that same prior conditioned on a fixed positive compact
interval I containing both m0=6/5 and mf=4/15 in its interior, for example
I=[1/10,10]. This prior is fixed before data and retains both roots.
It is a new supplied-prior experiment, not a data-dependent restriction
or a proof about the original untruncated law.

T65's null events have e_i=1/i. On any fixed positive compact I,
uniformly in candidates m,t in I,

$$\|P_i(t)-P_i(m)\|\le C_I/i,\qquad
\sum_{i\ {\rm null}}V_i(m,t)=O_{a.s.}(1).$$

The second claim follows from
sum_i E[i^-2||Z_i||^2]<infinity and nonnegative summands.
T65 also proves almost-sure convergence of the null centered profile,
score and conditional-covariance fields, with the needed derivatives,
on I. If M_N is the informative event count, their bounded contributions
perturb the informative profile/score geometry only by O_a.s.(1), while
the informative fields and their pairwise V sum have order M_N.

The informative profile has its unique maximum at m0 on I. Uniformly on
I outside any fixed true neighborhood, its profile gap is of order M_N.
For a fixed rival neighborhood V0 of truth, the full pairwise denominator
is O_p(sqrt(M_N)), and consequently

$$\inf_{m\in I\setminus U}A_N(m)=\Omega_p(\sqrt{M_N}).$$

The supported profile maximum is uniquely interior near truth with
probability tending to one. Local Hessian order M_N and the bounded
null-field perturbations give O_p(1) maximum gain on its M_N^-1/2
sampling scale. Improving rivals occupy prior mass O_p(M_N^-1/2), so
the local penalty tends to zero. The section 3 normalization and tail
proof, with n replaced by M_N and the full domain replaced by the
fixed supplied support I, proves the complete normalized scalar Godambe
TV limit and central-interval coverage. In particular the false-root
mass tends to zero and the median converges to truth in this compact-
supplied-prior experiment.

### The original full-prior T65 case is still open

With the unchanged full half-Cauchy, the same compact rival neighborhood
gives a diverging lower bound for the penalty at false-basin candidates.
It does **not** bound the penalty at true-basin candidates, because that
penalty still integrates every remote rival. Contributions from rivals
outside a fixed compact set require a separate argument. A false-basin
lower bound alone supplies no normalized false/true basin ratio.

T65's covariance shapes do not have the uniform compactified field control
used in section 3. No global profile separation or remote-prior tail bound
is inferred from its compact-region calculations. This theorem therefore
asserts neither a full-prior repair of T65, a full two-point limit, a
median limit for that original experiment nor a result on all identifying
heterogeneous histories. The same caveat applies to a local rival-only
calculation: omitting remote rivals would define a different criterion.

## Scope and remaining freedom

**T68 continuation.** A dimension-free self-normalized Gaussian comparison
bound now controls remote rivals under T65's original full half-Cauchy.
The pairwise penalty vanishes uniformly on the true sampling neighborhoods,
the conditional true-basin Gaussian law is retained, and the named false
basin probability tends to zero. Full posterior-tail control, unconditional
concentration and a global median limit remain open. This strengthens the
local part of section 5's caveat without changing its compact-prior proof.

The pairwise construction avoids reducing all candidate comparisons to
one global prior-variance scalar. Its regular limits permit a full proper
prior; their validity does not depend on cutting off its remote masses.
Its compact T65 result separately shows how pair-specific normalization
can avoid the already proved local trace-dilution mechanism.

None of these results forces the positive part, rival measure, denominator,
finite offset, prefactor or complete global rule. Every fixed kappa>0 has
the named limits and generally gives a different finite law. The new use
of the supplied prior and the admissible experiment state remain questions
under G24/G26/G27 and L19. No signal population is learned, but absence
of that borrowing operation is not a proof of permission for this kernel.
Arbitrary weak histories, unrestricted geometry, cross-event dependence,
other noise laws, finite-noise calibration and uniqueness remain open.
