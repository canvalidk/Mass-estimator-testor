## Standard mathematics

Profiling a likelihood over the split of a total is infimal convolution of the two negative
log-likelihoods (epigraphical addition; Rockafellar 1970, §5): the tropical counterpart of the
convolution of laws that R-2026-10-28-1 clause (b) asks of the composite's law. With one part
unconstrained the profiled statistic has the boundary law of Chernoff (1954).

## Statement

Let $K_1$, $K_2$ be the curves (T73) of two bodies measured independently. Define

$$K_{\rm lump}(M)=\inf_{m_1+m_2=M}\big[K_1(m_1)+K_2(m_2)\big],\qquad
K_{\rm force}(M)=\inf_{1/m_1+1/m_2=1/M}\big[K_1(m_1)+K_2(m_2)\big],$$

the infimum over $m_1,m_2\in[0,\infty]$.

**(1) Exact inputs add.** As every σ shrinks on readings that fit $m_1^*$ and $m_2^*$ exactly,
$K_{\rm lump}$ tends to 0 at $m_1^*+m_2^*$ and to infinity elsewhere; the same for $K_{\rm force}$ at the
reduced mass. This is clause (a) of R-2026-10-28-1.

**(2) Gaussian curves add their variances.** If $K_j(m)=(m-\mu_j)^2/s_j^2$ then
$K_{\rm lump}(M)=(M-\mu_1-\mu_2)^2/(s_1^2+s_2^2)$.

**(3) The two compositions are one operation under the swap.** Swapping force and acceleration in every
reading (with the covariance blocks) turns each curve into the curve of the inverse mass, and
$K_{\rm force}$ of the original readings is $K_{\rm lump}$ of the swapped ones at $1/M$, exactly.

**(4) A body with no evidence leaves the total one-sided.** If $K_2\equiv0$ (T73 (2)) then
$K_{\rm lump}(M)=\min_{m_1\le M}K_1(m_1)$: every total above the measured body's best mass is fully
compatible, and a lighter total costs what the measured body pays to be that light. A body at
$5\pm0.5$ kg beside an unmeasured one gives the set $[4.02,\infty)$ at the $\chi^2_1$ threshold.

**(5) Cut at the $\chi^2$ threshold, the composite set under-covers at weak signal.** Two bodies of mass
2 and 3, five 3D readings each, one instrument: the set $\{M:K_{\rm lump}(M)-K_{1,\min}-K_{2,\min}\le3.84\}$
contains the true total in 0.840, 0.910 and 0.943 of 300 series at per-reading signal 1, 3 and 6 noise
units. The raw curve's weak-signal shortfall (T73, "What it does not give") passes to the composite.

**(6) The composite with a kept promise.** Fix a total $M$ and a split $(m_1,M-m_1)$. Given each
reading's along-line part at its own body's candidate mass, the leftovers of both bodies are independent
standard normal vectors (T75 (1), body by body), so the statistic
$K_{\rm lump}(M)-K_{1,\min}-K_{2,\min}$ has a known conditional law and a p-value $p(m_1;M)$ that is exact if
the split is the true one. The largest $p(m_1;M)$ over all splits tests the total, and the set of totals
it keeps contains the true total with at least the stated probability. Measured: at the true split it
keeps the true total in 0.973 and 0.947 of 150 pairs of bodies at signal 1 and 3, where the raw set of
(5) keeps 0.900 and 0.920 on the same series. At strong signal the kept totals are a one-parameter
set, not a two-parameter one: 2.3 standard deviations below the best total the largest p-value is 0.022
(a one-parameter test gives 0.021, a two-parameter one 0.071), and 0.040 above. One body at $5\pm0.5$
beside a body with no evidence, under a zero-safe weight, is tested one-sidedly, $p=1-\Phi((5-M)/0.5)$:
the kept totals are 4.18 upward, where the raw curve of (4) gives 4.02 upward. The common-force version
is the same test on the swapped readings, by (3).

## Proof

(6) a largest p-value over a set of hypotheses that contains the true one is at least the true one's; the
statistic's law at the true split is T75 (1) applied to the two bodies together. The one-sided law is
Chernoff's (1954) with one part unconstrained. (1) each $K_j$ tends to the indicator of $m_j^*$. (2) minimise a sum of two quadratics along a line.
(3) $K$ of a swapped reading at $w$ is $K$ of the reading at $1/w$ (T5 at the level of T36's subspaces),
and $1/m_1+1/m_2=1/M$ is $w_1+w_2=W$. (4) set $K_2=0$; $m_2=M-m_1\ge0$ is the only constraint.

## Against the composition rows

- **No starting law enters,** so B26's obstruction (no starting law of the family is reproduced by
  lumping two bodies that have it; O6, 17 Nov) has nothing to act on. This is by construction, not a
  result.
- **Clause (b)** of R-2026-10-28-1 says the composite's law is computed from the parts' laws. Here the
  composite's curve is computed from the parts' curves, by (1)–(4). But by (5) and (6) a set with a kept
  promise is not computed from the two curves alone: (6) conditions on each reading's along-line part
  and so needs the readings, or their within-span summaries (E13 says the 2×2 matrix of dot products
  and the count are enough on one instrument; not checked). What a set or a normalised law loses, and
  that the unnormalised curve keeps it on one instrument, is T77 (4).
- **Clause (c)** (a new measurement of the composite is consistent with it, as coverage) is what (5)
  measures for the raw set, where it fails at weak signal, and what (6) repairs for the total. (6) is a
  test of a total given two bodies' readings. It is not yet a rule for combining a composite's result
  with a later direct measurement of the composite; that is the same retained-state question.
