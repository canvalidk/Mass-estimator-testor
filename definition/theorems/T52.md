# T52: Infinite active information does not reveal an unknown signal allocation

**From:** T41's Gaussian nuisance-mixture testing method; L1/L2's allowed zero and nonzero fixed signals, the unrestricted reading type and L19's arbitrary regimes. No sign-invariance premise is needed.
**Test:** `tests/test_sparse_identification.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). Even divergent energy of every positive power, and divergent known-magnitude directional information, do not guarantee one estimator is consistent for every unknown fixed allocation. This strengthens T41's scope warning without selecting an identification criterion or changing L11. **Harvested 7 Nov from the exploration branch `codex/ours32-consistency` (Codex, 3–4 Nov) as input; not checked by Claude.**

## 1. Fixed worlds and the question being tested

Use independent 1D Gaussian readings with identity covariances, full
subspaces, fixed windows and the same fixed supplied prior at every mass.
For m>0 put u_m=(m,1)/sqrt(1+m^2). Fix b>0 and a deterministic
set S of active reading indices. The allowed world is

$$Y_i=b\,1_{\{i\in S\}}u_m+Z_i,\qquad
Z_i\stackrel{\rm ind}{\sim}N(0,I_2).$$

Every active mean has positive force and acceleration; there are no
unknown signs in this construction. Every inactive true pair is zero-zero,
while its observed reading still has the declared Gaussian error.
This is not exact-zero observed padding. The active set is not supplied.

If S is infinite, its prefix count K_N diverges and, for every q>0,

$$\sum_{i\le N}r_i^q=b^q K_N\longrightarrow\infty,
\qquad
A_N=\sum_{i\le N}\frac{r_i^4}{1+r_i^2}
=\frac{b^4}{1+b^2}K_N\longrightarrow\infty.$$

Thus the issue survives replacing total quadratic energy by quartic
energy, or by this frequently useful known-magnitude information scale.
The theorem asks whether one measurable estimator can be consistent at
every positive mass for every fixed infinite S. It does not ask whether
the finite-sample mean model is structurally identifiable, or whether an
oracle that knows S can recover m.

## 2. A proof-only sparse Gaussian mixture

Sparse Gaussian mixture testing is standard; for an introduction to its
dependence on both allocation sparsity and amplitude, see
[Donoho and Jin (2004), section 1.1](https://doi.org/10.1214/009053604000000265).
We do not import that paper's detection boundary: the nonidentical,
fixed-amplitude stream and its explicit bound are derived here.
As in T41, the mixture is an auxiliary measure over allowed deterministic
worlds, not a physical signal population or a nuisance prior passed to an
estimator.

For the proof only choose independent activations B_i with

$$P(B_i=1)=p_i=\frac{c}{i+1},\qquad 0<c\le1,\quad i\ge1.$$

Their sum of probabilities diverges, so independence and the second
Borel--Cantelli lemma imply S={i:B_i=1} is infinite almost surely.
In contrast sum_i p_i^2=c^2(pi^2/6-1) is finite.

Let phi_2 be the pure-noise density. The active Gaussian shift and the
activation mixture have likelihood ratios

$$L_u(y)=e^{b u^Ty-b^2/2},\qquad
R_{u,i}(y)=1-p_i+p_iL_u(y).$$

For unit u,v with rho=u^T v, the Gaussian moment-generating function gives

$$E_0L_u=1,\quad E_0L_uL_v=e^{\rho b^2},\qquad
E_0R_{u,i}R_{v,i}=1+p_i^2(e^{\rho b^2}-1).$$

In particular chi2(q_(u,i)||phi_2)=p_i^2(e^(b^2)-1).
Let P_(u,N) be the product activation mixture, and define

$$A_N^*=\prod_{i\le N}[1+p_i^2(e^{b^2}-1)],\qquad
B_N^*=\prod_{i\le N}[1+p_i^2(e^{\rho b^2}-1)].$$

Products are valid because the proof activations and errors are independent.
By Cauchy--Schwarz, relative to the pure-noise product measure,

$$\operatorname{TV}(P_{u,N},P_{v,N})
\le\sqrt{(A_N^*-B_N^*)/2}.$$

For positive masses, rho>0 and B_N^*>=1. Also log(1+x)<=x gives the
uniform-in-N bound

$$\operatorname{TV}(P_{u,N},P_{v,N})
\le\min\left\{1,
\sqrt{\frac{\exp\{(e^{b^2}-1)c^2(\pi^2/6-1)\}-1}{2}}\right\}.
$$

At b=c=1/2 this upper bound is 0.1530672599563... . Every test between,
for example, masses 1 and 2 consequently has equal-weight average error
at least 0.4234663700218... for every N. This is a testing lower bound for
all measurable procedures, including independently randomized ones.

## 3. One fixed infinite allocation measure gives the contradiction

Suppose an estimator were consistent for every fixed infinite S at both
masses 1 and 2. Conditional on such S its error probability at either mass
would tend to zero. The one infinite Bernoulli product measure above gives
infinite S with probability one. Error probabilities lie between zero and
one; dominated convergence over that fixed measure therefore makes each
of the two mixed error probabilities tend to zero.

Choosing the nearer mass from the estimator would then distinguish the
two product mixtures consistently, contradicting section 2. Thus

**For every estimator there exist a positive mass and a fixed infinite
active set S on which it is not consistent, despite all the divergences
in section 1.** No per-reading sign invariance or assumed randomized push
is needed. This is stronger than a minimax bound that chooses a different
support at each N; the proof uses one probability measure on fixed streams.

The conclusion is not that a single fixed known pair of Gaussian mean
histories is indistinguishable to every estimator. Conditional on knowing
the history, ordinary Gaussian mean information can diverge. The failure
is the universal quantifier over unknown allowed nuisance allocations.

## 4. Known-allocation contrast

For any fixed infinite S, an oracle given its active indices can form

$$T_N=\sum_{i\le N:i\in S}Y_iY_i^T,\qquad
E T_N=K_N(I_2+b^2u_mu_m^T),$$

$$E\|T_N-E T_N\|_F^2=6K_N(1+b^2).$$

The mean eigenvalue gap is K_N b^2 and the relative fluctuation tends to
zero. Its principal line is therefore consistent as K_N tends to infinity.
This construction respects independent reading sign reversal. With the
all-positive convention an oracle active sample mean is also consistent,
but that particular operation is not independently sign invariant.

The support is precisely the extra information absent from section 1.
The oracle contrast does not authorize a support input, infer one signal
from another or declare a population of setups. It demonstrates why
divergent oracle information is not an operational identification theorem
under the original information set.

## 5. Consequence and numerical reference

T42's aggregate condition remains a sharp theorem for its named Gaussian
procedure and a sufficient benchmark. This result does not prove it is
necessary for every individual fixed history, nor does it select a
replacement for L11. A general consistency target must specify the allowed
nuisance-history class and what allocation/protocol information is known;
a latent sum that would identify direction after that information is
revealed is not sufficient by itself.

`met/sparse_identification.py` evaluates the one-reading likelihood ratio,
exact finite-prefix product moments, their testing bounds, the harmonic
schedule's uniform bound, and the separate oracle scatter identity. The
tests use independent Gaussian Hermite integration for likelihood moments,
a four-dimensional two-reading product integral, and Gaussian fourth
moments for the oracle contrast. They sample no physical signal population
and implement no mass estimator or production adapter. Bounds are ordinary
floating-point evaluations of analytic inequalities, not exact TV or
interval-certified enclosures.

L11's wording and grade, L19, the prior slot and every production rule
remain unchanged. The unknown-allocation obstruction is recorded rather
than repaired by an arbitrary assumption about the signal sequence.
