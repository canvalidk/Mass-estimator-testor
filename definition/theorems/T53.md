# T53: An attainable minimax consistency criterion for Gaussian energy classes

**From:** T41's sign-mixture lower bound and Gaussian scatter moments; T42's procedure-specific growth condition. The theorem below changes the quantifier to an explicit energy-only nuisance class.
**Test:** `tests/test_energy_minimax.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). Uniform consistency over Gaussian energy-only classes is possible exactly when Delta_N^2/(N+Delta_N) diverges. This is an estimator-independent benchmark for that class, not a necessary condition for every individual history, a full-type theorem or a replacement ruling for L11. **Harvested 7 Nov from the exploration branch `codex/ours32-consistency` (Codex, 3–4 Nov) as input; not checked by Claude.**

## 1. Specify the experiment and the uniform target

Fix a nondegenerate compact arc Theta=[theta_min,theta_max] strictly
inside (0,pi/2). For each N observe independent readings

$$Y_i=r_i u_\theta+Z_i,\qquad
u_\theta=(\sin\theta,\cos\theta),\qquad
Z_i\sim N(0,I_2),\quad 1\le i\le N.$$

The nuisance vector r is arbitrary, signed and fixed, with exact energy
sum_i r_i^2=Delta_N>=0. Define the energy-only class

$$\mathcal E_N(\Delta_N)=
\{(\theta,r):\theta\in\Theta,\ r\in\mathbb R^N,
\ \|r\|^2=\Delta_N\}.$$

Uniform consistency means that for every epsilon>0 the supremum error
probability over this class tends to zero as N tends to infinity. Directions
are unoriented lines; their projective angle is arccos(|u^T v|).
On the fixed positive arc this identifies mass m=tan(theta) continuously.
The estimator need not receive Delta_N. Allowing it that extra information
would not evade the lower bound.

**Theorem.** There exists one estimator uniformly consistent over these
classes if and only if

$$\mathcal I_N=\frac{\Delta_N^2}{N+\Delta_N}\longrightarrow\infty.$$

The principal line of the unweighted scatter matrix attains the sufficient
direction. The negative direction applies to every measurable estimator,
including independently randomized ones, on any subsequence where I_N
is bounded. It already holds for two fixed distinct angles inside Theta.

This is a rank-one rectangular Gaussian matrix-denoising experiment:
Y=u_theta r^T+Z, with signal singular value sqrt(Delta_N), two rows and N
columns. [Cai, Li and Ma (2021), Theorem 15](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume22/20-610/20-610.pdf)
studies the corresponding nonnegative left-subspace problem and its minimax
scale sqrt(N+Delta_N)/Delta_N. The elementary proof below supplies the
specific consistency statement and quantifiers used here; no distribution
of the right singular vector or physical signal population is adopted.

## 2. A uniform attainable upper bound

Put S_N=sum_i Y_iY_i^T. Gaussian second and fourth moments give, for every
theta and every allowed allocation and signs,

$$E S_N=NI_2+\Delta_Nu_\theta u_\theta^T,\qquad
E\|S_N-E S_N\|_F^2=6(N+\Delta_N).$$

The population eigenvalue gap is Delta_N. Write E_N=S_N-E S_N and let
delta_N be the projective error of a principal eigenvector of S_N.
If ||E_N||_op<Delta_N/2, the eigenvector equation and the population
spectral separation give sin(delta_N)<=2||E_N||_op/Delta_N.
If ||E_N||_op>=Delta_N/2 the same inequality is automatic, since its
right side is at least one. Hence for Delta_N>0 and 0<epsilon<=pi/2,

$$\sup_{\mathcal E_N(\Delta_N)}P\{\delta_N\ge\epsilon\}
\le\min\left\{1,
\frac{24(N+\Delta_N)}{\Delta_N^2\sin^2\epsilon}\right\}.$$

This is Markov's inequality, using the Frobenius moment to dominate the
operator norm. Eigenvalue ties can be resolved by any fixed measurable
convention; they do not affect the bound. The estimator uses only S_N.
If a positive mass point is required at every datum, use the line's positive
angle whenever it lies in (0,pi/2), and any fixed positive fallback otherwise.
Compactness of the true arc makes the fallback event vanish uniformly when
I_N diverges. No true energy, signs or allocation are estimator inputs.

## 3. A two-mixture lower bound with exact energy

Choose distinct fixed angles theta_a,theta_b in Theta, with
rho=u_a^T u_b in (0,1). For a given N and Delta_N average over independent
fair nuisance signs at the homogeneous radius sqrt(t), t=Delta_N/N.
Every sign vector in this auxiliary measure has exactly energy Delta_N.
Thus the proof mixtures are supported entirely inside the stated class.

Relative to pure-noise density phi_2, one-reading ratios are

$$L_u(y)=e^{-t/2}\cosh(\sqrt t\,u^Ty),\qquad
E_0L_uL_v=\cosh(\rho t),\quad E_0L_u^2=\cosh t.$$

Since L_u>=exp(-t/2), division by its density gives

$$\chi^2(q_v\|q_u)
=E_0\frac{(L_v-L_u)^2}{L_u}
\le2e^{t/2}[\cosh t-\cosh(\rho t)].$$

For 0<=t<=1, integrate t*sinh(st)<=s*t^2*cosh(t) from rho to 1 to obtain

$$\chi^2(q_v\|q_u)
\le e^{1/2}\cosh(1)(1-\rho^2)t^2.$$

Jensen's inequality gives KL(q_v||q_u)<=log(1+chi2(q_v||q_u)); KL adds
over independent readings. Consequently the N-reading mixtures obey

$$\operatorname{KL}(P_{v,N}\|P_{u,N})
\le e^{1/2}\cosh(1)(1-\rho^2)\frac{\Delta_N^2}{N}
\quad\text{when }\Delta_N/N\le1.$$

If their KL is bounded by K, their affinity a=int sqrt(p_u p_v) satisfies
a>=exp(-K/2): apply Jensen to E_v sqrt(p_u/p_v). Cauchy--Schwarz applied
to (sqrt(p_u)-sqrt(p_v))(sqrt(p_u)+sqrt(p_v)) then gives

$$\operatorname{TV}(P_{u,N},P_{v,N})\le\sqrt{1-e^{-K}},$$

$$\text{equal-weight binary error}\ \ge
\frac{1-\sqrt{1-e^{-K}}}{2}
=\frac{e^{-K}}{2(1+\sqrt{1-e^{-K}})}>0\quad(K<\infty).$$

Unlike a second-moment TV estimate exceeding one, this remains a strictly
positive mathematical lower bound for every finite K.

## 4. Necessity and the quantifier distinction

If I_N does not diverge, extract a subsequence with I_N<=M<infinity.
Solving Delta_N^2<=M(N+Delta_N) gives
Delta_N<=M+sqrt(MN). Thus on a further tail of that subsequence
Delta_N<=C sqrt(N) for some fixed finite C, and t<=1.
Section 3 bounds KL by the fixed constant
K=exp(1/2)*cosh(1)*(1-rho^2)*C^2.

An estimator uniformly consistent on E_N would be consistent under both
mixtures. Choosing the nearer of theta_a and theta_b from its output would
give a consistent test, contrary to the fixed positive error lower bound.
This proves necessity, including along a bad subsequence. The same argument
works if the estimator knows the energy, since it is identical at both
hypotheses and for every mixed nuisance vector.

This is explicitly a uniform/minimax assertion over a triangular array
class. It does not produce one fixed infinite nuisance stream on which
every estimator fails, or imply that every history with bounded I_N is
individually unestimable. T41 and T52 use separate fixed-stream dominated
convergence arguments for their different universal promises.

T52 also shows why divergent sum r_i^4/(1+r_i^2) cannot simply replace
the energy-only condition as a universal target when allocation is unknown.
Conversely, a known protocol or allocation can enable recovery outside the
unrestricted energy-class benchmark. Such information changes the class
or the information set and requires its own authority.

## Numerical and physical scope

`met/energy_minimax.py` evaluates the one-reading relative chi-square bound,
its product KL/affinity certificate, and the uniform scatter probability
bound. Independent Gaussian quadrature checks the relative chi-square and
KL integrals and the scatter fourth moment. The log binary-error bound is
retained when its exponential underflows; outputs are floating-point
evaluations, not certified enclosures or exact TV.

This supplies an attainable Gaussian consistency target under named
quantifiers. It does not require a signal distribution, infer a signal
from another reading, prove uncertainty calibration or uniqueness, cover
the full heterogeneous covariance/subspace/noise type, or prescribe a
replacement wording or grade for L11. No production rule or ruling changes.
