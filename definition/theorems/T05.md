## Statement

$R(Y,X)=1/R(X,Y)$

## Proof or support

The premise behind the swap is the equal-information symmetry of force and
acceleration, stated in R-2026-09-24-1 and R-2026-09-07-1. The argument below
checks that symmetry for the **declared median** of the current law. It does
not rederive the choice of the median from T35 (which itself uses the swap),
or pass through T4's former ratio-of-means readout.

### The two coordinate forms of the swap

Write $y=(F;a)$, with the same declared subspace $N$ in both channels, and
$C_c(\phi)=(c\sin\phi\,N;\cos\phi\,N)$ as in `equation.md`.
There are two equivalent ways to express the symmetry:

- A **physical channel swap** uses $S=(\begin{smallmatrix}0&I\\I&0\end{smallmatrix})$,
  $y'=Sy$, $\Sigma'=S\Sigma S^\top$, and $c'=1/c$. It reads mass as
  $m'=1/m$. With $\phi'=\pi/2-\phi$ modulo $\pi$,
  $C_{1/c}(\phi')=c^{-1}SC_c(\phi)$.
- At the **same declared centre**, swap the dimensionless channels using
  $A_c=(\begin{smallmatrix}0&cI\\c^{-1}I&0\end{smallmatrix})$,
  $y'=A_cy$, $\Sigma'=A_c\Sigma A_c^\top$, and $c'=c$. Then
  $C_c(\phi')=A_cC_c(\phi)$ and $m'=c^2/m$. This is the brief's
  fixed-centre form; raw channel exchange at an arbitrary fixed $c$ would
  not be that transformation. L3 supplies the corresponding unit scaling.

Both maps preserve $N$, its dimension $k$, and the positive-mass candidate
interval (with its ends reversed). On the whole projective line, reducing
$\phi'$ modulo $\pi$ may multiply a displayed candidate matrix by $-1$;
the candidate subspace and all quantities below are unchanged by that sign.

### Every factor of the law transforms

In either form, write $y'=Ay$, $\Sigma'=A\Sigma A^\top$, and
$C'(\phi')=aAC(\phi)$ for a nonzero scalar $a$ ($a=1/c$ or $1$ above,
with the possible projective representative sign just noted).
For $G=C^\top\Sigma^{-1}C$ and $b=C^\top\Sigma^{-1}y$,
$$
 G'=a^2G,\qquad b'=ab,\qquad H'(\phi')=b'^\top G'^{-1}b'=H(\phi).
$$
The explained energy $E$ in the stacked declared space is also invariant:
the change of data and covariance preserves the noise-metric projection
onto that space. Hence $Q'_+=E'-\max_{\phi'\in[0,\pi/2]}H'(\phi')=Q_+$.
Also $\kappa'=k-\tfrac12=\kappa$, so $g'=\min(1,\kappa'/Q'_+)=g$
(including the convention $g=1$ at zero misfit). Therefore
$\ell'(\phi')=\ell(\phi)$ for each reading's log factor.

Here the prime on $\ell'$ denotes the transformed function. Differentiating
with respect to the new angle gives
$\partial_{\phi'}\ell'=-\partial_\phi\ell$ and
$\partial^2_{\phi'}\ell'=\partial^2_\phi\ell$. The whole-line maximizers
correspond under the same reflection. At corresponding selected maximizers,
$\mathcal H'=-\sum_i\partial^2_{\phi'}\ell'_i=\mathcal H$ and
$J'=\sum_i(\partial_{\phi'}\ell'_i)^2=J$, so $\eta'=\eta$, including
the $J=0$ branch. A unique maximizing projective line is sufficient for this
selection condition. For ties the argument requires an equivariant choice,
or that the tied choices give the same $\eta$; it does not prove that an
arbitrary numerical tie rule has that property.

For the same-centre map $T_c(m)=c^2/m$, L4 is the probability-law identity
$$
 \pi_c(T_c(m))\,|T_c'(m)|
 =\pi_c(c^2/m)\frac{c^2}{m^2}=\pi_c(m).
$$
For physical exchange, the corresponding identity is
$\pi_{1/c}(u)=\pi_c(1/u)/u^2$: it follows from L4 and L3 for the same
declared shape, and holds directly for the current half-Cauchy. The data
factor $\exp(\eta\sum_i\ell_i)$ has already been shown to compose with
these maps. Multiplication by the prior and normalization thus make the
transformed law exactly the image of the original law under $T_c$, or
under $m\mapsto1/m$ for physical exchange.

### The median transforms with the law

For a continuous strictly increasing CDF $F$ and the decreasing map $T_c$,
the image CDF is $F_T(t)=1-F(c^2/t)$. Thus $F(q)=1/2$ implies
$F_T(c^2/q)=1/2$. The current half-Cauchy is positive on $(0,\infty)$ and
the finite-data factor is positive and bounded, so its law has a unique
median. Consequently the fixed-centre median becomes $c^2/q$; physical
exchange with $c'=1/c$ gives $q'=1/q$, which is the unchanged Statement.

For a more general law with a median interval $[a,b]$, the image interval
is $[c^2/b,c^2/a]$. The midpoint in $\ln m$, $\sqrt{ab}$, transforms
accordingly. A lower-quantile convention on a flat CDF need not do so;
median uniqueness or a swap-equivariant interval convention is needed.
