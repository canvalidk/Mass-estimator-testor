# T43: Fitted-score calibration needs a leverage condition

**From:** T18, T31, T41, T42; the stated flat Gaussian submodel of L1/L13/L18. The quoted-interval convention is R-2026-09-30-7. The spectral control is a research construction, not an adopted equation.
**Test:** `tests/test_leverage_calibration.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 3 Nov 2026 (Codex). A sampling limit, an asymptotic undercoverage counterexample for the current Bartlett rule, and a data-only Z-safe comparison achieving the Gaussian coverage target. Exact finite-N form and full-type calibration remain unforced (G3/G26).

## Standard mathematics and scope

This is the distinction between a model-based score variance and a covariance
estimate using fitted residuals, with leverage affecting the latter. It is
the issue behind regression leverage corrections, not a new meaning of
uncertainty; compare MacKinnon and White,
[Some heteroskedasticity-consistent covariance matrix estimators with improved finite sample properties](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-4076(85)90158-7).
The exact Gaussian score distribution and the limits below are derived
directly for this experiment; no regression correction is imported as an axiom.

Use T42's independent standardized 1D Gaussian pairs, common noise ratio,
fixed interior true angle, and fixed proper prior with positive continuous
density near truth. Amplitudes and signs are deterministic, with arbitrary
allocation across readings. Let

$$\Delta_N=\sum_i r_i^2,\quad B_N=N+\Delta_N,\qquad
\Delta_N^2/B_N\to\infty.$$

Write H for the observed scatter eigengap, J for squared scores at the
whole-line fitted angle, and $K=\eta H=\min(H,H^2/J)$ with the current J = 0
fallback. This is the **flat Gaussian** procedure, not finite-ν ours32 or
data-dependent misfit weights. Intervals are equal-tailed quantiles of its
continuous law; monotone conversion from angle to mass preserves coverage.

## 1. The fitted point's sampling limit does not need diffuse leverage

In the true parallel/perpendicular basis, take $X_i=r_i+\xi_i$ and
$W_i=\zeta_i$, absorbing fixed signs into symmetric Gaussian errors. The
true-angle score is $S_N=\sum_iX_iW_i$. Conditional on the parallel vector,

$$S_N\mid X\sim N(0,Q_N),\qquad
Q_N=\sum_iX_i^2\sim\chi_N^2(\Delta_N).$$

Equivalently $S_N\overset d=\sqrt{Q_N}\,G$ with an independent standard
normal G. Since $E Q_N=B_N$ and $\operatorname{Var}Q_N=2N+4\Delta_N$,
$Q_N/B_N\to1$ whenever $B_N\to\infty$. Thus

$$S_N/\sqrt{B_N}\Rightarrow N(0,1).$$

The exact characteristic function and fourth moment are

$$E e^{itS_N}=(1+t^2)^{-N/2}
\exp\left[-\frac{\Delta_Nt^2}{2(1+t^2)}\right],$$
$$E S_N^4=3B_N^2+6N+12\Delta_N.$$

They depend on total energy, not its allocation. T42's scatter calculation
and the two-dimensional eigenvector formula give

$$\widehat\theta_N-\theta_0
=S_N/\Delta_N+o_p(\sqrt{B_N}/\Delta_N),$$
$$\frac{\Delta_N(\widehat\theta_N-\theta_0)}{\sqrt{B_N}}
\Rightarrow N(0,1).$$

This holds without an iid signal or maximum-leverage condition, including
fixed-N amplification. The true leading angular sampling variance is
$B_N/\Delta_N^2$.

Given the data, the exact quadratic kernel of T42 with $K_N\to\infty$
has a local normal limit centred at the fitted line. Its equal-tailed
interval endpoints are
$\widehat\theta_N\pm z/\sqrt{K_N}+o_p(K_N^{-1/2})$,
where z is the desired standard-normal quantile. This statement about the
law's local shape does not identify its sampling calibration.

## 2. Why the fitted J can miss the sampling variance

Put $\pi_i=r_i^2/\Delta_N$ and $s_i=X_iW_i$. Under the information condition,
the fitted-score vector has the linearization

$$s(\widehat\theta_N)=s-\pi S_N+o_p(\sqrt{B_N})$$

in Euclidean norm. To check the remainder, use the exact score rotation
in T42. Its extra linear term involves
$\sum_i[(X_i^2-W_i^2)-r_i^2]^2=O_p(B_N)$;
the fitted-angle rate makes its contribution $o_p(\sqrt{B_N})$.
The cosine error is $o_p(\sqrt{B_N})$ because $\delta_N\to0$ and
$\|s\|=O_p(\sqrt{B_N})$. The cubic sine remainder is at most
$O_p(|\widehat\theta_N-\theta_0|^3\Delta_N)=o_p(\sqrt{B_N})$.
Replacing the fitted angle by $S_N/\Delta_N$ costs the same order because
$\sum_i\pi_i^2\le1$.

If $\max_i\pi_i\to0$, the exact Gaussian fourth moments show
$\sum_i s_i^2/B_N\to1$; its variance divided by $B_N^2$ is
$(8N+16\Delta_N+2\sum_i r_i^4)/B_N^2\to0$.
The fitting subtraction has squared norm
$S_N^2\sum_i\pi_i^2=o_p(B_N)$. Hence J estimates the target:

$$J_N/B_N\to1.$$

The cap is asymptotically harmless for the resulting width:
$K_N\sim\Delta_N^2/B_N$, so nominal asymptotic coverage follows.
This is a sufficient leverage condition, not a necessary condition for
nominal coverage after capping. A sole dominant reading in the much stronger
regime $\Delta_N/N\to\infty$ is one exception: its self-fitting J can vanish
relative to Δ while the cap gives the correct raw width.

## 3. An identifying Gaussian sequence with limiting undercoverage

Let $N\to\infty$, one true amplitude have square $\Delta_N=N/\tau$, and all
other true signals be zero, with fixed $\tau>0$. This is allowed heterogeneity,
with correct independent unit Gaussian errors. Its T42 information grows
linearly in N, so law and median consistency hold.

For the dominant reading let W be its perpendicular Gaussian error. The
other readings' product-noise score, divided by $\sqrt N$, tends to an
independent standard normal G. Part 1 and direct fitted-score expansion give

$$h_N=\sqrt{\Delta_N}(\widehat\theta_N-\theta_0)
\Rightarrow W+\sqrt\tau G,$$
$$J_N/\Delta_N\Rightarrow\tau(1+G^2),\qquad H_N/\Delta_N\to1.$$

Indeed the dominant fitted score divided by $\sqrt\Delta_N$ tends to
$W-h=-\sqrt\tau G$, while the null readings' squared-score sum divided by
Δ tends to τ. The same G therefore moves the fitted line and its quoted
width; treating those two limits as independent would be incorrect.

For a central nominal probability p, put $z=\Phi^{-1}((1+p)/2)$. The exact
limiting coverage is

$$C_\tau(p)=\int_{\mathbb R}\varphi(g)\left[
\Phi\left(z\sqrt{\max(1,\tau(1+g^2))}-\sqrt\tau g\right)
-\Phi\left(-z\sqrt{\max(1,\tau(1+g^2))}-\sqrt\tau g\right)
\right]dg.$$

At $\tau=1/4$, $\Delta_N=4N$ and

$$C_{1/4}(0.95)=0.9272811852285586\ldots<0.95.$$

Independent quadratures, conditioning on G and on h respectively, check
this number. It is an asymptotic coverage calculation, not a simulation
ranking or a non-identifying null example. It fails both exact 95% coverage
and an at-least-95% confidence guarantee. At τ = 1 coverage instead tends
to 0.9602930310. The cap alone does not establish conservativeness across
all signal allocations. T42's TV comparison also transfers this identified
regime's local normal quantiles to its stated T40 research maps. They
therefore inherit this coverage failure with their stated scales; the
tail-preserving shape change does not correct the fitted-J target here.

### The failure can occur in one fixed reading history

The triangular-array example above can also be embedded into prefixes of
a single deterministic signal stream. Fix an integer $K>1$, put $n_k=K^k$
and $r_{n_k}^2=4(1-1/K)n_k$, with all other signals zero. Then
$\Delta_{n_k}=4(n_k-1)$. For $n_k\le N<n_{k+1}$, eventually

$$\mathcal I_N=\frac{\Delta_N^2}{N+\Delta_N}
\ge\frac{4N}{K(K+4)}\longrightarrow\infty.$$

At prefixes $n_k$, τ tends to 1/4 and the ranked signal shares tend to
$p_j=(1-1/K)K^{-(j-1)}$. The score linearization in part 2, truncation of
this summable profile and Gaussian second-moment bounds give the joint limits

$$h_K=\sqrt\tau G+\sum_{j\ge1}\sqrt{p_j}W_j,\qquad
j_K=\tau+\sum_{j\ge1}(\sqrt{p_j}W_j-p_jh_K)^2,$$

for independent standard normals $G,W_1,W_2,\ldots$. Here h is the fitted
error multiplied by $\sqrt\Delta$, and j is J divided by Δ. The null
product-noise contribution gives τ; the tail of the displayed residual
vector is controlled in mean square by the summable p profile.

As K increases, the profile converges to a sole dominant share in $\ell^1$.
Under this Gaussian coupling, $h_K\to W_1+\sqrt\tau G$ in mean square and
$j_K\to\tau(1+G^2)$ in mean absolute error. The coverage boundary has
probability zero conditional on G, so the limiting coverages approach
$C_{1/4}(0.95)<0.95$. Some finite fixed integer K therefore already gives
strict undercoverage along the subsequence $n_k$. This is a fixed history
with identifying information growth at every prefix, not only a new
experimental design selected at each N.

For comparison, if a fixed number M of equal signal shares dominate and
$\Delta_N/N\to\infty$, the fitted J divided by Δ tends to
$V=\chi_{M-1}^2/M$, independently of the standardized fitted error.
The cap then gives coverage
$E[2\Phi(z\sqrt{\max(1,V)})-1]\ge p$. For p = 0.95 this is
0.9550110583 at M = 2 and 0.9566739976 at M = 3. Such overcoverage by
itself need not violate a conservative interpretation of uncertainty;
the mixed-regime undercoverage above is the stronger counterexample.

## 4. A data-only, Z-safe Gaussian control reaches the target

Let $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge0$ be the observed scatter eigenvalues,
$H=\lambda_1-\lambda_2$. Consider the research control

$$\eta_{\rm sp}=H/\lambda_1,\qquad
K_{\rm sp}=H^2/\lambda_1.$$

At zero scatter choose η = 1 and K = 0; at a positive isotropic tie η = 0
and the law is again the prior. These are not rejection branches. The
positive-semidefinite spectrum gives $0\le\eta_{\rm sp}\le1$.

T41's scatter bound and the eigenvalue perturbation inequality give

$$\lambda_1=N+\Delta_N+O_p(\sqrt{N+\Delta_N}),\qquad
\lambda_1/B_N\to1.$$

Together with $H_N/\Delta_N\to1$ this implies
$K_{\rm sp}\sim\Delta_N^2/B_N\to\infty$. The local-law calculation and
the universal fitted-point CLT now yield

$$ (\widehat\theta_N-\theta_0)\sqrt{K_{\rm sp}}
\Rightarrow N(0,1).$$

Every fixed central probability p therefore has asymptotic nominal coverage
for this control under every deterministic amplitude array in T42's
identifying Gaussian regime. Fixed-N amplification is included.

An exact-zero reading adds the zero matrix, so all spectral quantities and
the resulting law are Z-invariant. Independent per-reading sign reversals
leave the scatter unchanged; swapping channels conjugates it by a
permutation. Changes of physical units and declared SDs leave standardized
data unchanged. The correction uses neither a caller-supplied N nor a true
signal amplitude. It uses the observed common-mass geometry.

This proves attainability within the Gaussian submodel. It does not select
the spectral expression uniquely at finite N, justify it for arbitrary
covariances/subspaces or finite-ν factors, or satisfy all remaining premises.
For example the unweighted Gaussian reading factors retain their separate
T30 misalignment problem. The control is not a new production default.
Its use of absolute projected energy as calibration state under L19 also
remains unsorted. Being data-only and Z-safe does not itself settle that
permission or make an aggregate signal-size inference admissible.

## Sort and authority

The asymptotic sandwich target in this model is $B_N=N+\Delta_N$.
Fitted J estimates it under additional leverage conditions; the universal
claim that raw fitted J is forced by Z does not follow. Z excludes a naive
Bessel count multiplier, but the observed spectrum supplies a different
Z-safe estimator of the target. Thus the coverage defect names missing
conditions in the fitted-score calibration argument, not an incompatibility
between Z and attainable Gaussian uncertainty.

R-2026-09-29-4 asks for uncertainty to remain a true statement. It does not
by itself demand full Gaussian shape or exact coverage. R-2026-09-30-7
nevertheless makes the quoted widened-law interval the tester's standard.
Part 3 fails even the weaker at-least-nominal coverage target for that
interval. No premise, output interface or ruling is rewritten here.

G26 records the leverage/variance-estimation issue in the long-series
argument; G3 still concerns the forced finite-N body. T42's consistency
result remains valid. The new control shows one way to meet the Gaussian
coverage target without an added input, while its global selection and
full-type justification remain open.
