# T48: A normalized Gaussian residual mixture has one more residual dimension

**From:** T36, T37's precision-normalized inverse-gamma noise law, T39's residual identity and amplitude limits; the explicitly specified latent integration measure below. This is a conditional model derivation, not a claim that the current cut or L21 already forces this model.
**Test:** `tests/test_residual_mixture.py`; `tests/test_latent_measure_exponent.py` (B17's scale-measure counterexample); `tests/test_latent_scale_exploration.py` (E5's moment-preserving and proper-limit counterexamples).
**Status:** proved below, 3 Nov 2026 (Codex). Normalized residual integration gives an ordinary Student residual density, repairs the raw single-reading nu<1 amplitude failure, and leaves the global-power obstruction intact. No factor, input interpretation, prior, ruling or production default is changed.

## 1. Specify the object and latent measure before integrating

In the fixed declared whitened span of one reading, the data z have dimension
2k. A mass candidate is a k-dimensional plane L(m). Choose an orthonormal
basis U(m), and write the mean as U(m)*t. The conditional noise density is
the fully normalized N(0,v I_(2k)) density. Integrate t against flat
arclength dt in the **declared** Sigma metric, independent of v.

Completing the square gives

$$\int_{\mathbb R^k}(2\pi v)^{-k}
\exp[-\|z-U(m)t\|^2/(2v)]dt
=(2\pi v)^{-k/2}\exp[-K(m)/(2v)],$$

where K=||[I-UU^T]z||^2. This is a normalized Gaussian density in its
k residual coordinates. It is not a normalized density on all observed
data after integrating an improper latent measure, nor a probability
density of mass.

With physical latent coordinates alpha and candidate map C_m, the declared
arclength is sqrt(det G_m)*dalpha, G_m=C_m^T Sigma^-1 C_m. Its Jacobian
cancels the determinant from the Gaussian latent integral. Using flat
physical dalpha instead leaves det(G_m)^(-1/2), a different mass-dependent
factor that generally fails exact-zero neutrality. The present derivation
specifies the declared-metric arclength convention; it does not infer that
choice from the words 'flat' or 'normalized' alone.

## 2. The same declared noise law gives a Student residual density

Keep exactly T37 G20's v~IG(nu/2,nu/2), with nu=nu_eff>0. In general,
mixing a normalized r-dimensional residual Gaussian gives

$$f_{\nu,r}(K)=
\frac{\Gamma((\nu+r)/2)}{\Gamma(\nu/2)(\pi\nu)^{r/2}}
\left(1+K/\nu\right)^{-(\nu+r)/2}.$$

Indeed the variance integral is proportional to
int v^[-(nu+r)/2-1] exp[-(nu+K)/(2v)]dv. Substitution of reciprocal v
gives the gamma integral and the displayed coefficient. It is the ordinary
multivariate Student density in whitened residual coordinates. In the
projected 2k-dimensional model, r=k, so the exponent is (nu+k)/2.

There is no change to nu's meaning or to its inverse-gamma rate. In this
precision normalization E[1/v]=1; where the marginal covariance exists it
is Sigma*nu/(nu-2), not Sigma. Requiring covariance normalization instead
would be a different specification and would require nu>2.

The current best-fit cut uses the fitted residual count k-1 and averages
unnormalized Gaussian factors, yielding exponent (nu+k-1)/2. Here the
normalized residual at a *specified candidate mass* has k dimensions.
Changing which object is integrated is not a correction to a dropped
algebraic constant in unchanged ours32.

### Full covariance and restricted subspaces

The same calculation works after any fixed whitening. If all ambient 2d
coordinates are retained, while the candidate mean plane has dimension k,
the residual rank is r=2d-k and its cost is K+B_out. The outside-span cost
B_out is mass-independent at fixed v but contains information about the
shared v. It cannot simply be discarded before mixing v. Projecting to
the 2k span and mixing there is a distinct data/model operation.

### A remaining scale-measure choice

The conditional measure must be specified across v as well as across mass.
Multiplying declared arclength by v^b gives, with
$a=(\nu+r-2b)/2>0$,

$$\frac{(\nu/2)^b\Gamma(a)}{\Gamma(\nu/2)(\pi\nu)^{r/2}}
(1+K/\nu)^{-a}.$$

The condition a>0 makes the pointwise variance integral finite. This is
not generally a normalized residual density: its residual-space integral
is E[V^b], finite only when b<nu/2. In particular arclength in the
realized v*Sigma metric has an additional v^(-k/2), giving (nu+2k)/2 in
the projected model, or (nu+2d)/2 in the full ambient model. The present
Student residual density is forced **once** the fully normalized Gaussian,
declared inverse-gamma law and v-independent declared-metric latent measure
are fixed. Those specifications are not all forced by the current cut
interpretation of L21. A mass-independent constant at fixed v can affect
the mass law after v is mixed.

### B17: the declared metric does not force independence from v

**4 Nov 2026, Codex; pressure, refuted provisional forcing argument.**
The standard issue is the conditional nuisance measure in an integrated
likelihood, including the relative constants of an improper measure at
different nuisance scales. See Berger, Liseo and Wolpert (1999),
[*Integrated Likelihood Methods for Eliminating Nuisance Parameters*](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~berger/papers/brunero.pdf),
§§1.1 and 4.2, for the distinction between specifying a likelihood and
specifying its nuisance integration measure. The argument below concerns
this particular conditional Gaussian/IG construction, not a claim of a
second solution of the full premise list.

T36 supplies the declared metric and hence its canonical arclength dt.
But a weighted measure v^b dt is a multiple of that **same** arclength
at each fixed v. It need not be called the canonical metric volume.
Excluding a different metric therefore does not exclude all such
weighted measures. In particular b=-1 is a counterexample to the proposed
implication, without identifying it with realized-metric volume
(that particular convention has b=-k/2).

| Written requirement | What v^b dt does, for every fixed finite b |
| --- | --- |
| T36, declared metric | Retains Sigma^-1, its graphs, projectors and K; changes a scalar nuisance weight, not the metric. The metric alone does not assert that every integration measure must equal its canonical volume with one normalization across v. |
| L13, Gaussian working model | Leaves the normalized conditional Gaussian N(0,v Sigma) untouched within T37/T48's augmented model. Specifying that conditional density does not specify the nuisance dominating measure. Read literally without this augmentation, L13 states fixed Gaussian covariance and does not itself derive any finite-nu mixture, including b=0. |

For the brief's b=-1,-1/2,0,1/2,1 at nu=10 and k=1,3, all
pointwise integrals are finite: a=(nu+k-2b)/2>0. None is excluded
by the named requirements above merely because b differs from zero.
For general nu,k, a<=0 instead makes the variance integral divergent;
that is an integrability exclusion, not a metric selection of b=0.

There is also a correction to the brief's proposed normalization check.
Writing alpha=nu/2, Tonelli's theorem gives the residual-space total

$$M_b=E[V^b]=\alpha^b\frac{\Gamma(\alpha-b)}{\Gamma(\alpha)},
\qquad b<\alpha,$$

and infinity for b>=alpha. Thus **both b=0 and b=-1 have total one**,
because E[1/V]=1 in the declared precision normalization. Strict
convexity, (log M_b)''=psi_1(alpha-b)>0, shows these are the only
unit-total exponents. For b=-1 the residual density is a Student with
degrees of freedom nu+2 and scale matrix nu/(nu+2)*I_k, for every
nu>0. Its relative factor differs from b=0 by
(1+K/nu)^(-1), so it is not the same mass evidence times a constant.
T48's earlier statement "not generally a normalized residual density"
was correct; the brief's stronger "only b=0" was not.

**Exactly what would force b=0.** Require a joint probability model on
the residual and V whose V marginal is the declared q(dv) and whose
residual conditional is normalized N(0,v I_k) at every v. The weighted
joint measure has residual integral v^b q(dv), so retaining marginal
q requires v^b=1 q-almost everywhere. IG has positive density on all
v>0; hence b=0. Equivalently require the integrated residual to have
total one **at each fixed v**, or specify the nuisance measure as dt
independent of V. T48 §1 explicitly assumes that independence; the
premise rows and T36 do not establish it. Normalization only after
mixing is weaker, as b=-1 proves.

This distinction matters for L24 too. The alternative can be rewritten
as a residual mixture under the tilted measure q_b(dv)=v^b q(dv)
(a probability at b=-1). Keeping the explicit IG factor in the
improper nuisance integral is therefore not a proof that every reading
of "the noise law unchanged" is satisfied. If that phrase requires
the joint marginal condition just stated, b=0 follows from **that
additional condition**. L24 is candidate/unruled, does not specify
this joint object, and T37 already leaves the variance-dependent
normalization of the improper latent measure unforced. No L24
admissibility or changed noise law is adopted here.

**Stopping result.** B17's T36/L13 forcing route is refuted as written.
The missing cross-scale measure/normalization requirement is a

This does not automatically make b a physical input: no calibration
practice supplying it has been established under TASK §5's cost guard.
Per the brief's explicit stop instruction, the downstream totality,

### E5: what preserving the declared noise law would have to mean

**5 Nov 2026, Codex, user-authorized exploration following B17.** The
standard mathematics is integrated likelihood with incidental parameters,
proper approximations to improper conditional measures, and elimination by
group invariance. Berger, Liseo and Wolpert (1999),
[*Integrated Likelihood Methods for Eliminating Nuisance Parameters*](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~berger/papers/brunero.pdf),
author-hosted PDF, §1.3.1, printed pp. 3–4, distinguishes a random nuisance with a specified
distribution from fixed nuisance parameters requiring integration weights.
Its §§4.1–4.4, printed pp. 11–13, discuss vague proper priors, coordinate dependence,
group conditions and compact normalizations. The concrete constructions
below are this exploration's specialization. They test the proposed forcing
route rather than selecting an exponent from performance.

#### 1. Precision normalization selects b=0 only within the power family

For $q=\operatorname{IG}(\alpha,\alpha)$, normalizing $v^bq(dv)$, where
$b<\alpha$, gives $q_b=\operatorname{IG}(\alpha-b,\alpha)$. Hence

$$E_{q_b}[1/V]=(\alpha-b)/\alpha.$$

Retaining $E[1/V]=1$ forces $b=0$ **within this family**. B17's normalized
$b=-1$ example has mean precision $1+1/\alpha$ and fails that extra
requirement. The restriction to powers matters. Even preserving both mean
precision and noise covariance leaves positive weights outside this family.

Take $\nu=10$, $\alpha=5$, and $X=5/V\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(5,1)$.
Define

$$p(x)=x^3-18x^2+90x-120,\qquad
h(v)=1+p(5/v)/240,\qquad q_h(dv)=h(v)q(dv).$$

The cubic's only positive critical points are $6\pm\sqrt6$.
Together with $p(0)=-120$ and its positive leading coefficient, their
values show $p(x)\ge-120$ for $x\ge0$, so $h\ge1/2$.
Gamma moments give

$$E[p(X)]=E[Xp(X)]=E[p(X)/X]=0.$$

Therefore $q_h$ is a probability law with exactly the same
$E[1/V]=1$ and $E[V]=5/4$ as $q$. But

$$E_{q_h}[V^2]=65/32\ne25/12=E_q[V^2].$$

Consequently their centered Gaussian residual mixtures have equal covariance
and different fourth moments. These are T37 G20's precision-normalized
scales, so that covariance equals $(5/4)I$, not $I$. If instead covariance
normalization is desired, replacing $V$ by $4V/5$ in both constructions
preserves their equality of the two first moments and their difference.

The difference affects candidate evidence explicitly. Let $f_0(K)$ be the
scalar residual density from $q$ and $f_h(K)$ that from $q_h$. At residual
cost $K$, $X$ has conditional law
$\operatorname{Gamma}(a,\rho)$ with $a=11/2$, $\rho=1+K/10$. Thus

$$\frac{f_h(K)}{f_0(K)}=1+\frac1{240}
\left[\frac{a(a+1)(a+2)}{\rho^3}
-\frac{18a(a+1)}{\rho^2}+\frac{90a}{\rho}-120\right].$$

The ratio is $639/640$ at $K=0$ and $110367/109744$ at $K=9$.
It is nonconstant, so the difference survives normalization of a mass law.
Both residual densities integrate to one. Moment preservation therefore
does not force the relative evidence, even in this explicit Gaussian mixture
class. This example alters the full mixing distribution and is excluded if
that full distribution is required for the reduced residual experiment.

#### 2. Proper diffuse approximations expose an additional distinction

Work in one candidate plane's orthonormal coordinates $z=(x,r)$, with
$x\in\mathbb R^k$ parallel and $r\in\mathbb R^k$ perpendicular. At fixed
$v$, let $z\mid t,v\sim N_{2k}((t,0),vI)$ and keep $V\sim q$.
For any positive finite $h(v)$ and finite $A>0$, give $t\mid v$ the proper
uniform density on the cube

$$[-A h(v)^{-1/k},A h(v)^{-1/k}]^k.$$

Its density is $h(v)/(2A)^k$ inside the cube and integrates to one at every
$v$. Every finite-$A$ model therefore has exactly the original marginal
$V\sim q$, and all have the same conditional density $z\mid t,V$.
Their distributions $V\mid t$ generally differ. When $E_qh<\infty$, at
$t=0$ the cube contains the signal for every $v$, and the conditional-density
version of Bayes' formula gives
$V\mid t=0\sim hq/E_qh$ at every $A$. This coupling survives the limit.
Write $p_{A,h}(z\mid m)$ for the resulting full-data density. Then

$$(2A)^k p_{A,h}(z\mid m)\ \longrightarrow\
\int h(v)\phi_k(r;0,vI)q(dv)=f_h(r).$$

To see this, the rescaled conditional density is
$h(v)\phi_k(r;0,vI)$ times the probability that
$N_k(x,vI)$ lies in the growing cube. That probability increases to one.
Monotone convergence proves the limit. Whenever the displayed integral is
finite, it also supplies a dominating function. The examples $h=1$,
$h(v)=v^{-1}$ and the cubic weight above all have finite limits for finite
$r$. The rescaling $(2A)^k$ is common to all mass candidates and both models.

In contrast, the **actual residual sampling distribution** in every finite
model is $\int\phi_k(r;0,vI)q(dv)=f_0(r)$, since projection cancels $t$ and
its proper conditional density integrates to one. Thus preserving $q$ in a
proper full model does not determine the common-volume diffuse limit of its
integrated likelihood. The dependence of the approximating signal distribution
on $v$ survives that limit. These proper signal priors are diagnostic
approximations, not proposed signal populations for the estimator.

B17's fixed-$v$ residual-normalization argument is valid for its specifically
required **reduced residual/V joint law**. It cannot be extended merely by
requiring the full proper generative model to retain $q$. The latter is
satisfied by every model just constructed. A common $v$-independent diffuse
signal prior does recover $h=1$.

There is a concrete conditional forcing route. Require the **same calibrated
scale law conditional on every possible true signal**, $V\mid t\sim q$,
and require a proper diffuse approximation to preserve this experiment.
Together with the marginal $V\sim q$, this means $t$ and $V$ are independent.
For any common proper signal density $g_A(t)$, the integrated joint uses
$g_A(t)dt\,q(dv)$. If $g_A$ is uniform on $[-A,A]^k$, removing the common
volume $(2A)^k$ gives exactly $f_0(r)$ by the preceding proof with $h=1$.
In the displayed cube family, independence directly requires $h$ constant
$q$-almost everywhere, which gives the same relative mass evidence as $h=1$.
Thus a requirement about instrument calibration at every true signal can
justify the separation, conditional on choosing this coherent diffuse-limit
route for eliminating the fixed signal. Existing premise rows have not
established both parts of that route for the finite-$\nu$ model.

#### 3. An exact residual law still needs a bridge to mass evidence

At the true candidate, the orthogonal residual is nuisance-free:
$r_m=B_m^Tz$ with $B_m^TU_m=0$, and its conditional law is
$N_k(0,vI)$. Requiring this conditional density and the full mixing law $q$
specifies $f_0(r_m)$, giving the $(\nu+k)/2$ Student exponent without an
improper signal integral. Within that specified residual experiment the
normalization is fixed.

Using its density as mass evidence remains a substantive reduction of the
original experiment. The observed residual coordinates depend on the
candidate. There is no single nonconstant statistic invariant under signal
translations for every scalar candidate line: invariance under translations
along two distinct lines implies invariance under all translations of
$\mathbb R^2$, hence a constant statistic. Accordingly the standard
fixed-group maximal-invariant/Haar argument cannot be invoked for all
mass candidates by simply naming their separate residuals. This does not
invalidate the residual construction as an explicitly specified working
likelihood. It identifies the additional justification its uniqueness needs.

## 3. The corrected raw one-reading law concentrates for every nu>0

Use one scalar reading with true interior angle theta0 and

$$Y_R=R(\sin\theta_0,\cos\theta_0)+e,$$

where e is any fixed finite error. Its observed positive angle alpha_R
tends to theta0 and its radius rho_R/R tends to one. With the uniform
angular prior c=s=1, the corrected raw kernel is

$$[1+\rho_R^2\sin^2(\theta-\alpha_R)/\nu]^{-(\nu+1)/2}.$$

Inside a width c/rho_R window around the observed angle the kernel is
bounded below by a positive constant, so its normalizer is at least
c1/rho_R. Outside any fixed neighbourhood of truth it is at most
c2*rho_R^[-(nu+1)]. The outside posterior mass is therefore O(rho_R^-nu),
which tends to zero at every nu>0. The law and its median concentrate at
the true positive mass. This contrasts with the nu/2 exponent and tail
index nu in T39's permitted nu<1 counterexample.

The same peak/tail argument covers fixed finite N with compatible signals
growing at one common rate R, at least one nonzero fixed signal vector,
fixed declarations and bounded errors: each informative residual factor
has power-tail index nu_i+r_i>1, while a common 1/R neighbourhood has
positive-prior mass of order 1/R and all factors bounded below there.
For a fixed proper prior with positive continuous density near the interior
true mass, an informative factor alone supplies an integrable sufficient
outside bound. This is a fixed-N statement, not an arbitrary heterogeneous
long-series information or calibration theorem.

The corrected factor also preserves whole-reading exact-zero neutrality:
K=0 gives a mass-independent constant. Units and frame covariance follow
from the declared whitened geometry. These properties do not choose the
integration measure or establish sampling uncertainty.

### Fixed incompatible readings keep bounded relative evidence

Keep a measured reading fixed and shrink its declared covariance to
sigma^2*Sigma0. In the compact positive-angle closure write its residual
cost as K_sigma(m)=kappa(m)/sigma^2. Suppose kappa is continuous and
has positive infimum kappa_min>0. For finite nu and a=(nu+r)/2, the
normalized residual factor relative to its positive-domain supremum is

$$\frac{f_{\nu,r}(K_\sigma(m))}{f_{\nu,r}(\kappa_{\min}/\sigma^2)}
=\left[\frac{\nu\sigma^2+\kappa(m)}
{\nu\sigma^2+\kappa_{\min}}\right]^{-a}
\longrightarrow[\kappa(m)/\kappa_{\min}]^{-a}.$$

The convergence is uniform, since kappa is bounded on the compact angle
domain and bounded away from zero. The limiting relative factor is positive
and finite everywhere. If kappa is nonconstant it remains nonconstant, so
the reading's relative information is bounded but does not vanish. The
ordering of candidate masses is unchanged; no rejection gate is introduced.

For the scalar anti-parallel reading (F,a)=(1,-2),
kappa(theta)=(cos(theta)+2*sin(theta))^2 ranges from 1 to 5. Its relative
log-factor oscillation therefore tends to a*log(5), with a=(nu+1)/2.
The positive-domain infimum is a boundary limit here, not a fitted finite
mass. This is the raw relative-evidence part of T30 under the specified
mixture model. It excludes compatible/boundary cases with kappa_min=0
and does not prove any series-level eta behavior or sampling calibration.

## 4. The global Bartlett power can cancel this repair exactly

For the symmetric scalar pair Y_plus/minus=R*u0 +/- d*v0, write

$$\ell_i=-a\log[\nu+K_i(\theta)],\qquad a=(\nu+1)/2.$$

The denominator product is T39's same polynomial. In its strict central
global-maximum regime, with A=nu+d^2,

$$\mathcal H_R=\frac{4a[R^2(\nu-d^2)-d^2(\nu+d^2)]}{A^2},
\qquad J_R=\frac{8a^2R^2d^2}{A^2},$$

$$\eta_R=\min\left(1,
\frac{\nu-d^2}{2ad^2}-\frac{\nu+d^2}{2aR^2}\right).$$

When the cap is inactive, a*eta_R is independent of a. Thus the entire
quoted pair law is independent of this prefactor, not just its limiting
tail. At nu=10,d=3, the new eta tends to 1/99 rather than 1/90, but the
combined tempered tail index is still 2/9. The quoted law is exactly T39's
old quoted law at every radius in this uncapped regime; its limiting
median remains 1.3107867539725..., instead of mass 2. The corrected raw
law has combined tail index 22 and concentrates.

More generally, for any finite a>0 choose d^2<nu sufficiently close to nu.
The limiting cap is inactive and the quoted tail index
2*(nu-d^2)/d^2 is less than one. This is still compatible with the strict
central maximum at all sufficiently large R. Merely changing the finite
prefactor, including this normalized residual correction, does not repair
