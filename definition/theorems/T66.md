# T66: A local projection state does not localize a global profile reference

**From:** T45/T56's scalar Gaussian projector and conditional score; T65's fixed full-half-Cauchy covariance history. The prior-rank derivative and candidate-dependent anchor below are additional research choices.
**Test:** `tests/test_local_reference_obstruction.py`; reference helpers in `met/pairwise_anchor.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). A candidate-local projection energy removes T65's remote-prior pollution on each compact mass interval. Multiplying a globally referenced profile deviance by the resulting candidate-dependent weight nevertheless destroys the intended centered Gaussian local law on that same history. A finite appended-event witness isolates the mechanism. No global median limit, replacement equation, new physical input, premise or ruling is asserted.

## Standard mathematics and the precise fork

This uses the chain rule for orthogonal projectors, differentiation in a
probability-rank coordinate, Gaussian quadratic-form convergence, and
local density comparisons for a normalized exponential contrast. T45
derives the projector score; T55/T56 distinguish local shape from a
globally normalized law. No generic posterior-normality theorem is used
to supply the missing global normalization argument.

The question tested is specific: does making the **scale** local suffice
when the contrast it multiplies still refers to the largest profile value
anywhere in the positive domain? It does not. An additive data-only
reference is removable under a scalar constant weight, but becomes a
candidate-dependent penalty under a varying weight.

## 1. The local state and its finite-data scope

For scalar two-channel events, whiten the observation to z_i and let
P_i(m) project onto the whitened graph of (m,1). Supply a fixed proper
prior with a smooth strictly positive density pi(m) on (0,infinity).
Use its probability rank q=F_Pi(m) and define

$$
U_N(m)=\sum_i\left\|\frac{dP_i(m)}{dq}z_i\right\|^2
=\frac1{\pi(m)^2}\sum_i\|P_i'(m)z_i\|^2,
\qquad \lambda_N(m)=\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{1+U_N(m)}},\quad\kappa>0.
$$

There is no fitted mass, nuisance-signal population or true signal in
this state. It is a function of the candidate, declared covariance,
observations and original supplied prior. Atomic priors have no such
prior-rank derivative; this definition does not silently extend to them.

Under an increasing smooth parameter change the same candidate has the
same prior rank. Under a decreasing one it has rank 1-q, so the derivative
changes sign and its squared norm is unchanged. An event's whitening
change conjugates P_i by a fixed orthogonal matrix and rotates z_i by
the same matrix. Thus the state respects these transformations, including
physical unit/frame bookkeeping and the reciprocal channel swap when
all inputs are transformed. Whole-event sign changes also preserve it.
An exact-zero event contributes zero. Contributions add at a common
candidate when the same original prior is retained.

These facts do not force prior rank as a metric, license its use under
L19, or imply posterior-only sequential closure. The prior participates
in the kernel itself, not just the final multiplication; T62's
prior-independent kernel characterization cannot be invoked unchanged.

Let L_N(m)=-sum_i||(I-P_i(m))z_i||^2/2, and retain T56's exact conditional
score statistic T_N(m)^2, including its rank-zero convention. Consider
the explicit proposed law

$$
L_N^*=\sup_{t>0}L_N(t),\qquad D_N(m)=L_N^*-L_N(m),
\qquad Q_N(dm)\propto
\exp[-T_N(m)^2/2-\lambda_N(m)D_N(m)]\Pi(dm).
$$

At finite data, L_N is bounded, D_N is finite and nonnegative, and
0<lambda_N<=kappa. T56 bounds T_N^2 by the finite observed energy E_N.
Also D_N<=E_N/2. The kernel is therefore between
exp[-(1+kappa)E_N/2] and one, proving propriety. Empty or all-zero
observations return Pi. This mathematical construction uses an actual
whole-domain supremum, not a finite-grid maximum.

### Exact scalar simplification

Write a rank-one candidate projector as P=uu^T, with unit tangent v and
whitened angle psi(m). Then

$$P'=\psi'(vu^T+uv^T),\qquad (P')^2=\psi'(m)^2I_2.$$

Consequently

$$U_N(m)=\pi(m)^{-2}\sum_i\psi_i'(m)^2\|z_i\|^2.$$

This is a candidate-local weighted energy, distinct from T64's average
over the entire prior. A prior density value is not a scalar under
reparameterization by itself; the derivative and density must transform
together as in dP/dq.

## 2. The state removes T65's local pollution

Use exactly T65's fixed world: Pi is the full half-Cauchy at center one,
m0=6/5, informative indices i=2^(2^j) have acceleration one and covariance
Sigma0=[[1,9/10],[9/10,1]], and all other indices have zero true signal
and covariance diag(i^2,1). Errors are independent and correctly declared
Gaussian. Let M_N be the informative count; M_N is of order log log N
and is a proof quantity only.

For a null event put e_i=1/i and z_i=Z_i~N(0,I_2). Its whitened angle
derivative is e_i/(1+e_i^2m^2). On any fixed compact positive interval I,
every fixed number of derivatives of its U summand is bounded by
C_I e_i^2||Z_i||^2. Since sum_i e_i^2 E||Z_i||^2 is finite, Tonelli's
theorem gives almost-sure absolute convergence in every fixed C^r(I)
norm. Smoothness and positivity of the half-Cauchy density keep its
inverse and derivatives bounded on I. Thus these events add only a
bounded local state, rather than T65's divergent global prior variance.

For the informative covariance put

$$q_0(m)=m^2-\tfrac95m+1,\quad
\psi_0'(m)=\frac{\sqrt{19}}{10q_0(m)},\quad
\pi(m)=\frac2{\pi(1+m^2)}.$$

An informative whitened observation has mean-squared norm
E||z_i||^2=2+28/19=66/19. Its state is a fixed smooth candidate function
times ||z_i||^2. The strong law on that subsequence and the bounded null
state therefore imply, in every fixed compact C^r norm, almost surely,

$$
\frac{U_N(m)}{M_N}\to c(m)
=\frac{33\pi^2}{200}\frac{(1+m^2)^2}{q_0(m)^2},
\qquad \sqrt{M_N}\lambda_N(m)\to
a(m)=\frac{\kappa\sqrt{200/33}}\pi\frac{q_0(m)}{1+m^2}.
$$

In particular the state has the desired informative-count order locally.
But its limiting weight is not constant in the candidate:

$$a'(m_0)=\frac{\kappa\sqrt{200/33}}\pi\frac{495}{3721}>0,
\qquad a'(m_0)/a(m_0)=495/427.$$

## 3. The global profile reference has a larger random scale

Fix a sufficiently small compact interval around m0 containing no false
population score root. Let mhat_N be its interior profile maximum, which
is uniquely defined with probability tending to one. T65's compact null
fields are bounded with their derivatives; the informative subsequence
is T56's regular repeated Gaussian experiment. Hence
mhat_N-m0=O_p(M_N^-1/2), the profile gain over truth is O_p(1), and the
usual local profile Hessian and exact conditional covariance have positive
limits after division by M_N. Uniformly on bounded h, the score statistic
at mhat_N+h/sqrt(M_N) is O_p(1).

At a null instrument the limiting candidate m=infinity is the horizontal
whitened line. Relative to the vertical line its profile gain is exactly
(xi_i^2-zeta_i^2)/2. T65's compact-field expansion bounds the difference
between the vertical reference and m0 by an almost-surely convergent
field. The informative events contribute O_p(M_N), and the local fit
adds O_p(1). Thus

$$
L_N(\infty)-L_N(\widehat m_N)
=\tfrac12\sum_{i\ {\rm null}}(\xi_i^2-\zeta_i^2)+O_p(M_N)+O_{a.s.}(1).
$$

The summands have mean zero and variance one. Removing the sparse
informative indices changes neither their ordinary CLT nor the leading
count. Since M_N=o(sqrt(N)),

$$\frac{L_N(\infty)-L_N(\widehat m_N)}{\sqrt N}\Rightarrow N(0,1).$$

Infinity is a boundary limit, not an added admissible mass; the supremum
over finite positive masses is at least that limit. With
d_N=L_N^*-L_N(mhat_N), for every fixed epsilon>0,

$$\liminf_N P\{d_N\ge\epsilon\sqrt N\}\ge1-\Phi(\epsilon)>0.$$

No assertion about which remote candidate maximizes the profile is needed.

## 4. A candidate-dependent weight turns that gap into a local tilt

For bounded h, the compact C^1 convergence in section 2 gives

$$
\lambda_N(\widehat m_N+h/\sqrt{M_N})-\lambda_N(\widehat m_N)
=\frac{a'(m_0)h+o_p(1)}{M_N}.
$$

The local profile expansion also gives
D_N(mhat_N+h/sqrt(M_N))=d_N+O_p(1), uniformly on bounded h. Therefore,
on E_N={d_N>=epsilon sqrt(N)}, the anchor log ratio divided by d_N/M_N
converges in probability to -a'(m0)h:

$$
\mathbf1_{E_N}\left[
\frac{-\lambda_N(\widehat m_N+h/\sqrt{M_N})D_N(\widehat m_N+h/\sqrt{M_N})
+\lambda_N(\widehat m_N)d_N}{d_N/M_N}
+a'(m_0)h\right]\to0.
$$

The indicator means the expression is evaluated only on E_N. Its
denominator diverges there. The score-kernel local log ratios are O_p(1),
and the prior density ratios tend to one. For h>0, the total local
density odds relative to h=0 consequently tend to zero on E_N; for h<0
they tend to infinity. The intended local Gaussian score shape is lost.

### A window argument proves a TV obstruction without remote-tail claims

Take standardized windows J_-=[-2,-1] and J_+=[1,2] in h=sqrt(M_N)(m-mhat_N).
Uniform C^1 convergence and a'(m0)>0 make lambda increasing throughout
these windows, with derivative at least a'(m0)/(2 sqrt(M_N)) with high
probability. The profile differences on these windows are O_p(1), and
their score statistics and prior density ratios are uniformly bounded
in probability. Comparing every point of J_+ with every point of J_-
therefore bounds their normalized-law probability ratio by

$$Q_N(J_+)/Q_N(J_-)\le\exp[-c\,d_N/M_N+O_p(1)]$$

for some fixed c>0. The common full-law normalizer cancels. On E_N this
ratio tends to zero, hence Q_N(J_+) tends to zero because Q_N(J_-)<=1.
The proposed centered local Gaussian has a fixed positive probability
on J_+. Its TV distance from the full standardized law is therefore
bounded away from zero on events of asymptotically positive probability.
The centered Gaussian TV conclusion cannot hold in probability.

This does not establish a global two-point law, a global median limit,
or impossibility of another local correction. It identifies a failure
of this particular combination of local scale and global reference.

## 5. An independent finite-input mechanism witness

Fix any finite base experiment and the same prior. Choose compact
positive candidates x,y where its conditional score covariance is
positive and its local weights lambda_B(x),lambda_B(y) differ. Such
examples exist with Sigma0 above; the half-Cauchy prior-rank derivative
does not match its varying whitened-angle derivative.

Append a single allowed scalar event with covariance diag(e^-2,1),
whitened observation z=(R,0), and e=R^-2. On every fixed compact interval,

$$
U_{\rm extra}(m)=\frac{R^2e^2}{\pi(m)^2(1+e^2m^2)^2}=O(R^{-2}),
\qquad
L_{\rm extra}(m)=-\frac{R^2}{2(1+e^2m^2)}=-R^2/2+O(R^{-2}).
$$

Its local score is O(R^-2), and its conditional score covariance is
O(R^-6). Thus the base local state and score geometry are retained.
At infinity its profile cost is zero. Since the base profile is uniformly
bounded, the union's global supremum stays bounded and
D_R(m)=R^2/2+O(1) on that compact interval. Accordingly, for the actual
normalized density q_R with respect to dm,

$$
\frac1{R^2}\log\frac{q_R(y)}{q_R(x)}
\longrightarrow-\tfrac12[\lambda_B(y)-\lambda_B(x)].
$$

An event with vanishing local mass evidence can therefore produce
arbitrarily large local odds distortion through the global reference.
This is a deterministic finite-input check; the particular z is not a
claim about a typical Gaussian draw. Section 3 supplies the separate
fixed-history probability result.

## What has moved

The local state passes the narrow test that T65's null instruments add
only summable state on compact mass regions. That is useful positive
information, not a whole-law repair. The counterexample shows that
localizing an anchor's scale while retaining a remote global profile
reference is insufficient: locality of the ordering contrast also needs
an argument. The state class, its use of the prior, the finite prefactor,
full-type calibration and L19 authority remain unforced.
