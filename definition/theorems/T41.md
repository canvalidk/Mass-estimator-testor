## Standard mathematics and the requirement being tested

This is a testing lower bound with nuisance parameters: Le Cam's method with
two mixtures, Gaussian likelihood-ratio second moments, and contiguity.
[Polyanskiy and Wu, *Dualizing Le Cam's method*, introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/1902.05616)
explains the reduction from estimation to discrimination and the use of
auxiliary priors for deterministic nuisance parameters. Below the identities,
finite bound and fixed-sequence argument are written out for this problem.
The constructive comparison uses the spectral gap of a sample scatter matrix.

The question is consistent estimability as a series grows. The finite-sample
Gaussian model is structurally identifiable at a nonzero true signal. The
proof compares mixtures over unknown reading-specific nuisance signs; it
does not make two fixed known Gaussian mean vectors equal in distribution.

The current L11 promises consistency whenever total true push diverges. Read
literally over the input type, this includes every fixed infinite sequence of
latent force/acceleration pairs satisfying one positive mass, with independent
Gaussian errors and the declared covariances correct. We test convergence in
probability, already weaker than almost-sure convergence. Estimators and
tests are measurable throughout.

The universal growth wording is carried from the lemma-list specification.
It is graded **strong**; there is no new verbatim ruling here assigning Can
these asymptotic quantifiers. Newton II and persistence themselves only say
that the same mass relates each true pair. They do not say that every
divergent-energy sequence is statistically identifying.

## 1. An allowed sequence with infinite energy

Use 1D readings, full subspaces, identity error covariances, equal fixed
windows, and one common fixed starting law, for example the half-Cauchy with
c = 1. The same declared inputs are used at both compared masses. For any positive mass m let

$$u_m=\frac{(m,1)}{\sqrt{1+m^2}},\qquad
Y_i=\epsilon_i r_i u_m+Z_i,\qquad
Z_i\stackrel{\rm ind}{\sim}N(0,I_2),$$

where each $\epsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}$ is a deterministic nuisance sign. Every
true pair satisfies $F_i^*=m a_i^*$, whatever the signs. Set

$$r_i=\frac{b}{\sqrt i},\qquad b>0.$$

Then the whitened signal energy is
$\Delta_N=\sum_{i\le N}r_i^2=b^2\sum_{i\le N}i^{-1}\to\infty$.
The acceleration and force energies separately also diverge, by fixed
positive factors $1/(1+m^2)$ and $m^2/(1+m^2)$. Thus the example does not
depend on choosing one of those channels to name total push. However,

$$\sum_{i=1}^\infty r_i^4=b^4\pi^2/6<\infty.$$

No repeated setup, signal-size distribution or information about a nuisance
sign is added to the estimator's input.

## 2. Exact second moments of the proof mixtures

For the proof only, average the conditional data laws over independent fair
signs. Let $p_{u,r}$ be the resulting density of one reading and $\varphi_2$
the standard Gaussian density. Direct division gives

$$L_{u,r}(y)=\frac{p_{u,r}(y)}{\varphi_2(y)}
=e^{-r^2/2}\cosh(r u^Ty).$$

The Gaussian moment-generating function gives, for unit directions u, v and
$\rho=u^Tv$,

$$E_0 L_{u,r}=1,\qquad
E_0[L_{u,r}L_{v,r}]=\cosh(\rho r^2).$$

For example expand both coshes into four exponentials and use
$E_0e^{t^TY}=e^{\|t\|^2/2}$. In particular
$\chi^2(p_{u,r}\|\varphi_2)=\cosh(r^2)-1$.

Let $P_{u,N}$ be the product of these sign mixtures, $\Phi_N$ the pure-noise
product, and write

$$S_N=\sum_{i\le N}\log\cosh(r_i^2),\qquad
C_N=\sum_{i\le N}\log\cosh(\rho r_i^2).$$

Independence implies $E_{\Phi_N}L_{u,N}^2=e^{S_N}$ and
$E_{\Phi_N}L_{u,N}L_{v,N}=e^{C_N}$. Therefore Cauchy--Schwarz yields

$$\begin{split}
\operatorname{TV}(P_{u,N},P_{v,N})
&=\tfrac12E_{\Phi_N}|L_{u,N}-L_{v,N}|\\
&\le\sqrt{\frac{e^{S_N}-e^{C_N}}2}.
\end{split}$$

Here TV ranges from 0 to 1. Since $C_N\ge0$ and
$\log\cosh x\le x^2/2$ (integrate $\tanh x\le x$ for x nonnegative),

$$\operatorname{TV}(P_{u,N},P_{v,N})
\le\min\left\{1,\sqrt{\frac{\exp(\tfrac12\sum_{i\le N}r_i^4)-1}{2}}\right\}.$$

At $b=1/2$ this is at most 0.1624012273 for **every N** and every pair of
directions. Thus for any test between, for example, masses 1 and 2, the
equal-prior average error is at least

$$\frac{1-\operatorname{TV}}2\ge0.4187993863.$$

This is a bound for every measurable test, not a simulation of a particular
estimator. A consistent mass estimator at both masses would give a test
whose error tends to zero by choosing the nearer mass.

## 3. Why this refutes pointwise consistency on fixed sequences

A minimax bound with a different nuisance sequence chosen at each N would
not by itself refute pointwise consistency. That is not the argument here.
Use **one fixed probability measure on infinite sign streams**, whose
prefixes generate the mixtures in part 2.

Suppose a sequence of estimators were consistent at every mass and for every
fixed infinite deterministic sign stream in part 1. For a fixed mass m and
positive tolerance d, its conditional error probabilities

$$q_N(m,\epsilon)
=P_{m,\epsilon}(|\widehat m_N-m|\ge d)$$

would tend to zero for every stream $\epsilon$. They lie in [0,1]. Dominated
convergence over that one sign measure then gives

$$P_{u_m,N}(|\widehat m_N-m|\ge d)
=E_\epsilon q_N(m,\epsilon)\longrightarrow0.$$

Apply this at masses 1 and 2 with, say, d = 1/4. The resulting nearer-mass
test contradicts the fixed positive error bound in part 2. Consequently
**no estimator is consistent at every allowed fixed signal sequence merely
because its total energy diverges**. For each estimator there is a mass and
an allowed fixed signal history on which consistency fails; this does not
say that every estimator fails on every history. The conclusion covers any
point readout of any returned law and any calibration rule. Independent randomization of
an estimator cannot improve the testing bound.

If the true coefficients $t_i=\epsilon_i r_i$ were supplied, the matched sum
$\Delta_N^{-1}\sum_i t_iY_i\sim N(u_m,I_2/\Delta_N)$ would converge to the
true direction. That is a different information set. This proof does not
claim that one fixed pair of known Gaussian mean histories defeats every
test; it rules out one estimator working for every unknown nuisance history.

The auxiliary sign measure is a proof device averaging over allowed
deterministic worlds. The estimator neither assumes nor learns it. L10 and
L19's ban on a declared population of setups is not bypassed.

### The obstruction is not restricted to small b

More generally, let $\sum r_i^4<\infty$. Then $M=\sup_i r_i^2<\infty$.
Because $L_{u,r}\ge e^{-r^2/2}$,

$$\chi^2(p_{v,r}\|p_{u,r})
\le2e^{r^2/2}[\cosh(r^2)-\cosh(\rho r^2)]
\le e^{M/2}\cosh M\,(1-\rho^2)r^4.$$

The last inequality follows by integrating
$a\sinh(ta)\le t a^2\cosh a$ from $|\rho|$ to 1, with $a=r^2$.
Product likelihood-ratio second moments are consequently bounded by a finite
constant K, uniformly in N. For any event $A_N$,

$$P_{v,N}(A_N)\le\sqrt{K P_{u,N}(A_N)}.$$

Thus a sequence of events vanishing under either mixture vanishes under the
other: they are mutually contiguous. The same fixed-stream dominated
convergence argument contradicts consistency at two different masses.
Finite quartic energy supplies an entire class of such counterexamples when
quadratic energy diverges; the small-b example adds an explicit finite bound.

## 4. L16 makes an all-positive version sufficient

For an estimator respecting independent frame/sign conventions, simultaneous
sign reversal of both channels of any reading leaves its output unchanged:

$$T_N(\epsilon_1Y_1,\ldots,\epsilon_NY_N)=T_N(Y_1,\ldots,Y_N).$$

All reading inputs are transformed together. Identity covariances, full
subspaces as geometric objects, and windows remain the same in this example.
This is precisely the per-reading bookkeeping symmetry recorded in
R-2026-09-28-3.

Starting from $Y_i=r_i u_m+Z_i$, independent sign reversals give
$\epsilon_iY_i=\epsilon_i r_i u_m+\epsilon_iZ_i$, with the same independent
Gaussian errors. The invariant estimator therefore has the same distribution
under the **fixed all-positive signal sequence**, every deterministic sign
sequence, and their mixture. The testing bound already prevents consistency
at both masses for that all-positive schedule. No physically randomized push
is needed even in the example's description.

## 5. What an attainable growth condition looks like in one benchmark

The obstruction does not remove the fixed-N amplification or repeated fixed
nonzero signal regimes in T17/T39/T40. In the isotropic 1D Gaussian setting,
the raw unweighted scatter matrix $B_N=\sum_iY_iY_i^T$ obeys

$$E B_N=NI_2+\Delta_Nu_mu_m^T,\qquad
E\|B_N-E B_N\|_F^2=6(N+\Delta_N).$$

For one reading, Gaussian second and fourth moments give
$6(1+r_i^2)$; independent centered matrices add their squared Frobenius
expectations. Markov's inequality bounds the operator fluctuation by
$O_p(\sqrt{N+\Delta_N})$. The population eigenvalue gap is $\Delta_N$.
Consequently the principal line, and its mass ratio at an interior positive
truth, are consistent under the **sufficient** condition

$$\frac{\Delta_N^2}{N+\Delta_N}\longrightarrow\infty.$$

In the true parallel/perpendicular basis, the exact variance of each of
$B_{11}-B_{22}$ and $2B_{12}$ is $4(N+\Delta_N)$, and their means are
$\Delta_N$ and 0. Hence the fitted angular error is
$O_p(\sqrt{N+\Delta_N}/\Delta_N)$ under that condition. The raw Gaussian law
also concentrates: its fitted eigenvalue gap is $\Delta_N(1+o_p(1))$ and
its angular kernel is proportional to
$\exp[-\tfrac12\mathrm{gap}_N\sin^2(\theta-\widehat\theta_N)]$.
A fixed proper prior with continuous positive density at the truth supplies
the local normalizing mass.

This condition holds for fixed-N amplification and for repeated fixed
nonzero signal, and fails for $\Delta_N=b^2 H_N$. It is not a necessary
condition for every estimator or every input structure. In particular,
divergent quartic energy alone is not proved sufficient for the definition.
These quantities use the true latent signals to state a theorem; they are
not new caller inputs, a data gate, or a selected weighting rule.

**Further continuation (T42).** The same growth condition is necessary and
sufficient for the flat Gaussian factors with the existing Bartlett power
and a fixed regular prior, as N grows. The proof covers arbitrary
deterministic amplitudes and signs, including η tending to zero. This
characterizes one procedure; it does not replace L11 for every estimator
or the full covariance/subspace/noise type.

## Sort and consequence for the project

**Fails:** literal L11 over all allowed divergent-total-push sequences, for
every possible estimator. **Passes under named conditions:** the existing
fixed-N and repeated fixed-signal results, and the Gaussian scatter benchmark
above. **Unknown:** the necessary and sufficient growth condition for the
full input type and a justified replacement statement of L11.

The mis-stated implication identified here is that divergent total signal
automatically supplies divergent identifying information about mass when
each reading has its own unknown true signal. This is upstream of G23/G24:
changing the global power, the law's tails or its point cannot restore that
unrestricted promise. Their conditional results and their separate
counterexamples remain valid.

Under GOAL's guard this is evidence against consistency of the **current
premise list**, because L11 is strong. The proof requires no change to
bedrock L1/L2/L3, and does not select a new mechanical commitment. L11's
statement and grade are retained with the conflict exposed. O19/G25 records
the missing identification condition; the replacement remains unsorted.
