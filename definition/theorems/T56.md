# T56: Exact conditional score tests do not supply a globally identifying law

**From:** T45/T54's Gaussian conditional score, T55's repeated-block sensitivity and variability, and the explicitly proposed normalized score criterion below.
**Test:** `tests/test_joint_score_law.py`; `tests/test_score_law_obstruction.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). The score-weighted law is proper, exact-zero neutral and locally has the Godambe width. Nevertheless an allowed scalar Gaussian experiment with the unchanged half-Cauchy prior gives a false positive-mass peak of limiting probability 0.7535547094..., and its median converges to that false mass. The exact conditional test retains its stated coverage. No production law, prior, ruling or score-root selection is adopted.

## 1. Specify the proposed law and its genuine properties

Let S(m) be the vector Gaussian profile score and C(m) its exact conditional
covariance given all own-event candidate projections, as in T45/T54.
The proposed construction, with a fixed supplied proper prior Pi, is

$$Q_S(dm)=Z^{-1}\exp[-T(m)^2/2]\Pi(dm),\qquad
T(m)^2=S(m)^TC(m)^+S(m).$$

At rank zero the score is zero and set T^2=0. The score lies in the range
of its conditional covariance, by T50's stacked-map representation.
Consequently the statistic is finite and nonnegative at every finite
candidate and data set. Its weight is strictly positive and at most one,
so 0<Z<=1 and Q_S is proper. An appended exact-zero event adds zero to S
and C and leaves the law unchanged. Empty and all-zero data return Pi.

More precisely, the stacked map gives T^2=v^T Proj_col(A) v, so
T^2<=||v||^2<=sum_i ||z_i||^2 uniformly in the candidate. Its finite-data
weight is therefore also bounded below by a strictly positive constant.
Every divergent nonnegative prior moment remains divergent in Q_S;
the local Gaussian limits below do not assert finite posterior covariance.

The quadratic statistic is unchanged by an invertible smooth parameter
coordinate change: both score and covariance transform by the same
Jacobian, and the stacked score map has the same column space.
With the prior pushed forward this preserves Q_S. Whitening/bookkeeping
changes and independent whole-event sign reversals also preserve it.
These algebraic properties do not make the criterion a data likelihood.

The construction is a continuously weighted quadratic estimating-function
criterion, of the generalized method of moments type. Normalizing an
exponential of such a contrast is a quasi-posterior construction; compare
[Chernozhukov and Hong (2003), sections 2 and 4](https://www.mit.edu/~vchern/ch_qbe.pdf).
Their framework requires identification conditions on the contrast. It
does not turn a candidate-dependent conditional test into a likelihood
or supply global identification from local score calibration alone.

## 2. Correct local Godambe width, conditional on the true basin

Use T55's fixed finite protocol block repeated n times at fixed noise.
At the interior true mass vector m0, let A>0 be profile sensitivity and
B=A+G>0 its score covariance. Write S_n and C_n for the summed profile
score and its conditional covariance. Then

$$C_n(m_0)/n\to B.$$

Indeed write U_i=P_i z_i and G_ij=R_i P_ij U_i in whitened coordinates.
The mean contribution to E[G_ij^T G_ik] is v_ij^T v_ik, and its projected
noise contribution is tr(P_ij R_i P_ik P_i)=tr(P_ij P_ik)/2.
Thus E[C_block]=A+G=B; iid block laws of large numbers apply. Smooth
projector derivatives give the same local uniform convergence at
consistent candidates.

Let mhat_n be the consistent profile maximum in the true regular basin.
For bounded h, the profile score equation and Hessian convergence give

$$S_n(\widehat m_n+h/\sqrt n)
=-\sqrt n\,A h+o_p(\sqrt n),\qquad
T_n(\widehat m_n+h/\sqrt n)^2
=h^TAB^{-1}Ah+o_p(1).$$

For a fixed sufficiently small neighbourhood U of m0, and a prior with
positive continuous Lebesgue density there, **Q_S conditioned on U** has
the centered TV Gaussian limit N(0,A^-1 B A^-1) in root-n coordinates.
The nonsingular local score derivative supplies a quadratic separation
bound within U; the local uniform laws control its tails, as for a regular
GMM contrast. This matches the profile center's sampling Gaussian limit.
It is a local shape and quantile result, not finite-law moment convergence.

An unconditioned conclusion additionally needs global separation of the
score criterion. A uniquely maximizing expected profile is insufficient:
every score zero, including a profile minimum, has criterion value zero.
The conditioning above is a theorem statement, not an instruction to
select a basin using the data or change the prior support.

## 3. An allowed one-body Gaussian counterexample

Take one scalar body, independent repeated readings with true acceleration
a=1, true mass m0=6/5, and fixed correctly declared covariance

$$\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&9/10\\9/10&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

Use the unchanged proper half-Cauchy prior pi(m)=2/[pi(1+m^2)], m>0.
There are no random amplitudes or fitted noise scales. This is already
an allowed one-body subcase of the proposed joint construction.

Put q(m)=m^2-(9/5)m+1. In whitened coordinates choose the increasing
candidate angle

$$\psi(m)=\arctan\!\frac{m-9/10}{\sqrt{19}/10},\qquad
\psi'(m)=\frac{\sqrt{19}/10}{q(m)}.$$

The physical positive domain becomes an interval of length
pi/2+arcsin(9/10), strictly between pi/2 and pi. The angular prior density is

$$w(\psi(m))=\frac{2q(m)}{\pi(1+m^2)(\sqrt{19}/10)}.$$

It is positive and continuous through the compact interval's endpoints.
The true whitened signal length squared is r_0^2=28/19. Its orthogonal
candidate line is also at a positive physical mass: solving
(m,1)^T Sigma^-1(m0,1)=0 gives

$$m_f=\frac{(9/10)m_0-1}{m_0-9/10}=\frac4{15}.$$

Thus the population profile has its unique maximum at m0 and a minimum
at mf, both inside the allowed positive domain.

### Exact criterion and the two continuum Laplace weights

Let Z_i be the whitened observations and F_N=sum_i Z_i Z_i^T. At angular
candidate u=(sin(psi),cos(psi)), choose its unit tangent v. Then

$$S_N=v^TF_Nu,\qquad C_N=u^TF_Nu.$$

Write the observed eigenvalues as lambda_+>lambda_->0, their gap H, and
delta for angle from the principal eigenline. For N>=2 these conditions
hold almost surely. Direct diagonalization gives

$$T_N^2=\frac{H^2\sin^2\delta\cos^2\delta}
{\lambda_+\cos^2\delta+\lambda_-\sin^2\delta}.$$

Both observed eigenlines have weight one, irrespective of whether they
maximize or minimize the original profile. By the strong law,

$$\lambda_+/N\to47/19,\qquad\lambda_-/N\to1,\qquad H/N\to28/19.$$

Their positive physical roots converge to m0 and mf. Near the principal
root the score-law angular width is sqrt(lambda_+)/H; near the minor root
it is sqrt(lambda_-)/H. One-dimensional Laplace integration therefore gives
the ratio of false to true neighbourhood masses tending to

$$r=\frac{w(\psi(m_f))}{w(\psi(m_0))}\sqrt{\frac{19}{47}}
=\frac{1159}{241}\sqrt{\frac{19}{47}}
=3.057695716833\ldots .$$

Here q(m0)=7/25, 1+m0^2=61/25, q(mf)=133/225 and 1+mf^2=241/225,
which give the angular-prior ratio 1159/241.

This is a statement about the normalized continuum law, not just two
pointwise maxima. The compact angular interval has exactly these two
population score zeros; neither endpoint is a zero. Uniform convergence
of F_N/N makes T_N^2/N bounded below away from zero outside their fixed
neighbourhoods. That region contributes exponentially little compared
with either N^-1/2 peak. Local Taylor expansions, uniformly for eigenvalues
and roots near their limits, give the two Gaussian Laplace factors above.
Consequently, almost surely,

$$Q_{S,N}\ \Rightarrow\
\frac1{1+r}\,\delta_{6/5}+\frac{r}{1+r}\,\delta_{4/15},
\qquad \frac{r}{1+r}=0.7535547094249\ldots .$$

Every sufficiently small interval about mf thus eventually has probability
greater than one half. The continuous positive law's unique median tends
to mf, rather than m0. The law and its reported median are inconsistent
even under repeated fixed nonzero Gaussian signal, where the ordinary
profile is identified and T55's regular limit applies to its true maximum.

In the restricted scalar common-covariance case where the allowed angular
arc contains only the true eigenline and excludes its orthogonal line,
the same calculation instead gives concentration on the true root and
the local Godambe width. A diagonal covariance and interior positive truth
have that property. It does not cover the allowed correlated example.

### Fixed-N amplification is a different limit

For this same scalar common-covariance geometry, keep a finite N>=2 and
nonzero fixed signal coefficients, and amplify all signals by R. With
bounded realized errors, lambda_+=O(R^2), lambda_-=O(1), and H is of
order R^2. For a continuous prior positive at both interior roots, the
true peak has order R^-1 and the false peak at most order R^-2; its
relative mass tends to zero. Almost-sure Gaussian data have the needed
bounded fixed error vectors and a positive limiting minor eigenvalue.
This named amplification regime does not rescue fixed-noise replication,
where both eigenvalues are of order N. No full-type global theorem follows.

## 4. A conditional-density determinant is not an automatic repair

Where C(m) is positive definite, multiplying by |C(m)|^(-1/2) resembles the Gaussian density of the
conditional score at zero. Under a parameter-coordinate Jacobian J,
C transforms to J^T C J, so that factor additionally changes by
|det J|^-1. With the same pushed-forward prior it is not an invariant
scalar weight. Candidate-dependent score conditioning is not a common
likelihood experiment in m.

It can also make a proper-prior construction improper. For the allowed
scalar datum Y=(1,-1), Sigma=I, let theta in (0,pi/2),
U=sin(theta)-cos(theta) and V=cos(theta)+sin(theta). Then S=UV,C=U^2.
Away from theta=pi/4 the determinant-weighted kernel is

$$\frac{\exp[-V^2/2]}{|U|},$$

which has a nonintegrable 1/|theta-pi/4| pole under a continuous prior
positive there. Assigning any value at the single rank-zero point does
not remove the pole. Exact-zero appended events still leave summed S,C
unchanged wherever the expression is defined; padding Z is not the
obstruction in this example.

## Sort

The proposed bounded score law passes properness, the stated symmetries
and local Godambe calibration. It fails global identifying concentration
and median consistency on the allowed Gaussian type. Inverting T45's
candidate tests still has its exact stated coverage; normalizing their
exponentiated statistics is a separate operation and acquires no global
likelihood justification from that fact. No score-root gate, support
restriction, alternative calibration or production adoption is inferred.
