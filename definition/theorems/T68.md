# T68: Gaussian pairwise bounds remove the original T65 false basin

**From:** T56's informative two-root Gaussian experiment, T65's unchanged full-half-Cauchy history and compact-region field convergence, and T67's pairwise profile penalty. The law is the explicit research construction of T67, not a selected mass definition.
**Test:** `tests/test_pairwise_gaussian_bound.py`; finite pairwise and continuum checks in `tests/test_pairwise_anchor.py` and `tests/test_pairwise_continuum.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). A dimension-free self-normalized Gaussian quadratic bound proves that T67's penalty vanishes at the true mass, in L1, on the original full-prior T65 history. A local uniform extension preserves its conditional true-basin Gaussian law and suppresses the named false basin relative to the true basin. Remote posterior tails remain uncontrolled: no full-law concentration, global Gaussian limit or median limit is asserted. No prior, production equation, input, premise or ruling changes.

## 1. Exact Gaussian comparison, with arbitrary event count

At a fixed true candidate m0 and fixed rival t, whiten each independent
correctly declared Gaussian event and stack the observations as
z=mu+g, with g standard Gaussian. Define the block diagonal matrix

$$D=\operatorname{diag}_i(P_i(t)-P_i(m0)),\qquad
X=\frac12 z^TDz=L(t)-L(m0),\qquad V=z^TD^2z.$$

Each true mean belongs to its true candidate plane. The two projectors in
each block have the same rank. Consequently

$$\|D\|\le1,\quad \operatorname{tr}D=0,\quad
\mu^TD\mu=-\mu^TD^2\mu=-a,\quad a\ge0.$$

Indeed (P_i(t)-P_i(m0))mu_i=-(I-P_i(t))mu_i, whose squared norm equals
the loss of squared mean projection. No signal population or nuisance
prior is used in this identity. The actual Gaussian world, rather than
merely a Gaussian working model, is a condition of the result.

Put tau=tr(D^2) and b=a+tau=EV. Direct Gaussian quadratic moments give

$$EX=-a/2\le0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X)=a+\tau/2\le b,$$
$$\operatorname{Var}(V)=2\operatorname{tr}(D^4)
+4\mu^TD^4\mu\le4b.$$

These statements also cover arbitrary fixed known covariance shapes
after event-specific whitening. The bounds do not require comparable
shapes, diffuse signal leverage, fixed dimension or a bound on event count.

### First moment: a universal bound

Let W=X_+/sqrt(1+V). Since EX<=0, X_+<=|X-EX|. For b>0,
Cauchy--Schwarz gives

$$EW\le\sqrt{b\,E(1+V)^{-1}}.$$

Chebyshev's inequality implies P(V<b/2)<=16/b. Splitting according to
this event gives E(1+V)^-1<=16/b+2/b. Therefore

$$\boxed{EW\le\sqrt{18}.}$$

If b=0 then tau=0 and D=0, so W=0. The constant is deliberately coarse.
It is independent of the rival, true signals, number of events and
instrument shapes. Its purpose is a domination theorem, not an optimal
finite-sample confidence bound.

### Second moment: a uniform-integrability bound

For s>0, diagonalizing D^2 with eigenvalues w_j in [0,1] gives

$$E e^{-sV}=\prod_j(1+2sw_j)^{-1/2}
\exp\left[-\sum_j\frac{sw_j\mu_j^2}{1+2sw_j}\right]
\le\exp\left[-\frac{s b}{1+2s}\right].$$

Here log(1+x)>=x/(1+x), and 1+2sw_j<=1+2s. Taking s=1/4
in the exponential Markov bound yields

$$P(V<b/2)\le e^{-b/24}.$$

Write Y=X-EX=mu^TDg+(g^TDg-trD)/2. Gaussian fourth moments give
E(mu^TDg)^4=3a^2 and

$$E\{(g^TDg-\operatorname{tr}D)/2\}^4
=\frac34\tau^2+3\operatorname{tr}(D^4).$$

Using |x+y|^4<=8(|x|^4+|y|^4), and tr(D^4)<=tau, shows

$$EY^4\le24(b^2+b).$$

On V>=b/2, EW^2 restricted to that event is at most
(2/b)EY^2<=2. On its complement it is at most

$$\sqrt{EY^4}\sqrt{P(V<b/2)}
\le\sqrt{24}\sqrt{b^2+b}\,e^{-b/48}.$$

Since sqrt(b^2+b)<=b+1/2 and
sup_(b>=0)(b+1/2)e^-b/48=48e^-95/96<18, this proves the safe bound

$$\boxed{EW^2\le100.}$$

The case b=0 is again zero. No independence between numerator and
denominator was assumed. This second bound supplies uniform
integrability in the following limit; a bounded first moment alone
would not do so.

## 2. The original full-prior T65 history: true-point penalty vanishes

Retain T65's full half-Cauchy prior at center one, true mass m0=6/5,
informative covariance with correlation 9/10 at indices 2^(2^j), and
zero true signals with covariance diag(i^2,1) at all remaining indices.
No support is truncated or changed with N. Let M_N be the informative
count; it diverges, of order log log N. The pairwise law remains

$$A_N(m)=\int\frac{[L_N(t)-L_N(m)]_+}
{\sqrt{1+V_N(m,t)}}\Pi(dt),\qquad
Q_N(dm)\propto e^{-T_N(m)^2/2-\kappa A_N(m)}\Pi(dm).$$

Fix any finite t>0 distinct from m0. The informative observations have
a strictly negative expected profile contrast against truth, so their
strong law and T65's convergent null contrast field give

$$[L_N(t)-L_N(m0)]/M_N\longrightarrow-c_t<0.$$

Thus the corresponding nonnegative ratio W_N(t) tends to zero in
probability. Section 1's uniform second-moment bound makes these ratios
uniformly integrable, proving EW_N(t)->0. At t=m0 the ratio is exactly
zero. For every t and N its expectation is at most sqrt(18).
Tonelli's theorem and dominated convergence under the **fixed supplied
prior** therefore give

$$\boxed{E A_N(m0)=\int EW_N(t)\Pi(dt)\longrightarrow0.}$$

In particular A_N(m0)->0 in L1 and in probability. Remote rivals are
included in this statement. The proof does not replace the supplied
prior by an empirical posterior, or interchange a prior depending on N
with the limit.

## 3. Uniform smallness on the true local sampling scale

For each fixed H<infinity, take the proof-only neighborhood
J_(N,H)={m:|m-m0|<=H/sqrt(M_N)}, lying in a fixed positive compact
interval for all sufficiently large N. It is not a support restriction
or a neighborhood supplied to the construction. Define

$$Y_(N,H)=\sup_{m\in J_(N,H)}|L_N(m)-L_N(m0)|,$$
$$W_(N,H)=\sup_{m\in J_(N,H)}
\sum_i\|(P_i(m)-P_i(m0))z_i\|^2.$$

Both are O_p(1). For Y, the informative true score is O_p(sqrt(M_N))
and its Hessian is O_p(M_N) uniformly near truth. Taylor expansion over
radius H/sqrt(M_N) gives the bound. T65's null score and Hessian fields
converge on that compact interval, adding only O_p(1) terms before
the shrinking-radius factors.

For W, informative projector derivatives are uniformly bounded in this
fixed compact neighborhood and their total observed energy is O_p(M_N).
Their squared differences contribute at most
C H^2 M_N^-1 times that energy. Null projector derivatives are O(1/i)
uniformly there; sum_null i^-2||Z_i||^2 is finite almost surely.
Their shrinking-neighborhood contribution is bounded by the same
radius-squared factor times that finite random sum.

Set

$$B_N=\int[1+V_N(m0,t)]^{-1/2}\Pi(dt).$$

For each fixed t!=m0 the informative pairwise squared projection
difference has strictly positive expectation. Its law of large numbers
gives V_N(m0,t)/M_N->v_t>0; null terms are nonnegative. The integrand
is at most one and tends to zero in probability and in expectation.
The half-Cauchy has no atom at m0. Prior dominated convergence gives
EB_N->0, hence B_N->0 in probability.

To transfer the true-point result uniformly, use the triangle inequality
on the augmented vectors (1,((P_i(t)-P_i(m))z_i)_i):

$$\sqrt{1+V_N(m0,t)}
\le\sqrt{1+V_N(m,t)}+\sqrt{W_(N,H)}
\le(1+\sqrt{W_(N,H)})\sqrt{1+V_N(m,t)}.$$

Also [L_N(t)-L_N(m)]_+ is at most
[L_N(t)-L_N(m0)]_++Y_(N,H). Integrating these inequalities gives

$$\sup_{m\in J_(N,H)}A_N(m)
\le(1+\sqrt{W_(N,H)})
[A_N(m0)+Y_(N,H)B_N]\longrightarrow0$$

in probability. This proves local uniform smallness with the original
full prior, rather than only at one parameter value.

## 4. Conditional true-basin Gaussian law and sampling center

Choose one sufficiently small fixed interval I_t about m0, with compact
positive closure and excluding the false score root mf=4/15. This basin
is used only to state a conditional theorem, not passed to Q_N.
The informative expected profile has strictly negative curvature there.
T65's bounded null-field perturbations preserve a unique local profile
maximum mhat_(t,N), with
sqrt(M_N)(mhat_(t,N)-m0)=O_p(1). Write A>0 and B>0 for sensitivity and
score variability of one informative observation in physical mass
coordinates. Then

$$\sqrt{M_N}(\widehat m_(t,N)-m0)
\Rightarrow N(0,V_G),\qquad V_G=A^{-2}B.$$

The null score changes the informative center by O_p(1/M_N), which is
negligible on this scale. The profile Hessian divided by M_N tends to A
and the exact conditional score covariance divided by M_N tends to B.
T56's expansion therefore gives the local score law with variance V_G.

Section 3 also applies uniformly on bounded root-M_N neighborhoods
around this random center. To verify it, for any desired high sampling
probability first choose H0 with
sqrt(M_N)|mhat_(t,N)-m0|<=H0 on an event of that probability. A bounded
neighborhood of the fitted center lies in J_(N,H0+H) there. Apply the
fixed-H0+H uniform result, then let the excluded probability decrease
to zero. Tightness of the random center is sufficient; no random-radius
dominated-convergence assertion is assumed.

For tails inside I_t the local score derivative and upper conditional
covariance bound give T_N(m)^2>=c M_N|m-mhat_(t,N)|^2 with high
probability, as in T56/T67. The penalty is nonnegative. A root-M_N ball
about the fitted center has penalty o_p(1) and supplies normalizing mass
of order M_N^-1/2. The score bound controls the remaining rescaled
conditional tails by Gaussian tails. Local expansion and prior continuity
then yield

$$\operatorname{TV}\left(
Q_N\{\sqrt{M_N}(m-\widehat m_(t,N))\in\cdot\mid m\in I_t,Y\},
N(0,V_G)\right)\longrightarrow0$$

in sampling probability. In particular the unnormalized true-basin
integral is

$$J_(t,N)=\int_{I_t}e^{-T_N(m)^2/2-\kappa A_N(m)}\Pi(dm)
=\frac{\pi(m0)\sqrt{2\pi V_G}}{\sqrt{M_N}}(1+o_p(1)).$$

This is a conditional local law and quantile result. It does not
establish that the unconditional law puts probability tending to one
on I_t, or that a finite-law covariance exists.

## 5. Suppression of the named false basin under the full prior

Choose a sufficiently small fixed interval I_f about mf, disjoint from
I_t, with compact positive closure. A smaller fixed rival neighborhood
V0 of m0 has Pi(V0)>0. On these fixed compact sets T65 gives bounded
null profile-contrast fields, while the informative profile gap yields

$$\inf_{t\in V0,\,m\in I_f}[L_N(t)-L_N(m)]\ge c M_N$$

with probability tending to one. Null pairwise squared projection
differences have a uniform summable i^-2 envelope there. Informative
ones have total order O_p(M_N). Thus

$$\sup_{m\in I_f,t\in V0}V_N(m,t)=O_p(M_N),\qquad
\inf_{m\in I_f}A_N(m)=\Omega_p(\sqrt{M_N}).$$

This lower bound uses only a fixed true-rival neighborhood; all remote
rivals remain included in A_N. With J_(f,N) the corresponding false-
basin integral, the score kernel is at most one and hence

$$J_(f,N)\le\Pi(I_f)e^{-c_p\sqrt{M_N}}$$

on arbitrarily high probability events. Combining this with section 4
proves

$$\boxed{\frac{Q_N(I_f)}{Q_N(I_t)}
=\frac{J_(f,N)}{J_(t,N)}\longrightarrow0.}$$

It also implies Q_N(I_f)->0, since Q_N(I_t)<=1. This removes the
persistent named false basin responsible for T65's positive basin ratio,
with its original full half-Cauchy and unchanged event history. It
does not quantify posterior mass outside those two basins.

## What remains unproved

This result advances T67's full-prior caveat: the true local penalty,
conditional true-basin Gaussian shape and the named false/true ratio are
now controlled. A global posterior-tail argument is still missing for
T65's degenerating instruments. No full concentration theorem, global
TV Gaussian limit, unconditional median limit or all-identifying-history
calibration theorem follows from the basin comparison. Other remote
regions could still receive material probability.

The dimension-free comparison bound holds in the correctly declared
Gaussian graph experiment; it is not a noise-model forcing argument.
The pairwise criterion, original-prior rival measure, positive prefactor
and L19 state permission remain unforced. No criterion is adopted or
selected by these limits or by numerical scores.
