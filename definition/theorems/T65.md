# T65: Prior-tail disagreement can still dilute a mass-sensitive anchor

**From:** T56's two positive score roots, T63's distinction between identification and ambient energy, and T64's prior projection-variance state. The fixed full half-Cauchy prior and covariance history below are explicit theorem conditions.
**Test:** `tests/test_prior_tail_anchor_obstruction.py`; T56's two-root reference in `tests/test_score_law_obstruction.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). T64's state repairs T63's fixed compact-prior example but does not cover every identifying covariance history under an unchanged full-support half-Cauchy prior. A fixed Gaussian history has summable local nuisance evidence while its prior projection-variance state grows like log N. The true and false basin probabilities retain a positive limiting ratio, so the full law cannot concentrate at truth. No global two-point limit, median limit, impossibility of another construction, or new premise is claimed.

## Standard mathematics and the scope being tested

The proposed state is an average over the supplied prior's whole
hypothesis domain. Local information about the true parameter and global
prior-averaged disagreement need not be comparable when covariance shapes
degenerate. The proof uses elementary Gaussian quadratic forms,
Kolmogorov convergence of an independent square-summable-variance series,
and the local Laplace calculation in T56. It does not apply a generic
Bernstein--von Mises theorem without checking global normalization.

## 1. Keep the prior and specify one fixed Gaussian history

Use the full supplied half-Cauchy at center one, without truncation:

$$\Pi(dm)=\frac{2}{\pi(1+m^2)}\,dm,\qquad m>0.$$

Fix true mass m0=6/5 and independent observations

$$Y_i=(m_0 a_i,a_i)^T+\varepsilon_i,\qquad
\varepsilon_i\sim N_2(0,\Sigma_i).$$

At informative indices i=2^(2^j), j=1,2,..., set a_i=1 and

$$\Sigma_i=\Sigma_0=
\begin{pmatrix}1&9/10\\9/10&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

At every other index set a_i=0 and Sigma_i=diag(i^2,1).
All these declarations are fixed before data; every covariance is SPD.
The informative count is M_N=floor(log_2(log_2 N)) for N>=2, hence is
of order log log N and tends to infinity. This count is used only in
the proof, not supplied to the research law.

Use exactly T64's state and anchor:

$$\overline P_i=\int P_i(m)\Pi(dm),\qquad
\mathcal V_i=\overline P_i-\overline P_i^2,\qquad
U_N=\sum_{i\le N}z_i^T\mathcal V_i z_i,\qquad
\lambda_N=\kappa(1+U_N)^{-1/2},\quad\kappa>0,$$

and Q_N proportional to Pi times exp[-T_N^2/2+lambda_N L_N].
The whole-positive-domain finite law remains proper and has all T64's
bookkeeping and exact-zero properties. The following is a consistency
counterexample to its unrestricted-geometry extension.

## 2. The null instrument's prior variance has an exact formula

For covariance diag(e^-2,1), e>0, whiten with diag(e,1). The candidate
projector is

$$P_e(m)=\frac1{1+e^2m^2}
\begin{pmatrix}e^2m^2&em\\em&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

Integrating against the unchanged half-Cauchy gives

$$\overline P_e=
\begin{pmatrix}a_e&b_e\\b_e&1-a_e\end{pmatrix},\qquad
a_e=\frac e{1+e},\qquad
b_e=\frac{2e\log(1/e)}{\pi(1-e^2)}.$$

At e=1 the continuous value is b_1=1/pi. For the diagonal integral,
partial fractions give E[(1+e^2m^2)^-1]=1/(1+e). For the off-diagonal,
the substitution t=m^2 reduces the integral to

$$\int_0^\infty\frac{m\,dm}{(1+m^2)(1+e^2m^2)}
=\frac{\log(1/e)}{1-e^2}.$$

The mean projector has trace one, so Cayley--Hamilton gives

$$\mathcal V_e=v_e I_2,\qquad
v_e=\frac e{(1+e)^2}
-\left[\frac{2e\log(1/e)}{\pi(1-e^2)}\right]^2.$$

It is positive for every e>0, is invariant under e -> 1/e, and obeys
v_e=e+O(e^2[1+log^2 e]) as e decreases to zero. A prior half-Cauchy
center c would replace e by ec; no changed prior is used here.

Thus each null event at index i has e_i=1/i and contributes
v_(1/i)||Z_i||^2 to U_N, where Z_i=(xi_i,zeta_i) is standard Gaussian.
The prior's remote mass tail is important: over any fixed compact mass
interval the projector variance is instead O(e_i^2).

## 3. Local nuisance evidence converges, while the state diverges

Fix any compact positive mass interval I, and fix mref in I. The null
event has the same local formulas as T63, now with e_i=1/i:

$$u_i=(e_i m,1)^T/\sqrt{1+e_i^2m^2},\quad
v_i=(1,-e_i m)^T/\sqrt{1+e_i^2m^2},\quad
b_i=e_i/(1+e_i^2m^2),$$
$$L_i=-\tfrac12(v_i^TZ_i)^2,\qquad
S_i=b_i(u_i^TZ_i)(v_i^TZ_i),\qquad
C_i=b_i^2(u_i^TZ_i)^2.$$

On I, uniformly with each fixed number of derivatives,

$$L_i(m)-L_i(m_{\rm ref})
=e_i(m-m_{\rm ref})\xi_i\zeta_i
+O(e_i^2\|Z_i\|^2),$$
$$S_i(m)=e_i\xi_i\zeta_i+O(e_i^2\|Z_i\|^2),\qquad
\sup_{m\in I}|C_i(m)|\le c_I e_i^2\|Z_i\|^2.$$

For derivatives of order greater than one, the displayed leading linear
profile term vanishes; for positive-order score derivatives its constant
leading term vanishes. The same remainder bound applies. These expansions
follow by differentiating the rational functions on I.

The independent leading terms e_i xi_i zeta_i have mean zero and
summable variances sum_i i^-2. Their series converges almost surely by
the square-summable-variance convergence theorem. The remainders are
absolutely summable almost surely because their absolute expectations
sum to a finite number. The null centered profile and score fields
therefore converge in each fixed C^r norm on I. The conditional-C field
also converges absolutely there. Removing the sparse informative indices
preserves these arguments. The uncentered profile includes a diverging
mass-independent constant, which always cancels from the normalized law.

In contrast,

$$\sum_i\left|v_{1/i}-\frac1i\right|<\infty,\qquad
\sum_i v_{1/i}^2<\infty.$$

The centered series sum_i v_(1/i)(||Z_i||^2-2) converges almost surely,
because its variance is 4 sum_i v_(1/i)^2. The sparse removed indices
also have summable reciprocals. The null part of U_N is consequently
2 log N+O_a.s.(1). Each informative event has a fixed positive variance
operator and finite-mean energy, so its contribution is O_a.s.(M_N)
by the strong law on that subsequence. It follows that

$$\frac{U_N}{2\log N}\to1\quad\hbox{almost surely},\qquad
\lambda_N M_N\asymp\frac{\log\log N}{\sqrt{\log N}}\to0.$$

More generally every fixed positive-power schedule
lambda_N=kappa(1+U_N)^-gamma, gamma>0, has lambda_N M_N ->0 on this
same history. Merely changing that exponent does not remove this example.

## 4. The local false-to-true probability ratio obstructs concentration

The informative subsequence is T56's iid Gaussian experiment. It has
simple positive population score roots at m0=6/5 and mf=4/15. Choose
disjoint fixed open intervals I_t and I_f around those two masses, with
compact positive closures and no other score root. By section 3, adding
the null events perturbs each informative score root by O_p(1/M_N),
which is negligible relative to its M_N^-1/2 local width. Conditional C
changes by O_a.s.(1), negligible relative to M_N.

On the compact interval containing both basins, the profile contrast is
O_p(M_N) plus a bounded null field. Since lambda_N M_N tends to zero,
the anchor log variation tends to zero uniformly there. The local two
Laplace integrals are therefore those of T56, with the same supplied
half-Cauchy. Their common full-law normalizer cancels in their ratio:

$$\frac{Q_N(I_f)}{Q_N(I_t)}\to
R=\frac{1159/241}{\sqrt{47/19}}>0$$

in sampling probability. This statement is local to two fixed basins;
it does not require a proof excluding posterior mass at remote masses.
Since the basins are disjoint,

$$\left[Q_N(I_t)-\frac1{1+R}\right]_+\to0,
\qquad \frac1{1+R}=0.246445290575037\ldots.$$

The full law therefore cannot concentrate at truth. No full two-point
mixture, precise total false-basin mass or median limit is asserted:
those would require additional global-tail control. The Gaussian
conditional candidate test retains its exact coverage.

## 5. No vanishing schedule of this state gives a universal repair

The fixed doubly exponential history above defeats every positive power.
A history tailored to a fixed schedule gives the stronger class result:
let f:[0,infinity) -> (0,infinity) be any fixed finite Borel function with
f(u) -> 0 as u -> infinity, and set lambda_N=f(U_N). No monotonicity or
continuity of f is required. Each finite law is still proper, because
f(U_N) is one finite positive data-dependent number and L_N and T_N^2
are uniformly bounded at fixed data. This is a comparison of research
rules, not a new function-valued physical input.

By the definition of the limit, choose deterministic nonnegative thresholds t_j such
that f(u)<=j^-2 for **every** u>=t_j. Recursively choose integer informative
indices n_j satisfying

$$n_j\ge\max\{\exp(t_j),\exp(j^2),2n_{j-1}\},$$

starting from any fixed positive n_0. Give these indices the same
informative covariance and unit signal as before, and give all other
indices the null covariance diag(i^2,1). This specifies one fixed
deterministic history before data, for the chosen rule f. It is not a
data-dependent selection of informative observations.

The removed harmonic terms sum to a finite number. Sections 2 and 3
therefore still give U_(bad,N)=2 log N+O_a.s.(1). On n_j<=N<n_(j+1),
the informative count is M_N=j and log N>=j^2, so M_N=o(log N).
The informative contribution is O_a.s.(M_N) by its own strong law.
Consequently U_N/(2 log N) -> 1 almost surely, and on a probability-one
event U_N>=log N for every sufficiently large N. On that event,

$$f(U_N)M_N\le j\,j^{-2}=j^{-1}\to0
\qquad(n_j\le N<n_{j+1}),$$

because U_N>=log N>=log n_j>=t_j. This uses the uniform tail bound
defining t_j, not a monotonicity assumption about f. The local null
profile/score/C convergence from section 3 is unchanged, and M_N tends
to infinity. Thus section 4's positive false-to-true basin ratio and
nonconcentration conclusion also hold for this fixed history.

It follows that no finite positive schedule f(U) vanishing at large U
can give consistency on **all** identifying covariance histories in this
class. The counterexample history may depend on f; a universal rule
would have to handle every such fixed history. This does not cover
nonvanishing schedules, different states, or a general mass definition.

## 6. What this failure does and does not name

The declared informative subsequence increases without bound, so a
profile estimator using it alone is consistent. This is a fixed
identifiable Gaussian experiment, not an information impossibility.
It is outside T61's comparable-shape/growth regime and T64's specific
compact-prior repair. The full-support prior has not been selected after
data, changed with N, or declared wrong.

The failure names a remaining mismatch: global prior-averaged projector
disagreement can accumulate information about remote candidate regions
faster than local mass information grows. T64's state removes T63's
ambient-energy defect in its proved regimes, but a wider definition needs
a further argument or another state class. Picking a successful exponent,
truncating the supplied prior to avoid this example, or asserting a new
noise/input restriction is not a derived repair. The finite law, state
class and L19 authority remain unsorted.
