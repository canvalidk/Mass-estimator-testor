# T50: Gaussian operator graphs, TLS and an exact conditional vector score

**From:** T45's declared independent Gaussian experiment, extended here to a specified matrix candidate with full free signal vectors; T49's operator-graph comparison. This is a research benchmark, not a change to the scalar mass type.
**Test:** `tests/test_operator_score.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). The vector score supplies exact conditional candidate tests without a signal population. It selects neither a proper matrix law nor a scalarization, a mass point, a starting prior, or a production estimator. **Harvested 7 Nov from the exploration branch `codex/ours32-consistency` (Codex, 3–4 Nov) as input; not checked by Claude.**

This is functional errors-in-variables/total least squares and conditional
Gaussian score testing. For the standard SVD and projection/Bingham
connections, see [Besson, Dobigeon and Tourneret, section I and section III-B,
equations 12–13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.3462), and
[Markovsky and Van Huffel's TLS overview](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/263855/).
The calculations below specialize those geometric objects and extend T45's
conditional test to the explicitly selected operator tangent family.

## 1. The declared operator experiment and its Gaussian profile

Let each independent reading have fixed positive-definite covariance
$\Sigma_i$ and observed $Y_i=(F_i;a_i)\in\mathbb R^{2d}$. For this comparison
the unknown relation is an unrestricted real matrix $M\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$:

$$E Y_i=\begin{pmatrix}M\I_d\end{pmatrix}x_i,$$

where every $x_i\in\mathbb R^d$ is an arbitrary fixed nuisance vector. No
amplitude distribution is declared or estimated. The reference accepts full
reading subspaces only. A restricted $N_i$ for a matrix relation requires a
separate declaration about the domain/range relation and is not silently
imported from the scalar type.

The operator and observations are expressed in a common known frame in
this benchmark. Independently changing each reading's frame for a general
operator would also require its candidate and tangent matrices transformed
per reading; that extra interface is not supplied by the scalar Reading
type here. The scalar family mI is invariant under those simultaneous
spatial rotations, as required by the existing scalar model.

The exact coverage assertions assume that the world's errors have the
declared Gaussian laws. A Gaussian working model alone does not establish
these assertions for arbitrary non-Gaussian errors or hidden scale mixtures.

Choose any whitening $W_i$ with $W_i\Sigma_iW_i^T=I$, and put

$$z_i=W_iY_i,\qquad B_i(M)=W_i\begin{pmatrix}M\\I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P_i=B_i(B_i^TB_i)^{-1}B_i^T,\quad R_i=I-P_i.$$

The matrix $B_i$ has full column rank for every finite M. Maximizing the
normalized Gaussian density over each free $x_i$ gives, up to constants
independent of M,

$$\ell(M)=-\tfrac12\sum_i\|R_i z_i\|^2
=\tfrac12\sum_i z_i^TP_i z_i+\text{constant}.$$

This is profiling. It does not integrate an improper flat amplitude prior
and does not acquire a determinant from such an integration. Those are
different operations and require the corresponding measure to be specified.

### Common isotropic noise: TLS and a Bingham kernel

If $\Sigma_i=\sigma^2I_{2d}$, write
$S=\sum_iY_iY_i^T$ and let $P_M$ be the Euclidean projector onto the graph of M.
Then

$$\ell(M)=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(SP_M)}{2\sigma^2}
-\frac{\operatorname{tr}S}{2\sigma^2}.$$

The first term is the Grassmann Bingham kernel: relative to a separately
chosen orthogonally invariant plane measure, a density would be proportional
to $\exp\{\operatorname{tr}(SP)/(2\sigma^2)\}$. The Gaussian profile alone
does not force that measure. The unrestricted plane maximum is the span of
the top d eigenvectors of S, the usual total least squares (TLS) fit. It is
a finite graph M exactly when the acceleration block of a basis for that
plane is invertible. Otherwise the plane optimum lies outside this graph
chart; finite matrices can approach its profile value. At tied eigenvalues
the optimizing plane need not be unique. Restricting M to symmetric matrices
or another family also restricts the plane optimization, so an unrestricted
TLS eigenplane is no longer the asserted optimizer of that smaller problem.

Restrict **before** optimization to $M=mI_d$. In block notation,

$$\operatorname{tr}(SP_{mI})=
\frac{m^2\operatorname{tr}S_{FF}+2m\operatorname{tr}S_{Fa}
+\operatorname{tr}S_{aa}}{1+m^2}.$$

Thus this scalar profile reduces exactly to the $2\times2$ scatter

$$A=\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{tr}S_{FF}&\operatorname{tr}S_{Fa}\\
\operatorname{tr}S_{aF}&\operatorname{tr}S_{aa}\end{pmatrix}.$$

This identity does not equate an unrestricted matrix posterior followed by
a matrix-to-scalar map with a scalar model imposed before normalization.
The former also needs that map and a matrix measure; the scalar family is a
measure-zero submanifold of the full matrix chart when $d>1$.

## 2. The exact vector score

Choose explicit fixed tangent matrices $E_1,\ldots,E_p$. They may be a full
matrix basis, a symmetric basis, or another specified family. For the local
coordinate $M(t)=M+\sum_a t_aE_a$, put

$$B'_{ia}=W_i\begin{pmatrix}E_a\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
U_i=P_i z_i,\quad V_i=R_i z_i,\qquad
G_{ia}=R_iB'_{ia}B_i^+U_i.$$

The projector derivative is

$$\partial_a P_i=R_iB'_{ia}B_i^++(R_iB'_{ia}B_i^+)^T.$$

It has only off-diagonal blocks between the candidate mean plane and its
orthogonal complement. Hence the profile score and its conditional covariance
are

$$s_a=\partial_a\ell(M)=\sum_iV_i^TG_{ia},\qquad
C_{ab}=\sum_i\langle G_{ia},G_{ib}\rangle.$$

At the true fixed M, the mean lies in the candidate plane. Gaussian
projections give $V_i\sim N(0,R_i)$, independently of every $U_j$; the
different residuals are independent too. Therefore, conditional on the
realized own-reading projections,

$$s\mid(U_i)_i\sim N_p(0,C).$$

Let $r=\operatorname{rank}C$. Whenever $r>0$,

$$T=s^TC^+s\mid(U_i)_i\sim\chi^2_r.$$

To verify the singular case, stack the residual-coordinate tangent maps as
A, so $s=A^Tv$ and $C=A^TA$, with v an independent standard Gaussian vector.
Then $T$ is the squared norm of the orthogonal projection of v onto
$\operatorname{range}A$, whose dimension is r. This also shows why selecting
one scalar component of a multivariate chi-square or using p degrees of
freedom despite rank deficiency is incorrect.

The rank is observed and conditioned upon, rather than guessed from the
nominal parameter count. For a full matrix tangent basis, A kills a direction
E exactly when $E\widehat x_i=0$ for all i, where
$\widehat x_i=B_i^+U_i$. Indeed $R_iW_i(E\widehat x_i;0)=0$ implies
$(E\widehat x_i;0)=(Mx;x)$ and hence $E\widehat x_i=0$. If these fitted
vectors span an h-dimensional space, the rank is $dh$. Each has a
nondegenerate Gaussian density and readings are independent, so
$h=\min(N,d)$ almost surely at a fixed candidate in a nonempty Gaussian
experiment. In particular one d-dimensional reading gives rank d, not
$d^2$. For the symmetric tangent family the analogous rank is
$d(d+1)/2-(d-h)(d-h+1)/2$.

This conditional rank is about the observed candidate projections, not
the true signal's excitation or identifying information. Noisy projections
can have the displayed generic rank even at true null signal. The result
does not infer that unexcited operator directions have been learned, or
establish consistency merely from that conditional rank.

On observed rank zero the score is zero. The reference sets T=0 and the
p-value to 1, including empty and all-zero experiments. This supplies a
total candidate test, is not a uniquely forced convention, and need not be
continuous at exceptional data. For a genuine nonzero full or symmetric
tangent family in a nonempty declared experiment, the exceptional rank-zero
event has probability zero. Arbitrary user-selected zero tangents need not
have that property. At positive rank, inversion of the appropriate
$\chi^2_r$ test gives exact pointwise confidence-set membership at each true
matrix, for every collection of fixed signals. It does not supply a proper
probability law over matrices or a simultaneous test over all candidates.

## 3. Invariances and the scalar restriction

An exact-zero appended reading has $U_i=V_i=0$ and hence contributes zero to
s and C and does not change their rank. It therefore leaves the test exactly
unchanged mathematically. Independent sign reversal of a reading changes
both U and V in sign and leaves its score and covariance contributions
unchanged.

An invertible reparameterization of the selected tangent family, with
$\widetilde E_a=\sum_b E_bJ_{ba}$, gives
$\widetilde s=J^Ts$ and $\widetilde C=J^TCJ$. Its stacked map has the same
column space, so the statistic and rank are unchanged, including singular C.

Under invertible force and acceleration coordinate maps H and K,

$$F\mapsto HF,\quad a\mapsto Ka,\quad
M\mapsto HMK^{-1},\quad E_a\mapsto HE_aK^{-1},$$

with covariance transformed accordingly, whitening changes only by an
orthogonal map. The inner products and statistic are preserved. This
includes ordinary unit and frame changes. For invertible M, channel swap
gives $M\mapsto M^{-1}$ and
$E_a\mapsto-M^{-1}E_aM^{-1}$, again preserving the test. A singular M has
no swapped finite graph in this chart; no matrix-inverse claim is made there.

Choosing only the scalar tangent $E=I_d$ at $M=mI_d$ reproduces T45's scalar
candidate test. T45's research implementation differentiates in
$\phi=\arctan m$, which multiplies the
score by $1+m^2$ and the variance by its square, leaving T unchanged. This
is a one-dimensional restriction of the operator experiment; it is generally
different from the full-matrix joint test at the same numerical matrix.

## 4. Numerical reference and validation scope

`met/operator_score.py` exposes `operator_tangent_basis`,
`gaussian_operator_profile` and `gaussian_operator_score_test`. The test
requires tangent directions and both singular-value rank tolerances. It uses
QR for each declared mean plane and an SVD of the stacked conditional score
map. Singular values strictly exceeding
$\max(\text{rank_atol},\text{rank_rtol}\,s_{\max})$ are retained. These are
explicit floating-point rank controls, not a statistical gate or a new
physical confidence threshold. Numerical rank may cease to respect exact
reparameterization identities at unresolved singular values. The theorem
uses exact rank, not a tolerance-dependent approximation to it.

The implementation recognizes exactly vanishing supplied floating-point
normal equations before QR self-normalization. As in T45, this is not a
certified cancellation detector for every ill-conditioned declaration.
Out-of-range arithmetic is rejected, rather than interpreted statistically.
No arbitrary-precision or general floating-point totality result is claimed.

The tests independently differentiate a raw-coordinate normal-equation
profile, differentiate its projector for the covariance, integrate fixed-U
Gaussian residuals by Hermite quadrature, and check a rank-two conditional
acceptance probability by adaptive Gaussian integration. They also check
the TLS eigenplane, scalar trace-scatter identity, rank deficiency, full
coordinate and tangent transformations, exact-zero padding, and agreement
with T45 on the scalar restriction. These are deterministic model checks,
not a production adoption or an estimator coverage ranking.
