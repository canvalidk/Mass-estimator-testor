## Statement

the error model must be a full $\Sigma_i$ per reading; ours26's diagonal model gives a different answer in a rotated frame

## Proof or support

### Rotating the same declared reading

Let $R$ be an orthogonal change of spatial axes and $T=\operatorname{diag}(R,R)$
its action on the stacked force–acceleration space. L16 requires that the
same reading be represented by
$$
 y'=Ty,\qquad \Sigma'=T\Sigma T^\top,\qquad N'=RN.
$$
For the candidate matrix in `../equation.md`, $C'(\phi)=TC(\phi)$. Since
$T^\top T=I$, $(\Sigma')^{-1}=T\Sigma^{-1}T^\top$, and therefore
$$
 G'=C'^\top(\Sigma')^{-1}C'=C^\top\Sigma^{-1}C=G,\qquad
 b'=C'^\top(\Sigma')^{-1}y'=C^\top\Sigma^{-1}y=b.
$$
Consequently $H'(\phi)=b'^\top G'^{-1}b'=H(\phi)$ for every candidate.
This also covers a proper declared subspace, since its columns rotate with
the data. An equivalent orthonormal basis $N'A$ gives $G'\mapsto A^\top G'A$
and $b'\mapsto A^\top b'$, again leaving $H'$ unchanged; thus a full subspace
may still be represented by $I_d$ after rotation. The whitened total energy
in the stacked declared span is likewise
unchanged. Thus the positive-domain maximum defining $Q_+$, the dimension
$k$ and $\kappa=k-\tfrac12$, and the resulting $g$ are unchanged. For a
series rotated reading by reading, each function $\ell_i(\phi)$ and all its
derivatives are unchanged, so the common best angle and the computed $\eta$
are unchanged. With the same declared starting law the mass law, its median
and its intervals are identical.

### Why a diagonal-only representation is insufficient

The class of diagonal covariance matrices is not closed under these changes
of axes: $R\operatorname{diag}(v_1,v_2)R^\top$ generally has off-diagonal
entries when $v_1\ne v_2$. Discarding them describes a different instrument,
not the same reading in new coordinates. L16 therefore requires the ability
to carry the **full covariance**. It does not require nonzero correlations
on every input; an isotropic covariance remains diagonal under rotation.

Here is an exact 2D example, with $c=1$, $N=I_2$,
$$
 F=(1,0)^\top,\quad a=(0,1)^\top,\quad
 \Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,4,1,1),\quad
 R=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Before rotation, the explained energy at candidate mass $m$ is
$$
 H(m)=\frac{m^2}{m^2+1}+\frac4{m^2+4}.
$$
The rotated force covariance is
$\begin{pmatrix}5/2&-3/2\\-3/2&5/2\end{pmatrix}$, and the acceleration
covariance is $I_2$. Keeping the full rotated covariance gives the same
$H(m)$. Keeping only its diagonal instead gives
$$
 H_{\rm diag}(m)=\frac{(4/25)m^2+1}{(2/5)m^2+1}.
$$
At $m=1$ these are $H(1)=13/10$ and $H_{\rm diag}(1)=29/35$; at $m=0$
both equal 1. Hence their difference is not a constant that normalization
could remove. In this example both readings have $g=\eta=1$ under ours30,
so even the actual tempered laws differ after the correlations are dropped.
The new named test checks the exact energies and the change of law; the
existing rotation test checks invariance with the full covariance retained.

The historical ours26 failure and ours27 repair are documented separately in
`../../docs/ours27/ours27_full_covariance_2026-09-27.md` §2. Its readout table
belongs to that historical equation; the exact example above states the
same loss of covariance information directly for the current mass factor.
