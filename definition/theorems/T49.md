# T49: Graph geometry gives conditional measures, not a forced mass prior

**From:** L1's scalar graph, L3's covariance bookkeeping, the full input type; T45's projector derivative and T48's explicit latent-measure distinction.
**Test:** `tests/test_graph_geometry.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). The canonical Grassmann metric gives an intrinsic scalar-graph half-Cauchy in the equal-noise case, independently of spatial dimension. Ambient graph volume, induced curve volume and latent-fiber volume are different measures. No prior, signal population, noise interpretation, production factor or ruling is adopted.

## 1. Standard geometry and its scope

Work over the reals. A rank-k orthogonal projector P represents a point of
Gr(k,n). The standard canonical metric is

$$g_P(\dot P_1,\dot P_2)=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\dot P_1\dot P_2).$$

For an orthonormal frame U and horizontal tangent W A, with W an
orthonormal complement, this is tr(A^T A). This is the canonical quotient
metric of [Edelman, Arias and Smith (1998), sections 2.3.2 and 2.5](https://math.mit.edu/~edelman/publications/geometry_of_algorithms.pdf).
Its normalized volume is the orthogonally invariant probability measure
on the compact Grassmannian. Coordinate densities require Jacobians;
[Jauch, Hoff and Dunson (2020)](https://doi.org/10.3150/19-BEJ1176)
develop such changes of variables for Stiefel and Grassmann manifolds.
The formulas below are direct graph-chart calculations of this standard
geometry, not a new invariance principle for physical mass.

An arbitrary p-by-k matrix M has graph C_M=[M;I_k] in R^(p+k).
Introducing all such operators is a mathematical comparison; L1 still
declares the single scalar m shared by all components and readings.

## 2. Graph-chart metric and volume, including full covariance

First let the ambient noise metric be identity. Put
R=(I_k+M^T M)^(-1), L=(I_p+M M^T)^(-1), and choose frames
U=[M;I]R^(1/2), W=[I;-M^T]L^(1/2). For a graph variation A,
the transverse frame derivative is W^T dU=L^(1/2) A R^(1/2).
Consequently

$$g_M(A,B)=\operatorname{tr}(R A^T L B),\qquad
d\mathrm{vol}(M)\propto\det(I_k+M^T M)^{-(p+k)/2}\,dM.$$

Indeed the coordinate metric is R tensor L. Its determinant is
det(R)^p det(L)^k; det(I+M^T M)=det(I+M M^T) gives the density.
For square d-by-d M the exponent is d, not d^2.
As an independent probability check, the span of independent standard
Gaussian blocks [X;Y], with X p-by-k and Y k-by-k, is Haar-uniform.
Almost surely Y is invertible and its graph is M=X Y^(-1).
The substitution X=M Y contributes |det Y|^p; integrating Y after
Y -> (I+M^T M)^(-1/2)Y gives the same determinant power.

Now fix any positive-definite ambient covariance Sigma. Let Omega=Sigma^-1,
C=[M;I_k], D=[I_p;0], and define

$$G=C^T\Omega C,\qquad
E=D^T\Omega D-D^T\Omega C G^{-1}C^T\Omega D.$$

After fixed whitening, put B_0=Sigma^(-1/2)C and let P project onto B_0.
The derivative P'_A=R_P B'_(0,A) B_0^+ + its transpose,
with R_P=I-P, yields

$$g_M(A,B)=\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}A^T E B).$$

Both G and E are positive definite. The block basis [D,C] has determinant
one, so its Omega Gram matrix has determinant det(Omega). Taking its
Schur complement gives det(E)det(G)=det(Omega). Therefore

$$d\mathrm{vol}_{\Sigma}(M)\propto
\det(\Omega)^{k/2}\det(C^T\Omega C)^{-(p+k)/2}\,dM.$$

The covariance-only prefactor is retained to make the metric calculation
explicit. Any other fixed whitening differs by an orthogonal map.
An invertible bookkeeping transformation with the complete hypothesis family
and its covariance transformed accordingly leaves the geometry unchanged.

## 3. Scalar graphs and correlated scalar noise

For M=m I_d in identity noise, the pullback along this curve is

$$ds^2=\frac{d}{(1+m^2)^2}\,dm^2.$$

The angle theta=atan(m) traces a Grassmann geodesic with speed sqrt(d).
Its intrinsic positive-curve volume, normalized on m>0, is thus
2/[pi(1+m^2)] dm. If the common force/acceleration noise ratio is s,
the standardized operator is (m/s)I_d, giving speed
sqrt(d)*s/(m^2+s^2) and normalized density 2s/[pi(m^2+s^2)].
The dimension factor cancels on normalization. This is a conditional
geometric construction, not a derivation that the input prior must equal it.

For arbitrary fixed Sigma on the scalar curve, use D_0=[I_d;0] in
the definitions of G and E above. Its intrinsic speed is

$$\sqrt{g(m)}=\sqrt{\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(P'(m)^2)}
=\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}E)}.$$

Generic Sigma does not give a half-Cauchy. Independent component noise
ratios s_j give g(m)=sum_j s_j^2/(m^2+s_j^2)^2. The same scalar
calculation for a declared k-dimensional subspace replaces I_d by N
and d by k in the equal-noise case.

In one dimension write
Sigma=[[sf^2,rho*sf*sa],[rho*sf*sa,sa^2]], |rho|<1, and s=sf/sa.
Then

$$\sqrt{g(m)}=\frac{\sqrt{\det\Sigma}}
{sa^2m^2-2\operatorname{cov}(F,a)m+sf^2}
=\frac{s\sqrt{1-\rho^2}}{m^2-2\rho s m+s^2}.$$

The positive arc has total length L=pi/2+arcsin(rho). Its normalized
intrinsic-volume CDF is

$$F(m)=\frac{\arctan\!\left((m/s-\rho)/\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\right)
+\arcsin\rho}{\pi/2+\arcsin\rho}.$$

This has F(0)=0, F(infinity)=1 and F(s)=1/2 for every permitted rho;
the last identity uses atan((1-rho)/sqrt(1-rho^2))
=pi/4-arcsin(rho)/2. It is reciprocal-symmetric under m -> s^2/m.
For rho=0 it is the half-Cauchy. Correlation changes the shape and arc
length, while preserving this conditional geometric median.

In x=log(m/s), the corresponding normalized density is
g_rho(x)=sqrt(1-rho^2)/[2L(cosh(x)-rho)], which is even. Thus this
geometric family belongs to O6's admissible unit/reciprocal family; the
calculation does not replace that declared family by a single member.

## 4. Ambient restriction and two different tube limits

For d>1 the scalar curve has ambient Haar probability zero: its dimension
is one, while the graph chart has dimension d^2. Evaluating the ambient
coordinate density at M=mI gives (1+m^2)^(-d^2), not a restriction of
that probability measure and not the induced curve volume.

Two explicit conditioning conventions demonstrate the difference. Fix a
compact positive interval I and write M=mI+A, tr(A)=0, using Euclidean
coordinates on the r=d^2-1 traceless directions. The fixed coordinate
Jacobian is sqrt(d). Conditioning ambient Haar on m in I and
||A||_F<epsilon gives the limiting m density

$$q_{\rm chart}(m)\propto(1+m^2)^{-d^2},\qquad m\in I.$$

Instead use a radius-epsilon normal tube in the canonical Grassmann
metric and project each nearby point to its base point on the curve.
At mI the chart metric is (1+m^2)^(-2) times the Frobenius metric.
Its normal ball therefore has Euclidean transverse radius
epsilon*(1+m^2)+O(epsilon^2). The cross-section contributes
(1+m^2)^r, so the limiting projected density is

$$q_{\rm intrinsic}(m)\propto(1+m^2)^{-1},\qquad m\in I.$$

Normal-coordinate expansions are uniform on I; endpoint caps are lower
order, or can be excluded by specifying the nearest-base projection.
These are two different tube conventions, not contradictory conditionals.
More directly, changing transverse coordinates to B=h(m)A multiplies the
substituted density by h(m)^(-r), without changing the curve or m.
No unique zero-set conditioning follows from ambient Haar alone.

## 5. Hypothesis geometry, latent measure and Fisher information

T48's declared-metric latent-fiber volume is sqrt(det G)*dalpha.
It measures vectors inside one fixed graph. The curve volume sqrt(g)*dm
measures changes between graphs. In equal-noise scalar geometry their
factors are respectively (1+m^2)^(d/2) and sqrt(d)/(1+m^2).
They cannot be substituted for each other.

For a Gaussian experiment with mean C_m alpha and known covariance,
eliminating the unknown alpha from its Fisher information by the Schur
complement gives I_eff(m,alpha)=alpha^T E alpha. This depends on the
signal and vanishes at alpha=0; tr(G^-1 E) does neither. In fact the
joint mean-manifold volume away from its zero-mean singularity is
sqrt(det G)*sqrt(alpha^T E alpha) dm dalpha.
Grassmann volume is not a signal-free proof of a Jeffreys
prior for this nuisance experiment.

Replacing Sigma by v*Sigma leaves each whitened graph projector and its
canonical metric unchanged: the common whitening scale cancels. It also
cancels in section 2's ambient chart-volume formula. By contrast, latent
arclength in the realized v*Sigma metric multiplies declared arclength by
v^(-k/2). Thus graph geometry does not choose the exponent beta in T48's
possible conditional measures v^beta dt, or force its noise-mixture model.

## 6. What the geometry leaves open

Full active O(2d) invariance supplies the ambient Haar construction but is
an additional hypothesis-space symmetry. Generic such rotations do not
preserve M=mI or its positive branch. Physical common spatial rotations
diag(Q,Q) preserve every scalar graph individually and therefore do not
force a distribution along the mass curve. Passive covariance bookkeeping
must not be promoted silently to this larger active symmetry.

With heterogeneous declarations one could choose a product Grassmann
metric, g_series=sum_i g_i, but that aggregation is another choice.
For example one scalar noise ratio 1 gives g_1=(1+m^2)^(-2); a second
ratio 2 adds g_2=4/(4+m^2)^2, which is not proportional to g_1.
If this internal default replaces the supplied fixed prior by normalized
sqrt(g_series), appending an exact-zero second reading changes that prior
and the resulting law, despite its constant likelihood contribution.
It cannot establish the existing Z claim at fixed input pi. Recomputing
a caller's separately declared prior changes the input and is not itself
a counterexample to the existing fixed-prior procedure.

These calculations identify standard conditional geometric measures and
their distinctions. They do not select a reference covariance across
readings, prescribe the input pi, enlarge L1's physical hypothesis or
replace any production rule.
