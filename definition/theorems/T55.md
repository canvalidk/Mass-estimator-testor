# T55: Joint profile uncertainty generally needs more than one scalar inflation

**From:** T54's explicitly declared common-acceleration Gaussian experiment; T49's projector metric; T51's centered-Gaussian covariance benchmark. The repeated-block regime and exact nominal calibration target below are explicit theorem conditions, not new VD requirements.
**Test:** `tests/test_joint_sandwich.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). The Gaussian profile has sensitivity A and score covariance B=A+G. Its local law width is inverse sensitivity, while its fitted center has sandwich sampling covariance. One scalar inflation matches every composite's Gaussian uncertainty exactly only when B is proportional to A. A fully declared two-body example fails that condition. Conservative scalar inflation remains possible. No joint calibration map, production rule, physical input or ruling is selected.

## Standard mathematics and the object being calibrated

This is estimating-function/Godambe information and the sandwich covariance
of an M-estimator. Standard background includes Godambe (1960),
[An Optimum Property of Regular Maximum Likelihood Estimation](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177705693).
The corresponding distinction for normalized exponential
contrasts is developed by Chernozhukov and Hong (2003),
[sections 3.3–3.4, Theorems 1–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.07782v1):
the local quasi-posterior shape uses inverse curvature, while sampling
inference generally requires the score variance as well. That link is an
archival author version of the published article, as its opening notice
states. The Gaussian score and local-limit calculations used here are
proved below; no theorem requiring a normalized data likelihood is
applied to a profile as if it were such a likelihood.

The parameter is a vector of K **scalar body masses**. It is not a matrix
of physical inertia. A covariance matrix for their joint uncertainty does
not change L1's scalar relation for each addressed body.

## 1. Repeat a fixed finite protocol block

Fix L physical event types, spatial dimension d, and an interior true mass
vector m0 in (0,infinity)^K. At event i, all bodies share one true acceleration
a_i in R^d. Keep a_i fixed, and let

$$C(m)=\begin{pmatrix}m_1I_d\\ \vdots\\m_KI_d\\I_d\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Y_{bi}=C(m_0)a_i+\varepsilon_{bi},\qquad
\varepsilon_{bi}\sim N(0,\Sigma_i),$$

where i=1,...,L and b=1,...,n. The world's errors are independent across
events and blocks, with fixed positive-definite declared Sigma_i. At least
one a_i is nonzero. Repetition here specifies a calibration benchmark:
there is no random population of accelerations, and their values are not
provided to an estimator. This does not require real experiments to repeat
one setup or make such repetition part of the definition's input.

Choose fixed whitenings W_i Sigma_i W_i^T=I. Put

$$X_i(m)=W_iC(m),\qquad
P_i(m)=X_i(X_i^TX_i)^{-1}X_i^T,\qquad R_i=I-P_i.$$

The Gaussian profile criterion for one event is

$$\ell_i(m;z)=-\tfrac12\|R_i(m)z\|^2
=\tfrac12z^TP_i(m)z+\text{constant},\qquad z=W_iY.$$

Its n-block sum is L_n. A proper supplied joint prior with a Lebesgue
density positive and continuous near m0 defines the raw research law

$$Q_n(dm)=\frac{\exp[L_n(m)]\Pi(dm)}
{\int\exp[L_n(u)]\Pi(du)}.$$

T54's bounded-factor argument makes this proper for every finite data set.
Its local Gaussian covariance below is not a promise that its actual
finite-n second moments exist. For example, a heavy-tailed supplied prior
can retain infinite second moments under a bounded profile factor.

## 2. Exact sensitivity and score covariance for one block

All derivatives and matrices in this section are evaluated at m0. Write
P_ij=partial P_i/partial m_j, and let D_ij be the raw mean derivative:
it has a_i in force block j and zero in every other block. Define

$$v_{ij}=R_iW_iD_{ij},\qquad
A_{jk}=\sum_{i=1}^L v_{ij}^Tv_{ik},\qquad
G_{jk}=\tfrac12\sum_{i=1}^L\operatorname{tr}(P_{ij}P_{ik}).$$

Then the block profile score s satisfies

$$E[s]=0,\qquad -E[\nabla^2\textstyle\sum_i\ell_i]=A,\qquad
B:=\operatorname{Cov}(s)=A+G.$$

**Proof.** The projector derivative has only off-diagonal blocks relative
to P_i and R_i. With mu_i=W_iC(m0)a_i, this gives

$$P_{ij}\mu_i=v_{ij},\qquad
\operatorname{tr}P_{ij}=0,\qquad
\mu_i^TP_{ij}\mu_i=0.$$

Writing z_i=mu_i+e_i, e_i~N(0,I), the event score is therefore

$$s_{ij}=\tfrac12z_i^TP_{ij}z_i
=v_{ij}^Te_i+\tfrac12(e_i^TP_{ij}e_i-\operatorname{tr}P_{ij}).$$

The linear terms have covariance v_ij^T v_ik. Centered Gaussian quadratic
forms have covariance tr(P_ij P_ik)/2, and the cross covariances vanish by
the zero third Gaussian moments. Independent events add these covariances.

For sensitivity, the expected profile loss relative to truth is
one half of sum_i ||R_i(m)mu_i||^2: the noise contribution is constant
because every projector has rank d. Its first derivative vanishes at
truth, and its Hessian is A. This proves the identities.

G is positive semidefinite: for every vector t,

$$t^TGt=\tfrac12\sum_i\left\|\sum_jt_jP_{ij}\right\|_F^2\ge0.$$

It is the summed canonical graph metric, not signal Fisher information.
A depends on the fixed accelerations. It is positive definite if some
a_i is nonzero: R_iW_i sum_j t_jD_ij=0 would put
(t_1a_i,...,t_Ka_i,0) in the candidate mean plane. Its bottom component
forces the plane coordinate to zero, and hence t=0.

In T54's raw coordinates this same matrix is

$$A=\sum_iD_i(a_i)^TE_i(m_0)D_i(a_i),\qquad
E_i=\Omega_i-\Omega_iC(C^T\Omega_iC)^{-1}C^T\Omega_i,\quad
\Omega_i=\Sigma_i^{-1}.$$

## 3. The fitted center and raw law have different local covariances

Let mhat_n be a consistent profile maximizer. Under the stated repeated
finite-block experiment,

$$\sqrt n(\widehat m_n-m_0)\ \Rightarrow\
N_K(0,V),\qquad V=A^{-1}BA^{-1},$$

whereas the conditional raw law in coordinate sqrt(n)(m-mhat_n) converges
in total variation, in sampling probability, to N_K(0,A^{-1}).
The corresponding Godambe information is V^{-1}=A B^{-1}A.

Here these regularity statements follow directly. For each event,

$$E[\ell_i(m)-\ell_i(m_0)]
=-\tfrac12\|R_i(m)\mu_i\|^2.$$

If a_i is nonzero, its expected loss is zero only at m0: the common
acceleration block fixes the candidate acceleration, and the force blocks
then fix every mass. Uniform separation also holds away from m0 after
compactifying the graph family by
(m_1,...,m_K,1)/sqrt(1+||m||^2). The closure is compact; its candidate
planes retain rank d, and no boundary plane contains that nonzero true
mean. Empirical second-moment matrices converge for each of the finite
event types, giving a uniform law of large numbers for the projected
quadratic criteria. This proves consistency and a separated loss gap.

Near m0, the projector derivatives are smooth, with integrable Gaussian
quadratic envelopes for the needed derivatives. The iid block score CLT
and the Hessian law of large numbers give

$$n^{-1/2}\nabla L_n(m_0)\Rightarrow N_K(0,B),\qquad
-n^{-1}\nabla^2L_n(m)\to A$$

locally at consistent m. Taylor expansion of the score gives the sampling
limit. Taylor expansion of the criterion about mhat_n gives the quadratic
local log law with curvature A. Local positive curvature bounds its
root-n tails; the separated loss gap bounds the remaining prior-weighted
integral exponentially. The proper prior and its local positive continuous
density then give the normalized TV Laplace limit. No finite prior variance
is needed for this local shape/quantile result. Actual moment convergence
would require additional moment and tail conditions.

For a differentiable scalar composite q with nonzero gradient g at m0,
the raw local law uses variance g^T A^{-1}g/n. Its fitted center has
sampling variance g^T Vg/n. These are two different probability statements.

This does not conflict with T54's fixed-event small-noise theorem. If each
covariance is epsilon^2 times a fixed shape, the projectors and G do not
change, while A_epsilon=epsilon^(-2) A_0. Thus the repeated-block Gaussian
sampling width is

$$\frac1n\left[
\epsilon^2 A_0^{-1}+\epsilon^4 A_0^{-1}G A_0^{-1}\right],$$

and its raw-law counterpart is epsilon^2 A_0^{-1}/n. The extra term is
lower order as epsilon decreases. Fixed-noise replication and reducing
the noise in a fixed experiment are different limits.

## 4. Exactly what one scalar inflation can and cannot do

Consider a regular calibration whose joint Gaussian local covariance is
lambda*A^{-1}/n, for one positive scalar lambda, and whose local center is
mhat_n+o_p(n^(-1/2)). A global power eta_n
with eta_n->eta0>0 has this form with lambda=1/eta0. The comparison here
also covers any other construction with that same scalar-inflated local
Gaussian shape and center; it does not reduce arbitrary joint calibration
rules to this class. A covariance specification alone would not give
the displayed coverage if the calibration moved its center at first order.

At a fixed central probability p, the composite interval's limiting
sampling coverage is

$$2\Phi\!\left(z_p
\sqrt{\frac{\lambda\,g^TA^{-1}g}{g^TVg}}\right)-1,\qquad
z_p=\Phi^{-1}((1+p)/2).$$

Consequently one scalar gives exact nominal coverage for **all** positive
weighted linear composites if and only if

$$V=\lambda A^{-1},\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad B=\lambda A.$$

Indeed exact coverage forces equality of the quadratic forms on an open
positive orthant; a quadratic polynomial vanishing there vanishes
identically, as in T51. Conversely equality gives the stated nominal
Gaussian coverage for every nonzero gradient. In two dimensions, exact
coverage of the two individual masses and their sum already forces this
matrix equality.

This is an exact centered-Gaussian calibration benchmark. Neither property N
nor the provisional L5/L14 ruling is asserted to demand this stronger
benchmark or every weighted composition.

### Conservative scalar calibration remains possible

For at-least-nominal Gaussian coverage of every real linear contrast, the
sharp Loewner condition is

$$\lambda A^{-1}-V\succeq0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\lambda\ge\lambda_{\max}(A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}).$$

G>=0 makes this maximum eigenvalue at least one. Thus an eta cap at one
does not itself obstruct a sufficiently conservative scalar. Restricting
the required gradients to the positive orthant gives a weaker
copositivity condition in general; the displayed Loewner condition remains
sufficient. Exact nominal matching and conservative coverage must not be
confused. No universal scalar-calibration impossibility is claimed for the
weaker at-least-coverage target.

## 5. A two-body, two-event declared Gaussian counterexample

Take K=2, d=1, true masses (1,1), and a_1=a_2=1. In observed coordinate
order (F_1,F_2,A), choose

$$\Sigma_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1),\qquad
\Sigma_2=\operatorname{diag}(1,9,1).$$

All measurements have strictly positive declared noise. The two event
types repeat as specified above. At truth, their force-block projected
precision matrices are

$$E_1=\begin{pmatrix}2/3&-1/3\\-1/3&2/3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
E_2=\begin{pmatrix}10/19&-1/19\\-1/19&2/19\end{pmatrix}.$$

The latent Gram values C^T Omega_i C are 3 and 19/9, respectively.
For a one-dimensional latent plane, the projector derivative calculation
gives G_i=E_i/(C^T Omega_i C). Since a_i=1, A_i=E_i. Therefore

$$A=E_1+E_2=\frac1{57}
\begin{pmatrix}68&-22\\-22&44\end{pmatrix},\qquad
G=\frac{E_1}{3}+\frac{9E_2}{19},$$

$$B=A+G=\frac1{3249}
\begin{pmatrix}5408&-1696\\-1696&3392\end{pmatrix},$$

$$A^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&17/11\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V=A^{-1}BA^{-1}
=\begin{pmatrix}80/57&40/57\\40/57&14504/6897\end{pmatrix}.$$

The eigenvalues of A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2} are
848/627 and 80/57, approximately 1.35247209 and 1.40350877.
They differ, so B is not proportional to A.

The following table gives the local variances multiplied by n. The last
column is the eta required to make that individual Gaussian interval
exactly nominal:

| Quantity | Gradient at (1,1) | Raw local variance | Sampling variance | Required eta |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First mass | (1,0) | 1 | 80/57 | 57/80 = 0.7125 |
| Second mass | (0,1) | 17/11 | 14504/6897 | 10659/14504 = 0.7349007170... |
| Sum | (1,1) | 39/11 | 11288/2299 | 8151/11288 = 0.7220942594... |
| Parallel sum | (1/4,1/4) | 39/176 | 1411/4598 | 8151/11288 = 0.7220942594... |

The parallel-sum row propagates this same joint experiment's mass law;
it is not a separately specified common-force acquisition. At equal
masses its gradient is parallel to the sum gradient, explaining their
identical required eta. Already the first two rows preclude one scalar
being exactly nominal for both.

For this example the sharp all-contrast conservative choice is
lambda=80/57, or eta=57/80. It makes the first-mass interval nominal and
the other displayed intervals conservative. Larger lambda remains
conservative. The example therefore does not contradict an
at-least-nominal coverage requirement.

## 6. An observable sandwich diagnostic and its limits

The population accelerations above are proof quantities, not estimator
inputs. At a consistent profile fit, use sums over the individual physical
events:

$$\widehat H_n=-\sum_{b,i}\nabla^2\ell_i(\widehat m_n;z_{bi}),\qquad
\widehat J_n=\sum_{b,i}s_{bi}(\widehat m_n)s_{bi}(\widehat m_n)^T.$$

Uniform local laws of large numbers give Hhat_n/n->A and Jhat_n/n->B.
Where Hhat_n is invertible,

$$n\,\widehat H_n^{-1}\widehat J_n\widehat H_n^{-1}
\ \to\ A^{-1}BA^{-1}.$$

For completeness, the event derivatives can be calculated without
projector finite differences. At any candidate set X=W_iC(m),
G0=X^TX, xhat=G0^{-1}X^Tz, e=z-X*xhat. Because X is linear in m,
let X_j=partial X/partial m_j and define

$$d_j=X_j\widehat x,\qquad
b_j=X_j^Te-X^Td_j.$$

The envelope score and Hessian are exactly

$$s_j=e^Td_j,\qquad
\partial_{jk}\ell=b_j^TG_0^{-1}b_k-d_j^Td_k.$$

They follow by differentiating X^Te=0, which gives
partial_k xhat=G0^{-1}b_k, and then differentiating the envelope score.

An independent exact-zero event contributes zero score and Hessian, so
these **sums**, their sandwich and their generalized inflation spectrum
are Z-safe when the raw fit is unchanged. No count-based rescaling is
needed. A copied shared observation is still one physical acquisition,
not another score contribution. At finite n the observed Hessian need
not be positive definite; this is no universally valid finite-sample
covariance formula or instruction to add a numerical regularizer.

These are realized own-event mass scores, not an estimated population of
latent amplitudes. That distinction avoids inventing a signal-size law;
it does not independently rule on every use of the resulting matrix
state under L19 or select a joint calibration operation. The protocol
extension, measurement-identity handling and required uncertainty
benchmark remain part of G27/G26; global law selection remains G24.
Nothing here changes those authority statuses.

`met/joint_calibration.py` evaluates these derivatives and Gaussian local
benchmarks. Complete QR residual coordinates avoid subtracting a large
fitted energy from total energy; longitudinal terms in the Hessian cancel
analytically before evaluation. This retains the small transverse curvature
at large candidate masses. True signals enter only the separately named
population-moment reference. The observed sums use the supplied acquisitions
and candidate mass alone. Tests independently integrate Gaussian moments,
differentiate physical constraint costs, and check the large-mass and
whole-zero regressions. Numerical finite matrices are not certificates of
global posterior moments or of finite-sample coverage.
