## Standard mathematics

Stein's identity (1981), $E[\xi\,g(\xi)]=E[g'(\xi)]$ for a standard normal $\xi$, gives the local power of a
weighted score; the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality bounds it. The outcome is the usual one for locally most
powerful tests: the efficient score is unweighted, and a weight buys robustness to one kind of
alternative at the price of efficiency against another.

## Statement

At a candidate $m_0$ let $S_i$ and $C_i$ be reading $i$'s conditional score and its conditional variance
(T45 §1) and $H_i=\|U_i\|^2$ the reading's energy along the candidate line. For any function $w\ge0$ put

$$T_w=\frac{\sum_iw(H_i)\,S_i}{\sqrt{\sum_iw(H_i)^2\,C_i}} .$$

**(1) Every member is exact.** At the true mass, given all the $U_i$, $T_w\sim N(0,1)$, on the full type,
for every set of pushes: the weights are functions of what is conditioned on. Every member borrows
nothing between readings and declares no size law. $w\equiv1$ is T45.

**(2) Every member is zero-safe, the constant weight included.** An exactly zero reading has
$S_i=C_i=0$, so it adds nothing whatever $w(0)$ is. (This differs from the likelihood-ratio tests of
T75 (3), where only a weight with $w(0)=0$ drops a zero reading.)

**(3) Weak signal everywhere: the constant weight is the only efficient one.** When every reading's
signal is small against its noise, the squared mean of $T_w$ under a nearby mass, relative to that of
$T_1$, is

$$e_k(w)=\frac{\big(E[H\,w(H)]\big)^2}{E[H]\;E[H\,w(H)^2]}\le1,\qquad H\sim\chi^2_k,$$

with equality only for a constant $w$. For $w(H)=H$: $e_1=9/15=0.60$, $e_3=225/315=0.71$.

**(4) One strong reading among noise-only readings: every member is drowned, at a count that depends on
the weight.** For one reading of signal $r$ beside $N$ readings of pure noise the squared mean of $T_w$ is
that of the strong reading alone divided by $1+N/N_w$,

$$N_w(r)=\frac{w(r^2)^2\,r^2}{E[H\,w(H)^2]} .$$

$N_w$ is finite for every weight that is not zero on noise. At $r=20$, $k=1$: $N_1=400$,
$N_H=20^6/15=4.3\times10^6$.

**(5) So no member is best at both, and none leaves a strong reading untouched by noise-only readings.**
(3) is maximised only by the constant weight, and the constant weight has the smallest drowning count of
the weights compared (by a factor above 1000 against $w=H$ in every $k$). (4) is exploration E2's result
inside this family: a block of noise-only readings changes the result, whatever the weight.

| weight | $e_1$ | $N_w(20)$, $k=1$ | $e_3$ | $N_w(20)$, $k=3$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (T45) | 1.000 | 400 | 1.000 | 133 |

## Proof

(1) T45 §1: given $U$, the $S_i$ are independent $N(0,C_i)$. (2) $U_i=V_i=0$. (3) Write a reading in its
noise coordinates as signal plus $\xi\sim N(0,I_k)$. Under a mass off by a small noise angle $\delta$ the
score's mean given $U$ is $\delta\,w(\|U\|^2)\,U\!\cdot\!\rho$ with $\rho$ the signal; averaging over a weak
signal, Stein's identity gives $\tfrac{\|\rho\|^2}kE[kw+2Hw']=\tfrac{\|\rho\|^2}kE[Hw]$, and the variance term is
$E[Hw^2]$; the ratio to $w\equiv1$ is $e_k(w)$, and Cauchy–Schwarz on $\sqrt H\cdot\sqrt Hw$ gives the bound
and its equality case. (4) The strong reading contributes $w(r^2)r\cdot r\delta$ to the numerator and
$w(r^2)^2r^2$ to the denominator's sum; each noise reading adds $E[Hw^2]$ to that sum and nothing to the
mean.

## What the simulation shows (`test_what_the_two_closed_forms_mean_in_a_simulated_series`)

400 noise-only readings halve the squared mean score of one reading at signal 20 under the constant
weight (0.493 measured) and leave it unchanged under $w=H$ (1.003). With 4000 readings at signal 0.3
each, $w=H$ has 0.574 of the constant weight's squared mean score (0.554 expected at that signal, within
the simulation's error; 0.60 in the limit).

## Against the rows

- **Uniqueness [F2].** The family is exact member by member, so exactness selects nothing. A selection
  needs a further requirement. Two candidates, neither on the premise list: "nothing is wasted when
  every reading is weak" selects the constant weight, uniquely, by (3); "a strong reading is not undone
  by readings that carry nothing" selects no member, by (4), and can only be approached by weights that
  rise without bound. E13's own verdict is that the choice is a preference about which signals matter
  and not a property of mass.
- **Consistency [L11].** In the weak-everywhere regime the squared mean score grows like $e_k(w)$ times
  (total signal energy)² / (number of readings), so every member with $e_k(w)>0$ meets the rate
  $\Delta_N/\sqrt N\to\infty$ and they differ by a constant. The rate does not select either. Claude's
  reading of (3); the consistency of the sets themselves is not checked.
- **"A 0/0 result enters with weight zero" [R-2026-09-29-3].** True of an exactly zero reading, by (2),
  which is what the ruling is about. A noise-only reading is a different thing and does move the result
  under every member, by (4); no row asks otherwise.
- **`../../problems/O14.md`, 18 Nov, items 2–3** ("readings are not counted by head; a reading counts by
  how much it carries", measured along the candidate line or by its own energy): the weights $w(H)$ are
  the along-line measure, with the right-angle blindness recorded there (T75 (3)).
