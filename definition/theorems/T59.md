# T59: A spectral half-power anchor separates global ordering from local width

**From:** T42/T53's identifying Gaussian information scale, T43's allocation-free sampling limit, T56's exact scalar score geometry, and T58's anchor-rate obstruction.
**Test:** tests/test_spectral_anchor.py; tests/test_weak_anchor_study.py.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). In the specified scalar common-covariance Gaussian world, the spectral half-power gives a proper, zero-event-safe score law with global concentration and asymptotically nominal central intervals on every identifying deterministic signal history. The half exponent is unique within the stated spectral power class for a combined guarantee over the permitted common-covariance experiments and histories. The class, a positive multiplicative constant, its permitted state and a general mass law remain unforced; no production rule, prior, physical input or ruling is changed.

## 1. Specify the experiment and the additional research rule

Take independent full scalar readings with one fixed correctly declared
positive-definite covariance Sigma. After whitening, write

$$Y_i=r_i u_{\psi_0}+Z_i,\qquad Z_i\sim N(0,I_2),\qquad
u_\psi=(\sin\psi,\cos\psi).$$

The amplitudes r_i are arbitrary fixed signed numbers. They are not
observed inputs, draws from a population or a distribution estimated by
the procedure. The physical true mass m0 is fixed and interior positive.
Let N tend to infinity along prefixes of this one deterministic history,
and put

$$\Delta_N=\sum_{i\le N}r_i^2,\qquad
\mathcal I_N=\frac{\Delta_N^2}{N+\Delta_N}\longrightarrow\infty.$$

The theorem also applies to deterministic triangular arrays with this
condition. No maximum-leverage or repeated-signal assumption is needed.
Gaussian correctness is a world assumption of this theorem, stronger
than merely choosing a working Gaussian model.

Use the same fixed proper half-Cauchy prior as the scalar definition,
with center c>0:

$$\Pi(dm)=\frac{2c}{\pi(c^2+m^2)}\,dm,\qquad m>0.$$

Write F_N=sum_i Y_iY_i^T, with eigenvalues a_N>=b_N>=0, gap H_N=a_N-b_N,
and a principal eigenline angle phihat_N. For any candidate angle psi,
let delta=psi-phihat_N. The raw profile relative to its maximum over all
lines and the conditional score statistic are exactly

$$\widetilde L_N(\psi)=-\frac{H_N}{2}\sin^2\delta,$$
$$T_N(\psi)^2=
\frac{H_N^2\sin^2\delta\cos^2\delta}
{a_N\cos^2\delta+b_N\sin^2\delta}.$$

Set T^2=0 when its denominator is zero, as in T56. When H=0 the
profile and statistic are identically zero; an arbitrary eigenbasis then
gives the same construction. Subtracting the unrestricted profile maximum
is a data-only shift, not a selection of allowed prior support.

For a fixed research exponent gamma>0 and fixed constant kappa>0, define

$$\lambda_N=\kappa(1+a_N)^{-\gamma},$$
$$Q_N^{\gamma,\kappa}(dm)\ \propto\
\exp[-T_N(m)^2/2+\lambda_N\widetilde L_N(m)]\,\Pi(dm).$$

The principal comparison below is gamma=1/2. Neither gamma nor kappa
is a licensed physical caller input. They specify a mathematical family
whose properties can be proved or refuted. The law receives the observed
scatter and declared geometry, not N or Delta_N as additional inputs.

## 2. Finite-data properties and angular geometry

The exponent is finite and nonpositive at every finite data set and
candidate. In fact T^2<=a_N and -H_N/2<=Ltilde<=0. Thus the kernel lies
between exp[-(a_N+lambda_N H_N)/2] and one. For every finite datum it is
bounded above and below by positive constants, so the law is proper.
Every divergent nonnegative prior moment remains divergent. None of
the Gaussian limits below asserts finite-law moment convergence.

An appended independent exact-zero observed event adds zero to F_N,
and therefore changes none of a,b,H,T,Ltilde or lambda. The law is
exactly unchanged. Empty and entirely zero observed data return Pi.
There is no count-based adjustment hidden in the schedule.

A different valid whitening changes F by orthogonal conjugation and
preserves its spectrum. Changing physical units or frame with the
covariance and candidate graph transformed consistently has the same
effect. Independent whole-event sign reversal preserves F. The score
statistic is a parameter-coordinate scalar, the profile is a scalar,
and the prior is pushed forward under any smooth invertible mass
coordinate change. The resulting law therefore has the corresponding
coordinate, units, frame and swap bookkeeping invariances. No new Haar
prior or physical rotation mixing force with acceleration is assumed.

To control the whole positive domain, write the fixed covariance as

$$\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}
\sigma_F^2&c_{FA}\\c_{FA}&\sigma_A^2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad d_\Sigma=\det\Sigma>0.$$

An increasing whitened-angle chart is

$$\psi(m)=\arctan\frac{\sigma_A^2m-c_{FA}}{\sqrt{d_\Sigma}},\qquad
\psi'(m)=
\frac{\sqrt{d_\Sigma}}
{\sigma_A^2m^2-2c_{FA}m+\sigma_F^2}.$$

The positive-mass arc has length
pi/2+arcsin(c_FA/(sigma_F sigma_A)), strictly less than pi. In this
chart the prior has density

$$w(\psi(m))=
\frac{2c}{\pi\sqrt{d_\Sigma}}\,
\frac{\sigma_A^2m^2-2c_{FA}m+\sigma_F^2}{c^2+m^2}.$$

It extends continuously with positive finite values to both endpoints
of the compactified arc. Hence w is bounded above and below there.
The proof below needs this regular angular geometry, not a prior
chosen from the observed eigenvectors. A fixed other prior with a
bounded angular density that is continuous and strictly positive near
truth also satisfies the argument.

## 3. Observable spectral scale and the fitted center

In the true parallel/perpendicular basis,

$$E F_N=N I_2+\Delta_Nu_{\psi_0}u_{\psi_0}^T,\qquad
E\|F_N-EF_N\|_F^2=6(N+\Delta_N).$$

The Gaussian fourth-moment calculation is the one used in T53. Its
linear signal-noise terms contribute 6 Delta_N and its centered
noise-scatter terms contribute 6N; their cross covariance is zero.
The information condition and eigenvalue perturbation therefore give

$$\frac{a_N}{N+\Delta_N}\to1,\qquad
\frac{H_N}{\Delta_N}\to1,\qquad
\widehat\phi_N\to\psi_0,$$

in probability, using the representative of the principal angle near
psi0. In particular the observable sampling precision scale is

$$h_N=\frac{H_N}{\sqrt{a_N}}\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
h_N^2/\mathcal I_N\to1.$$

The fitted center also satisfies

$$h_N(\widehat\phi_N-\psi_0)\Rightarrow N(0,1).$$

For completeness, rotate truth to the first coordinate. The off-diagonal
scatter W_N=sum_i(r_i+X_i)Z_i has the exact characteristic function

$$E e^{itW_N}=(1+t^2)^{-N/2}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{\Delta_Nt^2}{2(1+t^2)}\right].$$

Putting t/sqrt(N+Delta_N) in this formula proves its standard-normal
limit. The diagonal difference is Delta_N+O_p(sqrt(N+Delta_N)), and
the two-dimensional eigenvector formula then gives
phihat_N-psi0=W_N/Delta_N+o_p(1/sqrt(I_N)).
This is T43's allocation-free limit, valid even if one reading carries
almost all the signal energy.

## 4. The half-power gives the full normalized calibrated law

Take gamma=1/2 and any fixed kappa>0. Then

$$t_N:=\lambda_NH_N
=\kappa h_N\sqrt{\frac{a_N}{1+a_N}}\sim\kappa h_N
\longrightarrow\infty,$$
$$q_N:=\lambda_N\frac{a_N}{H_N}
=\frac{\kappa}{h_N}\sqrt{\frac{a_N}{1+a_N}}\longrightarrow0.$$

The first rate orders the two score roots. The second makes the
anchor's added local precision negligible relative to the calibrated
score precision h_N^2. Both are read from the observed spectrum.

**Claim.** The full normalized law of h_N*(psi-phihat_N) converges in
total variation, in sampling probability, to N(0,1). The law concentrates
at m0, its median is consistent, and its equal-tailed central intervals
have asymptotically nominal sampling coverage at every fixed level
p in (0,1).

Here is a direct global proof, allowing h_N to diverge arbitrarily slowly.
Work on data sequences for which a_N tends to infinity, h_N tends to
infinity and phihat_N tends to the fixed interior psi0; the preceding
probability limits justify this reduction. Write a,b,H,h,t for the
observed quantities and v=b/a in [0,1]. The negative log kernel is

$$D(\delta)=
\frac{h^2}{2}
\frac{\sin^2\delta\cos^2\delta}{\cos^2\delta+v\sin^2\delta}
+\frac{t}{2}\sin^2\delta.$$

For bounded z and delta=z/h, uniformly over v in [0,1],

$$D(z/h)=z^2/2+o(1),\qquad t\sin^2(z/h)=O(z^2/h)\to0.$$

The prior density tends locally to w(psi0). A neighbourhood |delta|<=1/h
supplies a normalizer bounded below by c/h: there T^2<=h^2 sin^2(delta)<=1
and t sin^2(delta)=o(1).

Fix a sufficiently small epsilon<pi/4. On |delta|<=epsilon,
the denominator cos^2(delta)+v sin^2(delta) is at most one, and elementary
sine bounds give

$$D(\delta)\ge c h^2\delta^2.$$

After rescaling, this controls all tails within the true neighbourhood
by integrable Gaussian tails. Outside that neighbourhood the full
positive arc has sin^2(delta)>=c_epsilon>0, uniformly eventually:
the arc has length less than pi and the fitted center stays near its
fixed interior truth. The anchor alone then gives

$$Q_N^{1/2,\kappa}\{|\psi-\widehat\phi_N|>\epsilon\}
\le C h_N\exp[-c_\epsilon t_N]\longrightarrow0.$$

The normalizing volume is only 1/h_N, and t_N is of order h_N.
Consequently this argument needs no logarithmic margin in N, excludes
no false basin by a gate, and also covers an interior orthogonal
score root. Combining global separation, local Gaussian tails and
the expansion proves the unconditioned TV claim.

Its quantile endpoints are

$$\widehat\phi_N\ \mathord{\pm}\
\frac{\Phi^{-1}((1+p)/2)}{h_N}+o_p(h_N^{-1}).$$

The fitted-center sampling limit now proves coverage p. Monotone
conversion between angle and mass preserves equal-tailed coverage and
median consistency. These are asymptotic quantile claims for a
correct Gaussian world, not exact finite-noise coverage or a statement
about variances of the finite proper law.

### A simpler scalar Gaussian comparison already exists

T43's spectral power supplies a simpler scalar reference:

$$\eta_{\mathrm{sp}}=H_N/a_N\in[0,1],\qquad
Q_{\mathrm{sp}}(dm)\propto
\exp[\eta_{\mathrm{sp}}\widetilde L_N(m)]\,\Pi(dm).$$

Use eta_sp=0 if a_N=0. Its relative exponent is exactly
-h_N^2*sin^2(delta)/2, so the same compact-arc and local rescaling
argument gives global concentration and the calibrated N(0,1) limit
at scale h_N in this experiment. It needs neither the conditional
score term nor an additional anchor. T59 is therefore not a first
Gaussian achievability result and does not show that a score anchor
is the only way to calibrate even this restricted experiment.

The contribution here is the conditional rate restriction on the
score-anchor class, contrasted with T58's failure of every fixed
oscillation power on some identifying histories. That family retains
a broader mathematical motivation: T55 shows that a single raw-profile
power generally cannot supply the full joint Godambe covariance and
all composite widths in a multi-body experiment. The present scalar
proof neither solves that matrix problem nor selects a matrix
correction, anchor or production law.

## 5. The exponent is sharp within the declared spectral power class

The following two fixed-history witnesses use the same class
lambda=kappa*(1+a)^(-gamma), with any fixed kappa>0.
They do not exclude other functional schedules.

### A smaller exponent can make identifying-law coverage vanish

Let 0<gamma<1/2, use Sigma=I and any fixed interior positive truth,
and choose

$$\frac12<\beta<1-\gamma,\qquad
r_i^2=i^\beta-(i-1)^\beta.$$

This is one fixed positive signal sequence with Delta_N=N^beta exactly.
Its information diverges, and

$$a_N\sim b_N\sim N,\quad H_N\sim N^\beta,\quad
h_N^2\sim N^{2\beta-1},$$
$$t_N\sim\kappa N^{\beta-\gamma}\to\infty,\qquad
q_N\sim\kappa N^{1-\gamma-\beta}\to\infty.$$

The orthogonal score root is outside the positive arc. T58's normalized
Gaussian calculation applies to any finite nonnegative anchor: the
actual local precision is K_N=h_N^2+t_N, and the full law of
sqrt(K_N)*(psi-phihat_N) tends in TV to N(0,1). The center's sampling
precision is still h_N^2. Thus central interval coverage tends to zero,
although the law and its median remain consistent. The same construction
also rejects the constant power gamma=0 for this benchmark.

### A larger exponent can leave a dominant false positive-mass branch

Let gamma>1/2 and choose

$$\frac12<\beta<\min(\gamma,1).$$

Use T56's fixed covariance with unit marginal variances and correlation
9/10, the fixed true mass m0=6/5 and the unchanged half-Cauchy center c=1.
Choose the fixed positive physical accelerations so their whitened
signal squares are r_i^2=i^beta-(i-1)^beta. Equivalently
a_i^2=(19/28)[i^beta-(i-1)^beta]. Again Delta_N=N^beta and the
information diverges. Now

$$a_N\sim b_N\sim N,\quad H_N\sim N^\beta,\quad
t_N\sim\kappa N^{\beta-\gamma}\to0,\qquad q_N\to0.$$

Both observed eigenlines approach interior positive masses: the true
one 6/5 and the orthogonal false one 4/15. Their score-kernel widths
are sqrt(a_N)/H_N and sqrt(b_N)/H_N; their ratio tends to one.
The anchor's false/true peak factor exp(-t_N/2) also tends to one.
T56's angular-prior ratio is still 1159/241.

For explicit normalized control, away from fixed neighbourhoods of
these two roots, sin^2(delta)cos^2(delta) is bounded below and
b_N/a_N tends to one. The score exponent is then at least c h_N^2;
dividing its exponentially small integral by a 1/h_N peak volume gives
zero. Local rescaling at each root gives the two Gaussian peak
integrals, with anchor variation tending to zero. Consequently

$$Q_N^{\gamma,\kappa}\Rightarrow
\frac{241}{1400}\delta_{6/5}
+\frac{1159}{1400}\delta_{4/15}
\quad\text{in sampling probability}.$$

The false branch has limiting probability 1159/1400=0.8278571428...,
so the continuous law's median tends to 4/15. This is an identifying
fixed Gaussian signal history, not a zero-signal corner or a changed
prior. The exact conditional score test has not failed; this is a
failure of the separately normalized law.

These witnesses and section 4 make gamma=1/2 the unique exponent in
this specified vanishing spectral power class that guarantees both
global concentration and nominal Gaussian local width over all allowed
fixed common covariances and identifying histories of this scalar
Gaussian experiment. This is not uniqueness for every individual fixed
covariance: for example, the diagonal-covariance positive arc has no
false positive-mass orthogonal root.

## 6. What is constrained and what remains unforced

The natural two-rate window is

$$H_N^{-1}\ll\lambda_N\ll H_N/a_N.$$

It is nonempty when H_N^2/a_N diverges. Its geometric-middle order is
a_N^(-1/2), which explains the successful half-power. T58's oscillation
state reads the profile signal gap; the present largest-eigenvalue state
also retains the noise contribution relevant to the fitted center.
Using the minor eigenvalue alone would miss this scale when signal
energy greatly exceeds the number of readings.

Every fixed kappa>0 succeeds in section 4 and gives a different finite
law on nonconstant profiles. This freedom cannot be absorbed into a
different fixed supplied prior while preserving the whole procedure:
all-zero data return Pi and hence fix that prior, while two distinct
kappa then have a nonconstant likelihood-ratio factor on a nonconstant
profile. The exponent result selects no such constant and no whole
functional class.

The observed spectrum estimates no population of true signal sizes and
predicts no other reading's acceleration. This identifies the operation
being performed; it does not itself supply a new L19 ruling permitting
the correction. The exact conditional-test construction does not force
the exponential score kernel or an anchor.

The centered Gaussian calibration benchmark used to distinguish the
power exponents is stronger than property N alone. G19/G24/G26/G27
retain their measure, class, uncertainty and protocol obligations.
Neither the sufficient information condition nor this family is adopted
as a replacement for the unresolved full-type consistency contract.
The theorem supplies a concrete compatible law in a named scalar
Gaussian regime and a conditional rate constraint, without a score-based
selection or an added caller tuning input.
