# T39: Ours32's consistency has two different limits

**From:** L1, L11, L13 (stipulated Gaussian conditional noise), L18, T31, T35 (current median, L23 remains provisional), T36, T37; the explicitly defined ours31/ours32 factors, not the unresolved forcing of those factors.

Part 5 uses ordinary ν = 10 and identifies the global Bartlett power as the cause. **Later continuation:** T41 proves literal total-energy consistency impossible for every estimator on unrestricted heterogeneous sequences; the appropriate identifying growth condition remains open (G25/O19). **Harvested 7 Nov from the exploration branch `codex/ours32-consistency` (Codex, 3–4 Nov) as input; not checked by Claude.**

## Standard mathematics

**CDF/residual continuation (T47/T48, Codex).** T48's specified normalized
residual integration gives a different raw exponent and concentration for
every positive nu in the single-reading amplitude example. Its outer
Bartlett power still reproduces the opposed-pair failure exactly. T47
instead recalibrates the already normalized CDF, preserving its median and
concentration when eta is bounded away from zero. This repairs the named
eta-caused failure for a concentrating raw parent, but cannot repair an
incorrect raw median. Together these are conditional compatibility results;
neither the raw measure nor the calibration class is adopted here.

**Normalization scope (T44).** The cancellation of Q₊ here concerns the
product of factor averages defined by ours31/ours32. A mixture of normalized
whole-series conditional mass laws is different: it preserves the frozen
noise marginal but reintroduces a positive-misfit weighted population bias
in the stated weak Gaussian example. Normalization order and temperature
placement are part of G19; none is silently substituted into this theorem.

Fisher consistency of a residual M-estimator, stochastic ordering of noncentral
chi-square distributions, concentration of a powered criterion, and the
integrability threshold of a regularly varying kernel. The noncentral chi-square
Poisson mixture used below is standard; see the
[R statistical manual](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/Chisquare.html)
and Baringhaus and Grubel,
[Mixture representations of noncentral distributions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10236).
The application to these factors and the scope split below are Codex's.

## Statement

1. The mass-dependent part of each ours31/ours32 factor is exactly
   $-a_i\log(\nu_i+K_i(m))$, where $a_i=(\nu_i+k_i-1)/2$,
   $\nu_i=\nu_{{\rm eff},i}>0$, and $K_i=E_i-H_i$.
   **The positive-domain fit $Q_{+,i}$ cancels from the law, score,
   curvature and Bartlett power.**
2. Under correctly whitened Gaussian scalar-scale-mixture noise, any fixed
   nonzero true signal gives this criterion its unique population maximum at
   the true mass. This holds for arbitrary fixed positive-definite declared
   covariance shapes and candidate subspaces. A zero signal has a flat
   population criterion. The declared $\nu_i$ need not equal the world's
   mixing degrees of freedom.
3. For iid isotropic readings at a fixed nonzero signal, independent positive
   scalar noise sizes with finite mean, a fixed finite declared $\nu>0$, and
   a fixed half-Cauchy prior, the mathematical ours32 law and median concentrate
   at the true mass as $N\to\infty$. This removes B05/G22's particular
   fixed-signal population-direction bias.
4. This is **not unrestricted L11**. A single 1D reading with allowed declared
   $0<\nu<1$, $c=s=1$, and true mass 2 has a nonconcentrating limiting law and
   a limiting median different from 2 as its signal tends to infinity. At
   $\nu=1$ concentration is logarithmic; for $\nu>1$ the single-reading law
   concentrates.
5. **The global Bartlett power can cause the failure even at ν = 10.** Two
   1D readings at true mass 2 with opposite perpendicular errors of size 3
   have $\eta_R\to1/90$, so their combined tail index is $2/9<1$. Their
   uncalibrated law concentrates, but the ours32 law tends to a nondegenerate
   law with median $1.3107867539725\ldots$. The failure persists on an open
   set of errors of positive probability in the Gaussian and ν = 10 worlds.

These statements concern the continuous law and an actual whole-line maximum.
They do not certify a finite scan or a numerical CDF grid.

## 1. The exact residual identity

Let $H_i^+=E_i-Q_{+,i}$. The factor as written in O18 is

$$\ell_i(m)=-a_i\log\left(1+
\frac{H_i^+-H_i(m)}{\nu_i+Q_{+,i}}\right)
=a_i\log(\nu_i+Q_{+,i})-a_i\log(\nu_i+K_i(m)).$$

The first term depends on the data, not on $m$. It cancels on normalization,
and its derivatives vanish. Thus the same $Q_+$ that creates a quadrant-dependent
power in ours30 does not create one in ours32. This does not decide G18/G19:
those are questions about the justification of the factors, not this identity.

Numerically, $K$ should be the squared perpendicular projection, rather than
the subtraction of two nearly equal large energies. The equivalent convention
$\ell_i=-a_i\log(1+K_i/\nu_i)\le0$ avoids that data-only constant altogether.

## 2. The population optimum: any covariance shape and declared subspace

Project into the fixed declared span and whiten it (T36). Its dimension is
$2k_i$; the candidate mass plane has dimension $k_i$. With $P_i(m)$ the
orthogonal projector in these coordinates, write

$$Y_i=\mu_i+\sqrt{V_i}Z_i,\qquad Z_i\sim N(0,I_{2k_i}),\quad
\mu_i\in\operatorname{range}P_i(m_0),\quad V_i>0.$$

The scalar $V_i$ is independent of $Z_i$. A fixed-size Gaussian is $V_i=1$.
This also includes T37's inverse-gamma world in its precision normalization;
the declared matrix is then the shape, not a claim that $E[V_i]=1$.
Assume $E\log(1+V_i)<\infty$, so the expected log criterion exists. Conditional
on $V_i=v$,

$$K_i(m)/v\sim\chi^2_{k_i}(\delta_i(m)/v),\qquad
\delta_i(m)=\|(I-P_i(m))\mu_i\|^2.$$

For $\lambda_2>\lambda_1$, couple Poisson variables by
$J_1\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda_1/2)$ and
$D\sim\operatorname{Pois}((\lambda_2-\lambda_1)/2)$, independently. Given them,
couple $X_1\sim\chi^2_{k+2J_1}$ and
$X_2=X_1+W$, $W\sim\chi^2_{2D}$ independently, with $W=0$ when $D=0$.
The Poisson mixture gives $X_j\sim\chi^2_k(\lambda_j)$. Then $X_2\ge X_1$
and $P(X_2>X_1)>0$. Since $\log(\nu+vx)$ is strictly increasing, its expectation
strictly increases with $\lambda$. Mixing over $v$ preserves the strict inequality.

If the true signal is nonzero, $\delta_i(m)>0$ for every $m\ne m_0$:
before invertible whitening, the graph planes
$\{(mN_i\alpha,N_i\alpha)\}$ for two distinct masses intersect only at zero.
Consequently $E\ell_i(m)<E\ell_i(m_0)$. At zero signal all $\delta_i(m)=0$;
the expectation is constant, although a realized null reading need not be zero.

Each informative reading therefore contributes a positive population loss gap
without pooling signal sizes. This does not by itself prove concentration for
an arbitrary heterogeneous triangular array: an appropriate aggregate separation
and uniform convergence are additional conditions.

An independent numeric reference follows from the chi-square Laplace transform.
For one reading with conditional scale $v$ and distance squared $\delta$,

$$E[-\ell(m)+\ell(m_0)]
=a\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t
\left(1+\frac{2vt}{\nu}\right)^{-k/2}
\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{\delta t}{\nu+2vt}\right)\right]dt.$$

It is strictly positive for $\delta>0$. `met/cut_consistency.py` evaluates it;
the test compares it with the separate noncentral chi-square density integral.
The Gaussian is not uniquely forced by this result: scalar scale mixtures share it.

## 3. Repeated iid readings at fixed signal

Use the isotropic noise angle $\theta=\arctan(m/s)$, true angle
$\theta_0\in(0,\pi/2)$, and fixed whitened signal length $r>0$. Let
$X_j\sim\chi^2_j$ independently of $V$. The population curvature at truth is

$$C=E[-\ell''(\theta_0)]
=2ar^2E[(\nu+VX_{k+2})^{-1}]>0.$$

To see this, $\delta=r^2\sin^2(\theta-\theta_0)$. Differentiate the Poisson
mixture at noncentrality zero and integrate the central gamma density by parts:
$\partial_\delta E\log(\nu+vX_k(\delta/v))|_0
=E[(\nu+vX_{k+2})^{-1}]$. Since $\delta''(\theta_0)=2r^2$, the formula follows.

At truth let $U$ and $W$ be the parallel and perpendicular coordinates.
Given $V=v$, they are independent, $U\sim N(\beta,vI_k)$,
$W\sim N(0,vI_k)$, and $\|\beta\|=r$. Then
$\ell'=2aU^TW/(\nu+\|W\|^2)$ has mean zero. With $Q=VX_k$ its second moment is

$$J=E[(\ell')^2]
=4a^2E\left[\frac{(r^2/k+V)Q}{(\nu+Q)^2}\right]\in(0,\infty).$$

Here $E[V]<\infty$ is sufficient for finiteness and for uniform laws of large
numbers for the criterion, its first two derivatives, and squared scores.
For example, on the compact angular circle their envelopes are bounded by
constants times $1+\|Y\|^2$ (the positive $\nu$ controls the denominator).
Part 2 supplies the separated unique population maximum. The empirical
whole-line maximum thus tends to $\theta_0$, and

$$\mathcal H_N/N\to C,\quad J_N/N\to J,\quad
\eta_N\to\min(1,C/J)>0.$$

Outside a fixed neighbourhood of truth the empirical criterion loses at least
$Nd_\epsilon$ eventually; inside a smaller neighbourhood its loss is less than
$Nd_\epsilon/2$. A fixed half-Cauchy assigns positive mass to that neighbourhood.
Bounding the numerator by the first exponential and the normalizer by the
second proves posterior concentration, hence median consistency. No assertion
of nominal finite-N coverage or a forced finite-N Bartlett formula follows.

The finite-mean condition holds in the fixed Gaussian world and in the stated
inverse-gamma world with world degrees of freedom greater than 2. For heavier
world scales part 2 still applies under its log-moment condition; this argument
does not settle the behavior of the empirical Bartlett ratio.

## 4. A permitted single-reading L11 counterexample

Set $k=N=1$, $s=c=1$, $\theta_0=\arctan2$, and $0<\nu<1$. Hold a finite error
vector $e$ fixed and increase the true signal:

$$Y_R=R(\sin\theta_0,\cos\theta_0)+e.$$

For all large R its observed angle $\alpha_R$ is positive,
$\alpha_R\to\theta_0$, radius $\rho_R/R\to1$, and $Q_+=0$. At its own line
the single reading has zero score, so $J=0$ and mathematically $\eta=1$.
The half-Cauchy induces a uniform prior on $(0,\pi/2)$. Its angular factor is

$$f_R(\theta)=\left(1+\frac{\rho_R^2\sin^2(\theta-\alpha_R)}\nu\right)^{-\nu/2}.$$

Multiplying by the mass-independent factor $(\rho_R^2/\nu)^{\nu/2}$ gives
$[\nu/\rho_R^2+\sin^2(\theta-\alpha_R)]^{-\nu/2}$. Away from truth it tends
pointwise to $|\sin(\theta-\theta_0)|^{-\nu}$. A neighbourhood of radius d of
the moving peak has integral at most a constant times $d^{1-\nu}$, uniformly
in R. Thus splitting off that neighbourhood proves L1 convergence to the
integrable kernel. After normalization the limiting density is

$$p_\infty(\theta)=
\frac{|\sin(\theta-\theta_0)|^{-\nu}}
{I_\nu(\theta_0)+I_\nu(\pi/2-\theta_0)},\quad
I_\nu(b)=\int_0^b(\sin x)^{-\nu}dx.$$

The density is proper and nondegenerate. Since $\theta_0>\pi/4$,
$I_\nu(\theta_0)>I_\nu(\pi/2-\theta_0)$; its median lies strictly below truth.
For $\nu=1/2$ the incomplete-beta identity
$I_\nu(b)=\tfrac12 B_{\sin^2b}((1-\nu)/2,1/2)$ gives

$$P_\infty(m\le2)=0.6113338264869\ldots,\qquad
\operatorname{median}_\infty(m)=1.8219669037884\ldots.$$

This is a point-level failure, not just a failure of concentration. It holds
for every finite fixed error, hence almost surely under a coupled Gaussian
or finite scalar-mixture error. Newly sampled errors satisfy $e_R/R\to0$
in probability and give the same limit in probability. Total true signal
diverges while the median does not tend to 2.

For $\nu>1$, the peak has integrated mass of order $R^{-1}$ while the mass
outside any fixed neighbourhood is $O(R^{-\nu})$, so it concentrates.
For $\nu=1$, the normalizer is of order $(\log R)/R$ and the outside mass
is $O(1/R)$, which also concentrates, more slowly. These are single-reading
statements; an arbitrary multi-reading eta and growth schedule need their own proof.

## 5. The global power breaks amplitude consistency at ν = 10

Let $k=1$, $N=2$, $c=s=1$, $\theta_0=\arctan2$, and set
$u=(\sin\theta_0,\cos\theta_0)$,
$v=(\cos\theta_0,-\sin\theta_0)$. Use the finite fixed errors

$$Y_\pm=Ru\pm dv,\qquad \nu=10,\quad d=3.$$

For $x=\theta-\theta_0$, the two residuals are
$(R\sin x\mp d\cos x)^2$. Write $z=\sin^2x$ and $A=\nu+d^2$.
Their denominator product is exactly

$$P_R(z)=A^2+2[R^2(\nu-d^2)-d^2(\nu+d^2)]z+(R^2+d^2)^2z^2.$$

When $R^2>171$ the linear coefficient is positive, so this polynomial is
strictly increasing for $z\ge0$. The true line is the **global** projective
maximum, not just a stationary local choice. At it,

$$J_R=\frac{2\nu^2R^2d^2}{(\nu+d^2)^2},\qquad
\mathcal H_R=\frac{2\nu[R^2(\nu-d^2)-d^2(\nu+d^2)]}{(\nu+d^2)^2},$$

$$\eta_R=\frac{\nu-d^2}{\nu d^2}-\frac{\nu+d^2}{\nu R^2}
=\frac1{90}-\frac{19}{10R^2}\longrightarrow\frac1{90}.$$

The cap and fallback do not intervene. The quoted angular kernel, up to
constants, is $P_R(\sin^2x)^{-\nu\eta_R/2}$. Its tail exponent is
$p_R=2\nu\eta_R\to p=2/9$. After multiplication by the mass-independent
$R^{p_R}$ it tends to $|\sin x|^{-p}$. Also
$P_R(\sin^2x)/R^4\ge\sin^4x$ when the linear coefficient is positive.
Since $p_R<p<1$, the scaled kernel is bounded by $|\sin x|^{-p}$.
Dominated convergence proves the normalized limiting law, including its
nonconcentration and median:

$$p_\infty(\theta)\propto|\sin(\theta-\arctan2)|^{-2/9},\quad
\operatorname{median}_\infty(m)=1.3107867539725\ldots,\quad
P_\infty(m\le2)=0.6654942769223\ldots.$$

Without η the combined exponent is 20 and the law concentrates by the
peak/tail argument in part 4 (a width $1/R$ window has mass of order $1/R$,
while the outside mass is $O(R^{-20})$). Thus it is the global power that
introduces this particular break. A correct fitted mode and a positive η
are insufficient to establish concentration of a polynomial-tail law.

**Changing only the log-factor coefficient does not repair the mechanism.**
For the same denominator with any coefficient $a>0$ in
$\ell_i=-a\log(\nu+K_i)$, the uncapped limiting ratio is
$\eta_\infty=(\nu-d^2)/(2ad^2)$. Whenever that ratio is below 1 the
combined exponent is $4a\eta_\infty=2(\nu-d^2)/d^2$, independent of a.
Choosing $d^2$ sufficiently close to ν from below makes it less than 1
and keeps the ratio uncapped for any fixed a. Thus merely adjusting the
strong-signal residual count in the prefactor cannot fix this global-power
obstruction. This corollary is algebra on the same two-reading family.

**This is a positive-probability failure.** Allow arbitrary bounded errors
$e_1,e_2$, with perpendicular components $b_1,b_2$. Put
$h=(b_1-b_2)/2$ and $\bar b=(b_1+b_2)/2$. In local coordinates
$t=R(\theta-\theta_0)$ the denominator product converges to

$$[\nu+(b_1-t)^2][\nu+(b_2-t)^2]
=(\nu+h^2)^2+2(\nu-h^2)(t-\bar b)^2+(t-\bar b)^4.$$

For $h^2<\nu$ its unique minimum is $t=\bar b$, with strictly positive
curvature. Bounded parallel errors do not change these leading terms.
On a compact error box the denominator product at the true angle is uniformly
bounded. Each factor is at least ν, so every global minimizer has both residuals
uniformly bounded. Bounded errors then force $R|\sin(\theta-\theta_0)|=O(1)$.
Every global minimizer lies in this local t-window. Its unique limiting
minimum and positive curvature therefore control the actual global fit.
The fitted maximum, curvature and squared scores converge uniformly
on a sufficiently small compact error box around $(dv,-dv)$. The limiting
ratio and exponent are

$$\eta_\infty=\frac{\nu-h^2}{\nu h^2},\qquad
p_\infty=\frac{2(\nu-h^2)}{h^2}.$$

The strict inequalities $2\nu/3<h^2<\nu$ define an open set with
$0<p_\infty<1$, containing $h=3$ at $\nu=10$. The scaled kernels have the
same sine-power limit: outside an $O(1/R)$ neighbourhood their residuals
are $R^2\sin^2x(1+o(1))$; within a fixed small neighbourhood they are
bounded by constants times $[R^{-2}+\sin^2x]^{-p_R/2}$ uniformly on that
box. The integrable exponent can be bounded strictly below 1. This gives
uniform L1 convergence and median limits bounded away from 2 on a smaller
compact box. Gaussian joint errors have positive density there; the ν = 10
scale-mixture world also has full support. Consequently the probability
of a fixed-sized point error is bounded below by that box's positive
probability as R increases. Consistency in probability fails too.

## What the failures name

Total signal alone does not control concentration for a polynomial-tail factor.
Its **tempered** tail index and the way information grows matter. The first
counterexample uses allowed $\nu=1/2$; the second shows that imposing $\nu\ge1$
would not suffice. The present finite-N global power conflicts with L11 even
at ν = 10 with the current median, while its repeated fixed-signal limit is well behaved. This is a
new necessary constraint on a replacement for the finite-N correction, not
a refutation of asymptotic sandwich calibration. Changing the factor, the
finite-N calibration, readout, admissible inputs or L11 needs justification; none is
adopted here. Better coverage on existing finite cells does not decide the fork.

L11 is stated for the point. A mode could remain near the true line despite
the broad limiting law, so this result does not exclude every conceivable
point functional of that law. It excludes the present conjunction of cut
factors, global power, half-Cauchy and median. Changing the readout would
reopen G6/L23 rather than establish concentration of the quoted law.

In particular, on the two-reading family any replacement that keeps a global
power with a limiting value and the current median must avoid a limiting combined tail index below
1. At ν = 10 this requires $\eta_\infty\ge1/20$; the present value $1/90$
violates it. This is a necessary constraint, not a prescription for clipping
η at $1/20$: such a clip would need an independent argument and does not
settle other growth schedules, Z, coverage or G18/G19.

The native uniform log-mass grid also underresolves the integrable peak at high
R. A strict xfail records that separate numerical discrepancy against the
independent angle quadrature; the analytic inconsistency does not depend on it.
