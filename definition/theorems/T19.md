## Statement

$E_p[m]=\infty$ under the half-Cauchy

## Proof or support

**Standard mathematics:** comparison of positive integrals and preservation of a
power-law tail under a bounded positive likelihood factor. Let

$$L(m)=\frac\eta2\sum_i g_iH_i(m),\qquad
q(m)=\frac{c}{c^2+m^2}e^{L(m)},\qquad Z=\int_0^\infty q(m)dm.$$

The omitted half-Cauchy normalizing constant is irrelevant. For every fixed finite
measured series, T36's projection has
$0\le H_i(m)\le y_i^\top\Sigma_i^{-1}y_i<\infty$. With $0\le\eta,g_i\le1$,
there is a finite constant M such that $0\le L(m)\le M$ for every m. Therefore

$$\frac\pi2\le Z\le e^M\frac\pi2,\qquad
p(m)=q(m)/Z\ge\frac1Z\frac{c}{c^2+m^2}.$$

It follows directly that
$\int_0^\infty mp(m)dm\ge(c/Z)\int_0^\infty m/(c^2+m^2)dm=\infty$.
This covers no readings and all-zero readings as well as informative finite data.

The tail coefficient can also be given exactly. The candidate plane and its
positive-definite Gram matrix extend continuously to the infinite-mass endpoint.
Using `Reading.pre()` notation, with $u=N^\top\Sigma^{-1}_{F,:}y$ and
$K_{FF}=N^\top\Sigma^{-1}_{FF}N$,
$H_i(\infty)=u^\top K_{FF}^{-1}u$ is finite. Hence $L(m)\to L_\infty$ and

$$m^2p(m)\longrightarrow A=ce^{L_\infty}/Z\in(0,\infty).$$

Consequently $p_{\ln m}(x)=e^xp(e^x)\sim Ae^{-x}$ and the truncated first moment
satisfies $\int_0^M mp(m)dm\sim A\ln M$ as M→∞. The law is proper but has no
finite ordinary mean or variance. This is a finite-input statement. A sequence
of laws can concentrate at a finite mass in an infinite-signal limit while each
finite member still has this tail and infinite mean; concentration does not
provide uniform integrability or justify interchanging those limits.
