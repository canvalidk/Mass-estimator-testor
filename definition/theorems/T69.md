## 1. Standard mathematics and the experiment

This is Moreira's maximum invariant likelihood method (MILE):
[Moreira (2009)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.0613), Definitions 2.1--2.4,
Proposition 4.1, Theorems 4.1--4.3 and Proposition 4.2 (electronic pp. 3--4,
9--11). His IV reduction supplies the noncentral-Wishart likelihood and the
LIML connection. The derivation below specializes the experiment directly;
it does not assume an unrestricted positive-domain MLE always exists.

There are N independent readings with k unrestricted latent components each,
the same declared window and isotropic independent channel covariances
diag(sigma_F^2 I_k, sigma_a^2 I_k). No equality between their latent pushes
is declared. Put d=Nk, c=sigma_F/sigma_a, theta=atan(m/c), and
v_theta=(sin theta,cos theta), 0<theta<pi/2. The whitened rows form
Z=alpha v_theta^T+E, where E has iid standard-normal entries and alpha is a
fixed unrestricted vector in R^d. Set Delta=||alpha||^2, G=Z^T Z and
H(theta)=v_theta^T G v_theta. Here H is energy (O14 sometimes writes H^2).

## 2. The largest group, and the actual instrument classes

For one reading, let an affine isometry be x -> Ax+b. The candidate mean
planes are L_r={(rt,t):t in R^k}, r=m/c>0. Fixing every r requires b in
their intersection, hence b=0. Write A in k-by-k blocks P,Q,R,S. Preservation
of each plane gives rP+Q=r(rR+S) for every r, so R=Q=0 and P=S.
Orthogonality then gives A=P direct-sum P, P in O(k). Conversely each such
map works. It preserves ||alpha||, so cannot eliminate the push radius.

There is no larger measurable invertible group, except changes on common
Gaussian-null sets. A Borel automorphism T preserving all these Gaussian
families takes N(0,I) to itself: its image mean belongs to every L_r.
For any allowed mean mu, write its image mean nu(mu). Invariance of the
likelihood ratio gives, almost everywhere,
mu^T T^{-1}(y)-||mu||^2/2 = nu(mu)^T y-||nu(mu)||^2/2.
Choose a basis of 2k means from two distinct planes. These identities force
T^{-1} affine; preservation of N(0,I) makes it orthogonal and linear.
The preceding block argument applies. Noninvertible reductions are not a group.

The same proof with k replaced by d gives O(d) acting on rows of Z. Its
sample maximal invariant is G: two pairs of columns with equal inner
products are related by an isometry of their spans, extendible to O(d).
Its parameter maximal invariant is (m,Delta), because O(d)'s vector orbits
are spheres. At Delta=0 all m give the same distribution, although m remains
part of the parameter-orbit label.

**Qualification to B21's off-slice sentence.** Equal absolute covariances are
sufficient, not necessary. For independent diagonal channels write each
whitened coordinate's ratio as c_j and D=diag(1/c_j). The mean planes are
{(mDt,t)}. The block proof gives A=diag(P,S), PD=DS; orthogonality implies
S commutes with D^2, hence with positive D, and P=S. Thus the largest group
is product_g O(d_g), where classes have equal ratios c_j, not necessarily
equal absolute noise scales. Each class retains G_g and Delta_g.
For N_g readings of dimension k, d_g=N_g k. Distinct ratios forbid mixing
those classes. Anisotropic diagonal channels can have repeated-ratio blocks;
arbitrary correlated covariances require their own plane-preserving group.
No common-row construction for genuinely different line directions is used
here. This qualification changes no declaration or estimator.

## 3. Exact invariant likelihood and two proper mass laws

Put b=d/2 and psi_d(t)=0F1(;b;t^2/4). Averaging the Gaussian likelihood
ratio over alpha's sphere gives

    L_d(H,Delta) = exp(-Delta/2) psi_d(sqrt(Delta H)).

This is an orbit integration over a compact group, not a population of
per-reading pushes. For d>=2 and positive-definite G, its density is

    f(G|theta,Delta) = |G|^((d-3)/2) exp(-tr(G)/2)
                     / [2^d Gamma_2(d/2)] * L_d(H(theta),Delta).

The noncentrality is Delta v_theta v_theta^T. For d=1 the Wishart is singular;
L_d is still its likelihood ratio relative to the central Gram distribution
(psi_1=cosh). The parameter-dependent factor extends continuously to every
positive-semidefinite G, including G=0, where an ordinary density can vanish
or diverge. This extension, not a determinant evaluated at zero, is used.

**Profile:** P_d(H)=sup_{Delta>=0} L_d(H,Delta). Define
A_d(t)=I_(d/2)(t)/I_(d/2-1)(t). The score is

    partial_Delta log L = -1/2 + H A_d(t)/(2t),  t=sqrt(Delta H).

A_d(t)/t decreases strictly from 1/d to 0. One proof uses
A_d(t)/t = 0F1(;b+1;t^2/4)/(d 0F1(;b;t^2/4)):
the ratio of its numerator/denominator series coefficients is b/(b+n),
strictly decreasing; differentiating the series ratio and pairing n>j
terms gives a negative derivative. Its limit follows from 0<A_d(t)<1.
Consequently Delta_hat(H)=0 if H<=d. Otherwise there is a unique positive
solution t=H A_d(t), and Delta_hat=t^2/H. The supremum is finite since
psi_d(t)<=exp(t), so -Delta/2+sqrt(Delta H) tends to minus infinity.
P_d=1 below the threshold and is strictly increasing above it.

**Integrated comparison:** choose the single, unforced reference measure
dDelta on [0,infinity), flat in total dimensionless signal energy. It is
improper as a nuisance prior; it is stated for a simple reproducible
comparison, not selected by the group or claimed permissible under L10.
Termwise positive integration of the 0F1 series gives the finite factor

    J_d(H) = integral_0^infinity L_d(H,Delta) dDelta
           = 2 * 1F1(1;d/2;H/2).

The two reported laws are pi(m) P_d(H(m)) and pi(m) J_d(H(m)), each normalized
once on m>0. Both factors are positive, continuous and bounded on the compact
H range of any finite G. Any proper pi therefore gives a proper law and
finite positive interior quantiles; a positive continuous pi gives a unique
median. At G=0 the factors are respectively 1 and 2, so both return pi.

The integrated factor is generally different from exp(H/2); at d=2 it is
exactly 2 exp(H/2). Choosing instead Delta^(d/2-1) dDelta would reproduce
the flat factor for every d, up to a constant: invariant reduction does not
select a nuisance measure. The profile is not a constant power of the flat
factor either, as its zero-slope region already proves. Ours30 additionally
uses per-reading positive-misfit weights and Bartlett scores, which are
not determined by (G,d) alone.

**MILE caveats.** Where sup_theta H(theta)>d and the maximizing angle is
interior, profile MILE is the top Gram eigenvector with positive components,
i.e. positive TLS. It is not the median of either prior-weighted law.
If sup H<=d, every positive mass maximizes the profile with Delta_hat=0.
If H is nonconstant, G_12<=0 and sup H>d, the positive-domain supremum is
at theta=0 or pi/2 and is unattained: there is no finite positive MILE.
An isotropic Gram makes H constant and supplies no preferred mass.
At known Delta>0 maximizing L also maximizes H; at Delta=0 it is constant.
These cases qualify the brief's unconditional top-eigenvector statement.

## 4. Curvature: the target is recovered in its regular limit

At an interior strict maximum theta_hat of H with H_hat>d, the envelope
identity and the score equation give

    d log P_d(H)/dH = Delta_hat(H)/(2H),
    rho_d = [-partial_theta^2 log P_d(H(theta))]
            /[-partial_theta^2(H(theta)/2)] at theta_hat
          = Delta_hat(H_hat)/H_hat.                         (exact)

It is zero in the strictly flat H<d region. The equality uses H'=0;
at a positive-domain boundary there is no such curvature assertion.

Let d=Nk grow, k fixed, true angle interior and Delta/d -> tau in (0,infinity),
with deterministic signal vectors. Gaussian second moments give
G/d -> I+tau v_0 v_0^T in probability, hence H_hat/d -> 1+tau and
(lambda_+-lambda_-)/d -> tau. To see the profile limit directly, use the
sphere integral psi_d(t): for t=d w its normalized exponent is
w z + (1/2)log(1-z^2), -1<z<1. Its unique saddle solves
w=z/(1-z^2). Combining this with the profile score w=(H/d)z gives
z^2=1-d/H and Delta_hat/d -> tau. Therefore

    rho_d -> tau/(1+tau) = limit Delta/(Delta+M),  M=d=Nk.

This is asymptotic, not a finite-N identity. The Gram candidate
eta_G=1-lambda_-/lambda_+ has the same limit, but uses lambda_- where the
invariant likelihood uses the declared dimension d. They differ at finite d.
For fixed d and H_hat -> infinity, the large-argument Bessel expansion gives
Delta_hat=H_hat-(d-1)+O(H_hat^-1) and rho_d -> 1 (d=1 has exponentially
small correction). See [DLMF 10.40.1](https://dlmf.nist.gov/10.40.E1).

In the growing-d regime the profile's angular precision is
rho_d(lambda_+-lambda_-) ~ Delta^2/(Delta+d). The TLS angular sampling
variance is its inverse: in the true-line basis the cross-Gram is
(alpha+epsilon)^T zeta with variance Delta+d; conditional on epsilon it
is Gaussian, and its conditional variance divided by Delta+d tends to 1.
The diagonal gap is Delta+O_p(sqrt(d+Delta)). This gives the normal angular
limit and the local likelihood quadratic. Laplace integration over Delta
has a locally smooth prefactor, so J_d has the same leading local quadratic.
For a fixed positive continuous prior at the truth, both laws concentrate
there and their central intervals have the corresponding regular asymptotic
coverage. The fixed-signal 1D/3D cells are within this regime; the limiting
population optimum is the true mass, including m=2. This is not a uniform
weak-identification or full-covariance coverage theorem.

## 5. Premise verdicts and the predicted break

L2, L3 and the reciprocal swap L4 **pass** for both laws with a transformed
prior: H and d are unchanged by units; exchanging channels swaps G's axes
and theta with pi/2-theta. All-zero return is exact. Frames pass by the
group argument. The requested fixed-signal L11 regime **passes** by section 4.

Z/L20 **fails**: padding a measured zero leaves G but increases d. Profile
can change from nonconstant to pi when d crosses sup H; J_d changes too,
as its log derivative at H=0 is 1/d. All-zero return does not repair this.

T30 **fails generically**, including anti-parallel pairs. At fixed d,

    log P_d(H)=H/2-(d-1)log(H)/2+O(1),
    log J_d(H)=H/2+(1-d/2)log(H)+O(1).

The second expansion follows [DLMF 13.7.1](https://dlmf.nist.gov/13.7.E1).
For fixed incompatible data with noise scaled by sigma, H=H_0/sigma^2.
Two angles with different positive H_0 therefore have log-evidence contrast
(H_0(theta_1)-H_0(theta_2))/(2 sigma^2)+O(1), which diverges. Anti-parallel
data can drive concentration toward the positive boundaries; a symmetric
point need not expose that concentration. Isotropic G_0 is an exceptional
constant-H control, not a pass for all incompatible inputs.

Regular fixed-signal asymptotic calibration **passes** in section 4's scope;
full property N is **unknown** because its width promise is still open (G28).
Finite-N coverage is a measured study, and unrestricted finite-N coverage cannot
be uniformly positive (O15/B13). L10 permission for dDelta and L19 permission
for shared Delta are **unknown**, not settled by calling Delta a parameter.
The likelihood retains only (G,d), discards reading boundaries and depends
on the noise-component count. Its asymptotic target does not force G3's
finite-N body: it violates Z and T30, and choosing profile versus integration
is additional. This confirms B21's predicted break without changing T70,
the production equation, any premise, or the prior.
