# T46: Nonconstant monotone positive-misfit weights create weak-signal bias

**From:** L13/L18's independent unit-Gaussian scalar submodel; L21's fixed decreasing reading weight; T30's bounded-confidence tail within the weighted-quadratic class; T42 and T44. The class below is specified explicitly, not asserted to contain every admissible law.
**Test:** `tests/test_positive_weight_obstruction.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 3 Nov 2026 (Codex). A no-go result for fixed monotone functions of the reading's best-positive-mass misfit multiplying a quadratic criterion, and for the corresponding conditional-mean precision module. It excludes neither nonlinear residual-dependent factors nor every possible calibration procedure. No production change or new ruling is made.

## Standard mathematics and scope

This is Fisher consistency under data-dependent selection/weighting. We derive
the population crossmoment and a Gaussian change-of-measure bound directly.
The testing quadratures are deterministic checks, not estimator rankings.

For one standardized scalar reading let the observed force and acceleration be
X,Y. Define

$$Q_+(X,Y)=\begin{cases}0,&XY\ge0,\\\min(X^2,Y^2),&XY<0.\end{cases}$$

Fix a measurable nonincreasing function w on [0,infinity), with finite
$w_0=w(0)>0$ and $0\le w(q)\le w_0$. For the non-gating T30 class one may
further require w(q)>0. The function is fixed across readings; it is not
estimated from their population or changed as N grows. The quadratic
criterion is

$$\ell_\theta(X,Y)=\tfrac12w(Q_+)(u_\theta^T(X,Y))^2,
\qquad u_\theta=(\sin\theta,\cos\theta).$$

Write $v_\theta=(\cos\theta,-\sin\theta)$, so its score is
$w(Q_+)(u_\theta^TY)(v_\theta^TY)$. A population Fisher-consistent positive
line would have this score zero at every true interior direction.

## 1. Exact null crossmoment

Under independent standard Gaussian X,Y, put

$$B_w=E_0[w(Q_+)XY].$$

The signs of X,Y are independent of their absolute values. Same-sign
quadrants get weight w0; opposite-sign quadrants get the smaller weight
$w(\min(X^2,Y^2))$. Splitting the positive quadrant at x=y and using
$\int_x^\infty y\varphi(y)dy=\varphi(x)$ gives

$$B_w=4\int_0^\infty x[w_0-w(x^2)]\varphi(x)^2dx
=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty e^{-q}[w_0-w(q)]dq.$$

This is nonnegative. It is strictly positive whenever w differs from w0 on
a set of positive Lebesgue measure. A genuinely nonconstant nonincreasing
w has that property. (Changes confined to measure-zero inputs are invisible
to population assertions.) The diagonal weighted moments are equal by
channel exchange. Therefore the null weighted scatter is

$$M_0=\begin{pmatrix}d&B_w\\B_w&d\end{pmatrix}.$$

When $B_w>0$ its preferred positive line is theta=pi/4, mass 1 in noise units.
This is a geometric selection effect even though the true null carries no
information about a mass.

The controls used in the tests have exact values:

$$w(q)=\min(1,c/q):\quad B_w=[e^{-c}-cE_1(c)]/\pi,$$
$$w(q)=\nu/(\nu+q):\quad B_w=[1-\nu e^\nu E_1(\nu)]/\pi,$$
$$w(q)=e^{-q}:\quad B_w=1/(2\pi),\qquad w(q)=1:\quad B_w=0.$$

The exponential is a positive monotone bias control, not a T30-tail repair.

## 2. A fixed nonzero identifying signal still has the wrong fit

Fix an interior true angle theta different from pi/4 and a Gaussian world
$Y\sim N(ru_\theta,I_2)$ with r>0. Let
$f(Y)=w(Q_+)(u_\theta^TY)(v_\theta^TY)$.
At null,

$$E_0 f=B_w\cos(2\theta),\qquad E_0 f^2\le w_0^2.$$

The Gaussian likelihood-ratio chi-square identity gives

$$|E_{ru_\theta}f-E_0 f|\le w_0\sqrt{e^{r^2}-1}.$$

Similarly,
$E_{ru_\theta}[w(Q_+)XY]\ge B_w-w_0\sqrt{e^{r^2}-1}$.
At theta=arctan(2), choose the explicit witness radius

$$r_w=\sqrt{\log[1+(B_w/(4w_0))^2]}>0.$$

The shift bound is exactly B_w/4. The true-angle score is at most
$-3B_w/5+B_w/4=-7B_w/20<0$, and the crossmoment is at least 3B_w/4>0.
Thus the population scatter has a unique interior positive principal line
different from the true mass 2. This is not a non-identifying null case:
repeating this fixed nonzero signal gives Delta=N*r_w^2 and
$\Delta^2/(N+\Delta)\to\infty$, the identifying condition in T42.

For any fixed proper prior with positive continuous density at that wrong
interior optimum, a global positive temperature with N*tau_N tending to
infinity makes the weighted-quadratic law concentrate there. Its median is
therefore inconsistent. A temperature that changes the width cannot change
the population principal line.

### With the uniform angular prior, no nonnegative temperature repairs the median

There is a stronger point-level statement for the fixed half-Cauchy prior
with c=s=1, uniform on the angular interval (0,pi/2). For any acute fitted
angle a and any finite precision K>=0, let the continuous density be
proportional to exp[-K*sin^2(theta-a)/2]. Its median lies between a and
pi/4. To see this, symmetry about a makes its CDF at a at least one half
when a>=pi/4, and at most one half when a<=pi/4. Reflection about pi/4
gives the opposite CDF inequality there: the reflected point on the side
toward a has at least as large a density. These two inequalities bracket
the median. At K=0 the median is exactly pi/4.

For the weak-signal witness above, the population derivative at
theta0=arctan(2) is negative. A two-dimensional scatter with its unique
acute principal angle theta_star has derivative
-(gap/2)*sin[2(theta0-theta_star)]. Both angles are acute, so negativity
forces theta_star<theta0. Also pi/4<theta0. The empirical principal angle
converges to theta_star and is eventually acute. Consequently, for every
nonnegative global temperature sequence, regardless of its dependence on
the data or whether N*tau_N diverges,

$$\limsup_N\operatorname{median}(\theta\mid Y)
\le\max(\theta_\star,\pi/4)<\theta_0$$

almost surely in the fixed iid weighted-quadratic experiment. Arbitrarily
large finite precisions, zero temperature, and their limits obey the same
bound. Monotonic conversion to mass preserves this strict failure.

This stronger claim requires this uniform angular prior and one
weighted-quadratic law. The general-prior concentration statement above
still uses N*tau_N->infinity. The argument is not extended here to a
mixture of normalized whole-series conditional laws.

## 3. Consequence for T30 and normalized conditional cuts

Within this fixed weighted-quadratic class, bounded nonvanishing evidence
on a fixed incompatible reading as the declared noise shrinks requires a
nonconstant weight. The usual T30 tail w(q) asymptotic to c/q, c>0, is one
such requirement. The null crossmoment result shows that **no nonconstant
nonincreasing w(Q+) in this class is Fisher consistent for every positive
Gaussian signal direction and size**. Choosing a different monotone tail
or threshold cannot remove the mechanism. A constant weight avoids this
particular bias but retains the Gaussian T30 incompatibility.

The same obstruction applies to a normalized whole-series conditional
mixture using a fixed conditional-state kernel for every reading and N,
with independent frozen precisions A_i satisfying

$$E[A_i\mid Y_i]=w(Q_{+,i}),\qquad 0\le E[A_i\mid Y_i]\le w_0,$$

and finite nonnegative almost surely. Bounded conditional means and
Gaussian second moments imply $E[A_i\|Y_i\|^2]<\infty$; the joint pairs of
iid data and their conditional states are iid and therefore obey

$$N^{-1}\sum_i A_iY_iY_i^T\longrightarrow E[w(Q_+)YY^T].$$

With N*tau_N tending to infinity, each typical normalized Gaussian
conditional concentrates at the same wrong interior line. Its outside
probability is bounded by one. Averaging over the frozen conditional states
as in T44 proves concentration of the whole-series cut at that wrong line.
Only a first conditional-precision moment is needed for this fixed-kernel
LLN argument; no physical signal-size distribution is introduced. For
N-dependent or heterogeneous state kernels an appropriate uniform LLN is
an additional requirement; the conditional-mean bound alone is not asserted
to establish that extension.

## 4. Symmetry and authority

Q+ and the weight are unchanged by channel exchange. The weighted scatter
is conjugated by that exchange, so reciprocal symmetry can hold exactly
while Fisher consistency fails. The preferred null line at mass 1 is itself
fixed by reciprocity. Exact-zero padding is also harmless to the scatter;
Z does not remove this population effect.

The result is about fixed scalar best-positive-misfit weights multiplying a
quadratic criterion or serving as conditional mean precisions. It does not
cover the nonlinear Student residual factors of T39, whose entire function
of K(theta) matters, arbitrary data-dependent joint rules, or separate exact
uncertainty sets. L21's monotonic evidence interpretation, T30, and the
positive-best-fit noise module must therefore be sorted at the level of
this class; the theorem does not declare any premise of the full project
inconsistent or select a replacement definition. The cut interpretation is
provisional, and the recorded power form is not silently replaced here.

## Optional stronger stationarity observation

Even allowing a bounded nonmonotone w, without requiring its supremum to
occur at zero, cannot give universal Gaussian stationarity in this
particular Q+-weighted quadratic class, except for weights constant almost
everywhere. Here is a separate argument; it is not needed for the monotone
counterexample above, and the numerical helpers retain the monotone bounds.

Let W(y)=w(Q+(y)), bounded and even under y->-y, and define the entire
Gaussian Laplace transform F(mu)=E0[W(Y)*exp(mu dot Y)]. If the weighted
score vanishes at every positive Gaussian mean mu, then
$\mu_\perp^T(\nabla^2F)\mu=0$ on an open quadrant. Analyticity extends
this identity to all mu. In polar coordinates it reads
$r\,\partial_r\partial_\theta F-\partial_\theta F=0$, so
$\partial_\theta F=r c(\theta)$. Evenness and analyticity at zero give
$\partial_\theta F=O(r^2)$; hence c=0 and F is radial. Uniqueness of the
Fourier/Laplace transform of the integrable density W(y)*phi2(y) implies
that W is radial almost everywhere. But W=w(0) on the whole same-sign
quadrant at every radius, so radiality forces W=w(0) almost everywhere.
The positive continuous part of the null Q+ distribution then forces
w(q)=w(0) for Lebesgue-almost every q>0. A monotone w with the stated value
at zero is consequently constant. This observation does not remove the
scope restriction to a fixed function of Q+ and a quadratic fit.
