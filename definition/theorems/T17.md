**Status (B06, 30 Oct, Codex):** proved below for the current median in the fixed-N, increasing-amplitude regime, with fixed positive-definite covariances and finite errors. ## Statement

$\widehat m\to m$ as $R^{\ast2}\to\infty$

## Proof or support

### B06: fixed-N amplitude consistency from the projection law

**Standard mathematics:** concentration of a powered likelihood with a separated
maximum. The required growth regime is stated explicitly. Fix N, each declared
positive-definite covariance $\Sigma_i$, each candidate subspace, a finite positive
mass $m_0$, and finite error vectors $e_i$. Write the data as
$y_i(R)=R t_i+e_i$, where $t_i=(m_0N_i\alpha_i,N_i\alpha_i)$ and at least one
$\alpha_i\ne0$. The starting half-Cauchy is fixed, proper, and has positive density
at $m_0$. This is the regime tested by the older fixed-noise experiment; increasing
N with weak readings is not covered by the argument.

Whiten each reading by its fixed covariance. Let $P_i(\phi)$ be the orthogonal
projection onto its whitened candidate plane, with $m=c\tan\phi$. T36 gives
$H_i(\phi)=\|P_i(\phi)y_i\|^2$. At the true angle $\phi_0$, the signal belongs to
the candidate plane. Hence

$$Q_{+,i}(R)\le E_i-H_i(\phi_0)
\le\|e_i\|^2_{\Sigma_i^{-1}},\qquad
g_i(R)\ge\min\{1,\kappa_i/\|e_i\|^2_{\Sigma_i^{-1}}\}>0,$$

with lower bound 1 for a zero error. The energy $E_i$ projects into the full declared
span, so the second inequality also holds for a restricted subspace. All weights
are at most 1 and are constant in the hypothesized mass.

For an informative reading, $\|P_i(\phi)t_i\|^2$ has its unique projective maximum
at $\phi_0$: a nonzero pair $(m_0N_i\alpha_i,N_i\alpha_i)$ cannot belong to a different
mass plane. Its local curvature there is strictly negative, since the derivative
of that plane moves a nonzero signal in a nonzero normal direction. Compactness
of the projective line gives a strict gap outside each neighbourhood of $\phi_0$.
The weights' positive lower bounds therefore imply, for the weighted sum
$L_R=\sum_i g_iH_i/2$,

$$L_R(\phi_0)-L_R(\phi)\ge a_\delta R^2-O(R)
\quad\text{if }|\phi-\phi_0|\ge\delta.$$

The leading first derivative vanishes at $\phi_0$, so $L_R'(\phi_0)=O(R)$, while
the negative second derivative is bounded below by $aR^2$ in a fixed small
neighbourhood. Thus the whole-line best angle satisfies
$\widehat\phi_R-\phi_0=O(1/R)$, its curvature $\mathcal H_R\ge aR^2$, each fitted
score is O(R), and $J_R=O(R^2)$. The existing Bartlett definition consequently has
$\eta_R\ge b>0$ for all large R, including its fallback 1 when J vanishes.
This proves only the bounds needed here, not T31's forcing argument.

Normalize the law relative to its peak. On an angular window of width $1/R$ around
$\phi_0$, its exponent differs from the peak by O(1), so its normalizer is at least
$a/R$. Outside a fixed angular neighbourhood, the bound above and $\eta_R\ge b$
give probability at most $AR\exp(-a_\delta R^2)\to0$. Every median therefore tends
to $m_0$. This proves the stated point limit under the named conditions for any
fixed allowed covariance and subspace, using the current ours30 weights and power.
It does not establish the Statement for every way total signal can grow.

### The stronger claims g→1 and η→1 need additional conditions

Fixed errors need not disappear in noise units when the signal grows. For example,
in isotropic 2D use three readings
$F_i=(2R+u_i,b_i)$, $a_i=(R,0)$, with
$(u_i,b_i)=(0,8),(40,-6),(-30,4)$ and unit channel SDs. At large R the profiled
misfits tend to $b_i^2/5$, so

$$g_i\longrightarrow\frac{7.5}{b_i^2}
=(0.1171875,0.208333\ldots,0.46875).$$

Put $G=\sum g_i$ and $\bar u=\sum g_iu_i/G$ using those limits. The fitted curvature
is asymptotic to $5GR^2$ and the scores to $g_iR(u_i-\bar u)$, giving

$$\eta_R\longrightarrow
\min\left\{1,\frac{5G}{\sum_i g_i^2(u_i-\bar u)^2}\right\}
=0.01877229655\ldots.$$

Nevertheless the law's median tends to 2. Thus convergence does not require either
power to approach 1. Sufficient extra conditions are: $g_i\to1$ if the limiting
profiled misfit is at most $\kappa_i$, and $\eta\to1$ if
$\limsup J_R/\mathcal H_R\le1$. A concrete condition implying both is that every
error tends to zero in declared noise units while N remains fixed and the signal
amplitudes grow: then the misfits tend to zero and $J_R/\mathcal H_R\to0$.
These are additional assumptions, not properties of a generic fixed Gaussian draw.
