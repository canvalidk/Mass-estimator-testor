## Statement

$\mathrm{Var}_{\rm true}/\mathrm{Var}_{\rm law}=1+\sum_c\theta_c'^2\big/\sum_c\theta_c'^2r^{\ast2}_c$, $\theta_c'=s_c/(s_c^2+m^2)$

## Proof or support

### B06: the unweighted factor's score and information

**Standard mathematics:** sandwich variance for an estimating equation whose
expected curvature differs from its score variance. Here the calculation is for
the **unweighted** flat factor ($g_i=1$). Index every force/acceleration component
pair by c. Assume independent zero-mean unit Gaussian errors in standardized
coordinates, with no cross-channel or cross-component correlations. With diagonal
declared covariance this gives

$$x_c=r_c^*\sin\theta_c(m_0)+\epsilon_c,\quad
y_c=r_c^*\cos\theta_c(m_0)+\zeta_c,\qquad
\theta_c(m)=\arctan(m/s_c).$$

The squared $r_c^*$ is the **whole pair's** true standardized length, not merely
acceleration SNR squared:
$r_c^{*2}=(F_c^*/\sigma_{F,c})^2+(a_c^*/\sigma_{a,c})^2$.
For this calculation a negative true component is handled by reversing both of
its standardized channels. This preserves the Gaussian error law and squared
factor, so the pair length can be taken nonnegative without loss of generality.
For one noise pair all $s_c=s$ and all derivatives $\theta'_c$ coincide. The
displayed per-component formula also allows fixed unequal ratios when the error
pairs remain independent as stipulated.

At the true mass rotate the observed pair into its signal direction:
$T_c=x_c\sin\theta_c+y_c\cos\theta_c=r_c^*+Z_c$ and
$V_c=x_c\cos\theta_c-y_c\sin\theta_c=W_c$, with independent standard normals
$Z_c,W_c$. The flat log factor is $\ell_c=T_c^2/2$. Its angle score is $T_cV_c$
and its negative angle curvature is $T_c^2-V_c^2$. Therefore, exactly,

$$E[\ell'_c(m_0)]=0,\quad
A_c=E[-\ell''_c(m_0)]=\theta_c'^2r_c^{*2},\quad
B_c=E[\ell'_c(m_0)^2]=\theta_c'^2(1+r_c^{*2}).$$

The $\theta''_c$ term in the mass curvature has zero expectation because the angle
score has zero mean. Independence makes the total information terms
$A=\sum_cA_c$ and $B=\sum_cB_c=A+\sum_c\theta_c'^2$.

### Meaning and conditions of the variance ratio

Consider a growing independent series with an interior true mass, A→∞, a uniquely
identified expected optimum, a negligible maximum leverage/no dominant score, and
the usual law-of-large-numbers, central-limit and local Taylor conditions. These
hold, for example, for repeated fixed finite blocks of component types with fixed
positive noise ratios, bounded true signals and positive average signal energy.
Linearizing the score equation gives leading sampling variance $B/A^2$ for the
fitted mass. The untempered law's **local Gaussian (Laplace) width squared** is $1/A$.
Thus their ratio is

$$\frac{B/A^2}{1/A}=\frac BA
=1+\frac{\sum_c\theta_c'^2}{\sum_c\theta_c'^2r_c^{*2}},\qquad
\theta'_c=\frac{s_c}{s_c^2+m_0^2},$$

the retained Statement. Under the same local normal limit the median and mode have
the same first-order location. For one noise pair with M component pairs the ratio
reduces to $1+M/\sum_c r_c^{*2}$; a k-dimensional reading contributes k component
pairs, rather than one, to the numerator.

The local-width qualification is essential: T19 proves $E_p[m]=\infty$ under the
half-Cauchy starting law for every finite series. Consequently the full mass law
does not have a finite variance to insert literally in the Statement. A local
normal approximation can have the widths above while the exact law retains its
remote heavy tails. The all-zero/no-identification case A=0 is excluded here.

### What η estimates in this regime

For the same unweighted terms, evaluation at a consistent fitted angle preserves
the probability limits of normalized curvature and squared scores under the
regularity conditions just stated. Hence $\mathcal H/J\to A/B\le1$ and
$\eta=\min(1,\mathcal H/J)\to A/B$. The quoted law's local width squared then tends
to $1/(\eta A)=B/A^2$. The cap does not alter this limit. This states the asymptotic
target of T31's already-defined operation; it does not derive the form or its
finite-N rule.

For current ours30, the data-dependent $g_i(Q_{+,i})$ multiply scores and curvatures
and can alter even the expected score. Their information terms must be computed
from the actual weighted derivatives. The displayed raw-factor formula does not
prove its own validity after that weighting. Even a fixed common power h changes
$A$ to hA and $B$ to $h^2B$, so its uncalibrated local-width ratio becomes hB/A,
not B/A. The historical 1D tests use unweighted scores ($g=1$), as their formulas
and explicit weights show. No universal finite-N or weighted-width identity is
asserted by this proof, and no premise or production equation is changed.
