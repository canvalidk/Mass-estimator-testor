## Standard mathematics: variance of random projections

For a random orthogonal projector P with mean Pbar, its operator variance
is E[(P-Pbar)^2]=Pbar-Pbar^2. Applied to a fixed vector z, this is the
ordinary Hilbert-space variance identity

$$E\|Pz-Pbar\,z\|^2=z^T(Pbar-Pbar^2)z.$$

It is also the minimum mean squared discrepancy for predicting the random
projection Pz by one fixed vector: its minimizer is Pbar z. Here the
randomness is a draw of a candidate mass from the **supplied prior**.
It is not a population of experimental signals, a distribution learned
from the readings, or a claim about a true latent signal. The identity
motivates and evaluates a research state; it does not derive a squared
loss or this particular state from the physical commitments.

## 1. Finite-data construction and invariance

Use T54's full common-acceleration protocol with K scalar body masses,
dimension d, independent events Y_i, declared SPD covariances Sigma_i,
and a supplied proper joint mass prior Pi. Choose any whitening W_i and set

$$z_i=W_iY_i,\qquad X_i(m)=W_iC(m),\qquad
P_i(m)=X_i(X_i^TX_i)^{-1}X_i^T.$$

For each event define the prior mean projector and its variance operator

$$\overline P_i=\int P_i(m)\Pi(dm),\qquad
\mathcal V_i=\overline P_i-\overline P_i^2,
\qquad U_N=\sum_i z_i^T\mathcal V_i z_i.$$

There is no true signal, fitted mass, count or population size parameter
in this state. The original prior is held fixed when events are merged.
The integrals exist for every proper Pi without a density or moment
assumption, since all orthogonal projectors have norm at most one.

Write L_N=-sum_i ||(I-P_i(m))z_i||^2/2 and use the existing score S_N,
exact conditional covariance C_N and statistic T_N^2=S_N^T C_N^+ S_N,
including its rank-zero convention. For a fixed explicit research
constant kappa>0 form

$$\lambda_N=\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{1+U_N}},\qquad
Q_N(dm)\propto
\exp[-T_N(m)^2/2+\lambda_N L_N(m)]\Pi(dm).$$

The same supplied Pi both specifies the starting law and defines the
mean projector. This is a new prior-dependent research kernel. Unlike
T62's Pi-independent kernel class, its prior use is not reduced to one
final multiplication. That characterization is therefore not invoked as
if it applied unchanged; no posterior-only updating assertion is made.

Since P_i^2=P_i, direct expansion gives

$$\mathcal V_i=\int(P_i-\overline P_i)^2\Pi(dm),\qquad
0\preceq\mathcal V_i\preceq\tfrac14 I.$$

The upper bound follows by applying x(1-x)<=1/4 to the eigenvalues of
the positive contraction Pbar_i. In particular 0<=U_N<=E_N/4, where
E_N=sum_i ||z_i||^2, and 0<lambda_N<=kappa. The finite factor is bounded
above by one and below by exp[-(1+kappa)E_N/2]>0. Thus Q_N is proper at
every finite input, and divergent nonnegative prior moments persist.
No finite-law covariance or finite-noise exact coverage follows.

An appended measured exact-zero event adds zero to U,L,S,C. Empty or
entirely zero data return Pi. Independent event state contributions add
associatively, provided the same original Pi is retained. No signal
learned at one event is inserted into another event's projection.
This algebraic fact does not settle L19's authority over the new state.

Changing the whitening conjugates P_i, Pbar_i and V_i by that event's
orthogonal whitening change, and rotates z_i by the same matrix. The
state and law are unchanged. Passive physical frame/unit changes with
the complete graph, covariance and prior transformed consistently give
the same result. A smooth invertible mass-coordinate change merely
reindexes the prior integral; no parameter-space metric or prior density
value is selected. Independent whole-event sign changes also preserve
the state. Joint prior dependence is used as supplied, never inferred
from the marginal priors.

### The scalar two-channel case has an exact simplification

For K=d=1 each projector has rank one in R^2, hence tr Pbar_i=1.
The two-dimensional Cayley--Hamilton identity gives

$$\mathcal V_i=\det(\overline P_i)I_2,\qquad
U_N=\sum_i w_i\|z_i\|^2,\qquad
w_i=\det(\overline P_i)\in[0,1/4].$$

Thus this scalar state is an instrument/prior-dependent weighted trace,
not a fitted signal size. The weights are unchanged by an overall
covariance scale or an orthogonal whitening change; they can decrease
when the candidate graphs of an instrument become nearly indistinguishable
over the supplied prior. The general joint variance operator need not
be a scalar multiple of identity.

## 2. Positivity and uniform bounds over comparable covariance shapes

Assume that Pi has a positive continuous Lebesgue density in a neighbourhood
of some interior positive mass vector. Then V_i is strictly positive
definite for every SPD covariance in the full protocol.

Indeed, v^T V_i v=0 means P_i(m)v is one fixed vector w for Pi-almost
every m. Continuity extends this equality to the whole open neighbourhood.
The vector w belongs to all its graph planes. Two distinct channel
directions (m,1) give planes with zero intersection, also after whitening,
so w=0. Therefore X_i(m)^T v=0 throughout that open set. In the physical
channel blocks of W_i^T v this reads
sum_j m_j b_j+b_a=0 for every m in an open set. Varying each m_j gives
all b_j=0 and then b_a=0. Since W_i is invertible, v=0.

The projector is independent of the overall covariance scale. For shapes
in a fixed compact SPD class, Pbar and V depend continuously on the shape:
bounded projectors give dominated convergence in the prior integral.
Compactness therefore supplies constants 0<v_-<=v_+<=1/4 with

$$v_-I\preceq\mathcal V_i\preceq v_+I$$

uniformly over those shapes. The constants may depend on the fixed prior
and shape class; no uniformity over changing priors is asserted. The prior
needs no global density, moments or independence between masses.

## 3. T61's entire Gaussian regime is retained

Use exactly T61's deterministic independent Gaussian experiment and
covariance history Sigma_i=t_i Omega_i, with arbitrary t_i>0 and
uniformly comparable SPD shapes Omega_i. Let

$$\mu_i=W_iC(m_0)a_i,\qquad
\Delta_N=\sum_i\|\mu_i\|^2,\qquad b_N=N+\Delta_N,
\qquad h_N=\Delta_N/\sqrt{b_N}\to\infty.$$

Retain T61's fixed-prior regularity at truth, including no additional
singular component in that neighbourhood. Section 2 applies to this
prior. Independent Gaussian quadratic-form moments give

$$EU_N=\sum_i[\operatorname{tr}\mathcal V_i+
\mu_i^T\mathcal V_i\mu_i]\asymp b_N,$$
$$\operatorname{Var}(U_N)=\sum_i[
2\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal V_i^2)+
4\mu_i^T\mathcal V_i^2\mu_i]=O(b_N).$$

Consequently U_N is comparable in probability to b_N and

$$\lambda_N\Delta_N\asymp_p h_N\to\infty,
\qquad \lambda_N\Delta_N/h_N^2=O_p(h_N^{-1})\to0.$$

There is no sum of fourth powers of the signals, leverage bound,
covariance-type frequency or limiting normalized information matrix.
The variance calculation concerns a fixed prior-defined quadratic
operator at each event, not an estimate of the event's signal population.

These are precisely the two anchor bounds used in T61. Its uniform
profile separation, exact conditional score normalization, observed
matrix consistency and local quadratic bounds are unchanged. In
particular the local normalizer has order h_N^-K relative to the anchored
profile maximum; the outside-neighbourhood bound is
O_p(h_N^K)exp[-c lambda_N Delta_N] -> 0. This proves the same **full** TV
limit, not only a curvature comparison:

$$\operatorname{TV}\left(
Q_N\{\widehat C_N^{-1/2}\widehat H_N(m-\widehat m_N)\in\cdot\mid Y\},
N_K(0,I_K)\right)\to0,$$

in probability, while the identically standardized fitted-center error
converges in law to N_K(0,I_K). Here mhat_N is the global positive profile
maximum and Hhat,Chat are T61's fitted matrices. Actual pushforward
central intervals for every fixed smooth scalar composite with nonzero
truth gradient have asymptotically nominal coverage. These conclusions
do not assert convergence of finite-law moments.

## 4. The state repairs T63's fixed compact-prior experiment

Use exactly T63's fixed covariance/signal history and its prior supported
on I=[1/10,10], retaining both m0=6/5 and mf=4/15. Let M_N=floor(log_2 N)
be its informative count, an analysis quantity only.

For a bad event, e_i=4^-i and P_i(m) differs uniformly over I from one
constant projector by O(e_i). Its prior variance operator therefore has
norm O(e_i^2)=O(16^-i). Its whitened observation is standard Gaussian, so

$$\sum_{i\ {\rm bad}}E[z_i^T\mathcal V_i z_i]<\infty.$$

Since the summands are nonnegative, their sum is finite almost surely.
The informative events have one fixed positive-definite V_0 and are iid.
The ordinary law of large numbers gives

$$U_N/M_N\to u_0=\operatorname{tr}\mathcal V_0+
\mu_0^T\mathcal V_0\mu_0>0,\qquad
\lambda_N\sqrt{M_N}\to\kappa/\sqrt{u_0}.$$

The first convergence can be taken almost surely. Thus lambda_N M_N
diverges like sqrt(M_N), instead of vanishing as in T63's trace anchor.
The bounded bad-event centered profile/score/C fields leave the
informative population profile gap of order M_N and its local Godambe
width of order M_N^-1/2 unchanged.

For precise centering, let mhat_I,N maximize the profile over the **fixed
prior support** I. Uniform compact-field convergence makes this maximum
uniquely interior near m0 with probability tending to one. No claim is
made about a global profile maximum far outside I under degenerating
instrument geometry. The state U itself never fits a maximum or chooses
a score root; the maximizer is only used to state the asymptotic limit.

The profile gap outside a fixed true neighbourhood within I is at least
c M_N with high probability. A true-neighbourhood local ball supplies
anchored normalizing mass of order M_N^-1/2 relative to
exp[lambda_N L_N(mhat_I,N)]. The remaining mass is at
most O_p(sqrt(M_N)) exp[-c lambda_N M_N] -> 0. Locally the extra profile
log variation is O_p(lambda_N)=o_p(1), so exact conditional-score
normalization and the usual local quadratic bound give

$$\operatorname{TV}\left(
Q_N\{\widehat H_{I,N}(m-\widehat m_{I,N})/
\sqrt{\widehat C_{I,N}}\in\cdot\mid Y\},N(0,1)\right)\to0.$$

The corresponding standardized fitted-center error is asymptotically
standard normal. This is the complete law on the positive line with its
fixed support I: there is no omitted posterior tail or data-dependent
restriction to the true basin. In particular the median converges to m0,
the false-root mass tends to zero and central intervals are asymptotically
nominal. No untruncated-prior or unrestricted-geometry theorem is inferred.

## 5. Fixed finite experiments under signal amplification

The same construction also covers a fixed finite full-protocol experiment
with arbitrary fixed SPD covariances and signals R a_i as R tends to
infinity, provided some a_i is nonzero. The finite covariance menu is
automatically contained in a compact shape class. The fixed supplied
prior retains its regularity near the interior truth.

Here Delta_R is of order R^2, while the number of events is fixed. Section
2 and the same quadratic moments give U_R=R^2 u_0+O_p(R), u_0>0, so
lambda_R is of order R^-1. The uniform profile gap is of order R^2 and
the local score-based width is of order R^-1. Consequently the ordering
exponent lambda_R Delta_R grows like R, while the added local precision
is relatively O_p(R^-1). The Gaussian quadratic-field proof and exact
conditional normalization used above also apply to this limit: their
b_R=N+Delta_R variance bound does not require N itself to diverge.

A local ball supplies normalizing mass of order R^-K, and the global
tail bound is O_p(R^K)exp[-c R] -> 0. Thus the full joint TV limit and
smooth-composite central-interval conclusions of section 3 hold with
local scale R. This is the same compatible fixed-experiment small-noise
regime as T54 after the corresponding deterministic rescaling. It adds
no new state, count input, noise mixture or non-Gaussian assertion.
