# T34: The magnitude ratio is the geometric mean of the two pairings

**From:** L1, T1 (pairing form)  
**Test:** tests/test_theorems.py::test_t34_gain_is_the_geometric_mean_of_the_pairings; ::test_t34_swap  
**Status:** **new, 27 Oct (Claude, from the 26 Oct scratch session; not ruled).** Algebra on exact
pairs, in plain (δ) units. Not yet redone in whitened units (L9), where the estimator works.  
**Source:** scratch session of 26 Oct, §1 ("mass is the metric")

## Statement

For an exact pair with $\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf a>0$, let $m_1=\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf a/\lvert\mathbf a\rvert^2$
and $m_2=\lvert\mathbf F\rvert^2/(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf a)$ be T1's two pairings and
$\gamma=\lvert\mathbf F\rvert/\lvert\mathbf a\rvert$ the magnitude ratio (the gain). Then

1. $m_1m_2=\gamma^2$, so $\gamma=\sqrt{m_1m_2}$;
2. $m_1\le\gamma\le m_2$, with equality throughout iff $P=m_1/m_2=\cos^2\theta=1$;
3. under the swap ($\mathbf F\leftrightarrow\mathbf a$, $m\mapsto1/m$), $m_1\mapsto1/m_2$,
   $m_2\mapsto1/m_1$ and $\gamma\mapsto1/\gamma$: the gain is self-dual.

Statistics name: the reduced-major-axis slope, the geometric mean of the two
ordinary least-squares slopes.

## Proof or support

(1) is $(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf a)$ cancelling. (2): $m_1/\gamma=\gamma/m_2=\cos\theta\in(0,1]$.
(3): swapping the vectors swaps the roles of $\lvert\mathbf F\rvert$ and $\lvert\mathbf a\rvert$ and
leaves $\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf a$ fixed.

## What it does not show

- **That the gain is the only admissible per-reading mean.** Any $M(m_1,m_2)$ with
  $M(1/y,1/x)=1/M(x,y)$ respects the swap. Near isotropy every such mean agrees with
  the gain to fourth order (scratch §2, a toy script not kept), so the fork exists only
  under anisotropy. Which mean, under anisotropy, is open (`../../problems/O17.md`).
- **Composition.** For an anisotropic body $m_1$ passes L5 and $m_2$ passes L14 exactly;
  the gain passes neither (O17). Under L22 the three coincide.
- **O2.** It explains why a reading's size ratio is $\lvert\mathbf F\rvert/\lvert\mathbf a\rvert$ when
  the pair is exact. It does not touch O2's blockers.
