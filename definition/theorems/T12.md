**Status (B10, 31 Oct, Codex):** Raw factors multiply, and their log factors add. Compact weighted Gram addition applies when the readings share a common whitened channel geometry; general covariances retain additive functions of the candidate. The score-square state also adds at a common candidate, but separate best masses and scalar $\eta$ values cannot be combined by addition. The union recomputes $\eta$, so its tempered law generally differs from the product of separately tempered laws.

## Statement

exact, as a product of per-reading factors

## Proof or support

### Raw combination and the prior counted once

Take the law in `../equation.md` as given. For a set $A$ of readings, put
$\ell_A(m)=\sum_{i\in A}\ell_i(m)$, with
$\ell_i(m)=g_iH_i(m)/2$. The reading's $g_i$ is determined from that
reading alone, so it is unchanged when readings are regrouped. The
untempered data factor therefore obeys
$$
 L_{A\cup B}(m)=e^{\ell_A(m)+\ell_B(m)}=L_A(m)L_B(m)
$$
for disjoint $A,B$. This is exact multiplication of **per-reading factors**,
as in the Statement. Independence across readings (L13) and no shared
signal nuisance beyond mass (L19) justify the product; the prior is a
series-level declaration, counted once (T7):
$$
 p_{A\cup B}(m)\propto\pi(m)
 e^{\eta_{A\cup B}[\ell_A(m)+\ell_B(m)]}.
$$
Multiplying two normalized posterior densities would also count $\pi$
twice. Even after correcting that by $p_Ap_B/\pi$ wherever $\pi>0$, its
data exponent is $\eta_A\ell_A+\eta_B\ell_B$, which need not equal the
union's exponent. Normalization constants cannot repair an $m$-dependent
difference.

### Which state adds

For common isotropic whitened channels with one noise ratio $s$, write
$\theta=\arctan(m/s)$, $u=(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)^\top$, and
$$
 M_i=\begin{pmatrix}P_i&D_i\\D_i&A_i\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 P_i=\lVert F_i/\sigma_{F,i}\rVert^2,\quad
 A_i=\lVert a_i/\sigma_{a,i}\rVert^2,\quad
 D_i=(F_i/\sigma_{F,i})\cdot(a_i/\sigma_{a,i}).
$$
Here both vectors are projected into their declared subspace when one is
given. Then $H_i=u^\top M_i u$ and
$\ell_A=\tfrac12u^\top(\sum_{i\in A}g_iM_i)u$. The compact raw state is
the weighted Gram sum, so it is order- and grouping-independent. The common
noise ratio matters: `test_states_with_different_instruments_do_not_add`
checks the refusal in the historical compact-state implementation.

With arbitrary reading-specific full $\Sigma_i$, each $H_i(m)=b_i^\top
G_i^{-1}b_i$ can have a different candidate-dependent $G_i$. Their sum is
still exact, but generally is not a quadratic form of one common
two-dimensional $u$. The proof of raw multiplication does not require
that extra compression.

In a common angle coordinate $\phi$, the functions
$$
 \ell_A(\phi)=\sum_{i\in A}\ell_i(\phi),\qquad
 \mathcal H_A(\phi)=-\sum_{i\in A}\ell_i''(\phi),\qquad
 J_A(\phi)=\sum_{i\in A}\ell_i'(\phi)^2
$$
also add across disjoint groups **at the same candidate**. The union first
finds its own $\widehat\phi$ from $\ell_A+\ell_B$, and then evaluates
$\mathcal H_A+\mathcal H_B$ and $J_A+J_B$ there. Values evaluated at the
groups' separate optima are insufficient. Nor is the weighted Gram sum
alone sufficient for $J$: squaring each reading's score before summing
retains information lost by summing the scores first. In the compact case
this additional state can be stored as
$T=\sum_i\operatorname{vec}(g_iM_i)\operatorname{vec}(g_iM_i)^\top$.
With $v=\operatorname{vec}(u'u^\top)$, the score-square sum is $J=v^\top T v$.
Thus additive state does not
mean additive scalar $\eta$ or simple multiplication of tempered laws.

The O3 record §3's exponential-kernel characterization is about the raw
common-Gram state; it is not a claim that the calibrated law, including
$J$ and $\eta$, depends only on that Gram sum. No forcing step for the
kernel or for $\eta$ is added here.

### A two-reading example

Let $c=\sigma_F=\sigma_a=1$, and take two one-dimensional readings
$(F_1,a_1)=(4,1)$ and $(F_2,a_2)=(1,4)$. Both have $Q_+=0$, $g=1$ and
private $\eta=1$. Their raw states are
$$
 M_1=\begin{pmatrix}16&4\\4&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
 M_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&4\\4&16\end{pmatrix},\quad
 M_1+M_2=\begin{pmatrix}17&8\\8&17\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Hence $\ell_1+\ell_2=17/2+4\sin(2\phi)$. The common maximum is
$\widehat\phi=\pi/4$, or $\widehat m_{\rm factor}=1$. The individual scores
there are $15/2$ and $-15/2$, so
$$
 \mathcal H=16,\qquad J=2(15/2)^2=225/2,\qquad
 \eta_{\{1,2\}}=\frac{32}{225}=0.142222\ldots.
$$
For a numerical comparison of the actual densities, at $m=1$ and $m=3$
the summed log factors are $12.5$ and $10.9$. The half-Cauchy prior has
$\pi(1)/\pi(3)=5$, so
$$
 \frac{p_{\{1,2\}}(1)}{p_{\{1,2\}}(3)}
 =5e^{(32/225)(8/5)}=6.2776359466\ldots,
$$
whereas the prior-corrected product of the two private laws gives
$5e^{8/5}=24.7651621220\ldots$. Each private law used $\eta=1$; the union
uses $32/225$. This difference persists with the prior counted correctly
and is checked against `met.full.ours30` by the named test.
