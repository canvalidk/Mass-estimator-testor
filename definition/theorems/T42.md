# T42: Information growth for the Gaussian Bartlett law

**From:** T18, T31, T40, T41; the explicitly stated flat Gaussian submodel of L1/L13/L18. L11 is under review, not silently narrowed. The fixed-prior regularity assumptions are stated below.
**Test:** `tests/test_gaussian_information.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 3 Nov 2026 (Codex). A sharp consistency criterion for the continuous flat Gaussian law with the existing Bartlett power, plus a vanishing-power extension of T40. It is not the selected replacement for L11 on the full input type or an adoption of a new estimator.

## Standard mathematics and scope

This is spectral estimation in a rank-one signal-plus-Gaussian-noise model,
the central limit theorem for Gaussian scatter, and concentration of a
generalized posterior. Spectral perturbation is standard; see
[Yu, Wang and Samworth, *A useful variant of the Davis--Kahan theorem*](https://arxiv.org/html/1405.0680).
Concentration, local normality and coverage are distinct results; see
[Miller, *Asymptotic Normality, Concentration, and Coverage of Generalized Posteriors*](https://jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-469.html).
The two-dimensional identities, converse and calibration comparisons below
are written out rather than invoking a general result with unstated hypotheses.

Use independent 1D force--acceleration readings with uncorrelated Gaussian
errors, standardized to covariance $I_2$, and a common instrument ratio s.
The mass coordinate is the **noise angle** $\theta=\arctan(m/s)$, with a fixed
interior truth $\theta_0\in(0,\pi/2)$. Each observed standardized pair has
the form

$$Y_i=\epsilon_i r_i u_0+Z_i,\quad
u_0=(\sin\theta_0,\cos\theta_0),\quad
Z_i\stackrel{\rm ind}{\sim}N(0,I_2).$$

The amplitudes $r_i\ge0$ and signs $\epsilon_i\in\{-1,1\}$ are arbitrary
deterministic quantities. Triangular arrays are allowed; no common latent
size law, iid signal assumption, bounded amplitude or leverage condition is
imposed. The prior is fixed and proper, with a positive continuous density
in a neighbourhood of the truth. The half-Cauchy with c = s is one example;
its induced positive-angle prior is uniform.

The data factors here are **flat Gaussian**, $\ell_i(\theta)
=(u_\theta^TY_i)^2/2$, with no misfit weight. The result concerns their actual
T31 power. It does not cover ours30's sign weights or finite-ν ours32.
In fact T39's ν = 10 counterexample satisfies the growth condition below.
That alone prevents treating this theorem as a repair of that probe.

## 1. The law has an exact global concentration parameter

Put $B_N=\sum_iY_iY_i^T$. Let $\widehat\theta_N$ be its principal line on the
whole projective circle and $H_N\ge0$ its eigenvalue gap. Almost surely the
gap is positive under the stated Gaussian experiment. The total deviance is
exactly

$$D_N(\theta)=\widehat L_N-L_N(\theta)
=\tfrac12H_N\sin^2(\theta-\widehat\theta_N).$$

At that fit define

$$J_N=\sum_i[(u_{\widehat\theta_N}^TY_i)
(v_{\widehat\theta_N}^TY_i)]^2,\qquad
\eta_N=\min(1,H_N/J_N),$$

where v is the orthogonal angular derivative of u, and η = 1 when J = 0.
The quoted law, on positive mass, is therefore

$$Q_N(d\theta)\propto\pi_\theta(d\theta)
\exp[-\tfrac12K_N\sin^2(\theta-\widehat\theta_N)],$$

$$K_N=\eta_NH_N=\min(H_N,H_N^2/J_N),$$

using $K_N=H_N$ for J = 0. This is a global kernel identity, stronger than
curvature matching at a point. A single reading has J = 0 at its own line;
the cap/fallback still leaves its finite H, not infinite information.

K is angular precision. Its finite value depends on the named coordinate;
the divergence statement near an interior truth is unchanged under a fixed
smooth reparametrization with nonzero derivative. Neither K alone nor its
divergence certifies a correct fitted mass for other factor families.

## 2. An attainable condition, without iid signals or a leverage restriction

Let

$$\Delta_N=\sum_i r_i^2,\qquad
\mathcal I_N=\frac{\Delta_N^2}{N+\Delta_N}.$$

Assume $\mathcal I_N\to\infty$. T41's exact scatter identity gives

$$E B_N=NI_2+\Delta_Nu_0u_0^T,\qquad
E\|B_N-E B_N\|_F^2=6(N+\Delta_N).$$

Consequently the noise fluctuation is small relative to the population gap:

$$H_N/\Delta_N\to1,\qquad
\delta_N=\widehat\theta_N-\theta_0
=O_p(\sqrt{N+\Delta_N}/\Delta_N)=o_p(1),$$

where the projective representative is chosen near truth.

Work in the true parallel/perpendicular basis and absorb the deterministic
sign into a Gaussian error, so $X_i=r_i+\xi_i$, $W_i=\zeta_i$. The exact score
at angular displacement δ is

$$s_i(\delta)=\cos(2\delta)X_iW_i
-\tfrac12\sin(2\delta)(X_i^2-W_i^2).$$

Thus, at the random fit,

$$J_N\le2\sum_i(X_iW_i)^2+
\tfrac12\sin^2(2\delta_N)\sum_i(X_i^2-W_i^2)^2.$$

The Gaussian moments are

$$E\sum_i(X_iW_i)^2=N+\Delta_N,$$
$$E\sum_i(X_i^2-W_i^2)^2
=\sum_i r_i^4+4(N+\Delta_N)
\le\Delta_N^2+4(N+\Delta_N).$$

Markov's inequality and the fitted-angle rate give
$J_N=O_p(N+\Delta_N)$. Independence of the fit and these sums is unnecessary.
It follows that

$$K_N=\min(H_N,H_N^2/J_N)\longrightarrow\infty$$

in probability, including the J = 0 fallback. On an angular window of width
$K_N^{-1/2}$ around the fit, the kernel is bounded below and the prior density
is bounded below with high probability. Outside any fixed neighbourhood of
truth, the kernel is at most $e^{-cK_N}$. The normalizer is at least
$c'/\sqrt{K_N}$, proving law concentration and median consistency.

The argument also covers fixed N with increasing amplitude. **η need not
stay away from zero:** divergence of the quoted gap is what matters here.
No nominal coverage claim follows from this concentration proof.

## 3. The condition is sharp for this procedure as N grows

Suppose $N\to\infty$ and the deterministic condition fails to diverge. There
is a subsequence on which $\Delta_N=O(\sqrt N)$ and, after further extraction,

$$\frac{\Delta_N}{2\sqrt N}\to\kappa<\infty,\qquad
\frac{S_{4,N}}N\to q,\quad S_{4,N}=\sum_i r_i^4,\quad
0\le q\le4\kappa^2.$$

In the true basis the centered noise scatter has the ordinary Gaussian
quadratic-form CLT. Its two entries $(\xi_i^2-\zeta_i^2,2\xi_i\zeta_i)$ have
covariance $4I_2$. The signal--noise cross terms divided by $\sqrt N$ vanish
in mean square since their variance is $O(\Delta_N)$. Hence

$$Z_N=\frac{(B_{11}-B_{22},2B_{12})}{2\sqrt N}
\ \Rightarrow\ Z=(\kappa,0)+G,\qquad G\sim N(0,I_2).$$

Here the B entries are in the true basis. Put $R=|Z|$ and $\alpha=\arg Z$;
R is nonzero almost surely. Then $H_N/(2\sqrt N)\Rightarrow R$ and the fitted
angular displacement tends in distribution to $\alpha/2$ projectively.

To control J at that random line, write

$$A_N=\sum_i(X_iW_i)^2,\quad
C_N=\sum_i(X_i^2-W_i^2)^2,\quad
E_N=\sum_iX_iW_i(X_i^2-W_i^2).$$

For one reading the exact Gaussian moments are:

| quantity | mean | variance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $(XW)^2$ | $1+r^2$ | $8+16r^2+2r^4$ |
| $(X^2-W^2)^2$ | $4+4r^2+r^4$ | $128+256r^2+144r^4+16r^6$ |
| $XW(X^2-W^2)$ | 0 | $12+24r^2+9r^4+r^6$ |

Because $S_{4,N}\le\Delta_N^2=O(N)$ and
$S_{6,N}\le\Delta_N^3=O(N^{3/2})$, their summed variances divided by $N^2$
vanish. The exact trigonometric identity

$$J_N(\delta)=\cos^2(2\delta)A_N+
\tfrac14\sin^2(2\delta)C_N-
\sin(2\delta)\cos(2\delta)E_N$$

therefore implies, uniformly in δ,

$$J_N(\delta)/N
=1+\frac{S_{4,N}}{4N}\sin^2(2\delta)+o_p(1).$$

No bounded-amplitude assumption is used. At the fitted angle the limit is
$1+(q/4)\sin^2\alpha\ge1$. The cap is asymptotically inactive and

$$K_N\Rightarrow K(Z)=
\frac{4|Z|^2}{1+(q/4)\sin^2\alpha}.$$

The limiting positive-angle law is the normalized random measure

$$Q_Z(d\theta)\propto\pi_\theta(d\theta)
\exp[-\tfrac12K(Z)\sin^2(\theta-\theta_0-\alpha/2)].$$

It has finite random precision and does not concentrate. Median failure
requires a separate argument: choose a different interior angle θ1 in the
neighbourhood where the prior density is positive, and a small interval
around it separated from truth. The denominator in K is at most $1+q/4$.
An open event with $|Z|$ sufficiently large and $\alpha/2$ near
$\theta_1-\theta_0$ makes $Q_Z$ put more than three quarters of its mass
inside that interval. Gaussian full support gives this event positive
probability. Kernel integrals depend continuously on these finite
parameters, so the finite-N laws put more than half their mass there with
probability bounded away from zero. Every median is then separated from
truth. Thus the median is inconsistent along this subsequence too.

**Conclusion:** for this flat Gaussian Bartlett procedure, $N\to\infty$, and
the stated fixed regular prior, $\mathcal I_N\to\infty$ is necessary and
sufficient for law concentration and median consistency. It is a
method-specific characterization, not an estimator-independent information
threshold or a criterion for the full input type.

For homogeneous weak rows $r_i=r_N$, the condition is
$Nr_N^4/(1+r_N^2)\to\infty$, with critical size $N^{-1/4}$.
For a fixed stream $r_i=b i^{-p}$ it holds for $p<1/4$ and fails for
$p\ge1/4$. At p = 1/4, $\kappa=b^2$, q = 0; quartic energy diverges
logarithmically. Failure there belongs to this procedure and is not proved
unavoidable for every estimator by T41.

## 4. T40 can follow the powered law even when η vanishes

For either T40 map, put $T_N=\eta_ND_N$ and $b_N=a_N\eta_N$. Its exact gap
bound gives

$$0\le F_{\eta_N,a_N}(D_N)-\eta_ND_N
\le c\,\frac{T_N^2}{b_N}e^{T_N/b_N},$$

where c = 1/2 for the logarithmic map and c = 1 for the W map. Therefore

$$T_N=O_{Q_N}(1),\qquad a_N\eta_N\to\infty$$

imply TV convergence of $P_N\propto e^{-F(D_N)}\pi$ to Q, even if η tends
to zero. Indeed the relative weight $e^{-(F-\eta D)}$ is at most 1 and tends
to 1 in Q-probability; its Q expectation tends to 1. This supplies the
normalized-measure comparison. Merely $a_N\to\infty$ is insufficient when
η vanishes: if $a_N=A/\eta_N$, $\eta_N\to0$ and $d=t/\eta_N$, the two
losses tend respectively to $A(e^{t/A}-1)$ and $t e^{t/A}$, rather than t.
Their whole local kernels retain that nonlinearity for fixed finite A.

For the Gaussian flat factors the whole-line per-reading oscillation is
$o_i=\|Y_i\|^2/2$. Use T40's explicit research scale

$$a_N=\gamma\sum_i w(o_i/u),\qquad \gamma,u>0,$$

with either **nondecreasing** example $w(x)=x/(1+x)$ or $w(x)=1-e^{-x}$.
Noncentral chi-square stochastic dominance gives
$E[w(o_i/u)]\ge E[w(\chi_2^2/(2u))]=c_0>0$ for every true amplitude.
Independent bounded summands have summed variance at most N, so
$a_N\asymp_p N$ without iid signals. Monotonicity is material to this
uniform lower bound; bounded continuity alone does not supply it.

In the identifying regime of part 2, let $Z_N(k)$ be the integral of
$e^{-k\sin^2(\theta-\widehat\theta_N)/2}$ against the fixed prior. Rescaling
around the interior fit gives
$Z_N(k)\sim p_\theta(\theta_0)\sqrt{2\pi/k}$ when $k=K_N$ or $K_N/2$.
The local positive continuous density supplies this term; the remainder
outside a fixed neighbourhood is exponentially smaller. Consequently

$$Q_N(\eta_ND_N>t)\le e^{-t/2}\,
\frac{Z_N(K_N/2)}{Z_N(K_N)},\qquad
\frac{Z_N(K_N/2)}{Z_N(K_N)}\to\sqrt2.$$

This proves $\eta_ND_N=O_{Q_N}(1)$ explicitly. Also

$$\frac{J_N}{NH_N}=O_p(1/\Delta_N+1/N)=o_p(1).$$

Thus $N\eta_N\to\infty$ and $a_N\eta_N\to\infty$. Both research families
inherit consistency in this regime, including growing weak-signal series
where η vanishes.

On a bounded-information subsequence from part 3,
$N\eta_N/\sqrt N\Rightarrow2R/[1+(q/4)\sin^2\alpha]>0$ almost surely.
Thus $N\eta_N\to\infty$ there too, and
$\sup_\theta\eta_ND_N\le K_N/2=O_p(1)$. The same TV comparison applies.
Every subsequence of a deterministic array has a further subsequence where
$\mathcal I_N$ diverges or is bounded; in the latter case extract κ and q
as above. This proves TV convergence for **every** deterministic amplitude
array as $N\to\infty$ under the stated Gaussian/prior assumptions.

The research families therefore have the same sharp Gaussian consistency
criterion. For median failure use the wrong-angle event from part 3 with
its margin: more than three quarters of Q lies in a fixed interval away
from truth, so TV closeness leaves more than one half under P there.
TV closeness alone would not justify a general claim of median closeness.

The map, γ, u and saturation remain unforced, and their L19 admissibility
remains G24. This calculation does not select them or repair T39's separate
finite-ν Student failures.

## 5. What the diagnostic and the theorem license

H, J and their capped quoted precision already describe the permitted
series-width calculation from individual residuals (L19's recorded scope).
Here they are theorem quantities and inspectable diagnostics. The definition
returns its ordinary output when the growth condition fails; no rejection
branch, threshold, extra caller input or shared signal-size model is added.

The condition includes the aligned zero-noise limit at fixed N, fixed noise
geometry and nonzero compatible signal. **Codex's proposed sort:** Newton II fixes the value in
that identifying exact limit; statistical information growth determines
which noisy sequences approach it. Extending the limit to every divergent
total-energy sequence was the unsupported step exposed by T41. This
distinction does not itself select a replacement statement of L11.

G25 is partly advanced: a sharp attainable growth regime is now proved for
one existing procedure, and G24's vanishing-η comparison has a sufficient
scale condition. The full covariance/subspace type, finite-ν factors,
finite-N uncertainty, readout forcing and uniqueness remain open. No
production default, premise statement or ruling is changed.
