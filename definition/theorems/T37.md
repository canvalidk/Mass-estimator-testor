## The model

Believing the σ's means $\Sigma_i$ is the **covariance** of reading $i$'s noise (L18, L13). It does not
mean every reading's noise draw has that size. Give reading $i$ a noise size $v_i$, its shared state
in L8's sense: given $v_i$ its error is $N(0,v_i\Sigma_i)$ (Gaussian given the state: Herschel–Maxwell,
T6), with $v_i$ drawn from a law $P$ with $E[v]=1$, so the covariance is exactly $\Sigma_i$. Nothing is
assumed to have gone wrong: a misaligned reading most likely drew a large $v_i$, so its information
about $m$ is worth less. In standard terms this is a scale mixture of Gaussians (an elliptical model
for each reading); the Student-$t$ is the inverse-gamma case.

Under L21's cut the reading's precision is read from its own misfit, $g(Q)=E[1/v\mid Q]$, and to
first order its tempered evidence is $g\cdot H_i$ (the ruled power form); $\nu=k-1$.

## Statement

1. **T30 forces a power-law tail on the noise size.** If $P(v>x)$ falls like $x^{-\alpha}$, then
   $Q\,g(Q)\to\nu+2\alpha$ as $Q\to\infty$: a misaligned reading keeps weight $(\nu+2\alpha)/Q$, which is
   T30's bounded, non-vanishing tail. Lighter tails fail T30: bounded support gives
   $g\to1/v_{\max}$, and the lognormal gives $Q\,g(Q)\to\infty$; either way the confidence on a fixed
   misaligned pair grows without bound as σ → 0.
2. **With Σ the covariance, the tail coefficient exceeds $k+1$.** A finite mean needs $\alpha>1$, so
   $\nu+2\alpha>\nu+2=k+1$. ours29's coefficient $\nu=k-1$ needs $\alpha=0$, ours30's $\kappa=k-\tfrac12$
   needs $\alpha=\tfrac14$: both are noise sizes with **no mean**, i.e. a Σ that is a scale or a floor,
   not the covariance (`../../problems/O14.md`, 30 Oct).
3. **The clean end is above 1.** $g(0^+)=E[v^{-\nu/2-1}]/E[v^{-\nu/2}]\ge E[1/v]\ge1/E[v]=1$, strictly
   unless $P$ is a point mass. A reading that fits suspiciously well most likely drew small, and its
   precision exceeds the declared one. So the cap $g\le1$ (O12) holds only for the fixed-size
   Gaussian, which fails (1).
4. **The exact cut factor.** Averaging the Gaussian factor over $v$'s posterior given $Q$ gives, for
   the inverse-gamma law with index $\alpha$,
   $$\Big(1+\frac{A_i(m)}{2(\alpha-1)+Q_i}\Big)^{-(\alpha+\nu/2)},\qquad A_i(m)=E_i-Q_i-H_i(m)\ \ (\ge0),$$
   with $A_i$ the reading's along-line misfit at $m$ (T36). The power form $E_i^{g}$ is its first
   order in $A_i$. As σ → 0 with a fixed misaligned pair it converges to a σ-free curve **peaked at
   the reading's own best line**: low information, direction kept (a reading above the line still
   says "higher", only weakly). Redescending outlier mixtures, which send that pull to zero, are
   not this model.
5. **Z.** An exact zero reading has $A_i\equiv0$: its factor is constant in $m$ whatever $P$ is
   (T23's own test already shows an elliptical law passes the zero-reading check).

## G20 (30 Oct, afternoon): where α comes from, and a correction to (2) and (3)

**Standard name first.** A stated standard uncertainty is itself uncertain, and practice says by
how much: the **effective degrees of freedom** $\nu_{\rm eff}$ of the uncertainty (GUM, JCGM 100:2008,
Annex G: Welch–Satterthwaite for combined uncertainties, eq. G.3 $\nu\approx\tfrac12(\Delta u/u)^{-2}$ for
Type B), reported on calibration certificates with the coverage factor. If $\sigma$ was estimated from
$\nu_{\rm eff}$ degrees of freedom of Gaussian residuals (L13 applied to the calibration), the true noise
size relative to the stated one is $v\sim\mathrm{IG}(\nu_{\rm eff}/2,\ \nu_{\rm eff}/2)$: Student's 1908 result.

**The sort (Claude's, provisional): α is an input, and practice supplies it**, $\alpha=\nu_{\rm eff}/2$.
The inverse-gamma shape is forced too, by L13 applied to how the σ was obtained. TASK §5's guard is
met: it is on the certificate. T30 then reads as the GUM's own position that no uncertainty is
exact: $\nu_{\rm eff}=\infty$ is the fixed-size Gaussian, which fails (1).

**The correction.** The model above used $E[v]=1$ (Σ the covariance). Practice's convention is
different: the stated $u$ is the estimate $\hat\sigma$, for which $E[1/v]=1$ (the *precision* is right
on average; the covariance is $\Sigma\,\nu_{\rm eff}/(\nu_{\rm eff}-2)$). That normalisation was Claude's
choice, not R-2026-10-30-1's, and under practice's it changes:

- (2) becomes: the tail weight is $(\nu+\nu_{\rm eff})/Q$. ours30's $\kappa=k-\tfrac12$ is a σ declared with
  $\nu_{\rm eff}=\tfrac12$, ours29's $k-1$ one with $\nu_{\rm eff}=0$: by eq. G.3, σ's uncertain by about 100% and
  by more than any finite amount. No instrument declares that. The break stands, restated: not
  "no mean" but "an input value no practice supplies".
- (3) becomes: $g(0^+)=1+\nu/\nu_{\rm eff}$, **exactly 1 in 1D** and $1.04$ at $\nu_{\rm eff}=50$, $k=3$. The
  cap is right in 1D and nearly right for well-known σ's; it fails by $\nu/\nu_{\rm eff}$.
- (1), (4) and (5) are unchanged. The world check used the $E[v]=1$ law; not rerun.

## Proof or support

(1) For large $Q$ the posterior of $u=1/v$ is dominated by large $v$, where $p(v)\propto
v^{-\alpha-1}$; with the likelihood $v^{-\nu/2}e^{-Q/2v}$ it is $\mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha+\nu/2,\ \text{rate }Q/2)$,
whose mean is $(2\alpha+\nu)/Q$. Checked for inverse-gamma and Pareto laws ($\alpha=1.5,3$; $\nu=0,1,2$)
by quadrature; lognormal and uniform laws checked to fail. (2) is (1) with $\alpha>1$. (3): $s\mapsto
\log E[v^{-s}]$ is convex (Hölder), so $E[v^{-(s+1)}]/E[v^{-s}]$ is non-decreasing in $s$; at $s=0$ it is
$E[1/v]\ge1/E[v]$ (Jensen), strict unless degenerate. (4): the inverse-gamma posterior is
$\mathrm{IG}(\alpha+\nu/2,\ \alpha-1+Q/2)$ and $E[e^{-uA/2}]$ is its Laplace transform. All in
`tests/test_noise_size.py`.

**World check** (seeded, 3D, strong signal, $N=20$, $v_i\sim\mathrm{IG}(3)$ with mean 1, 300 series,
`MET_SLOW=1`): ours30 covers 0.98 with median log-width 0.093; the cut-mixture law with $\alpha=3$
declared and $\eta=1$ covers 0.96 at 0.084. The scratch run of the same day on six worlds
(1D and 3D; Gaussian, $\alpha=3$, $\alpha=1.5$) found all three laws (ours30, mixture, plain Gaussian)
near 95% at strong signal; the mixture law was 10–17% narrower than ours30 in 3D on the spread worlds
and slightly under-covered (0.92) when it declared spread on a fixed-size Gaussian world.

## What it does not show

- **The positive-mass misfit.** ours30 reads $Q_+$; its sign condition can only add to the
  dimensions $Q_+$ carries about $v$, so the bound in (2) should stand (coefficient above $k+1$), but
  this is argued, not computed.
- **Weak signal.** Everything above uses $Q\mid v\sim v\chi^2_\nu$, the strong-signal law.
- **The tail index α.** It is how heavy the instrument's per-reading noise is: something the readings
  of one series cannot supply without borrowing (L19), so an input. Its principal branch is
  **unknown yet**: $\alpha\to\infty$ is the fixed Gaussian (fails (1)); $\alpha\to1^+$ the heaviest with a mean.
- **η under this model.** With the series level declared exact, $\eta=1$ by the declaration; the world
  check ran the mixture law with $\eta=1$ and covered. Not argued further.
- **The ruled power form.** L21 (ruled 29 Sept) fixes $E_i^{g}$; (4) says that form is the first-order
  version of the cut. They agree near the reading's best line and differ far from it. Flagged, not
  resolved: a ruling decides.
