# T62: Posterior-only sequential closure is a stronger requirement than additive carried state

**From:** T12's distinction between additive carried state and products of already calibrated laws; T56's conditional-score law; T60's trace anchor. The sequential-closure axiom below is an explicit additional condition, not a recorded VD premise.
**Test:** `tests/test_anchor_coherence.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). Positive prior-multiplication updates admit posterior-only sequential closure exactly when their kernels multiply up to a data-only factor. Every positive trace-anchor prefactor fails that additional condition, and deleting the anchor does not repair it. The additive state still combines associatively. No failure of L5, L14, L19 or T12, no general impossibility of coherent inference, and no production or ruling change is inferred.

## 1. Standard mathematics and the extra condition

In generalized Bayesian inference, sequential coherence is the requirement
that updating with two independent information items separately agrees with
using their cumulative loss at once. Bissiri, Holmes and Walker (2016),
[equations (2)–(3)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5082587/),
formulate that property explicitly. Their exponential cumulative-loss update
provides the standard comparison. Here the issue is whether the *specific*
globally recalibrated kernel already used for a data union equals the
product of its separate kernels. It is not enough to rename that product
as the union loss.

Let D be a finite set of independently declared physical events. Suppose
an update of a supplied proper mass prior Pi has the form

$$U_D(\Pi)(dm)=\frac{K_D(m)\Pi(dm)}{\int K_D(u)\Pi(du)},$$

where K_D is strictly positive, independent of Pi, and each displayed
normalizer is finite and positive. All T60 finite-data kernels are bounded
and satisfy these conditions. The empty-data kernel is one. A general
characterization below needs only that each two-atom prior be admissible;
the finite T54/T60 research references allow such priors explicitly.

The additional **posterior-only sequential closure** condition is

$$U_{D\cup E}(\Pi)=U_E\bigl(U_D(\Pi)\bigr) \tag{SC}$$

for disjoint independent event sets D,E and every admissible Pi. Only the
already normalized mass law is passed from the first update to the
second; no old score, calibration state or data are passed. This is
stronger than order independence when all event state is retained.
The two events may have equal observed numerical values without being
copies of one physical observation.

## 2. Kernel characterization

**Theorem.** Condition (SC) holds for all priors if and only if there is a
positive data-only number c(D,E) such that

$$K_{D\cup E}(m)=c(D,E)K_D(m)K_E(m) \quad\hbox{for every candidate }m.\tag{1}$$

**Proof.** The sequential law is proportional to K_E K_D Pi, so (1)
immediately suffices. Conversely take any candidates x,y and the prior
(delta_x+delta_y)/2. Its posterior odds at y versus x are
K_{D union E}(y)/K_{D union E}(x) in the batch update and
K_D(y)K_E(y)/(K_D(x)K_E(x)) sequentially. Equality gives the same ratio
K_{D union E}(m)/(K_D(m)K_E(m)) at every x,y. That ratio is c(D,E).
QED.

Equivalently, log kernels must add modulo constants independent of mass.
Iteration reduces a finite union to the product of singleton kernels up
to such a constant. This characterization by itself selects neither a
singleton loss nor a learning-rate constant: fixed positive multiples
of any additive loss still satisfy it.

## 3. Every positive trace prefactor fails (SC)

Take one scalar body, d=1, Sigma=I_2, and two independent events with
the same observed numerical vector Y=(F,A)=(1,1). At a candidate mass m,
the Gaussian residual cost is

$$k(m)=\frac{(1-m)^2}{1+m^2}.$$

The candidate projection is nonzero at every positive m. The singleton
conditional-score statistic is exactly k(m); with two identical observed
vectors it is 2k(m). The total observed trace is two for one event and
four for both. Therefore T60's kernels with any fixed kappa>0 are

$$K_1(m)=\exp\!\left[-\frac{1+\kappa/\sqrt3}{2}\,k(m)\right],$$
$$K_2(m)=\exp\!\left[-(1+\kappa/\sqrt5)\,k(m)\right].$$

Use the admissible prior (delta_1+delta_2)/2. Since k(1)=0 and k(2)=1/5,
the batch and posterior-only sequential log odds of mass two versus one
are respectively

$$b=-\frac{1+\kappa/\sqrt5}{5},\qquad
s=-\frac{1+\kappa/\sqrt3}{5}.$$

Their difference is

$$b-s=\frac{\kappa}{5}\left(\frac1{\sqrt3}-\frac1{\sqrt5}\right)>0.$$

Thus no positive prefactor repairs (SC). The disagreement is not an
integration, prior-moment or weak-identification issue: these are exact
odds at two positive candidate masses. Appending a zero event, which
T60 ignores, is a different operation from appending this nonzero event.

## 4. Removing the anchor does not restore posterior-only closure

For the unanchored score law of T56, take independent observed events
Y_1=(2,1), Y_2=(1,2), still with identity covariance. In a unit line
coordinate u=(m,1)/sqrt(1+m^2), let q_i=u dot Y_i and
r_i=(Y_{i,F}-m Y_{i,A})/sqrt(1+m^2). Then

$$T_D^2=\frac{(\sum_{i\in D}q_i r_i)^2}{\sum_{i\in D}q_i^2}.$$

All displayed denominators are positive. At masses one and two,

| statistic | m=1 | m=2 |
|---|---|---|
| T_1^2 | 1/2 | 0 |
| T_2^2 | 1/2 | 9/5 |
| T_{1 union 2}^2 | 0 | 144/205 |

The signs of the two score contributions cancel at mass one; their
individual squares do not. Consequently, under the same equal two-atom
prior, the batch log odds are -72/205 whereas the sequential log odds
are -2/5. They differ by 2/41. This is precisely the nonadditivity of
the *pooled normalized* score contrast. It is not repaired by setting
the profile-anchor prefactor to zero.

## 5. The retained state does combine associatively

At each candidate m retain the four functions

$$\mathcal A_D(m)=(L_D(m),S_D(m),C_D(m),E_D).$$

For independent event sets every component adds:

$$\mathcal A_{D\cup E}=\mathcal A_D+\mathcal A_E.$$

The sum is associative and order independent. After merging state, form
T^2=S^T C^+ S and lambda=kappa/sqrt(1+E), and multiply the **original**
supplied Pi by the union kernel once. This gives exactly the batch law.
In the scalar common-covariance case the finite scatter is a sufficient
representation of this functional state. No claim is needed that an
arbitrary heterogeneous experiment has that same fixed-size scatter.
This is a statement of the correct combination operation for this
research construction, not a new production output slot.

Tests reconstruct these fields separately for three nonidentical events,
merge in different groupings and orders, and compare the resulting finite
law with direct batch evaluation. They also verify exact Gaussian profile
updating as a positive example of posterior-only closure. They do not
assert continuous-prior numerical quadrature accuracy.

## 6. What the recorded commitments do and do not force

T12 already records that the tempered union is not the product of the
tempered laws of its parts because calibration is recomputed on the
union. Its associative object is carried state. It therefore does not
assert (SC). L19 forbids borrowing a learned signal regime between
events; it does not state that all information relevant to a future
calibration has been compressed into a normalized mass law. L5/L14
propagate one supplied joint mass law through a physical composite and
compare coverage of a new acquisition; neither is a batching axiom.

Similarly, an independent-object marginal-autonomy axiom would be a
different additional condition: that appending data about an unrelated
object preserve the first object's standalone law under a product prior.
The recorded multi-body composition clauses do not state it. No such
axiom is used or disproved here, and no independence is inferred from
unknown-mass marginal laws.

The conditional result therefore closes a tempting forcing route: tuning
the prefactor cannot make T60 a posterior-only sequential update. If
(SC) is later required, it would change the admissible construction
class rather than select one positive kappa. Under the present recorded
commitments, this is **not** grounds to reject the family, to declare a
new caller input, or to claim that the finite law is unique. The remaining
finite-data freedom and L19 state permission remain G24/G26/G27.
