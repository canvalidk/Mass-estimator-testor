## Standard mathematics

- **Aczél's theorem on associative operations** (Aczél 1966, §6.2; Craigen and Páles 1989): a continuous
  operation on a real interval that is associative and strictly increasing in each argument is addition
  after one continuous relabelling of the interval, and the relabelling is unique up to a positive factor.
- **Randomisation tests** (Fisher 1935): a sign-flip test on $n$ scalar values has $2^n$ equally likely
  patterns, so its p-value is a multiple of $2^{-n}$ and, two-sided, never below $2/2^n$.
- **The effective number of terms** $(\sum c_i)^2/\sum c_i^2$ (Kish 1965; Satterthwaite 1946 for degrees of
  freedom): a count that is continuous in the weights.

## Statement

Plain words. A *block* is a series of readings of one object; its *result* is whatever is kept of it so
that two blocks' results can be joined into the result of the pooled readings. L25 as ruled says the order
of the readings does not matter. Its strong clause, proposed on the branch, says results join in any
order and grouping, with the all-zero experiment changing nothing.

**(1) If the result is one curve of the mass and curves join mass by mass, they add (E17-L1).** Suppose
each block carries one real number for each candidate mass, and the pooled block's number at a mass is a
fixed continuous function of the two blocks' numbers at that mass, associative, strictly increasing in
each, with the all-zero experiment's value as identity. Then, after one relabelling of the values, the
function is addition; commutativity follows and is not assumed. The unnormalised curve of T73 is this,
and so is any other curve that adds reading by reading.

*What the check adds.* The assumption that carries it is "mass by mass". **The strong clause of L25 does
not by itself exclude a normalised law.** With one fixed starting law, two blocks' normalised laws join by
multiplying them and dividing the starting law out once; that join equals the pooled law, is associative
and commutative, and has the starting law (the all-zero experiment's law, T24) as identity, all to
$10^{-12}$ in total variation. It is not a mass-by-mass rule, because normalising reads the whole curve.
The branch's own test of "normalising breaks the clause" multiplies two normalised laws as they stand,
which counts the starting law twice (0.198 in total variation). What does stand against carrying a
normalised law is already on main: it loses the level, so the score's variance cannot be recovered from
it (T77 (4)), and the starting law itself is not supplied (T80).

**(2) A result that adds and is continuous at a zero reading cannot hold a count of readings
(E17-L2 (1)).** If a number carried in the result adds across blocks and equals the same positive $k$ for
every single non-zero reading, then as a reading shrinks to zero the number stays $k$, while at the zero
reading it is 0 (the identity). So it is not continuous there. Any rule whose reference law needs the
number of readings, or of non-zero readings, is therefore either moved by an exactly zero reading or
jumps beside one. Two instances on main, measured:

- T82's joined test: twenty readings of size $10^{-9}$ return it to the declared Gaussian's set
  (T82 (8)).
- The scale-free test of T75 (4) on scalar readings. Five scalar readings, candidate kept at
  $p=2/32=0.0625$; beside an exactly zero reading, the same; beside one reading a thousandth of a noise
  unit in size, $p=2/64=0.031$ and the candidate is rejected. The same on five different readings. It is
  a sign test, and the tiny reading is a sixth sign. In exact arithmetic this happens for every size
  above zero; `met/conditional_lr.py` counts differences below $10^{-9}$ of the statistic as ties, so at
  $10^{-4}$ and below it still returns 0.0625. On readings of two components the same test is steady
  beside twenty tiny readings (its reference law has no atoms).

**(3) Near a zero reading an even, twice-differentiable additive result is quadratic (E17-L2 (2)); with
"a reading equals its equal parts" it is quadratic everywhere (E17-L2 (3)).** The first is Taylor's
theorem: each reading contributes $\Phi(y)$ with $\Phi(0)=0$ and $\Phi(-y)=\Phi(y)$ (a sign convention,
L16), so the first-order term vanishes. The second needs $n\,\Phi(y/\sqrt n)=\Phi(y)$, and that premise is
sorted in `../../problems/O20.md` (24 Nov): it holds for the point and is not a premise for the set, and
with it the Gaussian curve is assumed in another form.

**(4) What a weighting has to carry (Claude's reading of E17-L2 (4), with T82 (2) and (8)).** By
T82 (2) the curve and the score's variance cannot tell two readings far apart from fifty honest ones.
By (2) and (3), a result that adds, is smooth at a zero reading and treats a reading as its equal parts
is exactly those. So a rule that believes disagreeing readings less carries one of three things:

| what is carried beside the curve | what it gives up | on the pages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a count of readings | continuity beside a zero reading, by (2) | T82's joined test; the scale-free test on scalar readings |
| a further sum over readings that is not quadratic | "a reading equals its equal parts", which is not a premise for the set | not built. An effective count $(\sum c_i)^2/\sum c_i^2$ of the readings' curvatures is such a sum; it was noted on 30 Sept as the Z-safe correction (`../../problems/O14.md`) and gives up an exact reference law |
| the readings themselves | a result of fixed size | the 18 Nov pair weight; the scale-free test on vector readings |

This is T82 (8)'s "two of three" (an exact reference law, indifference to a zero reading, continuity
there) with the third column made explicit. It is a reading, not a theorem: nothing shows the three
rows exhaust the possibilities.

## Against the rows

1. **The order of readings does not matter [L25 as ruled].** Met by every rule on main: each is a
   symmetric function of the readings.
2. **Results join in any order and grouping [L25's strong clause, proposed].** Met by the curve with the
   score's variance (T77 (6)); met by a normalised law with a fixed starting law ((1)); met trivially by
   keeping the readings. So as a premise it selects nothing until "a result of fixed size" and
   "continuous beside a zero reading" are added, and neither of those is ruled. It stays proposed.
3. **A reading with no information changes nothing [L20].** The clause's identity. Continuity beside
   such a reading is a different requirement, not on the list; it was left as a question on 29 Sept and
   is the one T82 (8) could not sort.
4. **The discount [R-2026-11-17-3; R-2026-11-24-2].** (4): the weighting is not excluded by any of this.
   What it costs depends on which row it takes.

## What is not shown
