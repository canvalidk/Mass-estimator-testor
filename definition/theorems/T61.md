# T61: Joint Gaussian calibration allows changing covariance shapes and precisions

**From:** T54's physical common-acceleration graph and exact conditional score, T55's sensitivity/variability identity, and T60's trace-anchor construction. The covariance-shape class, Gaussian experiment and growth condition below are explicit theorem conditions.
**Test:** `tests/test_heterogeneous_anchor.py`; finite-law and bookkeeping checks in `tests/test_joint_trace_anchor.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). T60's complete joint-law and composite-coverage conclusions extend to deterministic event-dependent covariance shapes in a uniformly comparable class, with arbitrary declared overall noise scales. Neither repeated covariance types, limiting type frequencies, convergent normalized information matrices nor a maximum-leverage condition is required. Unrestricted degenerating covariance shapes and the other full-type obligations remain open; no finite law is uniquely selected or adopted.

## Standard mathematics and the extension being made

This is heteroskedastic functional errors-in-variables estimation with one
free, fixed nuisance acceleration per acquisition. The local sampling
matrix is Godambe information, and the normalized exponential-contrast
limit is a quasi-posterior Bernstein--von Mises statement; T55 identifies
the standard estimating-function and quasi-posterior background. Here the
exact conditional Gaussian score replaces an independent-array score CLT,
so no maximum signal leverage needs to vanish. The technical replacement
for T60's common scatter matrix is uniform control of smooth Gaussian
quadratic random fields. That control is proved in section 3, rather than
assuming a repeated finite menu of instruments.

## 1. Experiment, declared covariance history and finite law

Fix K scalar body masses, spatial dimension d and p=(K+1)d. For arbitrary
deterministic acceleration vectors a_i, observe independent events

$$C(m)=\begin{pmatrix}m_1I_d\\\vdots\\m_KI_d\\I_d\end{pmatrix},
\qquad Y_i=C(m_0)a_i+\varepsilon_i,
\qquad \varepsilon_i\sim N_p(0,\Sigma_i).$$

The true mass vector m0 is fixed and interior positive. Every event has
one shared acceleration observation and its declared full covariance;
no copied acceleration is counted twice. Each Sigma_i is known, positive
definite and declared before the data. Its correlations and anisotropy
may change at every event without repetition, a limiting frequency or
any convergence assumption.

For the asymptotic assertions assume that, in one fixed physical reference
system of units and frames, the deterministic covariance history admits

$$\Sigma_i=t_i V_i,\qquad t_i>0,\qquad
0<v_-I_p\preceq V_i\preceq v_+I_p<\infty,$$

with constants v_- and v_+ independent of i. The overall scales t_i may
vary arbitrarily, tending to zero or infinity or oscillating. Thus the
condition bounds covariance **shapes**, not absolute instrument precision.
Equivalently one can use fixed SPD comparison matrices instead of I.
Independent passive frame representations may use correspondingly
transformed covariances; the condition concerns the physical reference
representation, not numerical equality of matrices in different frames.

This decomposition is an analytical condition on the already declared
Sigma_i, not another estimator input, a fitted covariance factor or the
unknown common-noise-scale branch of O7. Fixing physical units to state a
uniform comparison is not an additional invariance of nature: after any
fixed invertible change of units, suitable finite comparison constants
still exist. Unbounded relative anisotropy is not covered.

Choose W_i=t_i^(-1/2)V_i^(-1/2), put z_i=W_iY_i, and write

$$X_i(m)=W_iC(m),\quad P_i(m)=X_i(X_i^TX_i)^{-1}X_i^T,
\quad R_i=I-P_i,\quad U_i=P_i z_i.$$

Let L_N=-sum_i ||R_i(m)z_i||^2/2, S_N=grad L_N and mathcal C_N be
T54's exact conditional covariance of S_N given the own-event U_i.
Define T_N^2=S_N^T mathcal C_N^+ S_N, with the existing rank-zero
convention. With a fixed explicit research constant kappa>0 and a supplied
proper joint prior Pi, use exactly T60's finite-data operation:

$$E_N=\sum_{i\le N}Y_i^T\Sigma_i^{-1}Y_i,
\qquad \lambda_N=\frac{\kappa}{\sqrt{1+E_N}},
\qquad Q_N(dm)\propto
\exp[-T_N(m)^2/2+\lambda_N L_N(m)]\Pi(dm).$$

No count, true signal, covariance-shape comparison constants or
decomposition t_i,V_i enters this operation. All of T60's finite-law
properties hold without the uniform-shape condition: propriety, positive
bounded factors at finite data, whole-event exact-zero neutrality and
the stated units, coordinates and independent-event-frame bookkeeping.
The finite factor bounds also retain divergent nonnegative prior moments.
The following Gaussian limits concern local shapes and quantiles, not
actual finite-law moments or finite-sample exact coverage.

## 2. Growth condition and conclusion

Set

$$\mu_i=W_iC(m_0)a_i,\qquad
\Delta_N=\sum_{i\le N}\|\mu_i\|^2,\qquad
b_N=N+\Delta_N,\qquad
h_N=\frac{\Delta_N}{\sqrt{b_N}}\longrightarrow\infty.$$

The acceleration and covariance histories are both deterministic; the
result also holds for deterministic triangular arrays with the same
uniform shape constants and this growth condition. The condition is
sufficient, not a theorem of minimax necessity for the heterogeneous
joint problem and not a replacement ruling for L11.

Assume that Pi has a positive continuous Lebesgue density near m0 and no
additional singular component in that neighbourhood. It can be any
proper law elsewhere. With probability tending to one the global profile
maximum mhat_N is uniquely interior and the matrices

$$\widehat H_N=-\nabla^2 L_N(\widehat m_N),\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal C}_N=\mathcal C_N(\widehat m_N),\qquad
M_N=\widehat{\mathcal C}_N^{-1/2}\widehat H_N$$

are well-defined with positive-definite Hhat and Chat. Then Q_N
concentrates at m0 and, in sampling probability,

$$\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
Q_N\{M_N(m-\widehat m_N)\in\cdot\mid Y\},N_K(0,I_K)\right)\to0,$$

while

$$M_N(\widehat m_N-m_0)\Rightarrow N_K(0,I_K).$$

Consequently the full joint law's pushforward central intervals are
asymptotically nominal for every fixed smooth scalar composite with
nonzero gradient at truth, including sum, parallel sum and reciprocal
mass coordinates. On the exceptional events where the displayed fitted
matrices are not positive definite, any reporting convention has
vanishing asymptotic probability. The law itself retains its always
defined pseudoinverse statistic. Mathematical exactness concerns exact
rank; numerical rank tolerances are not a statistical identification gate.

## 3. Uniform quadratic-field control replaces the pooled scatter

The projectors P_i(m) depend only on V_i: the scalar t_i cancels between
X_i and its Gram inverse. Compactify the graph by

$$u=(m,1)/\sqrt{1+\|m\|^2}\in S^K,
\qquad B_i(u)=V_i^{-1/2}(u\otimes I_d).$$

For every unit u, the eigenvalues of B_i(u)^T B_i(u) lie between
1/v_+ and 1/v_-. The graph projector therefore extends smoothly to the
whole sphere S^K. Its derivatives of every fixed order are uniformly
bounded over i,u, using a fixed finite smooth atlas. No boundary
singularity is introduced at infinite masses. The positive-mass graph
closure is only a compact subset of this larger sphere.

For any symmetric smooth coefficient field D_i(u) with these uniform
derivative bounds, define its centered quadratic sum

$$Z_N(u)=\sum_{i\le N}
\{z_i^TD_i(u)z_i-\operatorname{tr}D_i(u)
-\mu_i^TD_i(u)\mu_i\}.$$

Here z_i=mu_i+e_i with independent e_i~N_p(0,I_p). For a fixed symmetric
matrix D, expansion into linear and centered quadratic Gaussian terms
gives

$$\operatorname{Var}(z_i^TDz_i)
=2\operatorname{tr}(D^2)+4\mu_i^TD^2\mu_i
\le C_D(1+\|\mu_i\|^2).$$

The same bound applies to every fixed coordinate derivative of D_i.
Independence across i therefore bounds the expected squared L2 norm of
each such derivative of Z_N by C b_N. In each of the finite charts,
insert a smooth partition-of-unity cutoff. The resulting compactly
supported coordinate fields have expected squared H^s Sobolev norm at
most C_s b_N. For an integer s>r+K/2, the Sobolev embedding
H^s into C^r gives

$$\|Z_N\|_{C^r(S^K)}=O_p(\sqrt{b_N})$$

for any fixed finite r. Cutoff derivatives only change finite constants.
This is a uniform mean-square bound on a smooth random field, not a
CLT or a bound involving sum_i ||mu_i||^4. A single acquisition may carry
nearly all the signal energy.

Apply this lemma to D_i=P_i/2. It controls the centered profile and its
first several derivatives. The expectation of the noise contribution is
constant in m because tr P_i=d. Thus the deterministic expected loss
relative to truth is exactly

$$E[L_N(m_0)-L_N(m)]
=\tfrac12\sum_i\|R_i(m)\mu_i\|^2.$$

For the conditional covariance, T54 writes its j,k entry as a sum of
quadratic forms in z_i. Its coefficient is obtained from

$$G_{ij}=R_iW_iD_jX_i^+P_i z_i,
\qquad (\mathcal C_N)_{jk}=\sum_iG_{ij}^TG_{ik},$$

where D_j is the fixed graph-design derivative. In the coefficient
R_iW_iD_jX_i^+P_i, the factors t_i^(-1/2) and t_i^(1/2) cancel.
Off-diagonal entries use the symmetric part of the quadratic coefficient,
which gives the same scalar quadratic form and the same derivative bounds.
On any fixed mass neighbourhood its coefficients and all required
derivatives are uniformly bounded over the compact V_i class. A cutoff
on a slightly larger neighbourhood gives the same Sobolev argument.
In particular C_N(m0)-E C_N(m0)=O_p(sqrt(b_N)).
Also sup||grad C_N(m)||=O_p(b_N), directly from the bounded coefficient
derivatives and sum_i ||z_i||^2=O_p(b_N).

## 4. Signal sensitivity, graph variability and fitted normalization

At truth let P_ij=partial_j P_i and D(t)=sum_j t_jD_j. Define

$$t^TA_Nt=\sum_i\|R_iW_iD(t)a_i\|^2,
\qquad (G_N)_{jk}=\tfrac12\sum_i\operatorname{tr}(P_{ij}P_{ik}),
\qquad B_N=A_N+G_N.$$

By T55's Gaussian moment calculation, E S_N=0,
-E grad^2 L_N=A_N, Cov(S_N)=B_N and E C_N(m0)=B_N.
The identity E C=B can also be checked by integrating the fitted latent
acceleration, whose own-event covariance is (C^T Sigma_i^-1 C)^-1.

Compactness of the V_i class gives constants c,C>0, independent of the
signal, scale and covariance allocations, with

$$c\Delta_N I_K\preceq A_N\preceq C\Delta_N I_K,
\qquad cNI_K\preceq G_N\preceq CNI_K,
\qquad cb_NI_K\preceq B_N\preceq Cb_NI_K.$$

For the A lower bound, R_i V_i^-1/2 D(t)a=0 would place D(t)a
in col C(m0). Its zero acceleration block forces that column coordinate
to vanish, and nonzero a,t then give a contradiction. Uniform comparison
follows by minimizing over the compact V class and unit a,t; the scale
t_i divides both this squared norm and ||mu_i||^2. For G, the same
injectivity applied to the full tangent map gives a positive graph metric
for every V, with a uniform positive lower bound by compactness. These
bounds do not require acceleration directions to span R^d: the physical
parameters here are scalar body masses, not a general matrix of inertia.

Distinct compactified channel directions have disjoint nonzero mean
planes. Uniformly over V_i in the compact shape class and over unit
acceleration directions, the expected profile gap outside any fixed
neighbourhood of m0 is at least c_delta Delta_N. Section 3's uniform
O_p(sqrt(b_N)) error is o_p(Delta_N), proving consistency of global
profile maxima. The expected local negative Hessian equals A_N at
truth, and its deterministic change nearby is O(Delta_N||m-m0||).
Uniform third-derivative bounds and the field control therefore give
strict local curvature at least c Delta_N I, throughout one fixed
convex neighbourhood, with probability tending to one. All global
maxima eventually lie there, so the maximum is unique and interior.

The score's O_p(sqrt(b_N)) size and the score equation now give

$$\widehat m_N-m_0=O_p(h_N^{-1}),\qquad
\widehat H_N=A_N+O_p(\sqrt{b_N}),\qquad
\mathcal C_N(m_0)-B_N=O_p(\sqrt{b_N}),$$

and the local covariance derivative bound gives

$$\widehat{\mathcal C}_N-B_N
=O_p(\sqrt{b_N})+O_p(b_N/h_N)=o_p(b_N).$$

There is no requirement that A_N/Delta_N or G_N/N converge.
The deterministic comparisons give relative consistency of the fitted
matrices and eigenvalues of the local sandwich width of order h_N^-2.

At truth, conditional on all own-event U_i, S_N is Gaussian with covariance
C_N(m0), exactly as in T54. For any nonempty experiment that covariance
is positive definite almost surely: each fitted latent acceleration has
a nonsingular Gaussian law, and one nonzero fit makes the physical
mass-tangent map injective. Hence

$$\mathcal C_N(m_0)^{-1/2}S_N(m_0)\sim N_K(0,I_K)$$

exactly, without a leverage-sensitive CLT. The score equation gives
Hhat_N(mhat_N-m0)=S_N(m0)+o_p(sqrt(b_N)); relative matrix consistency
then proves the displayed observed-standardized sampling limit.

## 5. Trace scale, full normalization and composite coverage

Even with changing covariances, the whitened errors are independent
standard Gaussians in the same dimension p. Therefore

$$E_N=\sum_i\|z_i\|^2\sim\chi'^2_{pN}(\Delta_N),
\quad E E_N=pN+\Delta_N,
\quad\operatorname{Var}(E_N)=2pN+4\Delta_N.$$

Thus E_N is comparable in probability to b_N and

$$\lambda_N\Delta_N\asymp_p h_N\to\infty,
\qquad \lambda_N\Delta_N/h_N^2=O_p(h_N^{-1})\to0.$$

For bounded t and m=mhat_N+M_N^-1 t, the local score expansion and
relative covariance consistency give T_N(m)^2=||t||^2+o_p(1), uniformly
on bounded t sets. The added profile log variation is
O_p(lambda_N Delta_N/h_N^2)=o_p(1). The prior is locally continuous and
positive, so the local normalized shape is standard Gaussian.

For full normalization, local strict curvature bounds the score norm
below by c Delta_N||m-mhat_N||. The upper bound C_N(m)≼C b_N I
then gives T_N(m)^2≥c h_N^2||m-mhat_N||^2 in a fixed neighbourhood.
The anchor relative to the global profile maximum is at most one. A
radius-1/h_N ball supplies normalizing mass of order at least
exp[lambda_N L_N(mhat_N)]h_N^-K, and the bounded local prior density
controls the remaining rescaled local tails by Gaussian tails.

Outside that neighbourhood the profile separation bounds posterior mass
by O_p(h_N^K) exp[-c lambda_N Delta_N], which vanishes because the
exponent is of order h_N. This argument uses only proper prior mass
outside the neighbourhood; no global prior density or moment restriction
is needed. It proves tightness and the unconditioned TV limit, including
the positive-domain tails rather than merely local curvature.

For any fixed smooth scalar composite with nonzero truth gradient,
relative consistency permits its standardized fitted direction to be
replaced by a deterministic one. Such directions may rotate with N:
the exactly standardized score is spherical Gaussian for every N.
The fitted-center expansion, TV limit and delta method therefore give
the matching central-quantile sampling coverage. These are pushforwards
of the same supplied joint law, not separately recalibrated marginals.

## Scope and remaining freedom

Common covariance was a proof convenience in T60, not a necessary
restriction for this construction: varying correlations, anisotropies
within a comparable shape class, and arbitrary overall precision changes
are now covered. No type frequencies, repeated signals, limiting design
matrix or maximum-leverage condition is hidden in the extension.

The compact shape condition is material to the uniform derivative and
information comparisons. The theorem does not cover unbounded relative
anisotropy, arbitrary candidate subspaces or protocols, cross-event
dependence, incorrectly declared noise, non-Gaussian errors, or generic
matrix-valued inertia. It does not make the growth condition necessary
in those broader classes or resolve L11's full-type contradiction.

Every fixed kappa>0 still gives the same stated asymptotic conclusions
while generally giving a different finite law. No choice of kappa,
finite anchoring formula, L19 state permission, joint prior coupling,
production estimator, physical input or ruling is selected here.
