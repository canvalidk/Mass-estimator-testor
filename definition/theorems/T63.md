# T63: Degenerating instrument shapes can defeat a total-energy anchor

**From:** T56's two positive score roots and continuum weights, T60's trace-anchor formula, and T61's distinction between covariance scale and shape.
**Test:** `tests/test_degenerate_geometry_anchor.py`; the underlying two-root quadrature in `tests/test_score_law_obstruction.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). A fixed, correctly declared Gaussian covariance history is identifiable from an increasing informative subsequence, yet the trace-anchored score law retains positive limiting probability at a false mass. The example lies outside T61's covariance-shape and sufficient growth conditions. It refutes an unrestricted-geometry extension of this research construction, not the existence of a mass definition or T61. No new premise, input or production rule is adopted.

## Standard mathematics and the distinction at issue

This is functional errors-in-variables estimation with heterogeneous known
measurement error. Total observation energy need not be comparable to
information about the regression parameter when instrument shapes
degenerate. T55's sensitivity and score variability are parameter-specific;
the trace of whitened observation scatter includes other directions too.
The following example separates these quantities within independent
Gaussian observations, without a signal population or incorrect covariances.

## 1. One fixed experiment, including its supplied prior

Fix one scalar body, true mass m0=6/5, and independent events

$$Y_i=(m_0 a_i,a_i)^T+\varepsilon_i,\qquad
\varepsilon_i\sim N_2(0,\Sigma_i).$$

At indices i=2^j, j=1,2,..., use informative events

$$a_i=1,\qquad \Sigma_i=\Sigma_0=
\begin{pmatrix}1&9/10\\9/10&1\end{pmatrix}.$$

At every other index use

$$a_i=0,\qquad \Sigma_i=\operatorname{diag}(16^i,1).$$

These are fixed deterministic declarations, not a distribution learned
from the readings. Every covariance is strictly positive definite.
Let M_N=floor(log_2 N) be the number of informative events in a prefix.
It is an analysis quantity, not supplied to the law.

For a compact positive domain I=[1/10,10], supply the fixed proper prior

$$\Pi(dm)=Z_I^{-1}\frac{2}{\pi(1+m^2)}\,1_I(m)\,dm.$$

This is the half-Cauchy at center one conditioned on a reciprocal interval.
It remains invariant under m -> 1/m and has positive continuous density
near m0 and the false mass mf=4/15. The truncation is fixed before data;
it retains both roots. It is a permissible supplied proper prior for the
research family, not a replacement for the production prior. It simplifies
the global argument without imposing a root-selection procedure.

Apply the unchanged finite trace-anchor construction, for any fixed kappa>0:

$$Q_N(dm)\propto\exp[-S_N(m)^2/(2C_N(m))+
\lambda_N L_N(m)]\Pi(dm),\qquad
\lambda_N=\kappa(1+E_N)^{-1/2},$$

with the same zero-rank convention as T60 and observed
E_N=sum_i Y_i^T Sigma_i^-1 Y_i. The fixed prior makes this a law on the
whole positive mass line with support I, rather than a conditional-on-data
choice of a basin.

## 2. The other events have summable parameter-dependent evidence

At a noninformative index, whiten with diag(4^-i,1). Its observation
Z_i is an independent standard Gaussian pair. Set e_i=4^-i and write

$$u_i(m)=\frac{(e_i m,1)^T}{\sqrt{1+e_i^2m^2}},\qquad
v_i(m)=\frac{(1,-e_i m)^T}{\sqrt{1+e_i^2m^2}},\qquad
b_i(m)=\frac{e_i}{1+e_i^2m^2}.$$

The event's profile, mass score and exact conditional covariance are

$$L_i(m)=-\tfrac12(v_i^T Z_i)^2,\qquad
S_i(m)=b_i(u_i^TZ_i)(v_i^TZ_i),\qquad
C_i(m)=b_i^2(u_i^TZ_i)^2.$$

For any fixed derivative order r and fixed reference mref in I, there are
constants independent of i such that the coefficient matrices of
L_i(m)-L_i(mref), S_i(m) and their first r derivatives have norm at most
c_r e_i uniformly on I. Those of C_i and its derivatives have norm at
most c_r e_i^2. These bounds follow directly by differentiating the
displayed rational functions on the fixed compact interval.

Since E||Z_i||^2=2 and sum_i e_i<infinity, Tonelli's theorem gives almost
sure absolute convergence in each fixed C^r norm of the summed centered
profile and score fields. The conditional covariance field likewise
converges, using sum_i e_i^2<infinity. Thus the noninformative prefix has

$$L_{B,N}(m)-L_{B,N}(m_{\rm ref})=O_{a.s.}(1),\qquad
S_{B,N}=O_{a.s.}(1),\qquad C_{B,N}=O_{a.s.}(1)$$

uniformly on I, including the derivatives needed below. The uncentered
profile itself has an order-N candidate-independent constant; it is not
claimed to be bounded. That constant cancels from the normalized law.

## 3. Total energy nevertheless disables the ordering term

The informative whitened signal energy is r0^2=28/19 per event. Therefore
the prefix trace has the exact noncentral chi-square law with 2N degrees
of freedom and noncentrality Delta_N=(28/19)M_N. Its first two moments give

$$E_N/(2N)\to1\quad\hbox{in probability},\qquad
\lambda_N\asymp_p N^{-1/2},\qquad \lambda_N M_N\to0.$$

The informative profile has candidate-dependent oscillation O_p(M_N)
on I; the other profile contrast is bounded by section 2. Consequently

$$\sup_{m\in I}\left|\lambda_N
[L_N(m)-L_N(m_{\rm ref})]\right|\to0$$

in probability. On this experiment the added profile multiplier becomes
uniformly constant over the entire prior support. It cannot suppress the
false score root. This is about many nonzero noisy observations, not about
appending measured exact-zero events, which the construction ignores.

## 4. Both continuum score peaks survive

The informative subsequence is exactly T56's iid Gaussian experiment.
Its expected profile has a unique maximum at m0 and a minimum at mf=4/15,
and its population mass score has exactly these two simple zeros on I.
Its summed score and conditional covariance are of order M_N, with
conditional covariance uniformly positive after division by M_N.

Section 2 perturbs the informative score and its derivatives by O_a.s.(1).
Each simple observed informative score root therefore moves by
O_p(1/M_N). This is negligible relative to either peak's O_p(M_N^-1/2)
width. The conditional covariance perturbation is also negligible relative
to M_N. On each rescaled root neighbourhood the full statistic thus has
the same quadratic expansion as the informative-only statistic. Away
from both root neighbourhoods its order-M_N separation bounds give the
usual two-root Laplace tail control. The compact support excludes any
additional remote-tail issue. Section 3 changes none of the limiting
normalized weights.

The two density factors are those in T56: angular prior density times
the square root of the corresponding whitened scatter eigenvalue,
divided by their common gap. Conditioning the prior on I multiplies both
factors by the same constant. Thus, in sampling probability,

$$Q_N\Rightarrow(1-\rho)\delta_{6/5}+\rho\delta_{4/15},\qquad
\rho=\frac{R}{1+R}=0.753554709424963\ldots,\qquad
R=\frac{1159/241}{\sqrt{47/19}}.$$

The weak convergence means convergence of integrals of every bounded
continuous test function in probability. In particular, disjoint fixed
small neighbourhoods of the two masses receive the displayed limiting
probabilities. Since rho>1/2, the law's median converges to mf, not m0.
The exact candidate conditional-score test retains its coverage; it is
the normalized full law that fails to concentrate at the true mass.

## 5. Identification is available; total trace is the failing comparison

The declared covariances identify the informative indices before data.
Applying the ordinary profile estimator only to that subsequence is
consistent as M_N tends to infinity. Its sensitivity, variability and
information are all of order M_N, up to fixed positive constants; the
summable fields from the other events do not change that order. Hence
this experiment supplies increasing information about mass despite
Delta_N^2/(N+Delta_N) tending to zero.

There is no contradiction with T61: normalized covariance shapes here
have unbounded condition numbers, and its sufficient information-growth
condition also fails. Nor is this a new impossibility theorem for
heterogeneous instruments or a necessity claim about that condition.
It is a counterexample to extending the *same total-energy anchoring
rule* to all declared positive-definite geometries merely because some
increasing subsequence identifies mass.

What it names is a missing comparison between parameter-specific
information and ambient observation energy. A wider construction would
need an argument for that comparison or a different invariant state;
discarding inconvenient readings, choosing a successful prefactor or
moving an unspecified tuning parameter into the input is not derived
here. The family/authority questions remain G24/G26/G27.
