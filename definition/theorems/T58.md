# T58: A fixed power anchor can destroy calibration on an identifying history

**From:** T57's specified anchored-score law, T42/T53's Gaussian information scale, and T43's allocation-free Gaussian sampling limit. The new regime is one fixed deterministic sequence of progressively weaker signals, not repeated fixed-size blocks.
**Test:** `tests/test_spectral_anchor.py`; `tests/test_weak_anchor_study.py`; the score-law geometry checks in `tests/test_score_law_obstruction.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). Every fixed research exponent 0<alpha<1 admits an allowed identifying Gaussian history on which the T57 law remains consistent but its equal-tailed interval coverage tends to zero. This does not contradict T57's repeated-block theorem, reject anchoring generally, select another schedule or change a ruling.

## 1. An allowed fixed history with attainable information growth

Use independent scalar readings in identity noise, one fixed interior true
angle theta0 in (0,pi/2), and any fixed proper prior whose restriction to
a neighbourhood of truth has a positive continuous angular Lebesgue density,
with no additional singular component there. In particular the unchanged half-Cauchy with
center 1 has uniform angular density. Write

$$Y_i=r_i u_{\theta_0}+Z_i,\qquad
u_\theta=(\sin\theta,\cos\theta),\qquad Z_i\sim N(0,I_2),$$

with deterministic positive amplitudes and independent Gaussian errors.
For a fixed beta in (1/2,1), take the **one infinite sequence**

$$r_i^2=i^{\beta-1},\qquad
\Delta_N=\sum_{i\le N}r_i^2\sim N^\beta/\beta.$$

The true force and acceleration are r_i*sin(theta0) and r_i*cos(theta0),
so they obey one fixed positive mass. The information benchmark diverges:

$$\mathcal I_N=\frac{\Delta_N^2}{N+\Delta_N}
\sim\frac{N^{2\beta-1}}{\beta^2}\longrightarrow\infty.$$

This is within T53's attainable Gaussian energy regime. The example does
not use a nonidentifying history or a nuisance distribution. Its signal
sizes are neither provided to the law nor estimated as a population.

Let F_N=sum_i Y_iY_i^T have eigenvalues lambda_+>=lambda_-, gap H_N,
and principal angle phihat_N. T42/T43 give

$$\widehat\phi_N\to\theta_0,\qquad H_N/\Delta_N\to1,\qquad
\lambda_+/N\to1,\qquad\lambda_-/N\to1,$$

in probability, and the allocation-free center limit

$$\frac{H_N}{\sqrt{\lambda_+}}
(\widehat\phi_N-\theta_0)\Rightarrow N(0,1).$$

With probability tending to one, the principal line is interior positive
and its orthogonal line is outside the positive angular domain. There is
no false positive-mass stationary branch in this example.

## 2. Exact anchored criterion and its normalized Gaussian shape

Let delta=theta-phihat_N. The scalar conditional score statistic and
profile deviance are exactly

$$T_N(\theta)^2=
\frac{H_N^2\sin^2\delta\cos^2\delta}
{\lambda_+\cos^2\delta+\lambda_-\sin^2\delta},\qquad
L_N(\widehat\phi_N)-L_N(\theta)=\frac{H_N}{2}\sin^2\delta.$$

For any nonnegative finite data-dependent anchor lambda_N, the proposed
law relative to its value at the fitted angle has exponent

$$-D_N(\delta)=-\tfrac12T_N(\theta)^2
-\tfrac12\lambda_NH_N\sin^2\delta.$$

Its exact local precision is

$$K_N=K_{G,N}+\lambda_NH_N,\qquad
K_{G,N}=H_N^2/\lambda_+.$$

K_G is the calibrated Gaussian sampling precision of the fitted angle;
the second term is extra concentration introduced by the anchor.
Both are local width benchmarks, not finite-law inverse variances.

**Full-law Gaussian statement.** Under the preceding identifying regime,
the normalized anchored law of sqrt(K_N)*(theta-phihat_N) converges in
total variation, in probability, to N(0,1). In particular this is not just
a comparison of second derivatives or a law conditioned to a small box.

To prove it, consistency of phihat keeps the entire positive angular
domain a fixed distance from its orthogonal line, with probability tending
to one. Thus cos^2(delta) is bounded below there. Since the denominator
of T^2 is at most lambda_+, elementary sine bounds give constants c>0
such that, throughout the domain,

$$D_N(\delta)\ge c K_N\delta^2.$$

For bounded h and delta=h/sqrt(K_N), Taylor expansion gives

$$D_N(\delta)=\tfrac12K_N\delta^2+O(K_N\delta^4)
=h^2/2+o_p(1).$$

The remainder bound is uniform: H_N/lambda_+<=1, and the two nonnegative
precision contributions are each at most K_N. Here K_N>=K_G,N tends
to infinity. A root-K_N neighbourhood supplies a normalizer of order
K_N^(-1/2). The global quadratic bound and the locally bounded prior
density control its rescaled tails; the proper prior controls the region
away from truth. Completing the local normalization proves the TV claim.
It applies even when the anchor dominates K_G.

The law therefore concentrates at theta0 and every median does too.
The same statements hold after the monotone mass conversion m=tan(theta).

## 3. The coverage penalty is determined by a precision ratio

Define

$$q_N=\frac{\lambda_NH_N}{K_{G,N}}
=\lambda_N\frac{\lambda_+}{H_N}.$$

For any fixed central probability p in (0,1), equal-tailed interval
endpoints are phihat_N +/- z_p/sqrt(K_N)+o_p(K_N^(-1/2)). Combining
the full-law quantile result with the sampling CLT gives, if q_N tends
in probability to q in [0,infinity], limiting coverage

$$2\Phi\!\left(\frac{z_p}{\sqrt{1+q}}\right)-1,
\qquad z_p=\Phi^{-1}((1+p)/2).$$

If q_N tends to infinity the coverage tends to zero. Indeed the interval's
sampling-standardized half-width tends to zero, whereas its standardized
center error tends to a continuous nondegenerate normal distribution.
The smaller posterior endpoint remainders do not alter this conclusion.
Correct median consistency does not imply correct uncertainty width.

## 4. Every fixed power exponent has such a failure

For T57's power family, lambda_N=(1+R_N)^(-alpha), where R_N is the
profile oscillation over positive mass and 0<alpha<1 is fixed. When the
principal angle is positive, its oscillation is exactly

$$R_N=\frac{H_N}{4}(1+|\cos(2\widehat\phi_N)|)
\sim\kappa_{\theta_0}\Delta_N,\qquad
\kappa_{\theta_0}=\frac{1+|\cos(2\theta_0)|}{4}>0.$$

Consequently the extra-to-correct precision ratio is

$$q_N\sim\kappa_{\theta_0}^{-\alpha}
\frac{N}{\Delta_N^{1+\alpha}}
\sim\kappa_{\theta_0}^{-\alpha}\beta^{1+\alpha}
N^{1-\beta(1+\alpha)}.$$

For **every** alpha in (0,1), the interval

$$\frac12<\beta<\frac1{1+\alpha}$$

is nonempty. Choosing any such fixed beta makes I_N diverge and q_N
diverge simultaneously. The entire normalized law still concentrates at
truth, but its nominal central intervals cover with probability tending
to zero. The score covariance and profile center are observable; no true
signal information has been supplied to obtain this counterexample.

For example, set alpha=1/2, beta=3/5 and theta0=pi/4. Then r_i^2=i^(-2/5),
I_N is of order N^(1/5), and q_N is of order N^(1/10). The actual center
error has order N^(-1/10), whereas the anchored law's width has order
N^(-3/20). This is one fixed allowed history, not a different triangular
array selected anew at every sample size.

An alternative fixed history, useful for exact finite-N checks, is
r_i^2=i^beta-(i-1)^beta. It has Delta_N=N^beta exactly and is asymptotic
to beta*i^(beta-1). All preceding exponent comparisons and the coverage-zero
conclusion are unchanged; only their fixed multiplicative constants change.
It allows an exact sufficient-scatter Gaussian simulation without storing
the entire history, but the law receives no true-energy input.

At beta>1/(1+alpha), the ratio instead tends to zero and this particular
penalty disappears. At equality it tends to a finite positive constant,
giving the strictly subnominal coverage in section 3. Thus T57's repeated
fixed-signal limit, which has Delta_N proportional to N, remains valid;
it does not imply calibration throughout the larger attainable regime.

## 5. The two rates an anchor has to separate

The obstruction identifies two distinct scales. In T56's correlated
two-root geometry the profile height difference is of order Delta_N.
Under a locally negligible anchor, suppressing its persistent false branch
requires lambda_N*Delta_N to diverge. Preserving the regular true-root
Godambe width requires

$$\lambda_N\frac{N+\Delta_N}{\Delta_N}\longrightarrow0.$$

The second statement follows directly from the exact added-to-calibrated
precision ratio lambda_N*lambda_+/H_N. The first follows from the false
to true Laplace-weight factor exp[-lambda_N*H_N/2]; if the exponent stays
bounded on a subsequence, the false branch keeps nonzero limiting mass.
The two-root argument assumes its fixed interior roots and regular prior
weights, not arbitrary degenerate score sets.

The corresponding asymptotic scale window is

$$\frac1{\Delta_N}\ll\lambda_N\ll
\frac{\Delta_N}{N+\Delta_N}.$$

Such a window exists exactly when Delta_N^2/(N+Delta_N) diverges.
For example its geometric-middle order is (N+Delta_N)^(-1/2).
These are theorem scales, not new caller inputs or a selected implementable
rule. The profile oscillation tracks Delta_N here, while the score width
also reads the noise contribution of N; a fixed power of oscillation need
not separate those scales on every allowed signal history.

## Sort

The power family remains a conditional existence result in T57's stated
regular regime. Its finite symmetry and properness properties do not
extend that calibration result to every attainable Gaussian history.
This theorem exposes a rate requirement on that particular family; it
does not prove that no anchor or no globally calibrated joint law exists.
No exponent, experiment state, prior, new physical input, production
implementation, consistency contract or ruling is adopted.
