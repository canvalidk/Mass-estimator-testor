# T54: A physical joint Gaussian mass law with locally calibrated composition

**From:** O2's existing shared-observation bridge, G27's joint-protocol obligation, T45/T50's Gaussian graph profile and conditional scores. The physical experiment, supplied proper joint prior and Gaussian profile operation are specified below.
**Test:** `tests/test_joint_mass.py`; `tests/test_joint_limit.py`; the conditional-score integration in `tests/test_joint_sandwich.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). A concrete conditional construction is proper and exact-zero neutral, retains shared acceleration covariance, and gives calibrated joint and composite uncertainty in a fixed-protocol small-noise limit. It does not select the joint prior, extend the production type, assert generic summed-refit equality or establish full-regime calibration.

## 1. One observation of the shared acceleration

Let K bodies have positive scalar masses m=(m_1,...,m_K). In each of
n fixed physical events they share one true acceleration a_i in R^d.
Use one common spatial frame within the event. Its unique observation is

$$Y_i=(F_{1i};\ldots;F_{Ki};A_i)\in\mathbb R^{(K+1)d},\qquad
C(m)=\begin{pmatrix}m_1I_d\\\vdots\\m_KI_d\\I_d\end{pmatrix}.$$

Declare independent Gaussian event errors with covariance epsilon^2*Sigma_i,
where Sigma_i is fixed positive definite and can contain arbitrary within-event
correlations. Each event has its own arbitrary fixed a_i; no distribution of
accelerations or equality across events is declared. The mean is C(m)a_i.
The shared acceleration is part of this event's physical declaration, not a
signal inferred from another event.

This is the rectangular graph [M;I_d] with M=[m_1I_d;...;m_KI_d]. It is
a K-parameter physical subfamily, not an unrestricted matrix relation.
Duplicating A_i into K separate body pairs would require the copied joint
covariance and would make that enlarged covariance singular. Treating those
copies as independent changes the experiment. This is already proved in
[O2, "Physical composition and measurement coarsening"](../../problems/O2.md#physical-composition-and-measurement-coarsening-codex-28-oct).

Write Omega_i=Sigma_i^-1 and define

$$E_i(m)=\Omega_i-\Omega_i C(m)
[C(m)^T\Omega_i C(m)]^{-1}C(m)^T\Omega_i,$$

$$k_i(m)=\min_x\|Y_i-C(m)x\|_{\Omega_i}^2=Y_i^TE_i(m)Y_i.$$

Supply a proper **joint** mass prior Pi on (0,infinity)^K and set

$$Q_\epsilon(dm\mid Y)=
\frac{\exp[-\sum_i k_i(m)/(2\epsilon^2)]\Pi(dm)}
{\int\exp[-\sum_i k_i(t)/(2\epsilon^2)]\Pi(dt)}.$$

The kernel is strictly positive and at most one, so this is a proper joint
law for every finite observation and epsilon>0. An appended whole event
with observed Y_i=0 has k_i(m)=0 for all m and leaves Q exactly unchanged
at fixed Pi. At all-zero data Q=Pi. Fixed invertible bookkeeping changes,
with C and covariance transformed accordingly, preserve each residual cost.

This operation profiles the free accelerations; it does not integrate a
signal population. An improper latent integration measure, as in T48,
must be specified separately if that different construction is proposed.
Independent product mass laws arise from a separate, independent protocol
with separately free accelerations and a product prior. Independent force
instrument errors alone do not factorize the shared-acceleration law.

## 2. An exact conditional candidate test is available

In this paragraph whiten using W_i=(epsilon^2*Sigma_i)^(-1/2), so
z_i=W_iY_i and B_i=W_iC(m). Let P_i project onto B_i,
R_i=I-P_i, U_i=P_i z_i and V_i=R_i z_i.
For the jth mass tangent put D_j=[0;...;I_d;...;0], with its nonzero
block in force channel j and zero in the acceleration channel, and

$$G_{ij}=R_i(\epsilon^2\Sigma_i)^{-1/2}D_jB_i^+U_i,
\qquad s_j=\sum_i V_i^TG_{ij},\qquad
\mathcal C_{jk}=\sum_i G_{ij}^TG_{ik}.$$

T50's projector derivative proof gives $s\mid(U_i)_i\sim N_K(0,\mathcal C)$
at the true candidate. If any fitted acceleration B_i^+U_i is nonzero,
the covariance is positive definite: a null direction h would require
D(h)*ahat_i in col(C(m));
its zero acceleration block forces the column coefficient to zero, and
h_j*ahat_i=0 for every j then forces h=0. In a nonempty Gaussian
experiment a fitted acceleration is nonzero almost surely, even at true
null signal. Hence $s^T\mathcal C^{-1}s$ has the exact conditional chi-square_K law.

This provides candidate tests without a size population. It does not turn
their inversion into a proper law, prove identification from conditional
rank, or identify Q with an exact finite-sample confidence distribution.
The proper law in section 1 and the exact test are distinct constructions.

## 3. A fixed-event small-noise theorem

Fix K,d,n, the covariance shapes Sigma_i, an interior positive true vector
m_0, and deterministic accelerations a_i with at least one a_i nonzero.
The world's observations are

$$Y_{i,\epsilon}=C(m_0)a_i+\epsilon Z_i,
\qquad Z_i\stackrel{\rm ind}{\sim}N(0,\Sigma_i),
\qquad \epsilon\downarrow0.$$

Fix Pi independently of epsilon. Assume it has a Lebesgue density that is
positive and continuous near m_0, with no additional singular prior
component there.
Define the ((K+1)d)-by-K mean derivative D_i by putting a_i in force
block j of column j and zero in its other blocks. Put

$$\mathcal I=\sum_i D_i^TE_i(m_0)D_i.$$

Then I is positive definite. Indeed h^T I h is a sum of squared whitened
transverse mean derivatives. If it vanishes, D_i h belongs to col(C(m_0))
for every event. Its zero acceleration block forces the corresponding
column coefficient to zero. For an event with a_i nonzero this gives
h_j a_i=0 for every j, so h=0.

**Conclusion.** Q_epsilon concentrates at m_0. A unique interior profile
maximizer exists with probability tending to one. Define mhat_epsilon there
and use any fixed positive fallback on the exceptional event. Then

$$\epsilon^{-1}(\widehat m_\epsilon-m_0)
\ \Rightarrow\ N_K(0,\mathcal I^{-1}),$$

and, in probability, its centered joint law satisfies

$$\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
Q_\epsilon\{(m-\widehat m_\epsilon)/\epsilon\in\cdot\mid Y\},
N_K(0,\mathcal I^{-1})\right)\longrightarrow0.$$

Here no true acceleration is passed to Q; I^-1 is the covariance of the
local Gaussian limit. The fitted Hessian of (1/2)sum k_i tends to I;
its inverse times epsilon^2 estimates the local width benchmark.
This is a fixed finite-dimensional small-noise theorem, not an arbitrary
long-series claim with incidental signals. By homogeneity it also covers
fixed-event common amplification a_i -> R*a_i at fixed noise, with epsilon=1/R.

These are distributional and quantile claims, not convergence of moments.
At fixed data, 0<=sum k_i(m)<=sum Y_i^T Omega_i Y_i uniformly in m.
The likelihood is therefore bounded below by a strictly positive constant
for every fixed epsilon>0. Any infinite nonnegative prior moment remains
infinite in Q_epsilon; half-Cauchy priors can leave its covariance undefined
at every finite noise level. No finite-law or sampling-moment convergence
is inferred from the local Gaussian limit.

### Proof of concentration and the local Gaussian statement

Compactify the positive mass chart using
u(m)=(m_1,...,m_K,1)/sqrt(1+||m||^2). Its closed positive spherical
orthant is compact, and the graph planes u tensor R^d vary continuously
there, also after each fixed whitening. For noiseless data, the profile
cost is zero only at u_0: a nonzero vector (m_0,1) tensor a_i belongs
to u tensor R^d only when u=u_0. Thus the noiseless cost has a positive
gap outside every neighbourhood of m_0, including at chart boundaries.
Finite Gaussian perturbations change the cost uniformly by o_p(1).
A width-epsilon neighbourhood of m_0 has prior mass of order epsilon^K
and kernel at least exp[-O_p(1)]; the outside cost gap dominates this
normalizer exponentially. This proves concentration.

For bounded h, expand m=m_0+epsilon*h and the fitted latent vector as
x_i=a_i+epsilon*b_i. Minimizing the leading residual
||Z_i-D_i h-C(m_0)b_i||_Omega_i^2 gives the uniform local expansion

$$\log L_\epsilon(m_0+\epsilon h)-\log L_\epsilon(m_0)
=h^T\Delta-\tfrac12h^T\mathcal I h+o_p(1),\qquad
\Delta=\sum_iD_i^TE_i(m_0)Z_i.$$

E_i Sigma_i E_i=E_i, so $\Delta\sim N_K(0,\mathcal I)$ exactly. The limiting
quadratic is strictly concave. Locally the Hessian of the unscaled cost
(1/2)sum k_i is positive definite and converges uniformly to its noiseless
counterpart. Together
with the global gap this controls the rescaled posterior tails. Completing
the square and using the prior's local continuity gives the stated total
variation Gaussian limit. The profile estimating equation gives
mhat=m_0+epsilon*I^-1 Delta+o_p(epsilon), proving the sampling limit.

## 4. Shared-error covariance and composition

For one event set T_m=[I_(Kd), -m tensor I_d] and H_a=I_K tensor a.
The constraint residual has covariance Gamma=T_m Sigma T_m^T.
The residual precision identity E=T_m^T Gamma^-1 T_m gives

$$\mathcal I=H_a^T\Gamma^{-1}H_a.$$

With independent isotropic force variances v_j I_d and one acceleration
variance v_a I_d this gives the explicit leading mass covariance

$$V_m=\mathcal I^{-1}
=\frac{\operatorname{diag}(v_1,\ldots,v_K)+v_a m_0m_0^T}{\|a\|^2}.$$

The local Gaussian width covariance is epsilon^2*V_m. Its off-diagonal
entries retain the shared acceleration error. For M=sum_j m_j,

$$\operatorname{Var}_{\rm local}(M)/\epsilon^2
=\frac{\sum_jv_j+v_a M_0^2}{\|a\|^2}.$$

This equals the one-event coarse Gaussian calculation from (sum_j F_j,A)
with its correctly propagated covariance. It is a local one-event result,
not generic equality of the fine pushforward and a summed-data refit.
O2's omitted-contrast factor remains present at finite noise; heterogeneous
multi-event protocols can give different fine and coarse precision.

For every smooth scalar g with nonzero gradient at m_0, section 3 and
the delta method give the same local Gaussian sampling and quoted width,
with variance benchmark epsilon^2*grad(g)^T I^-1 grad(g).
Thus equal-tailed intervals of the
actual pushforward g_#Q_epsilon have asymptotic nominal coverage at each
fixed probability level in (0,1). This includes the sum and parallel sum
g(m)=(sum_j 1/m_j)^(-1). It does not make their medians additive.

The common-force dual uses unique observations (A_1;...;A_K;F), shared
fixed force f_i and inverse masses mu_j=1/m_j. Replace m by mu and
the shared acceleration by force in the same graph theorem. Propagate
the supplied joint prior through reciprocal coordinates; the reduced mass
is then 1/(sum_j mu_j). This is the declared dual physical experiment,
not an inverse of the rectangular operator M. A new composite measurement
has its own declared covariance and regular-prior/noise regime; coverage
consistency does not demand equality to the earlier fine-data law.

## 5. What has and has not been constructed

Shared observation identity and the declared physical relations determine
the graph and covariance propagation. Given one joint law, sum and
reciprocal composition are its ordinary pushforwards. The joint prior's
coupling, profiling versus latent integration, and a calibration operation
outside section 3's regime are further choices. Marginal priors or two
separately calibrated one-body laws do not determine those choices.

The numerical reference uses supplied finite joint prior atoms to check
profiles, covariance and pushforwards; those proper atomic laws are not
asserted to satisfy the continuous-prior Gaussian limit. The local
information helper is a research reference with explicit true signals,
not an input to the joint update. No population is learned and no current
one-body output, type slot, prior or production default is replaced.
