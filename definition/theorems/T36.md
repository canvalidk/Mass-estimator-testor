## Statement

**(1) The metric of a reading is forced, and it is the noise.** A reading lives in the stacked
space $W_i=\mathbb R^{k_i}\oplus\mathbb R^{k_i}$ (force components, acceleration components, inside
the declared subspace $N_i$). Its axes carry N and m s⁻²; there is no shared unit, so space's
metric $\delta$ (which the 10 Oct record found hidden in the one length unit, his "we won't need
tensors") supplies no length on $W_i$. Among the declared objects (the type), the only bilinear
form that makes $\lVert\mathbf y\rVert^2$ dimensionless is $\Sigma_i^{-1}$. So the metric of $W_i$
is $\Sigma_i^{-1}$, restricted to the span of $N_i$ in both channels. L9's "isotropy in noise
units" is then not a convenience: noise units are the only units in which a reading has a length.

**(2) A mass is a subspace; dividing is projecting.** Under L1 with L22, the hypothesis "the mass
is $m$" is the $k_i$-dimensional subspace
$$L_i(m)=\{(m\,N_i\alpha,\ N_i\alpha):\alpha\in\mathbb R^{k_i}\}\subset W_i,$$
the column space of `equation.md`'s $C(\phi)$, $m=c\tan\phi$ (the whole projective line: the
lines of the $(F,a)$ plane, tensored with $\mathbb R^{k_i}$). The explained energy $H_i(m)$ is the
squared length of the reading's orthogonal projection onto $L_i(m)$ in the noise metric, $E_i$ its
squared length in the span, and
$$K_i(m)=E_i-H_i(m)=\min_{\alpha}\lVert\mathbf y_i-(mN_i\alpha,N_i\alpha)\rVert^2_{\Sigma_i^{-1}}$$
is the squared length of what is left over. For the full subspace,
$K_i(m)=\mathbf r^\top V(m)^{-1}\mathbf r$ with $\mathbf r=\tilde{\mathbf F}-m\tilde{\mathbf a}$ and
$V(m)=[I,-mI]\,\Sigma_i\,[I,-mI]^\top$: "$F/a$" read as "the residual of $F$ against $m\,a$ in the
metric the noise defines". Cross-channel correlation needs no separate treatment: it is the
off-diagonal block of the metric.

**(3) The misfit is the least residual.** $Q_i=\min_m K_i(m)=E_i-\max H_i$ (T32), the residual
when the reading picks its own line. The raw law is $\pi(m)\exp(-\tfrac12\sum_iK_i(m))$ up to a
constant, so the law already reads **one residual**, $K(m)=\sum_iK_i(m)$, at a common $m$.

**(4) The dimension count.** $W_i$ has dimension $2k_i$ and $L_i(m)$ has $k_i$, so the residual
has $k_i$ dimensions per reading. If the noise is $v\,\Sigma_i$ (Gaussian, L13):

- at the true mass, $K(m_0)/v\sim\chi^2_{\sum k_i}$, exactly, at every signal strength;
- each reading at its own best line: $k_i-1=\nu_i$ dimensions (the line is one more fitted
  parameter); this is T30's $\nu$;
- one common mass fitted: $\sum_ik_i-1$ at strong signal; a mass model with $p$ parameters,
  $\sum_ik_i-p$ (scalar 1, symmetric tensor $k(k+1)/2$: O17's isotropy table is this count).

So the readings' disagreement with each other about $m$ (η's channel) and each reading's
misalignment (the $Q$ channel of 26 Oct) are **two components of one residual vector**. Splitting
them and fitting a line per reading first, as the 26 Oct pooled scale $\sum Q_i/\sum\nu_i$ did,
fits $N$ incidental parameters: that is the low bias Codex derived (O14, 27 Oct), the
Neyman–Scott incidental-parameter bias, a dimension count and not a property of the noise.

## Proof or support

(1): the type's declared objects carrying force or acceleration units are $\Sigma_i$ (and the
window, which carries time only); a dimensionless quadratic form on $W_i$ must pair each
channel with the inverse of its own units, which $\Sigma_i^{-1}$ does and $\delta$ cannot. An
argument, Claude's, from his 10 Oct conjecture; not a uniqueness proof (any declared object with
the right units would do; the type has no other).

(2)–(3): $H_i=\mathbf b^\top G^{-1}\mathbf b$ with $G=C^\top\Sigma^{-1}C$, $\mathbf b=C^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf y$
is the squared norm of the $\Sigma^{-1}$-orthogonal projection onto the column space of $C$. The
closed form for the full subspace is the standard errors-in-variables profile: $\mathbf r$ is
linear in the noise with covariance $V(m)$. Checked against `met.full._h_terms` and
`met.full.reading_state` on random readings, $k=1,2,3$, correlated $\Sigma$.

(4): at $m_0$, the residual is the projection of the noise onto the $k_i$-dimensional orthogonal
complement of $L_i(m_0)$ in $W_i$. Checked by simulation. **The profiled common mass is not
exact:** at weak signal fitting the one $m$ takes more than one dimension. Seeded, 3D, true $v=1$,
no signal: the one residual over $(Nk-1)$ averages 0.73, 0.85, 0.93 at $N=5,20,100$; the per-reading
plug-in stays at 0.50 for every $N$ (Codex's exact null value in 3D). At signal $r=1$: 0.84, 0.94,
0.99 against 0.58. Defined in 1D, where the plug-in is not ($\nu=0$).

## What it does not show

- **What η and $g_i$ are for once a noise scale is in the model.** If $\Sigma$ is declared up to a
  scale (O7's branch), the scale's information is $K$, which is η's and $Q$'s channels at once;
  at least one of η's jobs is then taken over. Open (O14).
- **The measure on $L_i(m)$.** The noise metric induces a volume on $L_i(m)$ that depends on $m$;
  proof step 3's flat candidate weight and π's shape meet here (O6). Not examined.
- **Integrating $m$ rather than profiling it** (what the law does) may change the weak-signal
  bias in (4). Unchecked.
- **A declared subspace and the noise scale.** (4) counts inside the span of $N_i$, which is
  all ours30 needs: data outside it carry nothing about $m$. They are pure noise, so on O7's
  "up to a scale" branch they carry $2(d_i-k_i)$ more dimensions of information about the scale
  ($2d_i-k_i$ residual dimensions per reading in all). Brief B11 checks it.
- **The positive half.** Everything above is on the whole projective line; L1's $m>0$ (ours30's
  $Q_+$) cuts $L_i(m)$ to $m>0$ and the dimension count at the boundary is O13's $\kappa=k-\tfrac12$.
