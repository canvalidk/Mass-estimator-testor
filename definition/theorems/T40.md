## Standard mathematics and scope

Sandwich calibration of generalized/composite likelihoods distinguishes a
magnitude adjustment from a curvature adjustment. See
[Ribatet, Cooley and Davison (2012), sections 2.1–2.3](https://www3.stat.sinica.edu.tw/statistica/oldpdf/A22n217.pdf)
and [Shaby (2014), sections 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/1204.3687).
The following uses scalar deviance transformations, regular local Gaussian
limits, and comparison of normalized measures. The two operators and the
dual-limit argument here are mathematical research witnesses.

**Authority distinction.** R-2026-09-29-4 asks that uncertainty remain a true
statement. It does not itself demand an entire Gaussian limiting law or exact
coverage at every level. We study that stronger, conventional regular-regime
benchmark explicitly. If the retained cap gives
$\eta_\infty=\min(1,C/J)=1<C/J$, the limit of a linear η correction is
conservative relative to the full sandwich variance; it is not exact sandwich
calibration. The exact-sandwich statements below concern cases where the cap
leaves C/J unchanged, including C/J = 1.

## 1. What a pointwise deviance map can and cannot do

Let $L(m)=\sum_i\ell_i(m)$, let $\widehat L$ be its actual maximum on the
whole projective line, and write $D(m)=\widehat L-L(m)\ge0$ on positive mass.
Replacing $L$ by $-F(D)$ changes no candidate planes; it changes their relative
mass-law weights. The law studied is
$P_F(dm)\propto\exp[-F(D(m))]\pi(dm)$. No derivative/Jacobian of F belongs in
this weighting operation: it is not a change of the mass variable.

Suppose $F_\eta(0)=0$, $F_\eta'(0)=\eta$, and F is continuous. At the fitted
stationary point the corrected curvature is $\eta\mathcal H$. This statement
is local. Under a regular quadratic limit, deviance on the $N^{-1/2}$ scale
is $D_N(h)\to h^2/2$, **not zero**. A fixed nonlinear map therefore has local
kernel $\exp[-F_\eta(h^2/2)]$ rather than a Gaussian merely because its
derivative at zero is η.
Here the true mass is interior and the fixed prior has a positive continuous
density there, so it is asymptotically constant on this local scale.

If the entire local law is required to equal the linear-correction Gaussian,
equality of the positive continuous densities implies

$$F_\eta(h^2/2)=\eta h^2/2+b\quad\hbox{for every }h.$$

The value at zero makes b = 0. Since $h^2/2$ ranges over $[0,\infty)$,
$F_\eta(d)=\eta d$ for every finite d. At fixed $0<\eta<1$ this is
incompatible with the tail-preservation condition $F_\eta(d)/d\to1$.

**Conclusion, within this class:** a single fixed function of scalar D and η
cannot both give that full Gaussian local benchmark and retain the raw
far-deviance rate. A data-dependent additional scale, a different class of
calibration such as a parameter transport, or a weaker calibration target is
needed. This is not a theorem against all possible corrections.

## 2. Two tail-preserving semigroups, still not a unique rule

For $a>0$, $0<\eta\le1$ and $d\ge0$, define

$$F^{\rm L}_{\eta,a}(d)=a\log[1+\eta(e^{d/a}-1)],$$

$$F^{\rm W}_{\eta,a}(d)=aW_0[\eta(d/a)e^{d/a}],$$

where $W_0$ is the nonnegative real Lambert W branch. These are conjugations
of multiplication by η through respectively $h(x)=e^x-1$ and $h(x)=xe^x$.
Consequently, **with a fixed**,

$$F_{1,a}=\operatorname{id},\qquad
F_{\eta,a}\circ F_{\kappa,a}=F_{\eta\kappa,a}.$$

This composition identity does not say that a may be recomputed after an
intermediate transformation. Nor is semigroup composition a newly ruled premise.

Both maps are increasing and convex, and satisfy

$$F(0)=0,\quad F'(0)=\eta,\quad
\eta d\le F(d)\le d,\quad
0\le d-F(d)\le a|\log\eta|,$$

$$F(d)-d\longrightarrow a\log\eta\quad(d\to\infty).$$

For the logarithmic map these follow directly from its formula and convexity
of the exponential. For the W map put $x=d/a$, $w=W_0(\eta xe^x)$. The relation
$we^w=\eta xe^x$ gives $\eta x\le w\le x$ and
$w-x=\log\eta+\log(x/w)\ge\log\eta$. Its derivative is
$w'(x)=w(1+x)/[x(1+w)]$. For η < 1 its logarithmic derivative is

$$\frac{d}{dx}\log w'(x)=
\frac{(x-w)(2+x+w)}{x(1+x)(1+w)^2}>0.$$

Thus it is convex. Finally w/x tends to 1, giving the common tail offset.
The expansions at zero are nevertheless different:

$$F^{\rm L}_{\eta,a}(d)=\eta d+
\frac{\eta(1-\eta)}{2a}d^2+O(d^3/a^2),$$

$$F^{\rm W}_{\eta,a}(d)=\eta d+
\frac{\eta(1-\eta)}{a}d^2+O(d^3/a^2).$$

Therefore even the same local curvature, the same tail rate **and offset**,
convexity, identity and semigroup composition do not select one operator.
This is a concrete nonuniqueness result within the research class.

Both remain proper updates of any proper π when D is finite: their factors
are positive and at most 1. They retain the maximizers of the data criterion
L; a nonconstant prior can move the mode of the full mass density. Neither
statement establishes anything about the median (T39).

### Why fixing the tails alone does not calibrate the local intervals

For a fixed finite a and $0<\eta<1$, both maps have $F'(d)>\eta$ for d > 0.
In the standardized sandwich coordinate z, where $D=z^2/(2\eta)$, their
density ratio to the target $N(0,1)$ is proportional to

$$\exp[-(F(z^2/(2\eta))-z^2/2)].$$

It strictly decreases with $|z|$. Thus $|Z_F|$ is strictly stochastically
smaller than the absolute standard normal: every central probability interval
is too narrow for that benchmark, and its variance is less than 1. At
η = 0.1, a = 1 the logarithmic map's local variance is 0.2018876780 and
its central 95% half-width is 0.8145822492, against 1 and 1.9599639845.
Independent quadrature regenerates these numbers. This is a limiting
quadratic experiment, not an empirical ranking of mass estimators.

## 3. The two limits can coexist when a carries experiment state

Let

$$Q_N(dm)\propto e^{-\eta_ND_N(m)}\pi(dm),\qquad
P_N(dm)\propto e^{-F_{\eta_N,a_N}(D_N(m))}\pi(dm).$$

### Repeated fixed signal: comparison to the powered law

Assume $a_N\to\infty$, η stays bounded away from zero, and
$D_N=O_{Q_N}(1)$ in probability over the data. The last condition requires
posterior localization/tightness; uniform local LAN alone does not establish it.
A regular powered-posterior Gaussian limit is one sufficient source.

On bounded d, either expansion gives
$F_{\eta_N,a_N}(d)-\eta_Nd\to0$ uniformly. Relative to Q the weight

$$W_N(m)=e^{-[F_{\eta_N,a_N}(D_N(m))-\eta_ND_N(m)]}$$

lies in [0,1] and tends to 1 in Q-probability. Boundedness yields
$E_QW_N\to1$. Since $dP/dQ=W_N/E_QW_N$,
$\|P_N-Q_N\|_{\rm TV}\to0$. Hence the powered-law Gaussian local limit
transfers; where $\eta_\infty=C/J$, its quantiles have the sandwich target.
The cap caveat in the scope paragraph remains.

### Fixed N, increasing amplitude: comparison to the raw law

Let $P_R^{\rm raw}(dm)\propto e^{-D_R(m)}\pi(dm)$. The bounds in part 2 give

$$e^{-D_R}\le e^{-F_{\eta_R,a_R}(D_R)}
\le\eta_R^{-a_R}e^{-D_R}.$$

If $a_R\le A<\infty$ and $\eta_R\ge\epsilon>0$, then for any set B,

$$P_R(B)\le\epsilon^{-A}P_R^{\rm raw}(B).$$

Thus raw concentration at a finite interior mass implies concentration and
median consistency after either transformation. Bounds in probability suffice
for the corresponding conclusion in probability. This condition repairs the
ordinary ν = 10, d = 3 family of T39: its raw law concentrates and
$\eta_R\to1/90>0$. It does **not** repair the raw single-reading ν < 1 failure,
nor prove anything for all schedules with $\eta_R\to0$.

### Null accumulation: a sharper comparison

For either map,

$$0\le F^{\rm L}_{\eta,a}(d)-\eta d
\le\frac{\eta d^2}{2a}e^{d/a},\qquad
0\le F^{\rm W}_{\eta,a}(d)-\eta d
\le\frac{\eta d^2}{a}e^{d/a}.$$

Use $\log(1+t)\le t$ for the first and $W_0(t)\le t$ for the second, then
$e^x-1-x\le x^2e^x/2$ and $e^x-1\le xe^x$. If, uniformly on the mass domain,

$$\sup_mD_N(m)=O_p(\sqrt N),\quad
\eta_N=O_p(N^{-1/2}),\quad a_N\asymp_p N,$$

then $\sup_m|F_N(D_N(m))-\eta_ND_N(m)|=o_p(1)$. Their normalized laws are
globally TV-close. The three random quantities need not be independent.
This preserves the reference law's bounded-null-tilt behavior under these
scaling assumptions; it does not prove that the null floor is optimal or
that every quoted interval has nominal coverage there.

## 4. A continuous Z-safe state exists, with unforced choices

For each reading's **data factor alone**, let
$o_i=\sup\ell_i-\inf\ell_i$ on the whole projective line. Constants added
to a factor and bijective re-expressions of the hypothesis leave its
oscillation unchanged. Put

$$a=\gamma\sum_iw(o_i/u),\qquad\gamma>0,\quad u>0,$$

using, for example, $w(x)=x/(1+x)$ or $w(x)=1-e^{-x}$.
Both are continuous, bounded by 1, zero at zero, and positive for x > 0.

- A constant factor adds zero, so exact zero-padding leaves a unchanged.
- Finite N gives $a\le\gamma N$, regardless of signal strength.
- For iid readings with positive probability of nonconstant factors,
  $a_N/N\to\gamma E[w(o_1/u)]>0$ by the strong law.
- The scale is continuous under uniform changes of the factor curves.
- Units, frame changes and the swap preserve it when the underlying factor
  curves obey those transformations.

When every factor is constant, D is identically zero; the corrected law is π
without needing to divide by a = 0. Counting nonconstant factors instead is
Z-exact but discontinuous when a small nonconstant reading tends to a flat one.
The bounded continuous versions avoid introducing that particular discontinuity.

The scale is computed from the readings' own mass-evidence curves; it does
not estimate or share latent signal sizes. This establishes absence of a
latent-size model, **not admissibility under L19**. The recorded series-level
permission concerns residual disagreement about m. Whether the new aggregate
is licensed calibration remains unsorted.

The choices of w, γ, u and operator family are parameters of mathematical
counterfamilies. They are **not newly licensed physical inputs** under TASK §5.
None is selected by the proved constraints. The exact conditional repair is
therefore evidence that the two consistency limits are compatible within a
larger class, not a proof that one family is the mass definition.

## What has moved

G23's global-power obstruction admits conditional tail-preserving repairs;
an ad hoc power floor is unnecessary for that family. But local η specifies
insufficient global structure. Even a semigroup and a common tail constant
leave different laws. Within pointwise scalar-deviance calibration, a further
experiment scale is needed to combine the Gaussian local benchmark with the
raw far-deviance rate. Selecting and justifying that state and global map is
G24; relaxing the benchmark or changing calibration class is another route.
Unrestricted L11, the finite-N uncertainty target, G18/G19 and readout G6/L23
remain open. No production factor, prior, point or ruling is changed.
