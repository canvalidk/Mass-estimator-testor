# T44: A frozen cut must normalize its conditional laws

**From:** T37, T39, L21's provisional cut interpretation, L19, L20; the explicitly specified frozen noise laws and conditional Gaussian mass laws below. This tests the interpretation, not a new ruling.
**Test:** `tests/test_cut_normalization.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 3 Nov 2026 (Codex). Factor averaging changes the effective noise mixing law. A mixture of normalized whole-series conditionals avoids the single-reading amplitude failure, but has a separate repeated-signal inconsistency with the current positive-misfit noise module. No replacement equation is adopted (G19/G22/G23).

## Standard mathematics and scope

This is normalization of mixture distributions and modular Bayesian inference.
The usual cut joint is the frozen upstream marginal times a **normalized**
downstream conditional. See
[Carmona and Nicholls, *Semi-Modular Inference*, section 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.06804).
That mathematical definition does not establish that the project's reading
of L21 must be a modular cut. The application and counterexamples below
belong to this research construction.

Three operations must be distinguished:

1. Average likelihood factors, then normalize the mass law.
2. Normalize each conditional mass law, then average under the frozen noise law.
3. Apply a power to an already averaged factor or mass law.

At power 1, operation 1 is ours31 and the raw ours32 factor law. Ours32
applies its η **outside** that average. The conditional temperature studied
below is **inside** each Gaussian conditional. These powers do not commute
with averaging or normalization. No claim that a missing denominator alone
repairs unchanged ours32 follows from this theorem.

## 1. The exact normalization identity

Fix observed data Y and a proper frozen joint noise-size law q on
$V=(v_1,\ldots,v_N)\in(0,\infty)^N$. For example current reading-level
modules give $q_Y=\prod_iq_i(dv_i\mid Q_{+,i})$. Let π be a proper starting
mass law, and for a specified conditional temperature $0<\tau\le1$ put

$$L_Y(m,V)=\exp\left[-\frac\tau2\sum_i\frac{A_i(m)}{v_i}\right],
\quad A_i(m)=H_i^+-H_i(m)\ge0\quad(m>0),$$

$$Z_Y(V)=\int\pi(dm)L_Y(m,V)\in(0,1],\qquad
P_V(dm)=\frac{\pi(dm)L_Y(m,V)}{Z_Y(V)}.$$

Tonelli's theorem gives the factor-average law

$$P_{\rm FA}(dm)=\frac{\pi(dm)\int q(dV)L_Y(m,V)}{E_qZ_Y(V)}
=\int q^*(dV)P_V(dm),$$

$$q^*(dV)=\frac{Z_Y(V)q(dV)}{E_qZ_Y(V)}.$$

In particular freezing q in the integrand does not keep it as the marginal
of V in the joint having the specified conditional $P_V$. This joint has
noise marginal $q^*$, different from q whenever Z is nonconstant q-almost
surely. Its mass marginal can coincide with another construction in special
cases, so no injectivity assumption about arbitrary mixtures is used here.

If both that conditional $P_V$ and the frozen marginal q are required, the
joint is instead uniquely $q(dV)P_V(dm)$ and the mass law is

$$P_{\rm C}(dm)=\int q(dV)\frac{\pi(dm)L_Y(m,V)}{Z_Y(V)}.$$

This is the **whole-series** normalized conditional mixture. The one common
π and $Z_Y(V)$ are for all readings together. A product of separately
normalized single-reading cut marginals is generally a different join;
it is not proved equivalent here.

Both laws are proper. If an appended reading has $A_i\equiv0$, its proper
noise law integrates to 1 and the mass law is unchanged. The normalized
construction introduces no shared population of signal sizes. Units/frame
and reciprocal transformations are inherited when π, q and the conditional
mass laws transform together.

There is also a representation check. Replacing $L_Y(m,V)$ by
$c(V)L_Y(m,V)$, for positive c with finite total evidence, leaves every
normalized conditional $P_V$ and therefore $P_{\rm C}$ unchanged. It
generally changes $P_{\rm FA}$. A factor constant in mass can thus matter
when averaging over V. The factor-average construction must specify that
absolute V-dependent normalization; it is not determined by conditional
mass laws given only up to proportionality. This does not prove that such
a specification is inadmissible, or force a latent measure convention.

### A finite, deterministic witness

Take a positive 1D observed line at mass 2, observed radius 10, uniform
angular prior (c = s = 1), τ = 1 and
$q=\tfrac12\delta_{0.01}+\tfrac12\delta_{100}$.
The component evidences are approximately 0.01595849 and 0.89099184.
The factor average assigns effective noise weights approximately
(0.01759577, 0.98240423); the normalized conditional mixture keeps (1/2,1/2).
The resulting mass medians are respectively 1.1753003505 and 1.9773123883.
This checks the identity rather than selecting either law by its answer.

## 2. Normalized conditional mixing preserves amplitude concentration

Here assume a fixed proper prior with positive continuous angular density
near the interior true angle. This excludes prior-support counterexamples.

For a single reading with positive observed angle $\alpha_R\to\theta_0$
and radius $\rho_R\to\infty$, condition on any finite $v>0$. Its normalized
angular law has kernel

$$\exp[-\tau\rho_R^2\sin^2(\theta-\alpha_R)/(2v)].$$

For a fixed proper q and a fixed positive temperature, every conditional
law concentrates at the interior true angle. Its outside probability is
bounded by 1, so bounded convergence under q proves concentration of
$P_{\rm C}$. No moment of V is required. In particular this holds for
$q=\operatorname{IG}(\nu/2,\nu/2)$ at **every ν > 0**, including ν < 1.

At τ = 1, rescale $U_R=\rho_R(\theta-\alpha_R)$. Conditional on each finite
v its limit is $N(0,v)$. Mixing this limit under that inverse-gamma q gives
the proper Student-ν law, with local density proportional to

$$\left(1+u^2/\nu\right)^{-(\nu+1)/2}.$$

The local half-power arises from normalizing each Gaussian conditional;
it is not a newly chosen power of a latent integration measure. This is a
local scaled limit, not an exact finite-radius global Student factor.
T39's factor-average kernel instead has exponent ν/2 and fails amplitude
concentration at ν < 1. For ν = 0.5, the normalized cut's probability of
mass in [1.9,2.1] is approximately 0.112033, 0.556413 and 0.857207 at radii
10, 100 and 1000. The factor-average limit assigns that band only 0.160952.

### Fixed N and temperature placement

In the common-noise 1D setting, hold N and finite error vectors fixed and let compatible
true signals grow as $R t_i$, with at least one nonzero $t_i$. Use the
Gaussian conditional law with τ inside it. On any compact noise-size set
$[a,b]^N\subset(0,\infty)^N$, the weighted scatter has gap at least cR²,
its fitted line tends uniformly to truth, and its normalized law concentrates
uniformly if $\tau_R R^2\to\infty$. A fixed regular prior supplies the local
normalizer exactly as in T42.

If the frozen laws $q_R$ are uniformly tight on such compact subsets, split
the mixture into that set and its arbitrarily small complement. This proves
concentration of the whole-series cut. The current inverse-gamma modules
have this tightness in this fixed-error limit: their shapes are fixed
positive numbers and their rates $(\nu_i+Q_{+,i}(R))/2$ remain between
positive finite constants because $Q_{+,i}(R)$ stays bounded.

For T39's ν = 10 opposed-error pair, the named η32 tends to 1/90. Putting
that temperature inside each normalized conditional therefore gives
concentration. This is an explicitly different operation from ours32's
outer power. Arbitrary vanishing temperatures or the unchanged outer-power
procedure are not repaired by that statement.

## 3. The current positive-misfit noise module creates another failure

The normalized mixture cannot be adopted merely because it repairs those
amplitude examples. In 1D its current frozen module is

$$q_i(V_i\mid Y_i)=\operatorname{IG}
\left(\nu/2,(\nu+Q_{+,i})/2\right),\qquad
E[1/V_i\mid Y_i]=w(Q_{+,i})=\frac\nu{\nu+Q_{+,i}}.$$

For observed coordinates X,Y,
$Q_+=0$ when XY is nonnegative and $Q_+=\min(X^2,Y^2)$ otherwise. At a
Gaussian null, sign symmetry and integration over the positive quadrant give

$$B_\nu=E_0[w(Q_+)XY]
=4\int_0^\infty\frac{x^3}{\nu+x^2}\varphi(x)^2dx
=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty e^{-t}\frac{t}{\nu+t}dt$$

$$=\frac{1-\nu e^\nu E_1(\nu)}\pi>0.$$

The two diagonal moments are equal. Thus this weighted population scatter
at null has a preferred positive line at mass 1. The signal need not be
zero for a counterexample. Take independent Gaussian data with

$$\mu=r(2,1)/\sqrt5,\qquad r=0.001,\qquad \nu=10,$$

unit error covariance, and the fixed half-Cauchy prior with c = s = 1.
Let u and v be the true-angle unit vector and its angular derivative.
For $f(Y)=w(Q_+)(u^TY)(v^TY)$, the null expectation is
$-3B_\nu/5$. Since $0<w\le1$ and the null U,V are independent standard
normals, $E_0 f^2\le1$. The Gaussian density ratio satisfies
$E_0[(dP_\mu/dP_0-1)^2]=e^{r^2}-1$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives

$$|E_\mu f-E_0f|\le\sqrt{e^{r^2}-1},\qquad
E_\mu f\le-3B_{10}/5+\sqrt{e^{10^{-6}}-1}<0,$$

using $B_{10}=0.0268547421339670\ldots$. The same bound for wXY gives
$E_\mu[wXY]\ge B_{10}-\sqrt{e^{10^{-6}}-1}>0$. Therefore the weighted
population matrix has a unique interior positive principal line different
from the true mass 2. Independent polar Gaussian quadrature checks both
the exact null identity and the strictly negative shifted true-angle score.

### Why that weighted matrix determines the whole-series cut limit

Under the joint law of iid observed data and independent conditional
cut-state draws, the weighted empirical scatter obeys

$$\frac1N\sum_i V_i^{-1}Y_iY_i^T
\longrightarrow E_\mu[w(Q_+)YY^T].$$

Integrability follows from Gaussian moments and uniformly bounded moments
of the conditional gamma precision: its rate is at least ν/2. This is a
law of large numbers on the joint data-and-cut-state experiment, not an
assertion that the finite mixed density's curvature equals mean precision.

For any data-level conditional temperature with $N\tau_N\to\infty$ in
probability, the Gaussian conditional mass law therefore concentrates at
that wrong interior line under the joint experiment. Its outside probability
is bounded by 1, so its joint expectation tends to zero. Averaging over
$V\mid Y$ and applying Markov's inequality proves that the whole-series
cut mixture conditional on Y concentrates at the same wrong line.

This covers τ = 1 and the positive-limit η32 in this fixed nonzero Gaussian
signal regime (T39). Total signal grows as Nr², so the experiment is
identifying; T42's information condition diverges. The failure belongs to
the current frozen positive-misfit construction, not to T41's insufficient
information regime. The world's errors here are Gaussian with correct
declared covariance; this does not claim the world's marginal noise law is
the finite-ν inverse-gamma mixture used by the probe.

## Sort and authority

**Passes:** proper frozen-noise marginal and normalized conditionals;
single-reading concentration for every ν > 0; the named fixed-N amplitude
limits with inside-conditional temperature. **Fails:** repeated fixed weak
Gaussian signal with the current positive-misfit module. The sign-weighted
population effect is G22 again.

L21's recorded power form and the phrase "The readings are evidence. Just
less" do not independently state every normalization or feedback choice.
O14's identification with a no-feedback cut was Claude's provisional sort;
T37 adopts a particular mathematical factor average. R-2026-11-01-1 keeps
G18/G19 standing until an argument forces a choice. Standard modular-cut
terminology alone cannot make this research mixture the definition.

The exact identity names a missing specification: what is frozen, the
factor or the normalized conditional law? With a specified conditional law,
preserving q has one mathematical meaning; that meaning must not be
conflated with factor averaging or outer tempering. The population
counterexample then shows that correcting the normalization order is not
enough to settle the definition. G19, the positive-misfit noise module,
the latent measure and full-type calibration remain open. No production
factor, readout, input or ruling is changed.
