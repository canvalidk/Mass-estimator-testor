# T25: Zero components carry nothing; $k$ does

**Source:** lemma list d4, row T25

## Statement

measured exact zeros on some axes of a signal reading leave $H_i$ exactly as without those axes, at every $m$, whatever their $\sigma$. The law changes only through $k_i$. A 3D reading declared $k=1$ equals the 1D reading exactly.

**Scope (31 Oct, Claude, from B06's proof):** the zero-axis clause needs the zero axes' noise uncorrelated with the retained axes and the candidate subspace split along the same blocks; correlated zero axes need not be inert. The $k=1$ clause holds with the 1D reading formed from the conditional (Schur-complement) data and covariance, not the marginal ones.

## Proof or support

**B06 derivation and conditions (Codex, 30 Oct).** The zero-axis assertion holds
when the retained and zero coordinates are orthogonal in the declared noise
metric and the candidate space splits along the same coordinates. Full candidate
subspaces with block-independent retained/zero noise satisfy this condition.
It does not hold for unrestricted correlated covariance or an arbitrary change
of the declared subspace. The Statement is retained as required by B06; these
conditions are additional qualifications to it.

To prove the assertion in that scope, reorder coordinates into retained force and
acceleration coordinates A and zero coordinates B. Suppose
$\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\Sigma_A,\Sigma_B)$ and the candidate map decomposes
into the same two blocks (in particular this holds when $N=I$). Its Gram matrix is
block diagonal, while $b=(b_A,0)$ because $y_B=0$. Thus

$$H=b^\top G^{-1}b=b_A^\top G_A^{-1}b_A.$$

The zero block contributes neither span energy nor explained energy. Its
covariance $\Sigma_B$ can be any positive-definite block: its individual standard
deviations and within-block correlations do not enter H. Therefore $Q_+$ is also
unchanged. When the enlarged reading declares additional candidate directions,
$\kappa=k-\tfrac12$ changes; this can change g and subsequently the series power
$\eta$, even though H itself has not changed. That is the stated dependence on k.
Holding the retained covariance, data and reading membership fixed is essential.

**Why correlations matter.** Take $d=2$, full subspace, $F=(1,0)$, $a=(0,0)$,
$\Sigma_{FF}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1/2\\1/2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$\Sigma_{aa}=I$ and no force–acceleration correlation. At m=1, T36's full-space
residual formula gives

$$H=\frac43-\frac{2}{4-1/4}=\frac45.$$

Dropping the zero second axis and retaining its marginal first-axis covariance
I instead gives $H=1/2$. Both are valid measured inputs. Exact observed zeros on
correlated axes can carry conditional noise information, so the unconditional
zero-axis wording cannot be proved on the full covariance type.

**The exact k=1 reduction.** Rotate the spatial frame so that the declared unit
direction n is its first axis, then reorder the stacked coordinates into the
along-n force/acceleration pair A and all remaining coordinates B. Write

$$\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^\top&D\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S=A-BD^{-1}B^\top,\qquad y_{\rm eff}=y_A-BD^{-1}y_B.$$

Here the candidate has coordinates $(m\alpha,\alpha)$ in A and zero in B.
Completing the Mahalanobis square yields

$$\|y-((m\alpha,\alpha),0)\|^2_{\Sigma^{-1}}
=y_B^\top D^{-1}y_B+
\|y_{\rm eff}-(m\alpha,\alpha)\|^2_{S^{-1}}.$$

The first term is independent of the candidate and of m. Consequently H is
exactly that of the 1D reading with data $y_{\rm eff}$ and covariance S. The
declared-span energy is $y_{\rm eff}^\top S^{-1}y_{\rm eff}$, so $Q_+$, g (k=1),
the series terms and hence the law agree as well, with the same declared $\pi$.
This uses the conditional covariance, not generally the marginal A. If the noise
blocks are independent, S=A and $y_{\rm eff}=y_A$, recovering the ordinary 1D
reading formed from the projected data. If $y_B=0$ but B is nonzero, the data
stay $y_A$ while S still differs from A.

For example the correlated covariance above, with subspace n=(1,0), reduces to
the 1D covariance $S=\operatorname{diag}(3/4,1)$. At m=1 it gives $H=16/21$,
matching the ambient k=1 reading exactly and differing from the marginal
first-axis value $1/2$. This is also distinct from the full-k value $4/5$:
declaring an extra candidate direction is a different input.
