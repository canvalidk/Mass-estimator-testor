## Statement

a reading's force and acceleration are averages over one window: the time pairing $(\Delta t)'\Delta t=1$ in $m=\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf a'=\Delta\mathbf p\cdot(\Delta\mathbf v)'$ needs the same $\Delta t$ in both. For a non-constant push, mismatched windows break Newton II with no noise at all: $F(t)=5(1+\sin2t)$ in a fixed direction, $m=2$, gives $\mathbf a'(\mathbf F)=2.000$ on one window, $1.935$ with the acceleration window shifted by half, $2.417$ with it doubled. Free for constant signals. Two measurements over the same window share one signal by construction, so under L19's reason they cannot be two readings: one reading, combined $\Sigma$. Measurements over different windows of an unchanged push are separate readings (O9).
