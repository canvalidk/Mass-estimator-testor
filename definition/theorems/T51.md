# T51: Universal rank recalibration cannot commute with independent composition

**From:** a new, explicitly conditional universal commutation axiom for a law-only CDF warp; T47's normal-score recalibration. The provisional law-composition proposal R-2026-10-28-1 is compared with this stronger axiom, not identified with it.
**Test:** `tests/test_composition_calibration.py`.
**Status:** proved below, 4 Nov 2026 (Codex). This closes a possible forcing route through universal independent composition. It does not establish an L5 failure, reject T47 under the actual commitments, select eta=1 for the definition, or construct a composite estimator. **Harvested 7 Nov from the exploration branch `codex/ours32-consistency` (Codex, 3–4 Nov) as input; not checked by Claude.**

## 1. State the additional axiom before applying it

Let H be a fixed continuous strictly increasing bijection of [0,1], with
H(0)=0 and H(1)=1. For a proper positive law P with CDF F, define W(P) by

$$F_{W(P)}(x)=H(F_P(x)).$$

This includes atomic laws, fixes point masses, and commutes with increasing
coordinate changes. These properties alone allow nonidentity H. T47 gives
$H_\eta(u)=\Phi(\sqrt\eta\,\Phi^{-1}(u))$, $0<\eta\le1$, which also
commutes with decreasing coordinate changes because
$H_\eta(1-u)=1-H_\eta(u)$.

The **additional axiom investigated here** says, for every pair of positive
laws P,Q and a fixed chosen operation g,

$$W\bigl(g_\#(P\otimes Q)\bigr)
=g_\#\bigl(W(P)\otimes W(Q)\bigr).\tag{C}$$

Thus the same warp must commute with composing independently before or
after calibration. Independence is preserved, and the same fixed operator
W is used on all three laws. The axiom does not allow a new
composition-dependent eta or an unspecified change of copula.

R-2026-10-28-1 provisionally describes exact-input composition, computation
of a composite law from the parts, and coverage consistency of a new
measurement of the composite. It does not state (C), supply independence
from marginal laws, or identify calibration of the raw composite with
calibration of each part. Its words and provisional status remain in
[`rulings.md`](../../rulings.md#r-2026-10-28-1--composition-is-restated-at-the-level-of-the-law-checks-first-then-the-median-into-met-provisional).
No new ruling is inferred here.

## 2. Identity is forced by independent addition, or by parallel sum alone

**Theorem.** If (C) holds for all positive laws under either addition
$g(x,y)=x+y$ or parallel sum $g(x,y)=(1/x+1/y)^{-1}$, then H(u)=u.

**Proof.** Take X,Y with common support {1,2}, independently, with
$P(X=1)=p$ and $P(Y=1)=q$, where p,q are arbitrary in (0,1).
For addition the ordered outputs are 2,3,4; for parallel sum they are
1/2,2/3,1. In either case the first two cumulative probabilities are pq
and $p+q-pq$. The warped marginals have lower-atom probabilities H(p),H(q).
Consequently (C) implies

$$H(pq)=H(p)H(q),\tag{1}$$
$$H(p+q-pq)=H(p)+H(q)-H(p)H(q).\tag{2}$$

For $t>0$, the function $f(t)=-\log H(e^{-t})$ is continuous, increasing
and additive by (1). Thus $f(t)=ct$ for some c>0, or $H(p)=p^c$.
Set q=p in (2) and divide by $p^c$:

$$ (2-p)^c=2-p^c.$$

Letting p decrease to zero gives $2^c=2$, hence c=1. The identity warp
conversely satisfies (C). No assumption about existence of a mean or a
finite variance was used. The same proof applies to any commutative
operation strictly increasing in both arguments, because the three
two-point output levels are strictly ordered. QED.

The parallel-sum proof does not need a decreasing-coordinate naturality
assumption. For reflection-symmetric H such as T47, one can alternatively
transfer the addition result through reciprocal coordinates.

### Continuous full-support laws do not avoid the obstruction

The theorem is not restricted to an atomic counterdomain. Suppose (C) were
required only for smooth laws with positive density everywhere on
$(0,\infty)$. Approximate each atom by a narrow lognormal component. The
resulting mixtures have that property and converge weakly to the two-point
laws. Continuity of H implies W preserves this weak convergence: at any
continuity point of the limiting CDF, the warped CDFs converge too.
Independent products converge weakly, and both addition and parallel sum
are continuous on the positive quadrant. Taking limits would give (C) for
the atomic laws, a contradiction. In particular some finite positive
component width retains a nonzero discrepancy. This does not assert that
every such law is exactly reachable as an ours32 posterior.

## 3. An exact T47 witness and a smooth positive witness

Let $P=Q=\tfrac12\delta_1+\tfrac12\delta_2$ and choose eta=1/4.
Each individual law is unchanged because $H_\eta(1/2)=1/2$.
Their independent composition has probabilities (1/4,1/2,1/4), on
(2,3,4) for addition or (1/2,2/3,1) for parallel sum. Warping that
already-composed law instead gives

$$ (h,1-2h,h),\qquad
h=\Phi\!\left(\tfrac12\Phi^{-1}(1/4)\right)
=0.3679661556049961.$$

The first cumulative probability differs by 0.1179661556049961. More
generally $H_\eta(1/4)>1/4$ for every $0<\eta<1$, so this same finite
example excludes every nonidentity member of the T47 family from (C).
This is a law statement, not a median-readout counterexample.

For a direct smooth full-support example, let X,Y be independent lognormal
variables with log means zero and log standard deviations 0.2. A T47 warp
with eta=1/4 exactly changes each log standard deviation to 0.4. Independent
one-dimensional density integrals at $X+Y\le2$ give

$$F_{X+Y}(2)=0.4723820114869846,$$
$$F_{W(X)+W(Y)}(2)=0.4475999380925859,$$
$$H_{1/4}(F_{X+Y}(2))=0.4861827208446029.$$

Thus the discrepancy also occurs for ordinary smooth strictly positive
densities, without a limiting discrete argument.

## 4. A supplied joint law is a different input

If a joint law Gamma for the two masses is supplied, its addition law is
$g_\#\Gamma$ by definition. This computation neither requires (C) nor
selects Gamma from its marginals. Correctly pushing a calibrated supplied
joint law through g is not the demand that marginal recalibration commute
with independently composing raw laws.

For the finite witness above, the nonindependent joint probability matrix

$$\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}h&1/2-h\\1/2-h&h\end{pmatrix}$$

still has the two unchanged Bernoulli marginals. Its pushforward has
probabilities (h,1-2h,h), so it matches the warped composite in this
particular example. Its correlation is $4h-1=0.4718646224199845$.
This is a concrete demonstration of the missing coupling choice, not a
universal correlated repair or a declaration that dependence should be
changed. The product joint gives a different answer from the same marginals.

### Marginal Gaussian calibration does not imply composite coverage

Suppose a regular centered Gaussian error vector has actual covariance
$V_{\rm true}$ and quoted covariance $V_{\rm quote}$. For an explicitly
specified scalar linearization with gradient g, the quoted central-p
interval has actual coverage

$$2\Phi\!\left(z_p\sqrt{
\frac{g^TV_{\rm quote}g}{g^TV_{\rm true}g}}\right)-1,
\qquad z_p=\Phi^{-1}((1+p)/2).$$

For $V_{\rm true}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho\\\rho&1\end{pmatrix}$ and
$V_{\rm quote}=I$, each marginal interval is correctly calibrated.
For the sum, $g=(1,1)$, the coverage is

$$2\Phi(z_p/\sqrt{1+\rho})-1.$$

At p=0.95 and rho=0.9 it is approximately 0.845. If the full joint covariance
is correct, the same calculation instead gives p. This is a dependence and
protocol gap, not a T47 failure under a correct joint model.

A shared observed acceleration supplies an elementary possible mechanism.
If $F_j=m_j a$, the local ratio error is
$\delta m_j=\delta F_j/a-(m_j/a)\delta A$. With independent force errors
but common acceleration error, the cross covariance contains
$m_1m_2\operatorname{Var}(\delta A)/a^2>0$. A product of marginal mass laws
discards it. This illustration selects no joint estimator for the project.

## 5. Gaussian local compatibility is weaker than universal commutation

For unrestricted real Gaussian laws $N(\mu_j,v_j)$ and a common fixed eta,
T47 maps the variances to $v_j/\eta$. Independent addition therefore
commutes exactly: both orders give
$N(\mu_1+\mu_2,(v_1+v_2)/\eta)$. These Gaussian laws live on the full real
line, so this exception is not the universal positive-law claim in section 2.

In a regular small-error joint Gaussian limit around positive masses, the
same statement holds to first order for any differentiable composition:
the correctly propagated joint variance is $g^TVg$, and a common inflation
of the **whole covariance** by $1/\eta$ inflates this by $1/\eta$. For
addition $g=(1,1)$; for parallel sum at positive x,y,

$$g=\left(\frac{y^2}{(x+y)^2},\frac{x^2}{(x+y)^2}\right).$$

This is conditional on a valid joint Gaussian approximation and the correct
joint covariance. Separate marginal calibration supplies neither. It also
does not establish exact finite-noise commutation for positive non-Gaussian
laws, as the lognormal example demonstrates.

### A positive forcing result within the centered Gaussian benchmark

Suppose the sampling error is exactly centered $N(0,V)$ and the quoted
joint law has the correct center and Gaussian covariance W. Exact nominal
central coverage at any single fixed $0<p<1$ for each individual part and
their sum forces $W=V$ in two dimensions. Indeed the preceding coverage
formula is strictly increasing in the quoted-to-actual variance ratio,
and equals p exactly when that ratio is one. The individual coverages
therefore force $W_{11}=V_{11}$ and $W_{22}=V_{22}$. The sum coverage then
forces $W_{11}+2W_{12}+W_{22}=V_{11}+2V_{12}+V_{22}$, hence $W_{12}=V_{12}$.

More generally, exact nominal coverage for all positive weighted linear
composites forces $g^T(W-V)g=0$ on the open positive orthant. This quadratic
polynomial must vanish identically, so W=V. At-least-nominal coverage gives
variance inequalities instead and does not select a unique covariance.
The result uses a centered full Gaussian calibration benchmark, stronger
than property N. It does not assert that actual VD commitments require that
benchmark, every weighted composition, or universal finite-law commutation.

## 6. Research helpers and validation

`met/composition_calibration.py` provides `FinitePositiveLaw`,
`warp_finite_law`, `compose_independent`, `pushforward_joint`, and
`gaussian_linear_coverage`. The operation, eta, covariance inputs and
probability level are explicit. Independent composition constructs a product
joint; given-joint pushforward instead uses precisely the supplied weights.
Finite-law normalization is numerical normalization of supplied weights,
not a prior or a dependence prescription.

The tests independently enumerate finite joint outcomes, integrate the
normal-rank witness and the lognormal sum densities, compare the supplied
dependent coupling, verify Gaussian convolution by density integration,
and check the joint-covariance coverage formula. They also check properness,
point masses, coordinate changes, reciprocal symmetry, the T47 semigroup,
and stable treatment of small outer atoms. There is no Monte Carlo ranking,
production modification, or new interpretation of a ruling.
