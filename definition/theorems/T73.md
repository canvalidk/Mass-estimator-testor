## Standard mathematics

The curve is the profile log-likelihood of the Gaussian errors-in-variables model with the pushes
profiled out, read without a prior (Royall 1997, *Statistical Evidence*). On one instrument it is total
least squares through the origin: the smaller eigenvalue of the series' 2×2 matrix of dot products
(Golub and Van Loan 1980). That it never vanishes at the ends of the mass line, so that any honest set
must be allowed to be unbounded, is Gleser and Hwang (1987) and Dufour (1997), already on the pages as
B13, B16 and T45 §4.

## Statement

For a series of readings in the full type write $K_i(m)$ for T36's residual of reading $i$ at candidate
$m$ (the squared length, in the reading's own noise metric and inside its declared span, of the part of
the reading that $m$ does not explain), and

$$K(m)=\sum_iK_i(m),\qquad m\in[0,\infty].$$

$e^{-K(m)/2}$ is the declared Gaussian's probability of the readings at the best pushes compatible with
$m$, up to a factor free of $m$. No $\pi$, no normalisation and no point are part of it.

**(1) It is total, and both ends are ordinary points.** For every declared $\Sigma_i\succ0$ and every
data, zero included, $0\le K_i(m)\le E_i$ (the reading's whitened energy in its span) on the closed arc.
$K_i(0)$ is the residual when only the acceleration is explained and $K_i(\infty)$ when only the force is.
So $e^{-K/2}$ is bounded away from zero at both ends on every finite series: the curve alone is never
integrable in $m$ (this is B16), and its set at any threshold above $K(\text{end})-K_{\min}$ is unbounded.
One ordinary reading $F=10\pm1$, $a=1\pm1$ has $K(0)=100$, $K(\infty)=1$ and the set $[3.28,\infty)$ at the
$\chi^2_1$ threshold.

**(2) The all-zero experiment is the identity of combination.** If every reading is exactly zero,
$K\equiv0$. Appending an exactly zero reading to any series adds exactly nothing at every $m$ (T24's
$H_i\equiv0$, read without $\pi$). Curves of independent series combine by addition (T12), so the
all-zero series is the element that changes no other curve. This is what §2.3 of the 29 Sept record
derives ("the identity of combination… a value, not a gap"). The record's next step, §2.4 ("the law is
already there": the output is $\pi$, proper, with a point $c$), needs a starting law to be part of the
definition; (2) does not.

**(3) A zero reading and an infinitely noisy reading are the same reading.** In whitened coordinates a
reading enters only as $z_i=\Sigma_i^{-1/2}y_i$ and the family of candidate subspaces, and the family is
unchanged when $\Sigma_i$ is multiplied by a number. So $y_i=0$ at finite $\Sigma_i$ and any fixed $y_i$ at
$t^2\Sigma_i$, $t\to\infty$, are the same point $z_i=0$. Every object that is a function of the $z_i$ and
the subspaces alone treats them alike: the curve, and T45's score with its conditional variance. An
object that also reads the count of noise components $\sum_ik_i$ treats neither as absent: the
probability bound in (5), and the unweighted conditional test of T75.

**(4) On one instrument the least is an eigenvalue.** With one noise ratio and full subspaces, let $G$ be
the 2×2 matrix of the stacked dot products in noise units. Then $K(m)=\operatorname{tr}G-u^\top Gu$ with $u$
the unit vector of the candidate line, so on the whole line of slopes the least is $\lambda_{\min}(G)$,
taken at the principal eigenvector; on the positive masses it is that value when the principal line has
positive slope, and otherwise the smaller of the two ends. This is the operation of
`claude/vector_division_common_line_2026-11-22.md` (project doc, 22 Nov): its leftover $\lambda_-$ is this
least, and its mass is this minimiser.

**(5) The leftover sits beside the curve.** $K_{\min}=\min_{m\in[0,\infty]}K(m)$ is what no single positive
mass explains (`../../problems/O14.md`, "Words"). At the true mass $K$ is $\chi^2$ with $M=\sum_ik_i$
components, and $K_{\min}$ is no larger, so $P(\chi^2_M\ge K_{\min})$ bounds from above how probable so
large a leftover was. $K_{\min}$ itself is not moved by a zero reading; the bound is, through $M$. For two
scalar readings of one mass the rise of the curve above its least does not depend on how far apart the
readings are: Can's near pair (5.5 and 5.6, each ± 0.1) has $K_{\min}=0.494$ (bound 0.78) and the far pair
(5 and 6) has $K_{\min}=49.4$ (bound $1.9\times10^{-11}$), and both curves rise by 3.84 at ± 0.139 from
their least. The gap is in the leftover and nowhere in the curve's shape (T75 (2)).

## Proof

(1) $K_i=\|(I-P_i(m))z_i\|^2$ with $P_i(m)$ the orthogonal projector, in the whitened span, on the range of
$\Sigma_i^{-1/2}(mN_i;N_i)$; a projector's complement has norm at most 1, and the range has a limit at each
end (the range of $(0;N_i)$ and of $(N_i;0)$). (2) $z_i=0$. (3) $P_i$ is invariant under
$\Sigma_i\mapsto t^2\Sigma_i$ and $z_i\mapsto z_i/t$. (4) is the Rayleigh quotient. (5) at the true mass
$(I-P_i)z_i\sim N(0,I-P_i)$, of rank $k_i$, independently over readings; for two scalar readings of one
mass with a nearly exact acceleration the curve is $\sum_i(x_i-m)^2/\sigma^2=2(\bar x-m)^2/\sigma^2+(x_1-x_2)^2/2\sigma^2$.

## What it does not give

- **A point.** The minimiser of $K$ is the first probe's point (the errors-in-variables best point, 1 Aug;
  `GOAL.md` §5 row 1), with what killed it: it sits at an end for any series whose principal line has
  negative slope, it does not exist when the curve is flat, and it is a mode, so it does not compose.
  E13 does not offer it as an output; its output is the curve, sets, and the leftover.
- **A set with a stated promise.** Cutting the curve at a $\chi^2$ threshold under-covers at weak signal
  (0.48 and 0.65 where 0.95 is asked, `tests/test_conditional_lr.py`); the sets that keep a promise are
  T75's.
- **A discount.** By (5) the curve's shape is blind to the leftover's size.
