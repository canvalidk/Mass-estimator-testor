## Statement

L8 and L9 force the Gaussian family $e^{-\lVert\mathbf v\rVert^2/2\omega^2}$ in noise units (Herschel–Maxwell). The flat weight is its $\omega\to\infty$ endpoint, and **since 30 Sept it is the one in use** (L10: no size law, so no $\omega$ to average).

## Proof or support

### 1. Coordinates and the conditional family

This writes out the O3 record, §§2 and 4b, while separating its argument from its
extra conditions. Suppress the reading index. Under L1 and the declared subspace
of L18, write the candidate as $C_m\alpha$, where
$C_m=(mN^\top,N^\top)^\top$, $N$ has $k$ independent columns, and $\alpha\in\mathbb R^k$.
With the positive definite declared $\Sigma$ (L2), set
$$
 z=\Sigma^{-1/2}y,\qquad G_m=C_m^\top\Sigma^{-1}C_m,\qquad
 v=G_m^{1/2}\alpha,\qquad U_m=\Sigma^{-1/2}C_mG_m^{-1/2}.
$$
Then $U_m^\top U_m=I_k$. Write $E_{\rm amb}=\lVert z\rVert^2$ and
$h_m=U_m^\top z$. T36(2)'s explained energy is $H(m)=\lVert h_m\rVert^2$.
Its $E$ is the energy projected onto the whitened stacked declared space
$W=\operatorname{col}(\Sigma^{-1/2}\operatorname{diag}(N,N))$, and
$K(m)=E-H(m)$. Thus $E_{\rm amb}=E+E_\perp$, with $E_\perp$ independent
of $m$. For a full subspace $E_\perp=0$; otherwise the ambient likelihood
below contains the extra common factor $e^{-E_\perp/2}$.

Fix the reading's shared state, including $N$ and $Q$. In the noise coordinates
used in the archive, L8's conditional independence makes the candidate density
a product of one-dimensional factors; L9 makes it invariant under orthogonal
changes of these coordinates. For a **proper probability density** and $k\ge2$,
the Herschel–Maxwell characterization gives $N(0,\omega^2 I_k)$, $\omega>0$.
See [Mukherjee (2017), Theorem 3.2, printed p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.02228)
(proof on p. 3); applying the two-coordinate result to every pair gives the
$k$-dimensional statement.

Here is a short proof without density smoothness or moment assumptions. Let
$f$ be the common coordinate characteristic function. Spherical symmetry makes
$f$ real and even; independence and radiality give
$$
 f(s)f(t)=f\!\left(\sqrt{s^2+t^2}\right).
$$
Thus $g(u)=f(\sqrt u)$ satisfies $g(u+v)=g(u)g(v)$ on $[0,\infty)$ and
$g(0)=1$. Since $g(u)=g(u/2)^2$, it is nonnegative. A zero would, by repeated
halving, contradict continuity at zero. Consequently $\log g$ is continuous
and additive, so $g(u)=e^{-au}$; $|f|\le1$ forces $a\ge0$. The joint
characteristic function is $e^{-a\lVert t\rVert^2}$, hence the law is
$N(0,2aI_k)$. Having a density excludes $a=0$.

This probability theorem alone does **not** classify an improper weight as a
proper Gaussian. For a finite, strictly positive, measurable product weight
with pointwise radiality, the corresponding elementary functional equation
does give
$$
 w(v)=A\exp(\tau\lVert v\rVert^2),\qquad A>0,\quad \tau\in\mathbb R.
$$
Indeed, writing $w(v)=A F(\lVert v\rVert^2)$ with $F(0)=1$, evaluate on one
and two coordinate axes to obtain $F(s+t)=F(s)F(t)$. Measurability of
$\log F$ makes it linear. Integrability restricts $\tau<0$, with
$\omega^2=-1/(2\tau)$; the flat weight $\tau=0$ and the growing weights
$\tau>0$ are improper. These statements use ordinary densities, or the
positive measurable weights just specified, not arbitrary singular measures.

### 2. The likelihood integral and the endpoint

By L13 the Gaussian likelihood in these coordinates is proportional to
$\exp[-\lVert z-U_mv\rVert^2/2]$. For $\tau<1/2$, completing the square gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
 &\int_{\mathbb R^k}
 e^{-\lVert z-U_mv\rVert^2/2}\,e^{\tau\lVert v\rVert^2}\,dv\\
 &\quad=(2\pi)^{k/2}(1-2\tau)^{-k/2}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{E_{\rm amb}}2+\frac{H(m)}{2(1-2\tau)}\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
For $\tau\ge1/2$ the integral diverges, including the boundary case $h_m=0$.
For a fixed state and fixed $\tau<1/2$, the prefactor and $E_{\rm amb}$ are
independent of $m$. A proper Gaussian weight therefore gives
$\exp[\omega^2H/(2(1+\omega^2))]$. Its unnormalized flat limit gives
$\exp(H/2)$. The unnormalized weights tend to 1 locally as $\omega\to\infty$;
normalized Gaussian probability laws have no flat probability limit.

L10 excludes a finite fixed size $\omega$, and also a law of sizes: either
would declare something about the setups' signal magnitudes that L10, with
L19's reason, forbids. Among the proper Gaussian family and its flat endpoint,
this selects the endpoint. As currently written, L10 additionally **states**
that each reading's factor is $e^{H/2}$, as a working likelihood. Using that
clause establishes the coefficient as a given premise; it is not an
independent derivation of the clause from absence of a size law. As the O3
record observes, absence of a typical size alone does not exclude the growing
improper weights $0<\tau<1/2$.

The integration here takes **T7's noise-volume convention as stated**. Since
$d\alpha=\det(G_m)^{-1/2}dv$, a flat weight with respect to physical
$d\alpha$ would introduce an additional $m$-dependent determinant. It is flat
with respect to $dv$ that gives the displayed factor. This calculation does
not settle T36's parked question of the induced volume on $L(m)$ and where
that convention meets the declared starting law $\pi$ (O6).

### 3. What the coefficient test establishes, and what L7 does not

The O3 record excludes the growing improper members using the condition
**the strong-signal width is Gaussian propagation of the declared $\Sigma$**.
The calculation under that condition is correct. Put
$\beta=(1-2\tau)^{-1}$. Near an interior true mass $m_0$, the factor is
$e^{-\beta K(m)/2}$ up to an $m$-free constant. If
$K(m)=K(\widehat m)+I(m-\widehat m)^2+o(1)$ on the local strong-signal scale,
and the declared prior has a continuous positive density there, the local
Gaussian variance is $1/(\beta I)$.

For a full subspace, put $a=a^\ast$, $B=(I,-m_0I)$ and
$V=B\Sigma B^\top$. Linear Gaussian propagation gives
$\sigma_{\rm prop}^2=(a^\top V^{-1}a)^{-1}=I^{-1}$ to leading order.
Consequently the central 95% width, divided by
$2\Phi^{-1}(0.975)\sigma_{\rm prop}$, tends to
$\beta^{-1/2}=\sqrt{1-2\tau}$. Matching propagation selects $\tau=0$.
This is a local or central-quantile width statement, not a claim about the
law's exact variance (the half-Cauchy tail in T19 prevents that variance).

The named test uses a correlated full covariance, $k=2$, and a single aligned
reading at increasing signal, explicitly checking $g=\eta=1$. It compares
the production flat-factor interval with independent quadrature and checks
$\tau=-1/4,0,1/5$: the squared width ratios tend to $1.5,1,0.6$.
It does not assume $g$ and $\eta$ approach 1 for arbitrary series, or assert
finite-signal exact coverage.

**B07 attempt / premise mismatch (Codex, 30 Oct).** The literal L7 row says

### 4. The two declared limits remain

- **G17, $k=1$.** Independence is vacuous and isotropy gives only an even
  weight. Herschel–Maxwell does not apply. T25 carries the implemented factor
  into 1D only with the extra condition that a reading declared $k=1$ is the
  1D reading. No premise row currently states that forcing condition; it is
  not proved here by restricting a higher-dimensional formula.
- **G13, dependence on $Q$.** Every family argument above holds with L8's
  shared state fixed. It permits $A$, $\tau$ or $\omega$ to depend on that
  state, including $Q$. Whether such dependence is allowed, and how it relates
  to the later misfit weight, is T30's question. The integral drops constants
  only when they are independent of $m$ at that fixed state; it does not
  resolve that later question.
