## Standard mathematics and the earlier construction

This is conditional Gaussian score-test inversion, related to weak-identification
inference. Moreira (2003), [section 2.2, Example 2](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00438),
uses independent Gaussian statistics to obtain an exact chi-square score test;
the scalar projection construction here is a specialization of that principle.
It is not a new statistical test or a conditional likelihood for all candidates
at once.

The repository already contains the common-noise isotropic pivot, its quartic
inversion and its power warning in
[`exact_confidence_sets.md`, sections 4–6](../../legacy/vd-newton-tools/mass_equation_20260915/joint/exact_confidence_sets.md).
The extension here is to arbitrary declared covariance and subspace, together
with its connection to T43 and a direct proof of O15's proper-law obstruction.
No legacy program is run or changed.

Throughout the exact-test claim, the **world's** independent errors have the
declared Gaussian laws. A Gaussian working model alone does not supply exact
frequentist coverage under non-Gaussian errors or hidden noise-scale mixtures.
The covariances, subspaces and reading structure are fixed declarations.

## 1. The exact conditional score in the full Gaussian type

For a fixed positive candidate m and each reading, whiten the observation to
$y_i=\Sigma_i^{-1/2}(\tilde F_i;\tilde a_i)$ and put

$$B_i(m)=\Sigma_i^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}mN_i\\N_i\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P_i=B_i(B_i^TB_i)^{-1}B_i^T,\qquad R_i=I-P_i.$$

Any whitening factor with identity error covariance suffices. The profile
Gaussian log factor is $y_i^TP_i y_i/2$. Write prime for differentiation in
m, set $U_i=P_i y_i$, $V_i=R_i y_i$, and define

$$S(m)=\frac12\sum_i y_i^TP_i'y_i,\qquad
C(m)=\sum_i\|R_iP_i'U_i\|^2.$$

Differentiating $P_i^2=P_i$ gives $P_iP_i'P_i=0$ and $R_iP_i'R_i=0$.
Consequently $P_i'$ has only off-diagonal blocks in this decomposition,

$$S(m)=\sum_i V_i^TP_i'U_i.$$

At the true mass the mean lies in the range of $P_i$. The Gaussian projections
$U_i$ and $V_i$ are independent, with $V_i\sim N(0,R_i)$. Independence across
readings is preserved. Conditional on every realized $U_i$, therefore,

$$S(m)\mid(U_i)_i\sim N(0,C(m)),\qquad
T(m)=S(m)/\sqrt{C(m)}\sim N(0,1).$$

This is an exact finite-sample identity, for every collection of deterministic
latent signals, not a Gaussian limit or an iid signal assumption.

### Nondegeneracy and a total numerical convention

For full-column-rank B the derivative is

$$P'=RB'B^++(RB'B^+)^T.$$

Here $RB'$ has full column rank. If $RB'a=0$, invertible whitening would give
$(N_i a;0)=(mN_i b;N_i b)$ for some b, forcing b = a = 0. Also $B^+U$ has a
nondegenerate Gaussian density in $\mathbb R^{k_i}$. Thus $C(m)>0$ almost
surely for each fixed true m in every nonempty valid experiment.

On exceptional observed $C=0$ the score is also zero. The research reference
sets T = 0 and p-value = 1 there, including the all-zero observed experiment
and an empty series. This supplies a total **test**, is not uniquely forced,
and is not claimed continuous at every such datum. In rank-one isotropic
data an alternative continuous squared-pivot extension exists (the legacy
note); multiplying a quartic by a zero denominator without a convention
would silently select a different set. None of these null-event conventions
changes coverage at a fixed candidate.

The floating-point reference separately recognizes zero physical normal
equations, to avoid QR normalization converting a zero projection into a
tiny nonzero self-normalizing denominator. This is not a certified exact
cancellation detector for every ill-conditioned covariance; the exact
coverage theorem concerns the mathematical statistic.

For a nonempty experiment and explicit $0<p<1$ let
$z_p=\Phi^{-1}((1+p)/2)$. Inversion gives

$$\mathcal C_p(Y)=\{m>0:T(m)^2\le z_p^2\},\qquad
P_{m,(a_i^*)}\{m\in\mathcal C_p(Y)\}=p.$$

For every nonempty experiment the pointwise guarantee is uniform over the
allowed fixed signals and positive masses. The empty-series convention
instead has coverage 1. Neither says the realized set is a probability law.

## 2. Information restrictions and invariance

The conditional variance uses each reading's **own** candidate projection;
it estimates no distribution of amplitudes and uses no signal from one
reading to predict another. Readings meet through their common-mass score.
Thus the operation that L19 explicitly forbids is absent. This does not
force this test or authorize replacing the quoted-law interval by a different
output object. R-2026-09-28-6 and R-2026-09-30-4 forbid borrowing and dependence
on a repeated setup; R-2026-09-30-5 explicitly allows width information from
each reading's own residual. No stronger blanket ban is inferred here.

An exact-zero appended reading has $U=V=0$ and adds zero to both S and C:
the test is Z-invariant without a count correction. Independent sign reversal
of a reading reverses U and V together and changes neither summand. Replacing
$N_i$ by another orthonormal basis for the same subspace leaves P unchanged.

Any invertible change of bookkeeping coordinates, with its covariance and
subspace transformed, induces an orthogonal transformation after whitening.
It preserves the inner products above. A smooth change of mass coordinate
multiplies S by the coordinate derivative and C by its square, preserving
$T^2$. This includes changes of physical units and channel swap. Pure noise
outside the fixed whitened span of $(N_i,0)$ and $(0,N_i)$ contributes to
neither S nor C.

Candidate-dependent conditioning establishes a test under **each null**.
It does not contradict O15's observation that the candidate-dependent
sufficient statistic does not supply one nuisance-free conditional likelihood
over the entire mass space. The two mathematical objects are different.

### B15: the conditional variance of the specified working residual

**5 Nov 2026, Codex.** This calculation uses T48's explicitly specified
declared-metric flat latent integration, in its projected 2k-dimensional
model. It is a variance under that working residual kernel, not an exact
sampling extension of the Gaussian test above to hidden scale mixtures.

Fix a candidate and use one common smooth coordinate for its derivatives
(the code uses phi=arctan(m/c)). Hold the reading's projection U=Py fixed,
and put t=RP'U. In orthonormal coordinates on the k-dimensional residual
complement inside the declared span, write r for the residual and
K=||r||^2. The projector identities in section 1 give

$$h'=y^TP'y=2t^Tr,\qquad
s_{33}=\frac{\nu+k}{\nu+K}\,t^Tr.$$

Under T48's normalized working kernel, r has the k-variate Student
density with degrees of freedom nu and identity scale, because the
residual N(0,v I_k) is mixed against IG(nu/2,nu/2). The standard
beta-prime radial transformation gives

$$X=\frac{K}{\nu+K}\sim\operatorname{Beta}(k/2,\nu/2),\qquad
E\!\left[\frac{K}{(\nu+K)^2}\right]
=\frac{E[X(1-X)]}{\nu}
=\frac{k}{(\nu+k)(\nu+k+2)}.$$

Spherical symmetry makes the score mean zero and gives
E[rr^T/(nu+K)^2]=I_k/[(nu+k)(nu+k+2)]. Thus the summed-score
working conditional variance is the closed form

$$C_{33,\mathrm{model}}(\phi)
=\sum_i\rho_i\|R_iP_i'U_i\|^2,\qquad
\rho_i=\frac{\nu_i+k_i}{\nu_i+k_i+2}.$$

It is finite for every nu_i>0, without requiring a finite residual
covariance, and tends to section 1's Gaussian C as nu_i tends to
infinity. Independence between readings supplies the sum. An exact-zero
reading has U=t=0 and contributes zero. Derivatives and C transform
with the coordinate derivative and its square, respectively.

The `factors="flat"` control in `met/eta_conditional.py` means precisely
section 1's **known unit-Gaussian** model, with
C_flat=sum_i||R_iP_i'U_i||^2. Its variance does not use nu_eff.
A flat score averaged under a finite-nu Student residual kernel would
instead contribute nu/(nu-2)*||t||^2 when nu>2 and have infinite
variance for nu<=2; that is a different model, not this control.

#### Why this is not the actual fixed-signal conditional under shared V

At a fixed true candidate in the forward world, let p be the k parallel
coordinates and mu their unknown deterministic mean. Conditional on V,
p-mu and r are independent N(0,V I_k); mixing their **shared** V does
not preserve that independence. With delta=||p-mu||^2, Bayes' rule gives

$$V\mid p,\mu\sim
\operatorname{IG}((\nu+k)/2,(\nu+\delta)/2).$$

This is the standard conditional multivariate Student calculation;
see Ding (2016), [section 3, Conclusion One](https://arxiv.org/html/1604.00561),
for the increased degrees of freedom and the projection-dependent scale.

Consequently, for X~Beta(k/2,(nu+k)/2), the actual conditional variance
of this same score is

$$C_{33,\mathrm{true}}(p,\mu)
=\frac{(\nu+k)^2\|t\|^2}{k}
E\!\left[\frac{(\nu+\delta)X(1-X)}{(\nu+\delta X)^2}\right].$$

This is one-dimensional beta quadrature, but it depends on the unknown
mu. For example at nu=10,k=1 its coefficient per ||t||^2 is
0.7922619047619 at delta=0 and 1.1325580686424 at delta=9, whereas the
working-kernel coefficient is 11/13. T45's nuisance-free Gaussian
sampling identity therefore cannot simply be carried over to finite nu.
The computable C_model follows from the probe's stipulated T48 measure;
it is not a new signal population or a claim that this measure is forced.

All these formulas first concern a **fixed candidate** under its null.
B15 then evaluates C_model(phi) at the fitted phi_hat as a plug-in
variance estimate and studies min(1,curvature/C_model). That does not
make it the actual conditional variance of the score at the random
fitted candidate: its summed score is stationary there. Neither the
plug-in nor the cap establishes a finite-sample coverage theorem.
The checks are `tests/test_eta_conditional.py`; the measurement and
its failures belong to O14, not an adoption into the equation.

## 3. The spectrum connects the set to T43, without selecting a law

In the standardized scalar common-noise case use $u_\theta=(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$,
$v_\theta=u_\theta'$, $\theta=\arctan m$, and scatter M. Then

$$A=u_\theta^TMu_\theta,\quad D=u_\theta^TMv_\theta,\qquad
T(\theta)=D/\sqrt A.$$

For positive definite scatter with eigenvalues $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2>0$,
$H=\lambda_1-\lambda_2$, and principal angle $\widehat\theta$,

$$T^2=\frac{H^2 z(1-z)}{\lambda_1-Hz},\qquad
z=\sin^2(\theta-\widehat\theta),$$

$$T'=\frac{\det M-A^2}{A^{3/2}},\qquad
\max_{\text{projective line}}T^2=(\sqrt{\lambda_1}-\sqrt{\lambda_2})^2.$$

In particular the pivot vanishes at both principal and minor eigenlines.
It need not be monotone on the positive-mass quadrant. For example scatter
with eigenvalues 100 and 1 and principal angle −0.1 has a 95% acceptance
set containing angle 0.01 and its minor eigenline $\pi/2-0.1$, but excluding
angles 0.6 and $\pi/2-0.01$. This is a disconnected positive-mass set, not
an equal-tailed interval of any law. The accepted minor line illustrates
limited power against a badly fitting stationary line; it is not a proposal
to select that line as a point estimate.

At the principal fit,

$$T'(\widehat\theta)=-H/\sqrt{\lambda_1}=-\sqrt{K_{\rm sp}}.$$

In T42's identifying Gaussian regime the principal fit approaches interior
truth and the positive-mass domain stays away from its perpendicular line.
The local inversion has endpoints
$\widehat\theta\pm z_p\sqrt{\lambda_1}/H+o_p(\sqrt{N+\Delta}/\Delta)$.
It therefore agrees asymptotically with T43's spectral-control intervals.
This gives a conditional-test explanation of that local width without a
signal-population model. It does not force a proper law whose global shape
has that width or settle all uses of spectral state under L19.

## 4. Direct null-corner obstruction for every proper mass law

Fix any finite valid series of declarations and any measurable procedure
returning a proper law on $(0,\infty)$ for almost every observation. Its
central p interval $[L_p(Y),U_p(Y)]$ has

$$0<L_p(Y)\le U_p(Y)<\infty\quad\text{almost surely}.$$

At all-zero **true** signals, the observation distribution $P_0$ is the
same nonsingular Gaussian law for every positive mass. Hence

$$P_0\{m\in[L_p(Y),U_p(Y)]\}
\le P_0\{U_p(Y)\ge m\}\longrightarrow0\quad(m\to\infty).$$

The infimum null coverage is exactly zero. No special form of π, η, weights
or readout was used. A finite returned point alone is not what proves this;
the finite upper endpoint follows from a proper positive mass law and a
central probability below one.

Excluding exact null signals does not restore a positive uniform guarantee
if arbitrarily weak nonzero signals remain allowed. At each fixed m, let
every compatible signal shrink to zero. The Gaussian observation laws
converge to $P_0$ in total variation, bounding the difference in probability
of the fixed measurable coverage event, regardless of any discontinuity of
the interval procedure. First choose m with null coverage below ε, then
choose nonzero signals with total variation below ε. The full infimum is
again zero. This argument holds separately at **every finite N**; it is not
a failure theorem along any fixed identifying long-series regime.

This proves the model-specific corollary sought in O15/B13 directly, rather
than asserting unverified hypotheses of the general Gleser–Hwang theorem.
Exact score sets evade it by admitting unbounded sets. There is no conflict
with prior-averaged calibration under a correctly specified random-mass
experiment, or with T43's asymptotic coverage under identifying growth.

## Sort and scope

**Operator continuation (T50).** For a specified matrix relation with full
free signal vectors, the same conditional-Gaussian argument gives a vector
score and its covariance matrix. The joint statistic is chi-square with
the observed covariance rank, not automatically the parameter count. The
scalar tangent I at M=mI recovers this theorem's scalar test. This extends
the exact benchmark, without supplying a matrix law, changing the scalar
physical hypothesis, or inferring true excitation from noisy conditional rank.

**Proved:** full declared Gaussian exact test coverage, invariances, absence
of signal-population borrowing, spectral local width, and the fixed-N
proper-law uniform-coverage obstruction. **Not established:** a selected
proper mass law, optimality or uniqueness of a confidence set, finite-ν
scale-mixture validity, T30's bounded-confidence requirements, or full-type
asymptotic law calibration.

Property N's direct ruling asks uncertainty to remain a true statement;
R-2026-09-30-7 chooses the quoted-law interval as the tester's scored interval.
Neither supplies the impossible universal finite-N proper-law guarantee.
The exact benchmark is therefore a research comparison under G26/G3, not
a replacement output or a reason to weaken the already attainable
identifying-regime target. No premise or ruling is changed.
