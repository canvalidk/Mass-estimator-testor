## 1. The separation argument is sound on the stated domain

Use the source's dimensionless positive integrable variables X and Y. The
readout is finite and positive. It obeys force additivity C1 and inverse-readout
acceleration additivity C2 for arbitrary common-channel couplings, depends only
on the magnitude-pair law C3, and is normalized by R(1,1)=1.

Write r=R(X,Y), x=R(X,1), and y=1/R(1,Y). Splitting R(X+1,Y+1) in the two
orders gives

    xr/(x+r) + 1/(y+1)
    (r+1/y)(x+1)/(r+1/y+x+1).

Their difference is

    -(ry-x)^2 / [(x+r)(y+1)(yr+1+xy+y)].

Every denominator factor is positive. Equality therefore gives ry=x, hence

    R(X,Y) = T(X)/S(Y),
    T(X)=R(X,1),  S(Y)=1/R(1,Y).

C1 makes T additive and C2 makes S additive. C3 makes each depend only on its
own variable's marginal law. This step uses no independence or particular
likelihood. I found no gap in this argument under its stated universal domain.

## 2. Strengthening: C5 is redundant on that universal domain

The source assumes constant-limit L1 continuity (C5). It follows already from
positivity, additivity, and availability of *every* positive finite-mean law.
Here is a proof for T; the same proof applies to S.

### Constants and order

An additive positive function of positive constants is increasing. Rational
approximation and T(1)=1 therefore imply T(c)=c for every real c>0. If X<=Y
almost surely, then for every epsilon>0,

    T(Y)+epsilon = T(X)+T(Y-X+epsilon) > T(X).

Thus T(Y)>=T(X). This conclusion needs no assumed continuity.

For nonnegative integrable Z define

    T0(Z) = T(Z+1)-1.

The choice of positive shift does not matter: additivity gives
T(Z+c)-c=T(Z+d)-d. T0 is nonnegative, additive, monotone, and agrees with T
on strictly positive inputs. Rational homogeneity follows by repeated addition;
real positive homogeneity follows by order and rational squeezing.

### Continuity at zero

Suppose nonnegative Z_n have E[Z_n] -> 0 while T0(Z_n) fails to tend to zero.
Take a subsequence, still called Z_n, for which

    E[Z_n] <= 2^(-n),   T0(Z_n) >= epsilon > 0.

Place these variables on a common probability space (for example by their
quantile functions of one uniform variable). This is permitted by law
dependence and the universal common-coupling domain. Let Z=sum_n Z_n.
Monotone convergence gives E[Z]<=1, so Z is finite almost surely and is an
allowed nonnegative integrable variable. Positivity and finite additivity give

    T0(Z) >= T0(sum_{n=1}^N Z_n) >= N epsilon

for every N. That contradicts finiteness of T0(Z). Therefore

    E[Z_n] -> 0  implies  T0(Z_n) -> 0.

If X_n -> c in L1, monotonicity and additivity give

    |T(X_n)-c| <= T0(|X_n-c|) -> 0.

This proves precisely the source's C5, without assuming it.

### Expectation then follows

For iid copies X_i of X, law dependence and rational homogeneity imply

    T((X_1+...+X_n)/n)=T(X).

The integrable iid sample mean converges in L1 to E[X]; the source supplies
an explicit truncation proof of this fact. The derived continuity gives
T(X)=E[X]. Likewise S(Y)=E[Y]. Hence R(X,Y)=E[X]/E[Y].

**Scope of the strengthening:** on the source's universal domain, C1/C2/C3,
finite positive output, and the one normalization R(1,1)=1 suffice. C4's
calibration on every deterministic pair and C5 are consequences. This is a
mathematical simplification, not an objection to the valid sufficient theorem.
The full-domain/coupling commitment is doing substantial work and should remain
explicit. This argument must not be silently applied to only the family of
posteriors generated by one Gaussian observation model.

## 3. An explicit, continuously tunable direction freedom

This extends the source's counterexample; it does not endorse a new estimator.
Let u be the shared unit direction, v=E_P[u], and s=(u dot v)^2. For any
finite lambda>=0 define

    h_lambda(u)=1+lambda s,
    R_lambda=E_P[h_lambda f]/E_P[h_lambda alpha].

The original point is lambda=0. Since 0<=s<=1, all required expectations remain
finite and denominators positive. If the same common direction law is retained
when adding forces or accelerations, h_lambda is unchanged; both composition
identities hold. It also retains channel reciprocity, independent unit
covariance, simultaneous rotation invariance, and exactness when f=m alpha
almost surely. For a magnitude approaching constant c in L1,

    |E[h_lambda X]/E[h_lambda]-c|
       <= (1+lambda) E[|X-c|],

so the source's constant-limit continuity holds too. It violates C3 by using
the association between magnitudes and direction after P has been constructed.

Define the acceleration-weighted probability law Q_alpha by
dQ_alpha=alpha dP/E_P[alpha], and let M=f/alpha. Then R_0=E_Q_alpha[M] and

    R_lambda - R_0
      = lambda Cov_Q_alpha(s,M) / (1+lambda E_Q_alpha[s]).

This is an exact sensitivity formula. The freedom changes the point precisely
when the selected direction feature and mass co-vary under Q_alpha. It has no
effect for a fixed-mass law, nor when that covariance vanishes. The derivative
at lambda=0 is Cov_Q_alpha(s,M).

For the source's two-state law,

    P(u=e1,f=1,alpha=1)=3/4,
    P(u=e2,f=2,alpha=1)=1/4,

the whole family is

    R_lambda=(80+29 lambda)/(64+28 lambda).

R_0=5/4, R_1=109/92, and dR_lambda/dlambda at zero is -3/32.

The important open question is the warrant for C3: once the magnitude law has
been induced by the measurement model, should any additional direction-based
tilt be excluded? Direction information already affects that induced magnitude
law; C3 does not tell us to ignore direction in constructing P.

## 4. The point necessarily discards coupling information

Once the magnitude marginals are fixed, the theorem's readout is fixed even
when the induced mass uncertainty changes completely. Compare equally likely
two-state laws in consistent units:

    P_plus:  (f,alpha)=(1,1) or (9,9).
    P_minus: (f,alpha)=(1,9) or (9,1).

Both have exactly the same force and acceleration marginals and both produce
R=5/5=1. Under P_plus, M=1 with certainty. Under P_minus, M is 1/9 or 9 with
equal probabilities; its centered log standard deviation is log(9).

Consequently a claim that the scalar equation uses all available information
would be false at the readout level. Some compression is part of what the
composition theorem enforces. An estimator output carrying uncertainty must
retain information from the joint law beyond the two means. This example does
not by itself criticize the point estimate or compare complete estimators.

## 5. Operational issues still outside the proof

- C1/C2 concern regrouping one propagated law. They do not identify that law
  with a fresh posterior fitted to new measurements of a combined object.
- Actual scalar addition requires the relevant common direction/sign
  conventions. Newtonian vector addition does not license adding arbitrary
  force magnitudes.
- The empirical claim that the definition's point summary must commute with
  both compositions remains a substantive commitment. Statewise Newton II
  alone does not force an uncertain quantity's point summary to have either
  property.
- The theorem applies over arbitrary couplings and all positive finite-mean
  laws, including constant-channel auxiliary problems. The intended physical
  operation must justify this extension, or a restricted-domain result is
  needed.
- It selects neither the reference measure nor the likelihood producing P,
  and it does not select a common-mass model for multiple experiments.

No flaw in the separation proof has been established. The derived C5 result,
the direction sensitivity identity, and the coupling example are independent
mathematical contributions. They do not settle the remaining operational
choices or amount to a ratified specification change.
