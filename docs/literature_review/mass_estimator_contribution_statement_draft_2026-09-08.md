# Positive-vector mass estimator: contribution statement draft

Date: 2026-09-08. Status: local active draft, awaiting transfer to VD-docs; **not submitted**. This is proposed wording for the research account, not a certification of historical priority or a record of independent specialist review.

## Proposed contribution statement

We develop a single prescription for inferring a positive inertial mass from a measured force vector, a measured acceleration vector, and their joint Gaussian measurement-error covariance, conditional on Newton's second law holding for the underlying true vectors. The construction assigns likelihood weight to compatible true pairs, uses a specified flat measure over their positive magnitudes and common direction, reports the ratio of posterior mean magnitudes as its mass estimate, and supplies the induced positive mass distribution for uncertainty. The mathematical prescription remains defined for every finite pair of readings and every positive-definite covariance, including measured zeros and directional disagreement. Its point estimate is finite, strictly positive and continuous; its uncertainty can remain broad and heavy-tailed.

The contribution proposed here is this particular combination of measure, readout, uncertainty law and computational prescription, together with its analysis and application to Newtonian mass inference. Bayesian errors-in-variables modelling, positive-parameter estimation, ratio distributions and weighted expectations have substantial precedents, which we acknowledge. **To our knowledge, the complete construction has not previously been reported in the literature we have examined.** This is a qualified claim about the combined method. Our review does not establish separate first-ever claims for the algebraic quotient, the prior form, or force–acceleration mass estimation in general.

## Exact object of the proposed claim

For finite observations $y=(\mathbf F,\mathbf a)\in\mathbb R^{2d}$, known $\Sigma\succ0$, positive magnitudes $f,\alpha$, and common direction $u\in S^{d-1}$, define

$$
dP(f,\alpha,u\mid y,\Sigma)
=Z^{-1}\exp\!\left[-\frac12\{y-(fu,\alpha u)\}^{T}
\Sigma^{-1}\{y-(fu,\alpha u)\}\right]df\,d\alpha\,d\Omega(u).
$$

The chosen base measure is part of the method. It is not an assumption-free consequence of $\mathbf F^*=m\mathbf a^*$. The outputs are

$$
\widehat m=\frac{E_P[f]}{E_P[\alpha]},\qquad
M=\frac f\alpha\sim P_M,
$$

with interior quantiles of $P_M$ used to describe uncertainty. The point is also the mean of $M$ under the joint posterior weighted by $\alpha/E_P[\alpha]$. It is not the ordinary posterior mean of mass: under this measure, $p_M(m)\sim C/m^2$ at large positive $m$, so that mean diverges. The point and uncertainty distribution must be labelled accordingly.

The computational prescription analytically integrates a radial amplitude and numerically integrates the remaining direction and ratio angle. The current reference implementation supports dimensions 1–3. Mathematical existence and continuity do not certify the accuracy of fixed-resolution quadrature or floating-point arithmetic for every possible input and condition number. Existing tests and their refinement evidence are recorded in the comparison reviews; no claim of universal numerical certification is made.

## Scope of the claims

At two measured zero vectors with independent isotropic channel errors, the chosen measure gives a half-Cauchy mass distribution with scale $\sigma_F/\sigma_a$, and $\widehat m=\sigma_F/\sigma_a$. This illustrates a defined conditional answer determined by measurement scales and the measure; it does not identify a physical mass from uninformative readings.

The procedure assumes the positive physical relation throughout the declared Gaussian domain. It does not apply an empirical rejection gate, estimate whether Newton's law is true, or generally select the minimum-error or maximum-likelihood reconciliation. Exact contradictory uncertainty-free inputs are outside that domain.

The separate [composition characterization](mass_readout_composition_uniqueness_2026-09-06.md) concerns the readout under its explicitly stated composition, magnitude-law, calibration, continuity and domain-closure assumptions. It does not uniquely determine the posterior measure or establish that Newton's law alone requires this estimator.
