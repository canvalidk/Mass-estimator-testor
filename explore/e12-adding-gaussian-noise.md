# Can adding known Gaussian noise select a mass law?

Use the same exact signal for both routes. In common whitened units a fine reading is \(X_s=\mu+\varepsilon_s\) with \(\varepsilon_s\sim N(0,sI)\). Add independent \(N(0,(t-s)I)\) noise to obtain \(Y_t\), where \(0<s<t\). Its law at each fixed \(\mu\) is exactly that of the coarse instrument. For scalar force and acceleration, the possible signals at mass angle \(\theta\) are \(\mu=b u_\theta^\top\) with \(u_\theta=(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)\) and an arbitrary signed list \(b\) of true pushes in noise-metric graph units. Two distinct mass lines give complementary signal subspaces in the space of \(n\) rows. This is deliberate noise added to an existing record and supplies no new reading conditional on that record. Both forward routes already have the same distribution for any rule applied to \(Y_t\). The additional question is how the *fine mass law* should determine the coarse one. [Comparison of experiments](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729032) · [Physical signal graph](../definition/theorems/T36.md)

**Forward degradation can be done from the Gram alone.** For scalar independent isotropic rows \(y\) of dimension 2, conditional on the fine record the coarse Gram is \(G'=(y+\sqrt{t-s}E)^\top(y+\sqrt{t-s}E)\), with independent unit normal entries of \(E\). If two fine records have the same Gram \(y^\top y\), an orthogonal map on the row index takes one to the other and preserves the law of \(E\). Thus the Gram specifies the exact distribution of the coarse Gram. Conversely any retained statistic that can simulate that full distribution must distinguish the fine Gram, since \(E[G'\mid y]=y^\top y+n(t-s)I\). This is a statement about a simulation kernel. It gives no additional probability inside a coarse mass law.

## Conditioning back under each hidden preparation precludes learning

Suppose the instrument gives a probability law \(Q_s(\cdot\mid x)\) on the same mass space at the fine noise level. Require its mean over the possible fine data, conditional on the observed coarse data, to equal one \(Q_t(\cdot\mid y)\) at **every** fixed exact preparation \(\mu\). This asks the rule to know how to average back without being told the push. The actual Gaussian bridge is
\[
 X_s\mid(Y_t=y,\mu)\ \sim\
 N(cy+(1-c)\mu,\ vI),\qquad c=s/t,\quad v=s(1-c).
\]
Write \(M(z)\) for the fine law averaged over \(N(z,vI)\). For each coarse record \(y\) the required identity says that \(M\) is constant, with value \(Q_t(\cdot\mid y)\), on each affine space \(cy+(1-c)G_\theta\), where \(G_\theta\) is the signal graph for \(\theta\). The equalities can be imposed on a common full-measure set of coarse records by first taking countable dense sets of pushes on two graphs. Gaussian convolution is continuous in total variation even for a measurable probability-valued readout, so the equalities then extend to all pushes for those records.

For any two possible bridge centers \(z_1,z_2\), write \(z_1-z_2=(1-c)(\mu_1-\mu_2)\) with \(\mu_i\) in the two distinct graphs. Some common \(y\) has these two bridge centers. Equivalently, the two affine graphs from any two coarse records always intersect. It follows that the coarse output is constant almost everywhere, and continuity of \(M\) forces \(M\) constant everywhere. Gaussian convolution is injective on bounded functions. To see this, for a bounded function with zero convolution, test its Fourier transform as a tempered distribution against compactly supported smooth functions divided by the everywhere nonzero Gaussian transform. Apply this to the fine probabilities of a countable generating class of mass events. The fine law is also constant almost everywhere. If it is continuous and obeys measured-zero neutrality, this constant is the starting law. The theorem allows arbitrary dependence of the readout on that prior and uses only one pair \(s<t\).

**Exact error for a probability of one mass event.** For any \(f(x)=Q_s(B\mid x)\) bounded between 0 and 1 put \(H(z)=E_{N(z,vI)}f\). If the two signal graphs above are allowed, the smallest uniform error over all coarse functions \(g(y)\in[0,1]\) in reproducing \(H(cy+(1-c)\mu)\) is
\[
 \inf_g\ \sup_{y,\mu}|H(cy+(1-c)\mu)-g(y)|
           =\tfrac12(\sup_z H(z)-\inf_z H(z)).
\]
Indeed any two centers are realized by two preparations at the same coarse record, and the midpoint of the full range attains the bound. In an essential-supremum version use dense sets and continuity to take limits on a common full-measure set of records. In particular uniform total variation error \(\epsilon\) at all preparations allows coarse laws at two records to differ by at most \(2\epsilon\), by comparing both to the bridge law at the intersection. This is a restriction on preparation-by-preparation conditional averaging, a stronger condition than calibration at the true mass or neutrality on one observed zero.

## Averaging under one joint predictive law recovers a preparation model

There is another physically meaningful tower identity. Choose the predictive law \(q_s(dx)\) of the fine reading, propagate it through the declared Gaussian noise, and use the resulting conditional distribution of the fine reading given the coarse one. Require this tower for all positive noise variances with arbitrarily fine readings, along with \(q_t=q_s*\phi_{t-s}\). Here \(\phi_t\) denotes Gaussian density of variance \(tI\).

**Representation.** All pairs of such predictive laws and mass updates arise by choosing one joint law \(J_0(dm,d\mu)\) for mass and exact signal, adding \(N(0,tI)\), and conditioning. If exact signals are required to lie on their own candidate graph, \(J_0\) supplies a conditional law of the true pushes given the mass. The existence of the tower leaves that law free.

To prove this, put \(J_t(dm,dy)=Q_t(dm\mid y)q_t(dy)\). The tower on every mass event gives \(J_t=J_s*\phi_{t-s}\) in the data coordinate. Use \(\log m\) as the real mass coordinate. The joint characteristic function of \(J_t\) multiplied by \(\exp(t\|\xi\|^2/2)\), where \(\xi\) is the data frequency, is independent of \(t\). This common function is continuous at zero by its expression at any one \(t>0\). Taking \(t\downarrow0\) and applying the continuity theorem gives a unique probability law \(J_0\) with this transform. Consequently \(J_t=J_0*\phi_t\). The converse follows by the ordinary conditional expectation identity. The mass marginal is common across all noise levels. Requiring it to be \(\pi\), the declared prior, is equivalent to imposing that marginal on \(J_0\). Exact graph support is a separate physical requirement on the joint law at variance zero. Since \(q_t\) has positive density, the Gaussian likelihood gives a canonical continuous version of the report at every coarse record. The fixed-data claims below use this version, which any continuous rule satisfying the tower must match. [Lévy's continuity theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.01603)

**Two coherent odds for the same data.** At each candidate take independent \(N(0,\tau)\) exact drive coordinates on the graph, with the same \(\tau>0\) in physical reference units at every noise level. The longitudinal covariance is then \(t+\tau\) and the transverse covariance is \(t\). All these models pass the route test. The prior-relative mass factor is proportional to
\[
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{\tau}{t+\tau}\frac{K_m(y)}{2t}\right],
 \qquad K_m(y)=\|R_m y\|^2 ,
\]
where \(R_m\) projects orthogonally off the candidate graph. For one row \((F,A)=(3,1)\) at \(t=1\), the costs at masses 1 and 2 are \(2\) and \(1/5\). The log odds gained by 2 over 1 are \(0.45\) at \(\tau=1\) and \(0.72\) at \(\tau=4\). A measured zero multiplies every candidate by the same factor, including when appended as an independent row. These examples meet Gaussian route agreement and measured-zero neutrality. Choosing either \(\tau\) supplies a push law of the kind withheld by L10 and, for independent draws across pushes, L19. [Reading premises](../definition/premises.md) · [Previous odds obstruction](e12-conditioning-nuisance.md)

Zero-readout neutrality also has a precise meaning for any singleton preparation in this class. At observed zero its factor is proportional to \(E[e^{-D_m^2/(2t)}\mid m]\), with \(D_m=u_m^\top\mu\) the signed exact graph coordinate of the prepared push. Equality of these likelihoods across masses for all \(t>0\) means that \(D_m^2\) has the same conditional law at almost every mass by Laplace uniqueness, taking a common full-measure set over a countable dense collection of \(t\)'s. If instead the *physical acceleration* has one common distribution independent of mass, \(D_m=\sqrt{1+m^2}\,A_{\rm true}\) in these units. Equality at two unequal masses then forces \(A_{\rm true}=0\) almost surely, since a nondegenerate absolute acceleration cannot have the same law after two distinct positive dilations. Thus exact-zero neutrality recovers a common distribution of graph lengths while leaving its shape free.

## Exact graph support forces collapse on some fixed conflict

This obstruction holds for every joint preparation law above with any nonzero signal on a scalar block of at least two rows. It requires neither a density nor full support of the push law or mass prior. Let the signal marginal have in its support a nonzero physical signal \(\mu_*=b u_*^\top\) at an interior positive mass angle \(\theta_*\). Such a point exists if the preparation is graph supported and assigns positive probability to nonzero signals. Choose a nonzero list \(w\perp b\) with \(\|w\|<\|b\|\) and let \(v_*\perp u_*\) be a unit channel covector identified with its vector by the noise metric. Use the **fixed** observed block
\[
                y_* = b u_*^\top + w v_*^\top .
\]
Its rows are incompatible with every common mass, since it has rank two. It can be arbitrarily close to \(\mu_*\). In the candidate channel coordinates its minimum squared distance over allowed pushes is
\[
 K_\theta(y_*)=\|w\|^2+
      (\|b\|^2-\|w\|^2)\sin^2(\theta-\theta_*).
\]
The only closest exact signal is \(\mu_*\). In particular the squared distance at every mass outside a neighbourhood of \(\theta_*\) exceeds \(K_{\theta_*}\) by a positive gap \(\delta\), because the physical arc contains each projective line at most once and \(\theta_*\) is interior. The evidence integral on that complement is at most \(e^{-(K_{\theta_*}+\delta)/(2t)}\) after factoring out the Gaussian prefactor. A small ball of signals about \(\mu_*\) has positive preparation probability \(a\) and squared distances below \(K_{\theta_*}+\delta/2\), giving a denominator at least \(a e^{-(K_{\theta_*}+\delta/2)/(2t)}\). Thus the law at \(y_*\) concentrates at \(\theta_*\) as \(t\downarrow0\). For example the strictly positive rows \((4,1),(1,4)\) arise by this construction if \(((2.5,2.5),(2.5,2.5))\) is in the preparation support.

Consequently a proper predictive tower over all precisions, exact Newton graph support, and retained spread on **every** fixed incompatible block force an all-zero signal preparation and its data-independent mass law on that block size. With measured-zero append neutrality this would also force the singleton law to return the prior. The conflict is with combining the tower and an exact-signal model with no intrinsic departures. It applies even if someone supplies the missing conditional drive law. [Earlier spread test](e12-product-obstruction.md) · [Executable checks](../tests/test_e12_added_noise.py)

Allowing a physical source of departures from the exact graph changes the result. For a proper example mix a smooth positive graph preparation at interior angles and drives with a component where \(m\sim\pi\) and the latent signal has an independent continuous positive data density. Use the same mass marginal for the graph component. Both components propagate through the very same sensor covariance. On a fixed conflict at positive distance from the closed graph the graph evidence tends to zero exponentially, and the second component leaves the spread of \(\pi\). At a compatible nonzero interior record the graph component contributes at least order \(t^{-(n-1)/2}\) to predictive density. A parameter ball of radius \(\sqrt t\) in the \(n+1\) mass and drive dimensions gives mass of order \(t^{(n+1)/2}\), multiplied by the \(2n\)-dimensional Gaussian normalization \(t^{-n}\). Its mass concentrates at the compatible angle by the same gap bound, and for \(n\ge2\) the other component has bounded predictive density there, e.g. for a Gaussian latent data density. This construction uses a new physical departure model and leaves its law and weight free.

The directional result that survives unknown fixed pushes is transport of candidate-specific covector tests. Averaging normalized reports without a predictive law and convolving raw residual factors give different conditions. [Covector transport and residual factors](e12-noise-covectors.md)
