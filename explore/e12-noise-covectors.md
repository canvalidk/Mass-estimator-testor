# What known added noise preserves in a covector test

On the common scalar Gaussian instrument write \(X_s\) for fine data with error covariance \(sI\) in the chosen noise-metric coordinates. Set \(Y_t=X_s+\varepsilon\), with independent error of covariance \((t-s)I\). Fix one candidate \(m\) and let \(G_m\) be its exact-signal graph and \(R_m\) its orthogonal residual projection. In force–acceleration coordinates this projection measures the force covector that annihilates \(F=mA\), in declared noise units. [Reciprocal graph geometry](../definition/theorems/T36.md) · [The mass-law obstruction](e12-adding-gaussian-noise.md)

## Exactly what transports without the drive

A bounded fine statistic \(f_s(X_s)\) has one conditional expectation given the full \(Y_t\) **at every fixed drive on this candidate's graph** precisely when \(f_s\) is a function of \(R_m X_s\), up to Gaussian null sets. Indeed its Gaussian smoothing at the bridge center must be invariant under translations in \(G_m\). Gaussian convolution is injective and commutes with translations, so the same invariance holds for the original statistic in the almost-everywhere sense. For example convolve with narrower Gaussians to obtain smooth translation-invariant functions, then take the local almost-everywhere limit by the Gaussian approximate identity. Fubini in longitudinal and transverse coordinates gives a version depending only on the residual. Conversely the transverse bridge has mean \((s/t)R_mY_t\), independent of the drive.

Write \(q=\operatorname{rank}R_m\), \(K_a=\|R_m X_a\|^2/a\) at variance \(a\), using \(X_t=Y_t\), and \(\rho=\sqrt{s/t}\). Under the candidate,
\[
 K_s\mid Y_t\ \sim\
 (1-\rho^2)\,\chi'^2_q\!\left(\frac{\rho^2 K_t}{1-\rho^2}\right),
 \qquad E[K_s-q\mid Y_t]=\rho^2(K_t-q).
\]
Project the Gaussian bridge and divide by \(\sqrt{s}\) to obtain this formula. In particular, at the coarse reading, the conditional probability that the *fine* level-\(\alpha\) residual test would reject is
\[
 \Pr\!\left\{\chi'^2_q\!\left(\frac{\rho^2K_t}{1-\rho^2}\right)
                  > \frac{\chi^2_{q,1-\alpha}}{1-\rho^2}\right\}.
\]
Rejecting with this probability has exactly size \(\alpha\) under every fixed candidate drive. It uses the declared added variance and the coarse covector alone. It gives the probability of a test action under candidate-specific sampling. Odds over different masses would require a further operation. The signed score and the conditional likelihood-ratio rank generally also depend on the fitted longitudinal coordinates, so the bounded-statistic classification excludes their direct transport under the fixed-drive requirement. [Existing residual test](e12-testing-a-mass-ray.md) · [Conditional likelihood-ratio ranks](e12-conditional-likelihood-ratio.md)

There is an additional obstruction for any exact probability rank. If \(P_s(X_s)\) and \(P_t(Y_t)\) are both uniform under a given true candidate and \(P_t=E[P_s\mid Y_t]\), equal variances force \(P_s=P_t\) almost surely. The conditional law of \(X_s\) has an everywhere positive density for each \(Y_t\). This makes a fine statistic determined by \(Y_t\) constant almost everywhere, contradicting uniformity. The condition also fails for replacing the coarse uniform rank by a super-uniform one. Its expectation is still \(1/2\) by the tower, and among super-uniform laws on \([0,1]\) only uniform attains that mean. Coarse recalibration of a diagnostic and conditional averaging of an already calibrated rank are different operations.

With independent added noise of arbitrary full-rank covariance \(\Gamma\), the fine bridge mean is \(B y+(I-B)\mu\) for \(B=\Sigma(\Sigma+\Gamma)^{-1}\), where \(\Sigma\) is the fine covariance. The invariant directions for bounded fine statistics are now \((I-B)G_m\). In common fine units \(\Sigma=I\), one added covariance proportional to \(I\) and another with a different increment in each channel can give two distinct directions for the same scalar mass. A statistic required to average back without the drive for **both** choices must then be constant. For example fine \(\Sigma=I\), added \(\Gamma=\operatorname{diag}(3,1)\), mass 1 and \(Y_t=0\) give the fine contrast \((F-A)/\sqrt2\) conditional variance \(5/8\) and conditional mean \(a/(4\sqrt2)\) at physical drive \(a\). The conditional expectation of \(1-\sqrt2 e^{-(F-A)^2/4}\) is \(1-(4/\sqrt{13})e^{-a^2/104}\). Conditioning on only the coarse covector still gives a drive-free quotient experiment because both fine and added covectors annihilate the exact candidate signal, so their projected means remain zero.

## Exact evidence for a specified transverse departure

Let \(e_s(r)\ge0\) be a mean-one evidence statistic under the Gaussian candidate residual \(r\sim N(0,sI_q)\). Under the candidate's residual bridge require \(e_t(R_mY_t)=E[e_s(R_mX_s)\mid Y_t]\) at every \(0<s<t\). Put \(q_s(r)=e_s(r)\phi^{(q)}_s(r)\). Bayes' rule for the centered Gaussian residual shows \(q_t=q_s*\phi^{(q)}_{t-s}\). By the same characteristic-function argument as for joint predictive laws, the entire class is given by one arbitrary probability law \(\nu\) of fixed transverse displacements:
\[
 e_s(r)=\int\exp\!\left[\frac{r^\top h}{s}-\frac{\|h\|^2}{2s}\right]\nu(dh).
\]
These are likelihood ratios on the residual space. A positive lower bound on some projection of a bounded set of displacements of positive \(\nu\)-probability would make the ratio grow without bound along that projection. Therefore the only member bounded in the observation at even one noise level is \(\nu=\delta_0\), with \(e_s=1\).

For a specified alternative Gaussian reference mean \(b\) in the original channels, the single displacement \(h=R_m b\) uniquely maximizes expected log evidence under the reference. For any competitor \(e\) with null mean at most one, \(E_{\rm ref}(e/e_*)=E_{\rm null}e\le1\) in residual coordinates, where \(e_*=\phi_s(r-h)/\phi_s(r)\). Jensen gives \(E_{\rm ref}\log(e/e_*)\le0\) with equality only at \(e=e_*\). The expected log gain is \(\|R_m b\|^2/(2s)\). This also upper-bounds what a full-data e-value can achieve under that reference subject to validity at every fixed null drive. Use the simple null mean \(P_m b\), which has the same longitudinal distribution as the reference. The specified reference makes the optimal statistic explicit. The added covariance leaves the reference displacement or its law free. [Reference-comparison criterion](e12-residual-bets.md)

## Convolving raw residual factors with the added noise

Gaussian averaging is linear on densities. It can be imposed directly on unnormalized candidate factors \(D_{n,t}(m;y)\) as an extra premise. This choice fixes the scaling of those factors across noise levels, whereas the returned mass laws see only same-record ratios. Consider positive factors which depend on the unstandardized total residual \(x=\|R_my\|^2\) through a function \(f_{n,t}(x)\), where \(n\) is the number of scalar rows. Assume a common function of \(x\) on the given instrument for all candidates. Require enough differentiability for the heat equation \(\partial_t f_{n,t}=2x\partial_x^2 f_{n,t}+n\partial_x f_{n,t}\) at \(x>0\), as follows from adding variance on the \(n\) residual coordinates. Fix a full-support mass prior, and require exact measured-zero neutrality **of the normalized mass law** at each \(t\).

**Classification.** The factors are constant in \(x\), or their shape is \(e^{-x/[2(t+a)]}\) with one fixed additive variance \(a\ge0\) shared by the block lengths. The two conclusions use all positive noise levels. To see this, adding an exactly measured zero leaves \(x\) unchanged. Thus \(f_{n+1,t}/f_{n,t}\) must be constant on the candidate costs realized by any one block. Axis rows with diagonal scatter realize every interval \((u,v)\), \(0<u<v\), at lengths \(n\ge2\). At \(n=1\) an axis row realizes \((0,v)\) for arbitrary \(v>0\). Overlapping intervals force the ratios constant in \(x\). Write \(f_{n,t}=c_n(t)f_t\) for the common shape. Divide by \(c_n(t)f_t\) and subtract the heat equations for two successive lengths. It follows that \(\partial_x\log f_t\) is independent of \(x\). For \(f_t=C(t)e^{-\lambda(t)x}\), matching the coefficient of \(x\) gives \(\lambda'=-2\lambda^2\), whose only solutions finite on all \(t>0\) are zero and \(1/[2(t+a)]\) with \(a\ge0\). These shapes are realized by constant factors or Gaussian densities of variance \(t+a\) on the residual space.

The raw profile is the \(a=0\) member. It can collapse on fixed contradictory readings as noise vanishes. For \(a>0\) even a compatible fixed nonzero reading tends to a finite tilted law with positive spread as \(t\downarrow0\) when the starting prior has full support. Then finite-width recovery of an exact compatible point fails. Here the measured-zero premise, heat transport, and a total-residual factor leave no shape that both learns at zero noise on compatible data and keeps spread on every incompatible block. For independent repeats at one true nonzero graph length \(r\), the principal-line sampling variance is asymptotically \(t(t+r^2)/(n r^4)\) in angle. Its derivation uses the off-diagonal scatter variance \(nt(t+r^2)\) and expected diagonal gap \(nr^2\). The factor with floor \(a\) has local variance \((t+a)/(nr^2)\). Matching one fixed central coverage level requires \(a=t^2/r^2\), so a fixed floor also fails to calibrate at two regular push lengths on the same instrument. [Scalar calibration](e12-odds-geometry.md)

If measured-zero append neutrality is relaxed, both
\[
     D_{n,t}(m;y)=\phi^{(n)}_t(R_my)+c\,\phi^{(n)}_{t+a}(R_my)
                    \qquad(a,c>0)
\]
and every choice of \(a,c\) satisfy raw Gaussian convolution. Here \(\phi^{(n)}_t\) is the residual-space Gaussian density. The second component is already spread out in directions normal to the graph at zero sensor noise. At a fixed block with strictly positive minimum residual the first term vanishes uniformly and the second gives finite spread. At an interior compatible nonzero block with \(n\ge2\), the integral of the first component against a continuous positive angular prior grows as \(t^{-(n-1)/2}\), so the mass law concentrates there. Appending a zero row reweights the two components differently, since the corresponding one-dimensional Gaussian factors at zero differ. This is an explicit family showing what raw convolution alone permits.

**Arbitrary covariance increments give a stronger restriction.** Suppose the same fine factors obey raw Gaussian convolution for all independent positive-definite added row covariances \(\Gamma_i\). Returning the prior at every coarse all-zero record then requires the integrals
\[
      \int D_m(y)\prod_i\phi_{\Gamma_i}(y_i)\,dy
\]
to agree across masses for every such increment. These integrals determine the sign-averaged factors. For a proof, tilt \(D_m(y)dy\) by \(\exp(-\sum_i\|y_i\|^2/2)\) to obtain a finite measure. Under the map \(y\mapsto(y_i y_i^\top)_i\), its moment-generating function on a neighbourhood of zero consists of exactly the Gaussian integrals above with changed positive definite precisions and known constants. Transform uniqueness determines the pushed-forward measure. Its fibres are precisely independent row sign reversals, so equality of those measures fixes the sign averages. This argument also works for a continuous prior and equality of factors almost everywhere in mass. Take a countable dense set of covariance probes and then extend by continuity, using domination by a slightly broader Gaussian. If the *normalized mass law* is invariant under independent row sign reversals, its relative factor for any pair of masses is constant on those fibres. Equality of the sign-averaged factors therefore forces that ratio to be one. Thus even factors with no total-residual restriction give the starting law under these stronger assumptions. The result uses arbitrary covariance matrices including off-diagonal probes and a common scaling of the unnormalized factors through the convolution relation.

## Direct Gaussian averaging of normalized laws

One can bypass reverse conditioning and simply mandate \(Q_{n,t}(y)=\int Q_{n,s}(y+z)\phi_{t-s}(z)\,dz\), where \(z\) includes independent added noise per row. This is an unweighted smoothing formula for the **normalized law**. For every length, also require neutrality under appending a measured zero at both levels and permutation invariance at the fine level. Already one pair \(s<t\) suffices to preclude learning.

For any mass event \(B\), apply neutrality at \((y,0)\) and commute the Gaussian averages in the \(n+1\) rows. Injectivity of convolution on the first \(n\) coordinates gives
\[
 Q_{n,s}(B\mid y)=\int Q_{n+1,s}(B\mid y,z)\phi_{t-s}(z)\,dz
\]
almost everywhere. Feed the rules artificial independent \(N(0,(t-s)I)\) rows. The displayed identity gives the expected readout after revealing each row. By permutation invariance, the readout based on any block of \(n\) rows is the conditional expectation of the readout based on \(kn\) rows given that block. Centered readouts on \(k\) disjoint blocks are orthogonal as functions of the artificial independent rows. The centered final readout has these as its orthogonal projections. Therefore \(k\,\operatorname{Var}(Q_{n,s}(B))\le \operatorname{Var}(Q_{kn,s}(B))\le1/4\). Letting \(k\) grow makes the fine probabilities constant almost surely for each length, and full support of the artificial rows gives Lebesgue-almost-everywhere constancy. Coarse smoothing gives constancy everywhere. If the fine law is continuous and returns the prior at zero, both constants are the prior. This argument never uses a law of true pushes.

The exact transport of candidate residual tests survives proportionate Gaussian degradation. The proposed consistency of the full normalized law either requires a predictive preparation model or imposes a condition that erases mass information. Even the weaker test on raw factors adds substantive restrictions when compared with the project's measured-zero and learning requirements.
