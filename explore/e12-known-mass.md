## What ordinary addition forces

Let \(y=(F,A)\), \(S_b=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&b\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) acting componentwise, and \(M=m+b\). The same record, expressed for the total, is \(y'=S_by=(F+bA,A)\) with covariance \(S_b\Sigma S_b^\top\). The candidate transverse defect is exactly \(F+bA-MA=F-mA\). The reciprocal tangent correction in physical mass units, its noise norm and the entire cost curve therefore agree. The possible total masses are \(M>b\). The starting law of the total is the translate of the given starting law of the body. Already at a measured zero, using a fresh full-support default on \(M>0\) would give positive probability to \(M<b\). This is a computation with the same accelerometer error on both routes. L5 distinguishes it from taking a new measurement. [General tensor proof, including correlated errors](e12-known-mass-geometry.md) · [L5](../definition/premises.md)

Here is a general reason for the ambiguity. Write \(P_D\) for *any* seed rule with the required units and swap behavior. On records reached by adding \(b\), invert \(S_b\) on the record, covariance and feasible mass range, apply the rule, and translate its law by \(b\). Composition is exact since \(S_bS_c=S_{b+c}\). This argument extends to words in positive additions, channel swap and positive unit changes, including reciprocal-mass additions by conjugating with swap. Each word corresponds, in fixed channel units, to an invertible matrix \(G\) mapping the original positive channel cone onto a cone with two ordered boundary rays (possibly interchanged). The induced map on mass is \(g_G(m)=(G_{11}m+G_{12})/(G_{21}m+G_{22})\).

**Independence of the chosen route.** If \(G\) and \(H\) produce the same feasible cone, \(H^{-1}G\) maps the original cone onto itself. It must permute the two extreme rays. Thus it is positive diagonal or positive diagonal times swap. Inverting the two routes from a common final record gives seed experiments related by precisely those units and swap transformations. Seed equivariance gives the same final law when the starting prior is transported too. This establishes existence of a single rule on the images of the seed experiments, without retaining a list of operations. Specifying only a smaller common subinterval of two *different* cones raises a further question. [Two histories on an overlapping interval](e12-known-mass-overlap.md)

## A rule evaluated directly on the combined measurements

For an implementation that takes only the final measurements, instrument and physical bounds, take a common positive definite \(2\times2\) channel covariance \(C_0\), independent readings and common spatial dimension \(k\). A known separable spatial covariance may be whitened within each reading first. Whiten the channel coordinates by \(L^{-1}\) for \(LL^\top=C_0\). A candidate determines the unit ray parallel to \(L^{-1}(m,1)^\top\). The two feasible endpoints give oriented unit vectors \(u_0,u_1\), with arc length \(0<\beta<\pi\). The endpoint \(\infty\) uses the ray \((1,0)\). Write \(v_\theta\) for the unit normal to the candidate line in this plane. These rays depend only on the final instrument and physically allowed masses. Changing force and acceleration coordinates together transforms the whitened configuration isometrically, since \(L'^{-1}GL\) is orthogonal for \(L'L'^\top=GC_0G^\top\). The feasible arc and its orientation, up to reversal, move with the record. For known addition applied to an independent-channel instrument the **transported** arc still has length \(\pi/2\), even though the total's two errors are correlated. [Positive mass aperture](e12-positive-aperture.md)

Let \(B_i\) be the \(2\times2\) Gram of reading \(i\) after whitening, \(B=\sum_iB_i,\ T=\operatorname{tr}B,\ L_B=\lambda_{\max}(B)\), and \(H\) its eigenvalue gap. Use \(K_i(\theta)=v_\theta^\top B_i v_\theta\) and \(h(x)=k(1-e^{-x/2})/(1-2^{-k/2})\). Over the physical arc form \(Q=\min\sum_iK_i,\ C_*=\min\sum_i h(K_i)\). Write \(q_j=\sum_iK_i(u_j)\) for the residual at each endpoint. Put
\[
 d=\min\{1,(C_*/Q)(L_B/Q)^{1/8}\},\qquad
 P=\min\{dH^2/L_B,\ 1+\min(q_0,q_1)^2\},\qquad
 n=\sqrt{q_0}\,u_1+\sqrt{q_1}\,u_0.
\]
For \(Q=0\) set \(d=1\). With \(\hat\theta\) the principal line, the log factor relative to the supplied prior is
\[
 -\frac{P\sin^2(\theta-\hat\theta)}{2(1+\tau a)}
 -e^{-H^2/L_B}\frac{Q}{1+Q}\frac{(v_\theta^\top n)^2}{1+\|n\|^2},
 \qquad
 a=\frac{4\det B}{(1+T)^3}\frac{C_*-h(Q)}{1+C_*}.
\]
At \(B=0\) use zero log factor. Interchanging the endpoints leaves \(n\) unchanged. Every term is an isometry invariant of the readings, candidate and feasible arc. For orthogonal endpoint rays these formulas reduce exactly to the original E12 endpoint cap and angular term. Hence this extends *each* original rule, agrees under all the transports above, and can be evaluated directly on a genuinely correlated instrument. The prior appears once on normalizing. The physical endpoints are part of the hypothesis being measured, regardless of whether the chosen prior gives substantial weight near them. [Implementation](e12_known_mass.py)

**Scope of the extension.** On each fixed feasible arc the [identifying-array proof](e12-joining-residuals.md) applies in the whitened angle. The transverse noise is standard normal, and each bounded residual threshold crosses at most twice on an arc shorter than \(\pi\). Thus \(d\to1\) and spectral precision divided by \(I=\Delta^2/(kN+\Delta)\) tends to one at a fixed interior truth on identifying arrays. Each endpoint residual has expectation \(kN+\Delta\sin^2(\theta_0-\theta_j)\) with fluctuations \(O_p(\sqrt{kN+\Delta})\). Both grow at order \(kN+\Delta\), so the cap becomes inactive. The local normal law and asymptotically nominal raw central intervals follow. For a fixed data shape with \(Q_0>0,H_0>0\), \(C_*\) tends to the known saturation value times the fewest readings that must be removed for a common fit on that arc. The limiting precision is finite and positive by the same calculation as on the right-angle arc. On an endpoint-only exact fit the cap tends to one. At \(B=tI\), the second term has a nonconstant limit along the bisector \(u_0+u_1\), which is nonzero because \(\beta<\pi\). This proves informative spread in the fixed incompatible cases. These complete-law statements concern the separable, common-channel-covariance model.

**A reporting limit on obtuse arcs.** Taking the interval hull with the entire conditional score set still gives at least nominal finite-sample coverage. Exact asymptotic nominal coverage of that *hull* need not extend. Suppose \(\theta_0+\pi/2\) lies strictly inside the arc and take regular repetitions at a fixed nonzero drive. With probability tending to one, the principal fitted line and its perpendicular both lie inside. At both the score is identically zero. Away from both lines it grows in magnitude at order \(\sqrt N\), since the eigenvalue gap has order \(N\). The hull's upper endpoint includes the remote perpendicular line. Its limiting coverage is consequently \(\Phi(z)=(1+p)/2\), e.g. 97.5% for a nominal 95% hull. The raw interval keeps nominal limiting coverage. On a transported right-angle arc a perpendicular to an interior truth lies outside. ## What the stronger composition premise costs

If a physical protocol may be forgotten and evidence must agree on *every overlap* of two final feasible ranges with matching observations and noise declarations, two different positive compositions can enforce rotations of the channel plane locally. This requires both ordinary and reciprocal-mass standards (or the equivalent channel duality). The additional condition excludes both original probes on a strictly positive scalar observation. At a vector singleton with perpendicular, equally long force and acceleration in unit noise, a channel rotation can be realized by a physical spatial rotation. The overlap condition, spatial frame invariance and reciprocity then force constant evidence across all masses. Requiring a nonconstant limiting mass factor on this conflict contradicts that stronger condition. The data can still be decisive evidence of poor fit. [Proof, exact rational contradictions and scope](e12-known-mass-overlap.md)

Even the weaker requirement of positive limiting lower angular variance on every incompatible scalar singleton faces an obstruction under those overlap, swap and sign symmetries. With a uniform starting angular law, concentration on every compatible interior singleton forces failure of that spread requirement on the force-zero or the anti-aligned ray. The proof transfers conditional probabilities between overlapping intervals of the same angular length. It needs neither a spatially balanced vector nor nonconstant evidence on conflicts. [Scalar obstruction](e12-known-mass-overlap.md)

Even this stronger condition need not select a unique amount of discount on ordinary positive conflicting data. Here is an explicit alternative family. In the last construction take both minima over the **entire projective circle**, omit the endpoint cap, and omit the term involving \(n\). Retain \(a\) and the spectral term. The resulting factor depends only on the whitened Grams and the candidate line. It is equivariant under every invertible real channel transformation by the whitening isometry, wherever the transformed physical candidate is defined. It is independent of the feasible endpoints. Restriction to any smaller interval therefore commutes with conditioning. The disagreement statistic still joins by addition of the bounded residual curves. The Gaussian local calibration proof still holds at interior truths on a fixed arc of length less than \(\pi\).

For two linearly independent strictly positive scalar observations in unit noise, reflecting any negatively sloped candidate onto the positive quadrant decreases its residual for each reading. The two minima therefore agree with the physical-arc minima. On \((2,1),(1,2)\) the previous exact calculation gives \(C_*=2h(1/2)>h(Q)=h(1)\) and positive spectral precision. Every distinct \(\tau\) again gives a distinct mass law, even with agreement on overlaps. On the asymmetric pair the projective versions give medians 1.528611 and 1.510203 at six decimals; their intervals differ slightly from the physical-arc versions. The cost is explicit. A balanced vector observation returns the prior; a high-precision singleton on an axis concentrates near the endpoint. This family retains spread on full-rank fixed conflicts but fails the project's stronger axis and balanced-data desiderata.
