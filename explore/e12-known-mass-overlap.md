# When two known standards give the same observed record

Attaching a known mass \(b>0\) on the same shared-acceleration record acts on the two observed channels by \(U_b=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&b\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), in chosen reference units. The channel-dual operation, adding a known reciprocal mass \(c>0\) on a shared-force record, gives \(L_c=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\c&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). On a candidate \(m\), a channel matrix \(A\) acts by \(g_A(m)=(A_{11}m+A_{12})/(A_{21}m+A_{22})\). In particular \(g_{L_c}(m)=m/(1+cm)\). The latter operation requires the reciprocal-channel dual as an admissible composition, for example from a channel swap, a known addition in the swapped channels and swapping back. The channel covariances transform with the observations on every leg. These are synthetic re-expressions of the same record. The protocol here makes no independence assumption about synthetic and original observations. [Known-mass geometry](e12-known-mass-geometry.md) · [L5 and L14](../definition/premises.md)

**Two different premises matter.** Ordinary transport sends both the mass law and the declared prior to their pushforwards under \(g_A\). It specifies the answer on the image of the original positive-mass interval. Consider the stronger requirement that relative evidence on the overlap of two such images be determined solely by the final observed record and covariance. In particular, once both routes have the same units, spatial subspace and noise declarations, their relative evidence ratios between any two candidates in the overlap must agree. This assumes that the acquisition history and the rest of each feasible mass interval carry no additional information for those **ratios**. We call this hypothesis *agreement on overlaps* below. A rule can instead treat the full feasible interval or composition protocol as part of the experiment. Multiplying an arbitrary prior by a data factor on one specified experiment, as in T8, does not prove agreement on overlaps between different experiments. [T8](../definition/theorems/T08.md) · [T12](../definition/theorems/T12.md)

Take a strictly positive continuous factor relative to the prior. Covariance of the mass law and its prior under an operation makes the factor after transport equal to the original at corresponding candidates, up to a constant. This follows by taking the Radon–Nikodym derivative of the transported law with respect to the transported prior. Continuity extends equality from almost every mass to the interior. No Jacobian remains in this comparison of relative factors.

## Two short paths and strictly positive data

Use the following exact matrices:
\[
 P=U_1 L_1=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q=L_{1/5} U_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\1/5&7/5\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 R=P^{-1}Q=\frac15\begin{pmatrix}4&3\\-3&4\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let \(X'=R^\top X\) in column convention and let both input covariances be \(I\). Then \(PX=QX'\) and \(PP^\top=QQ^\top\). No synthetic noise was discarded. The feasible mass intervals after the two paths are respectively \((1,2)\) and \((10/7,5)\). They overlap. For \(0<m<4/3\), \(r(m)=g_R(m)=(4m+3)/(4-3m)\) is a positive preimage in the first experiment of the target candidate \(g_Q(m)=g_P(r(m))\) in the second. Agreement on overlaps forces
\[
                 \ell_X(r(m))-\ell_{X'}(m)=\text{constant}
\]
on this interval of source masses, with \(\ell\) the log evidence factor.

Choose the strictly positive scalar singleton \(X'=(1,1)^\top\), so \(X=(7/5,1/5)^\top\). Both have squared length 2 in unit noise, and the rotation takes each candidate line to its corresponding line. The E12 singleton has zero minimum residual and log factor \(-K\sin^2(\theta-\hat\theta)/2\), \(\theta=\arctan m\). The cap \(1+\min(F^2,a^2)^2\) gives precision \(K=2\) for \(X'\) and \(K=626/625\) for \(X\). At \(m=1/4,1/2\), the matched original masses \(r(m)\) are \(16/13,2\). The squared angular sines from the fitted line of \(X'\) are \(9/34,1/10\). Thus the change between those two matched log contrasts is
\[
                 \frac{2-626/625}{2}\left(\frac{9}{34}-\frac1{10}\right)
                         =\frac{4368}{53125}.
\]
The effect holds for every \(\tau\), for the four-number witness and for the all-candidate residual witness. All readings and candidates in this example are interior. The baseline Gaussian profile has no cap on positive singletons, so its corresponding difference is zero. A second check isolates the E12 tie term. On a balanced \(B=I\) it gives \(\ell(m)=-(1-m)^2/[6(1+m^2)]\), yielding a difference of \(36/425\) across the same two pairs of matched masses. [Four-number witness](e12-joining-laws.md) · [All-candidate witness](e12-joining-residuals.md) · [Exact and numerical checks](../tests/test_e12_known_mass_overlap.py)

There is an operational reading even without a general theorem about data factors for arbitrary priors. Choose the same unit half-Cauchy prior at each start, uniform in \(\theta\). The orthogonal \(R\) sends the angle to \(\theta+\arctan(3/4)\), so the two pushed priors agree as measures on their overlapping output interval. Condition both on that interval. Their restricted priors are identical. If imposing a common narrower feasible interval simply conditions the returned mass laws, and the final record, covariance and restricted prior completely specify the inference, the two restricted laws must agree. Their distinct contrast above precludes this for the witnesses. Supporting this reading requires the ability to declare that restricted interval for inference and to forget any remaining protocol distinction. Ordinary transport alone allows both answers on their respective full intervals.

## A scalar obstruction using spread alone

The same two-path argument has a consequence even if conflict need only leave **positive angular variance**. Fix unit equal channel errors and the uniform prior in noise angle on \(A=(0,T)\), \(T=\pi/2\). Use one scalar observation
\[
       X_{\psi,t}=\sqrt t\,(\sin\psi,\cos\psi)^\top,\qquad t\to\infty .
\]
Assume the operations above, agreement on overlaps, positive continuous factors, and invariance under simultaneous reversal of the two observed signs. The latter also follows from a physical spatial rotation for collinear vectors in three dimensions and isotropic noise. Suppose every compatible fixed interior observation \(0<\psi<T\) gives concentration at its own angle as \(t\) increases. Then the force-zero observation \(\psi=0\) and the anti-aligned observation \(\psi=-T/2\) **cannot both retain positive limiting lower angular variance**. More precisely, if the anti-aligned observation has positive limiting lower variance, the force-zero observation concentrates at the zero-mass endpoint. The test is deterministic with the same amplitude scale in every orientation. It requires no uniformity across angles in either premise. Zero mass and infinite mass are limiting endpoints in the compact angular coordinate. Neither is an allowed reported finite positive mass.

**Interval comparison lemma.** Let \(L_{\psi,t}\) be the source density in angle. For any \(|\phi|<T\), \(L_{\psi+\phi,t}(\theta+\phi)\) is proportional to \(L_{\psi,t}(\theta)\) throughout the common interval where \(\theta,\theta+\phi\in A\). The proportionality can depend on \(t,\psi,\phi\). For sufficiently small \(|\phi|\) this follows exactly as in the two-path construction: use \(P_0=U_1L_1U_1\), \(Q_\phi=P_0R_\phi\); the positive factorization proved below also works for small negative \(\phi\), by continuity. \(R_\phi X_{\psi,t}=X_{\psi+\phi,t}\). The common starting angular prior is uniform, so the factor comparison also holds for the normalized densities up to constants. For a larger shift, split it into equal sufficiently small shifts. For every \(\theta\) in the endpoint overlap all intermediate candidate angles lie in \(A\). Multiplying the constants proves the lemma on the entire overlap at once. In particular, after conditioning on corresponding subintervals the laws are exact translates; no global normalization of a periodically continued density is needed.

**Axis proof.** The law at \(\psi=T/2\) is symmetric about \(T/2\) by channel swap with this prior. For every interior \(\psi\), transport a small symmetric neighborhood of \(T/2\) to one about \(\psi\) by the lemma. This gives exact reflection symmetry locally about \(\psi\). Interior consistency then implies that the probability of \((\psi,T)\) tends to \(1/2\): the probability inside the symmetric neighborhood tends to one, split equally between its two halves. The law conditional on \((\psi,T)\) consequently concentrates at \(\psi\). By the lemma, the axis law conditional on \((0,T-\psi)\) is that law translated by \(-\psi\). For any \(0<\epsilon<T/2\), take \(0<\psi<\epsilon\); thus the **unconditional** axis probability of \([\epsilon,T-\epsilon]\) tends to zero too. Every subsequential axis limit on \([0,T]\) is a mixture at the two endpoints.

**Anti-alignment proof.** Suppose such a limit along a subsequence has positive weight at \(T\). Along that subsequence the axis law conditioned on \((T/2,T)\) converges to \(T\), since \(T/2\) has no limiting mass. The interval comparison lemma sends the anti-aligned law conditional on \((0,T/2)\) to precisely that upper-half axis law, by a \(T/2\) shift. Its conditional distribution therefore concentrates at \(T/2\). At the anti-aligned observation, swapping the channels simultaneously reverses their signs. Sign invariance and swap symmetry imply exact reflection symmetry about \(T/2\), and each half has probability \(1/2\). The whole anti-aligned law consequently concentrates at \(T/2\) on this subsequence. Bounded angular variance tends to zero, contradicting positive limiting lower variance there. Hence that weaker spread demand on the anti-aligned ray alone forces all axis subsequential limits to be the zero endpoint.

This obstruction uses the project's choice to count an axis as conflict even though its minimum residual over the *closed* arc is zero. Unlike the balanced-vector argument, it requires no demand for a nonconstant limiting factor on conflict. Discarding an axis from the conflict requirement changes the theorem's implication. ## A restriction on every law at a spatially balanced conflict

A stronger conclusion uses actual spatial frame invariance. Assume known mass additions and their reciprocal-channel duals can be concatenated, the relative log factor is continuous in positive mass, spatial rotations and channel swap preserve the evidence in their respective coordinates, and agreement on overlaps holds. One genuine three-dimensional vector singleton with force \(F=\sqrt t\,e_1\), acceleration \(a=\sqrt t\,e_2\) and identical isotropic errors in both independent channels has **constant relative evidence at every positive mass**, for every \(t>0\). There is no \(m>0\) for which \(F=m a\).

To prove it, use \(R_\phi=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\cos\phi&\sin\phi\\-\sin\phi&\cos\phi\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) and \(P_0=U_1L_1U_1=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&3\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). For all sufficiently small positive \(\phi\), \(Q_\phi=P_0R_\phi\) has positive entries, determinant one, and both diagonal entries greater than one. Any such \(Q=(q_{ij})\) factors into physically allowed operations,
\[
 Q= U_{(q_{11}-1)/q_{21}}\, L_{q_{21}}\, U_{(q_{22}-1)/q_{21}} .
\]
Thus the two histories implement the same local angle-shift argument as above, without assuming that rotating the **channel** plane is a physical symmetry. For the balanced observation \(R_\phi^\top X\) can also be obtained by an ordinary rotation of its two orthogonal equal-length **spatial** vectors in the \(e_1,e_2\) plane. Hence the original and rotated observations have the same evidence function up to an additive constant in logs. The overlap relation implies that \(\ell_t(\theta+\phi)-\ell_t(\theta)\) is constant on \(0<\theta<\pi/2-\phi\).

Channel swap also gives an observation related by a proper spatial rotation, a half-turn about \(e_1+e_2\). Thus \(\ell_t(\pi/2-\theta)=\ell_t(\theta)\), since the possible additive constant vanishes at the midpoint. At \(\theta=(\pi/2-\phi)/2\) this reflection sets the constant angular increment to zero. Every sufficiently small positive move leaves \(\ell_t\) unchanged wherever both angles lie in the arc. Subdividing any angular interval into such moves proves constancy everywhere. The proper-rotation argument works directly in three dimensions. Two spatial dimensions suffice if reflected frames are also allowed.

With any given positive full-support prior and such a data factor, the returned law is therefore the prior on this fixed conflict, for every common scaling. This allows persistent spread, and contradicts the stronger demand of informative, nonconstant limiting evidence on *every* fixed conflict. It does not rule out a law on other observations or a protocol that retains its whole feasible mass interval. The choice between these readings of composition changes what can be proved.
