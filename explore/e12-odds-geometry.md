# Which odds follow from optimal comparison of two mass covectors?

Consider independent scalar force–acceleration readings with the same instrument in noise units. Put their whitened rows in \(Y\in\mathbb R^{n\times2}\). A candidate mass specifies a line with unit direction \(u_\theta=(\sin\theta,\cos\theta)\) and annihilating unit covector \(v_\theta=(\cos\theta,-\sin\theta)\). The Gaussian model is \(Y=b u_\theta^\top+E\), with independent standard normal noise entries and arbitrary deterministic signed drives \(b\in\mathbb R^n\) measured in graph arclength units. Write
\[
 K_\theta=\|Yv_\theta\|^2,\qquad H_\theta=\|Yu_\theta\|^2=\|Y\|_F^2-K_\theta .
\]
For two distinct mass angles, the question is how well the observed covectors distinguish them at finite signal. All assertions about optimality below refer to the explicitly specified binary decision objective. Equal total **noise-unit signal length** at the two candidates defines the performance comparison. Holding physical acceleration equal would give different signal lengths. The same geometry applies to a two-channel allocation measurement with candidate graph directions given by the noise-whitened allocation rays. ## A uniquely optimal preference at every positive energy

Fix a total signal length \(\delta=\|b\|>0\). Allow every allocation of energy, sign and direction among the pushes at either mass. Consider the worst error probability over these profiles and both candidate masses under equal-cost binary choice. **The unique minimax decision chooses the smaller pooled squared covector residual \(K\)**, up to Gaussian null sets. The same decision is optimal at every \(\delta>0\). With zero signal both distributions coincide.

**Proof.** Average the Gaussian densities under each candidate over the uniform direction of \(b\) on the sphere of radius \(\delta\). These averages lower-bound worst-case decision risk. Relative to the zero-signal Gaussian density the average under candidate \(\theta\) is
\[
  e^{-\delta^2/2}F_n(\delta\sqrt{H_\theta}),\qquad
  F_n(s)=E\,e^{sU_1},
\]
where \(U\) is uniform on the unit sphere in \(\mathbb R^n\). For one push \(F_1(s)=\cosh(s)\). Sign symmetry replaces the exponential by \(\cosh(sU_1)\), strictly increasing in \(s\ge0\) in expectation. Hence for equal candidate weights the average-risk optimum chooses larger \(H\). Its risk under either candidate is constant as \(b\) moves around the sphere, since an orthogonal row transformation takes one profile into any other, preserves the Gaussian noise law and leaves both scores fixed. A reflection interchanging the two channel directions also interchanges the two error probabilities. The maximum risk of this decision therefore equals the average-risk lower bound. Minimizers must achieve the same Gaussian mixture optimum, which is unique away from \(H_\theta=H_\phi\). This equality is a nontrivial quadratic zero set of Lebesgue measure zero. The averaging and row transformations prove an optimality statement over arbitrary *fixed* drives. They introduce no population of pushes into the readout and impose no physical permission to replace a recorded block by rotated pushes.

The same rule is minimax if each true mass can independently have **any** drive profile of length at least \(\delta_0>0\). To see this let \(e(\delta)\) be its common error at length \(\delta\). For \(0<d<D\), transform data from a length-\(D\) experiment to \((d/D)Y+\sqrt{1-(d/D)^2}Z\) with independent standard Gaussian noise \(Z\). This gives the length-\(d\) experiment at the same profile direction. Simulating a length-\(d\) decision at length \(D\) proves \(e(D)\le e(d)\) by shell optimality. Therefore the nearest-line rule's worst error above the floor is attained at the floor, where no rule can improve it. Uniqueness follows from uniqueness on that shell. The same comparison works at every positive floor without knowing its numerical value. If the floor is exactly zero, random guessing also attains worst-case error \(1/2\). In a symmetric two-force apparatus, the two allocations \(r,1-r\) at a fixed total have equal graph lengths at the same absolute physical acceleration, so the shell comparison has a direct shared-drive realization.

## Why the preference alone gives no odds magnitude

Price the two kinds of mistake by positive weights \(w_\theta,w_\phi\). Minimize the sum of the weighted worst-case errors, one supremum over drive profiles for each possible mass. At fixed length \(\delta\) the unique optimum selects \(\theta\) when
\[
 R_\delta(Y)=\frac{F_n(\delta\sqrt{H_\theta})}
                   {F_n(\delta\sqrt{H_\phi})} > C,\qquad C=w_\phi/w_\theta .
\]
Indeed the same two averages lower-bound that criterion and the decision based on their ratio has constant error over the profiles of each candidate. Ties have zero probability since the two directions are distinct and the projections \(Yu_\theta,Yu_\phi\) can be prescribed independently. For fixed \(Yu_\phi\), the other projection must lie on at most one sphere for a tie.

If \(H_\theta>H_\phi\), the set of values of \(R_\delta(Y)\) as \(\delta>0\) varies is **the entire interval \((1,\infty)\)**. It is continuous, tends to one at zero, and diverges at infinity. For the last assertion, if \(a=\sqrt{H_\theta}>b=\sqrt{H_\phi}\), choose \(h\in(b/a,1)\). The numerator is at least \(\Pr(U_1\ge h)e^{\delta a h}\) and the denominator is at most \(e^{\delta b}\). The reversed scores give \((0,1)\), and tied scores always give one. In particular, for *every* \(C>1\) the optimal decisions at two positive signal lengths give opposite strict answers on an open set of records. All Gaussian laws here have positive density there. No one rule minimizes the specified unequal-cost risk separately at every signal length. The case \(C<1\) follows by interchanging the candidates. This is a test of binary operating decisions; it does not exclude returning probabilities justified by a different criterion. See also the [fixed-physical-drive odds bounds](e12-mass-observables.md), which compare a different pair of preparations.

## Three-readout comparisons and multiplication of odds fix the factor

Consider a probability-law update with positive prior-relative singleton factor \(L_\theta(y)\), continuous in the observed row. Fix the instrument and starting declaration. Suppose that for independent readings the *final* prior-relative factors multiply, up to candidate-independent normalization. Compare any two candidates using their relative factors at equal starting odds, randomizing fairly at an odds tie. Require this decision to attain the preceding equal-cost minimax risk for blocks of just **one, two and three rows**, for at least one positive shell length per block size. Then, for one constant \(c>0\),
\[
 L_\theta(y)=h(y)\exp\{c(y^\top u_\theta)^2\},\qquad
 P_D(d\theta)\ \propto\ \pi(d\theta)e^{-c K_\theta(D)} .
\]
No dependence on residual cost alone, covariance under row mixing of the returned law, differentiability or parametric form for the noise discount is assumed. The criterion refers to preferences from one common factor across the candidate set. Allowing the factors to change when only the binary comparison changes falls outside this statement.

**Proof.** Fix a pair \(\theta,\phi\). Let \(g(y)=\log[L_\theta(y)/L_\phi(y)]\) and \(d(y)=(y^\top u_\theta)^2-(y^\top u_\phi)^2\). By optimality the sign of the sum of \(g\)'s matches that of the sum of \(d\)'s almost everywhere in each block experiment. When the sum of \(d\)'s vanishes, the sum of \(g\)'s must vanish **pointwise**. Indeed the sum of \(d\)'s takes both signs arbitrarily close to each of its zeros. It is an indefinite quadratic form, so this follows from its derivative at a regular zero or from perturbations in its positive and negative directions at the origin. Continuity of the sum of \(g\)'s then gives the assertion from the almost-everywhere signs. The form has two nonzero opposite eigenvalues on each row.

The range of \(d\) on one row is \(\mathbb R\). For rows \(y,y'\) with the same value \(x\), pair each with any row of value \(-x\). The two-row tie identities show \(g(y)=g(y')\), yielding an odd function \(G(x)\). Using three rows with values \(x,z,-(x+z)\) gives \(G(x+z)=G(x)+G(z)\). It is continuous by choosing a row of the required value along a positive or negative eigenvector with magnitude proportional to \(\sqrt{|x|}\). Thus \(G(x)=c_{\theta\phi}x\), and the singleton decisions give \(c_{\theta\phi}>0\).

Log ratios telescope around every triangle of candidate masses. Consequently for any three distinct angles the matrices \(P_i=u_i u_i^\top\) obey

There is a direct two-row check on the existing E12 singleton factors. Compare masses 1 and 2 on the positive observations \((F,A)=(1,2),(3,1)\) with unit channel errors. The row differences \(H_1-H_2\) are \(13/10,-9/5\), summing to \(-1/2\). The minimax choice is mass 2. The E12 singleton cap multipliers are \(2/5,1/5\), giving log odds \(+2/25\) in favor of mass 1 if the singleton odds are multiplied. This reverses the finite comparison. Both earlier E12 laws recompute their block evidence, so this is specifically a test of resetting from their singletons. [Singleton formula](e12-conditioning-nuisance.md)
\[
 (c_{12}-c_{13})(P_1-P_2)+(c_{23}-c_{13})(P_2-P_3)=0 .
\]
The traceless parts of \(P_i\) lie at distinct points of a circle in the two-dimensional space of symmetric traceless matrices. A line meets that circle in at most two points, so the two differences are independent. All three coefficients agree, and overlapping triangles fix \(c\) across the positive mass arc. Taking any reference mass yields the factor \(h(y)\) above. Conversely every \(c>0\) gives precisely the optimal equal-cost decisions on every finite block. At a fixed \(\delta\) the full weighted-decision odds \(R_\delta\) generally do not multiply across independent blocks whose individual energies are unreported.

## Valid repeated pushes already rule out a universal multiplicative width

On the same instrument let all true rows have mean \(r u_{\theta_0}\), with \(r>0\) fixed and \(\theta_0\) interior. The Gaussian errors remain independent. A continuous positive angular prior near \(\theta_0\) is assumed. Write \(B=Y^\top Y\), and let \(\widehat\theta\) denote its principal line chosen near \(\theta_0\). In coordinates along and perpendicular to the true line the off-diagonal entry \(B_{12}\) has mean zero and variance \(n(1+r^2)\). The normalized diagonal difference tends to \(r^2\). The two-by-two eigenangle identity \(\tan[2(\widehat\theta-\theta_0)]=2B_{12}/(B_{11}-B_{22})\) and the central limit theorem give
\[
 \sqrt n(\widehat\theta-\theta_0)\ \Longrightarrow\
 N(0,(1+r^2)/r^4).
\]
For the forced multiplicative law the angle-dependent part of log density relative to the prior is \(-c\,\operatorname{gap}(B)\sin^2(\theta-\widehat\theta)\), with \(\operatorname{gap}(B)/n\to r^2\). A Taylor expansion on \(n^{-1/2}\) neighborhoods, Gaussian domination there by the sine lower bound and exponential suppression off a fixed interior neighborhood give a centered local normal with asymptotic variance \(1/(2cnr^2)\). Hence nominal coverage of even one fixed central interval of level \(p\in(0,1)\) requires
\[
                      c=\frac{r^2}{2(1+r^2)} .
\]
On the same instrument \(r=1\) requires \(c=1/4\), whereas \(r=2\) requires \(c=2/5\). The limit as \(r\to\infty\) is the face-value Gaussian coefficient \(1/2\). Thus equality of the binary minimax decisions, strict multiplication of final odds, and regular uncertainty calibration across just two perfectly compatible data-generating regimes are incompatible. This conclusion does not use fixed conflict, a noise-only dataset or calibration under a hidden zero drive. The variance correction is the familiar scalar targeting factor from the project's Gaussian scatter analysis. The extra result here is the derivation of the *entire* exponential class from finite binary decisions and joins. **What remains possible.** Binary equal-cost optimality only fixes an order on each reported block. For example a law proportional to \(\pi(d\theta)\exp[c(Y) H_\theta]\) has that order for any positive block-dependent coefficient. It can use information in individual readings. T12 allows such whole-block recomputation. The binary objective alone gives no value to the finite coefficient, and the soundness of any proposed block rule still has to be checked against the other premises. In particular \(c(Y)=\operatorname{gap}(B)/(2\lambda_{\max}(B))\) for nonzero scatter, with prior returned at zero, attains all the binary equal-cost decisions and the nominal central coverage on every regular fixed-\(r\) repetition just studied. The coefficient converges to precisely \(r^2/[2(1+r^2)]\), so the local-normal argument applies. On a positive fixed conflict with a unique interior principal line, common noise shrinkage leaves this coefficient unchanged and sends the gap to infinity, collapsing the law at that line. The separate fixed-conflict spread requirement therefore still matters. [Gaussian scatter and width](e12-pooled-witness.md)

**A sharper limit, and witnesses on its attainable side.** Without assuming any factor multiplication, a block law continuous in its data must still give equal relative evidence whenever two candidates have tied \(H\), by the sign-change argument above applied directly to the block. Thus a fixed balanced conflict with \(Y^\top Y=tI\) must return the prior. Requiring a nonconstant mass factor on every such conflict is incompatible with this decision objective. Balanced conflicts are physically admissible Gaussian records; continuity is what makes requirements at these probability-zero records testable by perturbation.

Conversely retain the earlier scalar construction's capped precision \(K'\) and drop its bounded tie-breaking term \(U\). The law has log factor \(-K'\sin^2(\theta-\widehat\theta)/2\). Its factor is proportional to \(\exp[c(Y)H_\theta]\) with \(c(Y)=K'/(2\operatorname{gap}(B))>0\) whenever the gap is positive, and is constant at zero gap. It therefore attains the equal-cost binary optimum, is neutral to an observed zero, and has the earlier identifying-array consistency and central coverage: the original proof establishes those limits for the principal term and bounds the discarded log term by an amount tending to zero. On fixed conflicts with positive closed-arc minimum cost, the capped precision has a finite limit, positive when the gap is nonzero. On axis conflicts it is capped at 1, preserving spread and giving nonconstant evidence. Only a balanced conflict is forced back to the prior. Distinct choices of the existing discount exponent give different weights at the same data even with all these comparisons satisfied. For the two observed rows \((4,1),(1,4)\), the scatter eigenvalues are \(25,9\); with \(b=1\) the exponent choices \(\alpha=1,2\) land on opposite sides of the discount cap. This illustrates why getting every pairwise decision right still leaves the finite uncertainty law open.
