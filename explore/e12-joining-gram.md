# How much of a block must survive a join?

On the common scalar instrument write \(Y_i=(F_i/\sigma_F,a_i/\sigma_a)\) and \(B=\sum_iY_iY_i^\top\). The Gram matrix retains both channel energies, their contraction, the absolute residual at every mass, and the total squared exterior product. It joins by addition. Can *any* law computed from this tensor satisfy both fixed-conflict spread and regular concentration? The answer is no, even allowing an arbitrary measurable response to all three entries.

## The two experiments

Fix the instrument ratio and the prior. Use the bounded noise angle \(\theta=\arctan(m\sigma_a/\sigma_F)\) to measure the spread of the returned law. Let \(V(B)\) denote its angular variance. Assume the law depends on the standardized data through \(B\). An additional input giving the declared number \(n\) of rows is allowed if appending an exactly zero row leaves the law unchanged. For positive definite \(B\), choose the two rows of \(B^{1/2}\) and pad with zeros. This realizes \(B\) at every \(n\ge2\), so zero neutrality removes that input on the entire positive definite cone. A statistic computed from the separate readings can therefore contain information beyond the declared count and pooled Gram.

1. **Fixed conflict.** Fix any two linearly independent observed rows. Shrink both channel errors by the same factor. The whitened rows grow by \(\sqrt t\). Require positive limiting lower angular variance as \(t\to\infty\). The lower bound can depend on the pair. Every positive definite matrix occurs as one of these two-row Grams. For the theorem below it suffices to require this only for pairs with **both channels strictly positive in both readings**. Every positive definite shape with positive cross term has such a realization. The two columns of its square root have an acute angle, so one planar rotation moves both strictly inside the first quadrant, preserving the Gram. The two resulting rows are positive and linearly independent.
2. **Repeated valid readings.** At every repetition observe \(Y_i=z+Z_i\), with a fixed \(z=(z_F,z_a)>0\) and independent standard bivariate Gaussian \(Z_i\). The physical mass is \(s z_F/z_a\), where \(s=\sigma_F/\sigma_a\). Concentration in probability of the returned law at that mass entails \(V(B_n)\to0\) in probability, since the angle is bounded.

**Theorem.** For a Borel measurable Gram readout retaining positive limiting lower spread on every strictly positive fixed conflicting pair, the set of mean vectors \(z>0\) at which even \(V(B_n)\to0\) in probability has two-dimensional Lebesgue measure zero. This concerns uncertainty in the raw law. The principal line of \(B_n\) itself consistently estimates the true angle in this experiment.

## Proof

Write the trace \(t=\operatorname{tr}B\) and shape \(p=(B_{11}-B_{22},2B_{12})/t\). The positive definite shapes form the open unit disk, with matrix representative
\[
 \Gamma(p)=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1+p_1&p_2\\p_2&1-p_1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For repeated readings the limiting shape is that of \(I+zz^\top\). Its polar radius is \(\|z\|^2/(2+\|z\|^2)\) and its polar angle is twice the polar angle of \(z\). Thus \(z\mapsto p\) is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse onto the open upper half disk. In particular every such noise-broadened *limiting shape* is also the shape of some fixed conflict. A law could still distinguish the experiments via fine variations of shape or trace. The next argument handles precisely that possibility.

For each positive integer \(j\), define the Borel set
\[
 A_j=\left\{p:\int_j^\infty e^{-t}
 {\bf1}_{\{V(t\Gamma(p))<1/j\}}\,dt=0\right\}.
\]
Positive liminf on each fixed ray implies that these sets cover the open upper half of the shape disk. The integral formulation allows a null set of exceptional traces per shape, and avoids any continuity assumption. For Gaussian repetitions with \(n\ge2\) the joint law of trace and shape has a density. Indeed the matrix \(X\) of rows has a density and has rank two almost surely. The derivative of \(X\mapsto X^\top X\) maps \(X(X^\top X)^{-1}D/2\) to any symmetric \(D\). Local submersion coordinates then give absolute continuity, which is preserved under the smooth trace and shape change of coordinates and under mixing over \(z\). Consequently \(V(B_n)\ge1/j\) almost surely on \(\{p_n\in A_j,t_n\ge j\}\).

Suppose the set of concentrating shapes has positive area. It is measurable by the Gaussian expectation formula applied to the bounded Borel function \(V\), since convergence in probability to zero is equivalent here to convergence of its mean to zero. Restrict this set to a compact rectangle strictly inside the upper half disk with positive remaining area, and draw \(p\) uniformly from that subset. Couple the data for all \(p\) using the same noises. Conditionally on these noises the empirical matrix divided by \(n\) is
\[
 M_n+\bar Z_n z(p)^\top+z(p)\bar Z_n^\top+z(p)z(p)^\top,\qquad
 M_n=n^{-1}\sum_i Z_i Z_i^\top .
\]
Almost surely \(M_n\to I,\ \bar Z_n\to0\). On a slightly larger closed rectangle inside the domain, the resulting sample shape map \(F_n:p\mapsto p_n\) tends to the identity together with its first derivatives, by smoothness and a uniform positive trace bound. The sample traces \(t_n\) tend to infinity uniformly there.

In particular the distribution of \(F_n(p)\) tends in **total variation** to the distribution of \(p\). For details, on the larger rectangle \(\|D F_n-I\|<1/2\) eventually, which gives injectivity by integration along segments. Inverses on their images tend uniformly to the identity and Jacobian determinants tend uniformly to one. For a continuous density supported in the interior, change of variables gives convergence in \(L^1\) (the images contain its support eventually, for instance by the contraction mapping theorem on small balls). Approximate any \(L^1\) density supported on the smaller rectangle by these continuous densities. Pushforward contracts total variation. This works conditionally almost surely, hence after averaging over noises by bounded convergence. Ordinary weak convergence would be insufficient for the possibly irregular sets \(A_j\).

Some \(A_j\) has positive probability under the mixing distribution because their union covers its support. Hence the event \(p_n\in A_j,t_n\ge j\) has limiting probability \(\Pr(p\in A_j)>0\). On the other hand \(E[V(B_n)\mid p]\to0\) at every chosen \(p\). Dominated convergence gives \(E V(B_n)\to0\), contradicting the lower bound on that event. The smooth inverse transfers the area-zero conclusion back to \(z\). This proof uses two-row **observed** conflicts and repeated fixed nonzero **true** pushes, with the same declared ratio and the same Gram readout in both experiments.

## Three continuous coordinates cannot hide another necessary statistic

Suppose instead a block of \(n\) scalar readings is encoded by a continuous state \(S_n(X)\in\mathbb R^d\). Assume the state recovers \(B=X^\top X\) at each \(n\), the readout on states is Borel, and \(d\le3\). Recovery of the total squared exterior product after every single-row extension already implies recovery of \(B\), since \(\det(B+yy^\top)-\det B=y^\top\operatorname{adj}(B)y\) and \(\operatorname{adj}(B)\) determines \(B\) in two dimensions. [Joining-state theorem](e12-joining-state.md)

Fix \(n\ge3\). Encode the canonical \(n\)-row realization consisting of the two rows of \(B^{1/2}\) padded with zeros. This gives a continuous injection \(\sigma_n\) from the open three-dimensional positive definite cone into \(\mathbb R^d\), since distinct Grams are distinguishable. Invariance of domain rules out \(d<3\) by embedding into \(\mathbb R^3\), and gives an open image for \(d=3\). [Invariance of domain, Berkeley lecture notes](https://math.berkeley.edu/~nadler/215a.lecture21.pdf)

For fixed \(B>0\) the fibre \(X^\top X=B\) is connected. Multiplication on the right by \(B^{-1/2}\) identifies it with the orthonormal two-frames in \(\mathbb R^n\). Any such frame extends to a positively oriented orthonormal basis for \(n\ge3\), and \(SO(n)\) is path connected. The image of the fibre under \(S_n\) is therefore connected. Its intersection with the open canonical image consists of the single state \(\sigma_n(B)\), by Gram recovery. This singleton is both open and closed relative to that connected image, so it is the entire image. Thus for \(n\ge3\) the state depends only on \(n,B\) on full-rank samples. The law on the canonical sample is independent of \(n\) by exact zero padding, including the two-row fixed conflicts. The preceding theorem applies.

The lower bound is **four real coordinates** for continuous Euclidean states that preserve the full Gram and satisfy the two uncertainty requirements. It concerns a fixed instrument declaration. It gives no lower bound of four for a state that is allowed to discard the Gram, and no upper bound for protocols required to preserve finer per-reading queries. Four suffice on this instrument, even with exact associative joining, continuous raw laws and identifying-array calibration. [Matching construction](e12-joining-laws.md)

## A direct consequence for rotational invariance

Suppose the instrument allows two scalar pushes to be treated as the two spatial components of one vector reading, and requires this substitution to preserve the final law **in any larger block**. Require spatial frame invariance for that vector reading and allow the components to be read back as scalar pushes. This extra premise has a testable consequence. For any two rows of the scalar data matrix \(X\), rotating those two rows by a planar rotation and leaving every other row fixed must preserve the law. A two-dimensional spatial reading, or a plane in ordinary three-dimensional space, suffices.

For \(n\ge3\) these pair rotations generate \(SO(n)\). Any two full-rank \(n\)-row matrices with the same Gram differ by such a rotation, by the oriented frame-extension argument above. Equivalently a sequence of pair rotations performs a thin QR factorization and leaves just two nonzero rows. Zero padding identifies the laws of canonical realizations across \(n\). Thus the law must depend on the Gram alone on the experiments used in the theorem. **Exact interchangeability of spatial components and separate pushes, in arbitrary blocks, is incompatible with retaining spread on every fixed conflict and concentrating on all repeated valid pushes.** This conclusion assumes a measurable physical law and has no dimension bound on the internal state.

The Gaussian instrument makes the distinction subtle. Rotating two rows preserves the independent standard noise law; the two unknown true amplitudes simply rotate with the rows. The summed Gaussian candidate residual also stays exactly the same. Physical rotations of *one measured vector* are already a symmetry. Promoting this into a symmetry that mixes observations from *different pushes* is an additional premise about the acquisition protocol. If readings carry their physical grouping, a rotation of the spatial frame within each reading preserves that grouping. The four-coordinate construction respects that physical symmetry.
