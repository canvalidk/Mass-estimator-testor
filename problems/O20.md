## The item

`definition/type.md` says the definition returns a law of mass on $(0,\infty)$ and a point. Every probe
from ours21 to ours33 is built to that output, and four open items live inside it: the starting law's
shape and centre (O6, O11), the point (O2), and what the quoted width promises on noise (O15, G28).

E13 (Can, 21 Nov; branch `explore/e13-unnormalized-evidence`) proposes a different output: no starting
law and no normalisation. What is returned is the curve $K(m)$ (T73), sets of candidate masses that
keep a stated promise and are allowed to be unbounded (T75), the leftover beside them, and a
composition of curves (T74). The all-zero experiment returns the flat curve and every positive mass.

R-2026-11-22-1: this direction is pursued on main by harvesting, piece by piece, until it lands.
Nothing on the law-and-point line is struck in advance: `equation.md`, ours33 and the premise list
stand as they are, and a page changes when a harvested piece forces the change.

## What is forced about the kind of output (22 Nov; T80; R-2026-11-22-2)

From three things, two of them on the premise list (every σ is positive; no signal is promised; the quoted
uncertainty is a true statement for every true mass), the quoted uncertainty is a set of masses that can
be unbounded. An interval that is always finite fails however fast it widens. The one alternative is
truth on average over a starting law, which is what the law-and-point output states; it needs the
starting law to be a fact about the object, and nothing on the pages supports that. Sorted to the set,
provisional. The set has four shapes (an interval, "at least", "at most", every positive mass), decided
by which channel can be told from zero, and passes between them continuously in the noise angle. Where
every positive mass is kept the point's value does not matter, though a point is still returned for
type safety (Can, R-2026-11-22-2). So the kind of output E13 returns is
no longer only a direction being tried: it is what those three premises ask for. Which set is not forced
by this, and is the item below.

## What would close it

One of two things. The E13 output written as one body (which statistic, which null, which weight,
what is quoted) that is reported against every row of `GOAL.md` §4 as passes, fails or unknown, with
what replaces each row it contradicts. Or a row that the E13 output cannot meet and the law-and-point
line can, which sends the work back.

## Findings against the rows (22 Nov, Claude)

In plain words first; the labels are in brackets. A finding is against the row as it stands and does
not rewrite it.

1. **"A law of mass and a point on every measured input" [type.md Output; L2's reading of 29 Sept; T14,
   T24's "the median is c"].** The curve and its sets are returned on every input, the all-zero
   experiment included, so the output type does not depend on the data and nothing is "undefined".
   E13 as written returns no point. One is owed on every input, for type safety, and where every mass
   is compatible its value does not matter (R-2026-11-22-2, answering the question first asked here;
   T80 (5)). So E13 needs a point added, and which one, where the value matters, is open. At the all-zero experiment the answer is "every positive mass",
   which is the 29 Sept record's own §2.3 (the identity of combination) without its §2.4 and §4 (the
   law is π; the value is c): those two steps were derived on the footing that a starting law is part of
   the definition (R-2026-09-29-2 records "the value is c" as derived). If a point is wanted, the
   curve's own is the first probe's (the errors-in-variables best point), with what killed it (T73,
   "What it does not give").
2. **The starting law [L3′, L4; O6, O11].** Not used, so its shape and centre are not open under E13.
   What the starting law did goes with it: a proper law, and a point when the signal is weak.
3. **"Readings are evidence, just less" [L21, T30; R-2026-11-17-3].** The curve and the Gaussian null
   give the far pair the same width as the near pair, exactly (T75 (2)). E13 calls this Door A and puts
   the surprise beside the result. That is the outcome Can called wrong on 17 Nov ("if we just average
   the two, and quote the combined uncertainty, that seems wrong"). The join of T75 (6) does what
   17 Nov asks, on vector readings: the far pair is kept as about 5.5 ± 0.45 where the near pair is kept
   as 5.55 ± 0.13, with no σ revised and no number handed over, and for the far pair that width is the
   same at σ = 0.1, 0.02 and 0.004 (bounded, non-vanishing confidence as σ shrinks: T30's requirement,
   and the 17 Nov sort's "a width that stops depending on σ"). Two costs. Under the declared Gaussian the
   extra width is not a consequence of the inputs: the Gaussian null is already right at every level of
   disagreement, and the join is right and wider (T75 (5)). And on scalar readings it says nothing until
   there are six: Can's two scenes as he gave them (two scalar readings) are both kept at every mass.
4. **Z [L20].** The curve, the leftover, T45's score, a zero-safe weight and the scale-free null leave a
   zero reading out exactly. The unweighted Gaussian test and the probability bound beside the leftover
   count it. A zero-safe weight is blind at right angles (T75 (3): a single opposed reading is kept at
   the one mass it fits worst). This is the 18 Nov finding from the other side (`O14.md`, 18 Nov night,
   items 2–4: an exact, zero-safe, continuous verdict cannot read a leftover at right angles).
5. **Uniqueness [F2; TASK §5].** T75 (1) is exact for every statistic, and E13 offers a choice of null
   (its four rows), of weight (an exact family in which no member is best at both of two jobs, T76), of
   level and of domain. E13's own closing list
   calls the first "an instrument declaration" and the second "a power preference… not a property of
   Newtonian mass". Under F2 a choice the function makes is an alternative mechanics, and a choice the
   caller makes must be something practice supplies, declared before the data. Which of these choices
   are inputs and which are forced is the main open part of this item.
6. **Composition [L5, L14; R-2026-10-28-1].** T74: the curves compose exactly and with no starting law;
   the raw composite set fails clause (c) at weak signal, and E13's repair needs more than the two
   curves.
7. **A true statement on noise [property N; G28].** Met by allowing a set to be every positive mass,
   which is what B24 (Dufour) and T45 §4 already said any honest width must do.
8. **Nothing borrowed, no size law [L19, L10].** Met: each reading is split by its own candidate
   projection and nothing learned from one reading is used on another (as T45 §2).

9. **"A null result enters combination with weight zero" [R-2026-09-29-3], beside consistency [L11].**
   True of exactly zero readings and of a block declared to carry no signal. Not attainable for a block
   of noise-only readings together with L11's rate, by any procedure (T77 (1)): a reading at signal $r$
   is undone by about $r^4$ noise-only readings. Under E13 this shows as the set growing to every
   positive mass (T77 (5)); on the law-and-point line it is the 9 Nov result that the law cannot return
   to π on noise. So this is not a cost of E13: it is a conflict between two rows, and E13 states it
   more plainly.
10. **What is carried between results [composition clause (b); combination].** A set, a p-value curve and
   a normalised law are each too little to combine two results; the score and variance curves are
   enough; on one instrument the unnormalised curve is exactly enough, level included (T77 (4)). This
   is the strongest argument found so far for E13's choice not to normalise.

Not checked against any row: whether the kept sets shrink onto the true mass at L11's rate.

## What E12 says here (its scalar proof harvested and checked as T78; the rest read in summary only)

- **On the law-and-point line four requirements can be met together** (zero neutrality, L11's rate with a
  calibrated width, bounded confidence on readings no mass explains, a true statement on noise), on one
  scalar instrument at a fixed noise ratio, by a law with a starting law and a median (T78 (1)–(4)). So
  what E13 gives up, the law and the point, is not forced by those four. E13 has to be preferred for
  another reason: the starting law's shape and centre, the point, and what is carried (findings 2, 10).
- **They are met by a continuum** (T78 (5)). The uniqueness problem is the same on both lines: here an
  exponent and a count's constant, under E13 a weight and a null (finding 5).
- **What normalising erases** is the trace of the scatter, equivalently the least value of the curve
  (T78; T73 (5); T77 (4)). E12's "keep the relative curve and its minimum residual" is E13's curve.
- **E13's second insight, worded exactly.** It says a widening on disagreeing readings contradicts exact
  Gaussian errors with σ believed. What holds: the widening cannot be a consequence of those inputs
  (T75 (2)). What E12 adds: a rule for it can still be added on top without inconsistency (T78), and
  any such rule is one of a continuum.
- **A calibrated width and a persistent widening conflict** (T78 (6)). E12's witness keeps the first;
  T75 (6)'s join keeps the second and over-covers. Which is meant is asked below.
- **Final factors cannot multiply** if they stay bounded on conflicts (T78 (7)): state is carried and the
  discount recomputed, the conclusion of T77 (4) from the discount's side.
- **How much of a law the readings alone supply** (T79, from the conditional-ranks note): T45's signed
  ranks rise by $W=2\Phi(\sqrt L-\sqrt l)-1$ and fall by the same, so they are not one CDF; on the positive
  masses the share $M\le W$ of a law can be read off them and the rest cannot. On the old line the
  starting law speaks for $1-M$; under E13 it is left unsaid. That is the first question below, as a
  number: 0 of a law at the all-zero experiment, 0.38 for one weak reading, all of it at strong signal.
