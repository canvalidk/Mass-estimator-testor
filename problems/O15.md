# O15: The null floor

**Status:** open. B13 proves zero uniform coverage in the corner (every finite N); B14 closes the non-hierarchical cure table (the modified profile is not a law); the prior-averaged floor remains open. T45 (Codex, 3 Nov; harvested 7 Nov) proves the fixed-N uniform proper-law corollary directly; T41/O19 separate a third regime (divergent energy without identification). **9 Nov (exploration E10; Claude's sort, provisional):** an order-free law consistent at L11's rate cannot return to π on pure noise, so the bounded null tilt stays and what the quoted width promises there is the open statement (`../definition/proof.md` G28). **Kind:** proof or counterexample. **Last updated:** 2026-11-10 (Codex, B21; prior intake retained).

## The item (lemma list d4, verbatim)

**The null floor.** $\eta$ stops the null tilt growing but leaves it bounded (about 2 nats; the quoted width covers 75–85% against a mass drawn from $\pi$). Under Z and L19 null evidence does not accumulate (a long null series looks no more null than a short one), so this may be the most any law can do, with "we know nothing" living in the resolution.

## What would close it

a proof, or a counterexample law

### 30 Oct: two regimes, and what Gleser–Hwang closes (Claude's sort, not ruled)

Can, 30 Oct: "But full coverage is impossible." True in one regime and false in the other; the project
already split them on 27 Sept (`../docs/ours26/README.md`, "Coverage: two different failures"), and
the split had dropped out of the living layer.

**(a) The corner: signal → 0 (this item).** Standard name: Gleser & Hwang (1987); Dufour (1997) for the
general form. Near parameters where $m$ is not identified, no procedure whose interval is always finite
has a positive guaranteed coverage. Ours is always finite: L2 promises a mass on every measured input
and π is proper (G8), so every quoted interval is bounded. Claude's direct argument for our type, to be
checked (B13): as every true push → 0 the readings' law tends to one that does not depend on $m$, so the
interval's law tends to one law for every $m$; a.s. finite, so for $m$ far enough from $c$ its coverage
→ 0. Coverage at the null is therefore set by where $m$ sits against π (T24: the all-zero law is π), which
is why the floor is quoted against a mass drawn from π. **So the item's "may be the most any law can do"
is half answered:** no law covers uniformly here (a theorem, if B13 confirms the hypotheses hold); the
number 75–85% against π is not fixed by Gleser–Hwang and is still open.

**(b) Fixed nonzero signal, N growing.** Standard name: the Neyman–Scott incidental-parameter problem
(1948), here through the flat measure on the latent pairs (L10's endpoint; T18; Lindley 1966 in
`../docs/papers/jrsssb_28_2_288.pdf`). Gleser–Hwang does not reach it. The flat factor alone gets
**worse** as readings are added; ours30 with η approaches nominal (`tools/ours30_coverage_vs_n.py`,
100 series a cell, so ±2–3 points):

| 3D | N = 5 | 20 | 80 | 200 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| signal 1, ours30 | 0.84 | 0.91 | 0.95 | 0.93 |
| signal 1, without η | 0.84 | 0.79 | 0.69 | 0.72 |
| signal 2, ours30 | 0.90 | 0.92 | 0.91 | 0.95 |
| signal 2, without η | 0.89 | 0.90 | 0.87 | 0.82 |

**What follows.**
- **Property N is not mis-stated.** As ruled (R-2026-09-29-4) it asks for "a true statement", which
  step 11 reads as the Bartlett identity in the long-series limit; it never asked for nominal coverage
  in the corner. It is consistent with Gleser–Hwang. (This corrects Claude's chat suggestion of the same
  afternoon that property N demanded exact coverage and should be weakened.)
- **η has a real target and reaches it in the limit.** The shortfall at small N in (b) is G3's
  finite-N form (O14), not a wall. What is wrong with η is its form (a power on every reading: L24, B12),
  not its job. The standard non-hierarchical cures for Neyman–Scott (conditional likelihood; Cox & Reid

### 30 Oct (later): can a latent measure do η's job without a size law? (Claude's sort, not ruled)

Closes the "where to look" of the section above. The question is regime (b): with the pushes free
(L10) and nothing borrowed (L19), is there a measure on the latent pairs under which the law is a
genuine likelihood, so that no η is needed?

**What the flat law is, in standard terms.** Whitened, each push parameterised by its arc-length $t_i$
along the line $u_m$ (Beale's scaling; T6, T7): $y_i=t_iu_m+\varepsilon_i$. Integrating $t_i$ flat gives
$\exp(-K_i(m)/2)$ up to an $m$-free constant, which is also the **profile** likelihood (the maximum
over $t_i$), because the nuisance information $j_{tt}=\lvert u_m\rvert^2=1$ does not depend on $m$.
So the law is a profile likelihood with $N$ incidental parameters: Neyman–Scott (1948). Its score is
unbiased (the point is consistent: TLS), but the information identity fails, $E[J]>\mathcal H$, by a
per-reading term the noise alone contributes (T26). A likelihood whose identity fails is a
**quasi-likelihood** (Wedderburn 1974; McCullagh 1983), and the standard calibration of a
quasi-likelihood is the sandwich or Bartlett's factor: $\mathcal H/J$, which is η (T31).

**The non-hierarchical cures, one by one.** Every standard repair of a profile likelihood for
incidental parameters is checked against this model:

| cure | what it gives here | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cox & Reid (1987) adjusted profile, $L_p\lvert j_{tt}\rvert^{-1/2}$ | the flat law, unchanged | $j_{tt}=1$, $m$-free |
| integrated likelihood with the reference (flat) prior on a location nuisance (Berger, Liseo & Wolpert 1999) | the flat law, unchanged | same integral |
| conditional likelihood (Kalbfleisch & Sprott 1970; Andersen 1970) | **does not exist** | the sufficient statistic for $t_i$ given $m$ is $y_i\cdot u_m$, which depends on $m$: the case KS name as where conditioning fails |
| marginal likelihood on a $t$-free statistic | **does not exist** | a statistic free of $t_i$ for every $m$ must be orthogonal to the unknown true line |
| Barndorff-Nielsen's modified profile (1983), the Jacobian $\lvert\partial\hat t/\partial\hat t_m\rvert$ | **not flat, and not a law** (B14, 31 Oct) | the Jacobian is $\lvert\cos(\theta-\hat\theta)\rvert^{-1}$ exactly for $k=1$ (its $k$-th power by the expected-score approximation); it diverges where the candidate is perpendicular to the reading, which for an anti-parallel reading is a positive mass, so the law is improper on valid inputs |
| the hierarchical cure: a size law on the pushes (Lindley & El-Sayyad 1968; ours28) | **restores calibration** (below) | forbidden by L10 (a declared population of setups, R-2026-09-30-3). **4 Nov:** its shrinkage $\hat\beta=\hat\Delta/(\hat\Delta+M)$ is η's long-series limit (O14, 4 Nov): η is this cure's factor recovered without the declaration |
| Moreira (2009), maximal invariant likelihood (B21, 10 Nov) | **proper laws on the common-instrument slice; Z and T30 fail** | row rotations retain $(G,d)$ and one total signal energy $\Delta$; profiling recovers the regular asymptotic shrinkage, but zero padding changes $d$ and shrinking-noise evidence is unbounded. Flat $d\Delta$ integration is an unforced comparison; T69 and the B21 section below |

So (B14 closed the **per-reading** table, 31 Oct): **every non-hierarchical construction reviewed there

### B13 (31 Oct): hypotheses, a direct corner theorem, and the fixed-mass limit

**Result.** For every fixed finite reading count and declared design, any measurable
interval procedure with an almost surely finite upper endpoint has **infimum coverage
exactly zero** over the allowed positive masses and free latent signals. This remains
true if every latent signal is required to be nonzero. It confirms the corner's
provisional sort; it does not re-sort regime (b), propose a repair, or determine the
75–85% prior-averaged floor. The brief's stronger prediction of a zero limit at each of
$m=ce^{\pm3}$ is false for the current law; those limits are positive.

#### 1. Source status and the theorem actually checked

**Item 1 of the brief is incomplete at the original-source level.** The Gleser–Hwang
(1987) PDF was not fetched: the DOI/Project Euclid endpoint returned an access-rejection
page and JSTOR redirected to metadata. Its original theorem, section and equation
numbers are unverified. No PDF was added to this public repository.

A verified published restatement is [Berger, Liseo & Wolpert (1999),
*Integrated Likelihood Methods for Eliminating Nuisance Parameters*](https://www2.stat.duke.edu/~berger/papers/brunero.pdf),
§5.1, **Theorem 1, printed p. 16, condition (42)**. Paraphrased: a density model with
target $\theta$ and nuisance $\lambda$ has an unbounded target subset. At a common
nuisance limit in its closure, each fixed target's density converges pointwise to the
same proper density. A confidence procedure with a positive uniform level must then
produce unbounded sets with positive probability. **Theorem 1′, p. 17, (43)** allows
a joint sequence with divergent targets and a proper limiting density. This is stronger
than a conclusion about expected diameter alone.

[Dufour's author version](https://jeanmariedufour.github.io/Dufour_1994_ImpossibilityTheorems_W.pdf)
identifies itself as the September 1996 version of the paper published in *Econometrica*
65 (1997), 1365–1388. Its **§3, Corollary 3.4, printed p. 6, (3.9)** gives zero level
from almost sure boundedness at nonidentification. **Theorem 3.6 / Corollary 3.7,
p. 7, (3.10)–(3.11)** extend the result using common support. These are the verified
**author-version** numbers, not asserted journal-version numbers. Its boundary-event
assumption F.2 is not automatic for an arbitrary measurable interval. The direct
total-variation proof below needs no such assumption.

#### 2. Numbered hypotheses checked against the type

Fix all declared instrument facts, including $c$, the covariances, subspaces, reading
structure and windows. Here $N$ is the reading count and $N_i$ the declared subspace
matrix. Stack the finite-dimensional observations into
$$
 Y\sim P_{m,\alpha}=N(\mu(m,\alpha),\Sigma),\qquad
 \mu_i(m,\alpha)=(mN_i\alpha_i,N_i\alpha_i),\qquad
 \Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\Sigma_1,\ldots,\Sigma_N).
$$
This is the Gaussian model L13 with independent readings and known positive definite
covariances (L2). It is enough to establish a limit on uniform coverage in this model;
it is not a claim that every real instrument's errors are Gaussian.

| Hypothesis | Check against the type |
| --- | --- |
| H1. A dominated density model for a target and a nuisance | **Holds:** $m$ is the target, $\alpha=(\alpha_i)_i$ the deterministic nuisance. Gaussian densities are measurable and strictly positive on the same Euclidean data space. Each $N_i$ has its declared full column rank. |
| H2. An unbounded target subset | **Holds:** $(0,\infty)$ remains unbounded despite positivity and the line passing through the origin. The scale $c$ is declared information, not a restriction on possible planted $m$. |
| H3. A common nuisance limit in the closure | **Holds:** $\alpha\to0$ is allowed by L10, which declares no lower bound on signal size. Even if exact zero were excluded, it is in the closure, with every component vector nonzero along a suitable sequence. No population or size law is introduced. |
| H4. Pointwise convergence to a proper density independent of the fixed target | **Holds:** for each fixed $m>0$, $\mu(m,\alpha)\to0$, so the Gaussian density converges pointwise to $P_0=N(0,\Sigma)$, independent of $m$. The stronger total-variation convergence is proved below. |
| H5. A measurable confidence procedure claimed to have positive uniform frequentist level | **Fails for an almost surely bounded procedure on this full parameter space**, by the corollary below. In the source theorem it is the assumption that would force unbounded intervals. A nominal 95% posterior interval does not itself establish this hypothesis. |
| H6. Almost surely bounded quoted intervals, in applying the contrapositive | **Holds for the current law:** proper $\pi$ and the bounded explained energies give a proper law and finite positive central quantiles. L2's promise of a defined point alone would not prove bounded intervals. |

The brief's “G8 in premises” points to the starting-law properness discussion in
`../definition/proof.md` step 6 / G8; it is not a row of `premises.md`. For H6,
$0\le H_i(m)\le E_i<\infty$ is the noise-metric projection bound, so the current
data multiplier is between 1 and $\exp(\sum_iE_i/2)$. Integrating against a proper
starting law gives a finite positive normalizer. Its central probabilities strictly
between 0 and 1 have finite positive quantiles on $(0,\infty)$.

For Dufour's additional conditions, the Euclidean parameter spaces, common dominating
measure, density convergence and common support hold. Measurability of interval and
diameter events is required of a procedure. His F.2 boundary regularity is **unknown
in this generality** and is not needed for the corollary we establish independently.
Thus this section verifies the published restatement's sampling conditions, not every
extra condition of every version of the original papers.

#### 3. Corollary for the type and its proof

Let $I_N(Y)$ be any measurable interval procedure whose upper endpoint $U_N(Y)$ is
finite $P_0$-almost surely. Its guaranteed coverage on the type is
$$
 \gamma_N=\inf_{m>0,\,\alpha}
 P_{m,\alpha}\{m\in I_N(Y)\}.
$$
**Then $\gamma_N=0$.** Finite expected diameter, continuity of the interval rule,
and absence of probability on its boundaries are unnecessary.

Put $q_{0,N}(m)=P_0\{m\in I_N(Y)\}$. Since
$$
 0\le q_{0,N}(m)\le P_0\{U_N(Y)\ge m\}\longrightarrow0
 \quad(m\to\infty),
$$
the conclusion is immediate if zero signals are included: $P_{m,0}=P_0$ for every
$m$. This gives a zero **infimum**, not necessarily an attained zero coverage.

It also holds over nonzero signals alone. Take $m_j=j$ and
$\alpha_{i,j}=j^{-2}b_i$, with fixed $b_i\ne0$ for every reading. Then
$$
 \mu_{i,j}=(j^{-1}N_ib_i,j^{-2}N_ib_i)\to0,\qquad
 \delta_j^2=\mu_j^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mu_j\to0.
$$
For Gaussian shifts with this fixed covariance,
$D_{\rm KL}(P_{m_j,\alpha_j}\Vert P_0)=\delta_j^2/2$, so Pinsker's inequality gives
$\lVert P_{m_j,\alpha_j}-P_0\rVert_{\rm TV}\le\delta_j/2\to0$,
where total-variation distance means $\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|$.
This controls **every** measurable event, including the moving event involving $m_j$:
$$
 P_{m_j,\alpha_j}\{m_j\in I_N(Y)\}
 \le q_{0,N}(m_j)+\delta_j/2\longrightarrow0.
$$
Nonnegativity of probabilities proves the exact infimum 0, rather than merely a
shortfall below the nominal $1-\alpha$. Known noise, $m>0$, arbitrary declared
subspaces and a line through the origin do not remove this sequence.

**Reading-count quantifiers.** This holds separately for every finite $N$ and each
fixed declared design. Hence no family of always-finite procedures has a positive
guaranteed finite-sample level on the unrestricted type at any $N$. There is also a
triangular sequence with $N\to\infty$ and coverage tending to zero: choose $m_N$
so that $P_{0,N}(U_N\ge m_N)<\varepsilon_N$, then choose all latent signals nonzero
but small enough that the **aggregate** Mahalanobis mean norm $\delta_N<\varepsilon_N$,
where $\varepsilon_N\downarrow0$. The same bound gives coverage below
$3\varepsilon_N/2$. Merely shrinking each individual signal is not enough to assert
uniform convergence in $N$ without controlling that aggregate norm. This statement
does not address the fixed-nonzero-signal regime (b).

#### 4. Fixed planted masses and the requested test

For a **fixed** $m$, shrinking the latent signals instead gives
$$
 P_{m,\alpha}\{m\in I_N(Y)\}\longrightarrow q_{0,N}(m).
$$
Nothing in the infimum theorem says this limit is zero at every fixed mass. For the
current half-Cauchy, the two masses requested in the brief are inside the central 95%
prior interval:
$$
 F_\pi(ce^{-3})=0.0316692826\ldots,\qquad
 F_\pi(ce^3)=0.9683307174\ldots,
$$
whereas the endpoints have CDFs 0.025 and 0.975. In fact both fixed-mass null
coverages are **strictly positive**, not just unforced by the impossibility theorem.
As measured $y\to0$, set $B(y)=\sum_iE_i(y)/2\to0$. The bound
$1\le L_y\le e^{B(y)}$ implies
$\sup_t|F_y(t)-F_\pi(t)|\le e^{B(y)}-1\to0$, regardless of how $g$ and $\eta$
vary near zero. The two prior CDFs have a positive margin from the interval endpoints.
Thus an open neighborhood of measured zero covers both masses. The positive Gaussian
density gives this neighborhood positive $P_0$ probability, proving
$q_{0,N}(ce^{\pm3})>0$ for each fixed finite $N$.

`../tests/test_o15_corner.py::test_zero_push_coverage_at_fixed_mass_approaches_positive_null_floor`
checks the actual decline and approach to that positive floor. It uses 300 paired
series, $N=20$, 3D, $c=1$, known $\Sigma_i=I_6$, seed 31313. Directions $u_i$ cycle
the coordinate axes and are fixed before the data; the true acceleration is
$\alpha_i=r u_i/\sqrt{1+m^2}$, so each true stacked pair has noise-unit norm $r$.
The same independent Gaussian force/acceleration errors are used at every signal
level and both planted masses. The fast batch ours30 rule is anchored against
`met.full.ours30` on a null series. All intervals are resolved and finite.

#### 5. What remains open, and the distinction from T24

The measured all-zero event in T24 has probability zero under the positive definite
Gaussian sampling law. Zero **true** signals instead give random $Y\sim P_0$;
their interval law is generally not the deterministic prior interval. This corrects
the use of T24 in the 30 Oct sketch above. For a planted $M\sim\Pi$ independently
of pure-noise data, the average coverage is
$$
 \int q_{0,N}(m)\,\Pi(dm)
 =E_{P_0}[\Pi(I_N(Y))].
$$
It is not generally the posterior probability assigned to that random interval,
and the corner theorem fixes neither this average nor the stated 75–85% figure.
It also supplies no bound depending on $\pi$ alone. Those are the remaining O15
questions; no alternative law is proposed here.

Property N, R-2026-09-29-4, says that uncertainty should grow with a wandering null
point so the result remains a true statement. That wording is not a positive uniform
frequentist coverage assumption on this whole unbounded parameter space. The
impossibility corollary therefore does not contradict it or alter its premise.
Regime (b), its interpretation, and its proposed repairs are unchanged by B13.

### B14 (31 Oct): a local modified profile is not a global latent measure

**Result.** The Jacobian does depend on mass. A regular one-reading $k=1$ calculation
gives a reciprocal cosine, exactly; the Severini expected-score-covariance approximation
gives its $k$th power in the isotropic $k$-component model. It does **not** reduce to the
flat law. But the regular construction is undefined on allowed boundary/null inputs,
and its natural whole-projective continuation has nonintegrable poles at positive
candidate masses. Thus the brief's implication “nonconstant Jacobian, hence a modified
law to calibrate” fails. Following `../briefs/README.md` step 3, the calibration branch
stops at this obstruction. No prior, noise model, tempering or positive-mass restriction
is changed, and no population of latent signals is introduced.

#### 1. Formula, sample-space derivative, and sources

[Barndorff–Nielsen (1994), *Adjusted Versions of Profile Likelihood and Directed
Likelihood, and Extended Likelihood*](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1994.tb01965.x),
§2, p. 127 and §2.1, p. 128, equations (2.2)–(2.4), requires the MLE together with
an ancillary to specify the sufficient statistic. Its adjustment is
$$
 L_{mp}(\theta)=L_p(\theta)|j_{tt}(\theta,\widehat t_\theta)|^{-1/2}D(\theta),
 \qquad D=\left|\frac{\partial\widehat t_\theta}{\partial\widehat t}\right|^{-1}
 =\frac{|j_{tt}|}{|\ell_{t;\widehat t}|}.
$$
The sample derivative holds **the interest MLE and ancillary fixed**. It is not an
arbitrary derivative through a formula evaluated on a fixed observed data vector.
[Ventura & Racugno (2011)](https://www.research.unipd.it/retrieve/e14fb270-3213-3de1-e053-1705fe0ac030/euclid.ba.1339616470.pdf),
§3, pp. 415–416, equations (8) and (10), independently gives this inverse-Jacobian
form and its equivalent ratio $|j_{tt}|^{1/2}/|\ell_{t;\widehat t}|$.

When an exact ancillary construction is unavailable, the expected-cross-score
version of the Severini approximation replaces the sample-space cross derivative by
$$
 I_{t;t}(\theta,t;\theta_0,t_0)
 =E_{\theta_0,t_0}[s_t(\theta,t;Y)s_t(\theta_0,t_0;Y)^\top].
$$
This is the expected covariance form, not a covariance estimated by pooling readings.
Its definition and regularity conditions are discussed in [Severini (2025 online;
2026 volume), *Some properties of a parameterization useful in integrated likelihood
inference*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00184-025-01006-1), §§2 and 5.
The conditions include a regular interior fit and nonsingular information. Ordinary
fixed-dimensional asymptotics do not establish uniform accuracy when signal vanishes
or the nuisance dimension grows with the reading count. The 1983 and 1998 full texts
were not retrieved; the formulas used here are checked in the cited accessible primary
papers. No PDF is added to the repository.

#### 2. The exact regular $k=1$ control and the general approximation

In noise units write
$$
 U_\theta=\begin{pmatrix}\sin\theta\,I_k\\\cos\theta\,I_k\end{pmatrix},\quad
 Y=U_\theta t+\varepsilon,\quad \varepsilon\sim N(0,I_{2k}),\quad
 \widehat t_\theta=U_\theta^\top Y,\quad j_{tt}=I_k.
$$
The raw profile is $L_p\propto\exp(\|\widehat t_\theta\|^2/2)$, with a
candidate-independent factor suppressed. In the scalar case $k=1$, on a regular
whole-projective/open-angle chart with $Y\ne0$, the two MLE coordinates already specify
the two observations: $Y=\widehat t\,u_{\widehat\theta}$. The ancillary dimension is
zero. Holding $\widehat\theta$ fixed gives
$$
 \widehat t_\theta=\widehat t\cos(\theta-\widehat\theta),\qquad
 D(\theta)=|\cos(\theta-\widehat\theta)|^{-1}.
$$
This is an exact local Jacobian calculation, and directly shows why $j_{tt}=1$ does
not make the full adjustment constant. Its inverse is essential: multiplying by
$|\cos(\theta-\widehat\theta)|$ would reverse the prescribed determinant.
The information-only Cox–Reid adjustment remains flat in these arc-length coordinates.
It omits the additional sample-space determinant; using that lower-order adjustment
would not establish that the BN Jacobian is constant.

For $k>1$, no exact ancillary is established here. Keeping a Cartesian fitted residual
fixed would not establish one: the MLE satisfies $\widehat t^\top\widehat r=0$, so that
residual is not a free coordinate while all of $\widehat t$ varies. Its size distribution
also depends on the true signal. The general expression below is therefore explicitly
the **expected-cross-score approximation**, not an exact BN assertion for all $k$.
Since $s_t(\theta,t)=U_\theta^\top Y-t$, direct Gaussian expectation gives
the following result, holding the observed fitted parameter values fixed while
integrating over a fresh Gaussian replicate $Y$:
$$
 I_{t;t}(\theta,\widehat t_\theta;\widehat\theta,\widehat t)
 =U_\theta^\top U_{\widehat\theta}
 =\cos(\theta-\widehat\theta)I_k.
$$
Consequently
$$
 L_{\rm cov}(\theta)\propto
 L_p(\theta)|\cos(\theta-\widehat\theta)|^{-k}.
$$
This is nonconstant wherever the reference direction is fixed. It agrees with the
exact scalar control on its regular chart. It is a local higher-order adjustment;
the approximation does not authorize evaluating it through a zero of the determinant.
For general noise-orthonormal candidate matrices $U(m)$, the same Gaussian calculation
gives the reciprocal determinant $|\det[U(m)^\top U(\widehat m)]|^{-1}$; the common
cosine is the isotropic specialization, not a formula asserted for every covariance.
The isotropic subset is sufficient for the full-type obstruction below.

Changing nuisance units does not remove this dependence. If $t=A_\theta\alpha$, then
at the constrained fit $j_{\alpha\alpha}=A_\theta^\top j_{tt}A_\theta$ and the cross
derivative transforms by $A_\theta^\top$ and $A_{\widehat\theta}$ on its two sides.
The ratio $|j|^{1/2}/|\ell_{t;\widehat t}|$ changes only by the
candidate-independent factor $|A_{\widehat\theta}|^{-1}$. In unit-noise physical mass
coordinates $m=\tan\theta$, the approximation is proportional to
$(1+m^2)^{k/2}/|1+m\widehat m|^k$, the same reciprocal cosine up to a data-only factor.

#### 3. Series construction is not automatic multiplication of private corrections

Using each reading's private fit would give the formal product
$$
 \prod_i L_{p,i}(\theta)|\cos(\theta-\widehat\theta_i)|^{-k_i}.
$$
The modified profile for the **common-mass model** instead uses a common unrestricted
interest MLE in its sample coordinates. Its expected-score approximation has a
block-diagonal nuisance covariance and gives
$$
 L_{p,\mathrm{series}}(\theta)
 |\cos(\theta-\widehat\theta_{\mathrm{series}})|^{-p},\qquad p=\sum_i k_i.
$$
These are different constructions. For example, $k=1$, $Y_1=(1,1)$, $Y_2=(0,2)$
give private angles $\pi/4,0$, while the common fit is
$\tfrac12\arctan(1/2)$. Their covariance determinants at a generic candidate angle
do not agree. The common fit is reported only to check the standard construction;
it is not adopted as a cross-reading adjustment under the brief's reading-local
prescription. Sharing mass is already allowed by L19; this calculation does not
re-interpret that premise as forbidding all common-mass inference.

#### 4. Allowed inputs block a global mass law

Keep $m>0$ unchanged, and use unit noise and $c=1$. For the allowed scalar observed
pair $Y=(F,a)=(-1,2)$, the whole-projective fit has
$\widehat\theta=-\arctan(1/2)$. Its formal continuation to positive candidate masses
gives
$$
 H(m)=\frac{(2-m)^2}{1+m^2},\qquad
 |\cos(\theta-\widehat\theta)|^{-1}
 =\frac{\sqrt5\sqrt{1+m^2}}{|2-m|},\quad \theta=\arctan m.
$$
The raw factor is finite and nonzero at $m=2$, but the proposed modified law with
the existing half-Cauchy prior is proportional to
$$
 \frac{\sqrt5}{\sqrt{1+m^2}\,|2-m|}
 \exp\!\left(\frac{(2-m)^2}{2(1+m^2)}\right).
$$
It has a logarithmically divergent integral around the positive mass $m=2$.
The test checks the pole coefficient and growth of truncated integrals. In 3D,
$F=(-1,0,0)$ and $a=(1,0,0)$ similarly give a cubic pole at $m=1$ in the
expected-score continuation. These examples change no restriction or input.

The distinction between a continuation and a valid restricted-model construction
matters: on these anti-parallel data the MLE **within** the positive open angle
interval is absent or is a boundary limit. Regular BN theory does not apply there.
Using a whole-projective fit illustrates why the natural continuation fails; it is
not a claim of an exact regular BN posterior for the restricted model on those data.
On regular positive-fit data the local adjustment is well defined, but that does not
extend it to every input promised by the type.

This is not a probability-zero concern for the approximation. In the isotropic null
world each private best whole-line angle is negative exactly when $F_i\cdot a_i<0$,
apart from ties of probability zero. That event has probability $1/2$ by symmetry.
For independent readings, the private product has an interior pole with probability
$1-2^{-N}$, about $0.99999905$ at $N=20$. The common-fit continuation has such a pole
when $\sum_i F_i\cdot a_i<0$, with null probability $1/2$. At every finite nonzero
signal, Gaussian full support still gives a positive probability of problematic inputs.

#### 5. Z, units, swap, and why the Bartlett simulation stops

At a measured zero reading, $\widehat t=0$ and the direction MLE is unidentified;
the regular information is singular. There is no canonical private correction there.
Along paths $Y=\epsilon u_\beta\to0$, the raw factor tends to 1, but the correction
retains $|\cos(\theta-\beta)|^{-k}$ and depends on the approach direction. Assigning
an arbitrary fitted angle at exactly zero therefore neither proves Z nor supplies
a continuous reading-local extension. If a zero reading is added using the common
fit, the unchanged raw profile acquires an extra reciprocal-cosine power; that also
fails the required constant zero-reading factor.

For regular isotropic inputs the **formal factors** respect units and the swap:
scaling instrument units preserves the whitened angles, while a channel exchange
reflects both $\theta$ and $\widehat\theta$, preserving $|\cos(\theta-\widehat\theta)|$.
The tests check these identities. They do not turn an improper expression into a law.

Even the formal Bartlett moments need not exist. For the exact scalar control at a
true angle, let $a=u_\theta^\top Y=t+\varepsilon_\parallel$ and
$b=(\partial_\theta u_\theta)^\top Y=\varepsilon_\perp$, independent normal variables.
Differentiating the formal log factor gives
$$
 S_{mp}=ab+\tan(\theta-\widehat\theta)=b(a-1/a),\qquad
 C_{mp}=a^2-b^2-1-b^2/a^2.
$$
The normal density of $a$ is positive at zero, so $E|S_{mp}|=\infty$,
$E[S_{mp}^2]=\infty$ and $E[C_{mp}]=-\infty$. An iterated symmetric principal
value for the score is not the finite expectation required by a Bartlett identity.
Finite Monte Carlo averages would conceal this obstruction. The requested
3D/$N=20$ calibration cells are therefore not reported as a comparison of three
globally defined laws. No new study or coverage table is run after the properness
failure; the existing flat/ours30 records are left unchanged.

### 3 Nov: the model-specific corner is proved; exact sets are a different output (Codex)

for all candidates. The set may be unbounded or disconnected and cannot
serve as a proper law's central interval. Near the identifying Gaussian

### 9 Nov: the law cannot return to π on noise and keep L11's rate (exploration E10, Codex; Claude's check and sort)

On the branch `explore/e10-null-limit-prior` (`explore/e10-null-washout-consistency.md`, `explore/e10-null-limit-prior.md`,
`tests/test_null_washout.py`, `tests/test_null_limit.py`); read by Claude, left there. "E10" here is the exploration's
id, not VD-Newton's entry.

**Standard mathematics.** A constrained-risk inequality (Duchi and Ruan 2020; the Brown–Low line): a statistic made to
settle at one experiment is limited in its response at the experiments contiguous to it. Applied here to the returned
law, not to a point estimate.

**Three requirements**, on the scalar Gaussian slice of T41–T53 ($Y_i=r_iu_\theta+Z_i$, unit noise, one instrument, a
fixed full-support $\pi$, $\Delta_N=\sum_{i\le N}r_i^2$):

- **W**, washout: on pure noise the returned law tends to $\pi$ (in total variation, in probability). Not on the premise
  list; a candidate answer to what the law should do on a long null series.
- **P**: the output does not depend on the order of the readings.
- **C**: the law concentrates at the true mass on every series with $\Delta_N/\sqrt N\to\infty$ (L11 as restated 7 Nov,
  at the level of the law; for a $\pi$ with a strict unique median the argument reaches the median as well).

**Codex's results.** (1) W and C fail together on triangular arrays, for every procedure, with no use of P or Z.
(2) With P they fail together on one fixed infinite history with $\Delta_N/\sqrt N\to\infty$ and every $r_i\to0$.
(3) Controls: without P an order-weighted law has both; with P and Z a smooth attenuation has both only above a margin,
$\mathcal I_N/\log N\to\infty$ with $\mathcal I_N=\Delta_N^2/(N+\Delta_N)$, on a schedule nothing forces.
(4) T42's pure-noise limit, a bounded random tilt of $\pi$, does not preserve any full-support $\pi$ on positive mass
even on average (the half-Cauchy's average moves 0.0955 in total variation); $\eta_G$ (T70) has the same null limit as
the Bartlett η.

**Checked by Claude: (1).** Take a neighbourhood $B$ of the true mass with $\pi(B)<1$,
$A_N=\{Q_N(B)>(1+\pi(B))/2\}$, $\alpha_N=P_0(A_N)$. W gives $\alpha_N\to0$; C needs $P(A_N)\to1$. Mix over fair signs
at equal squared radius $t=\Delta_N/N$: the likelihood ratio to pure noise is $L=\prod_ie^{-t/2}\cosh(\sqrt t\,u_\theta\!\cdot\!Y_i)$,
with $E_0L^2=\cosh(t)^N\le e^{\Delta_N^2/2N}$, so by Cauchy–Schwarz
$P_{\rm mix}(A_N)\le\sqrt{\alpha_N}\,e^{\Delta_N^2/4N}$. Choose $\Delta_N=\sqrt N\,d_N$ with
$d_N^2=\min(\log(1/\alpha_N),\sqrt N)$: then $d_N\to\infty$, so the array identifies, and $P_{\rm mix}(A_N)\le\alpha_N^{1/4}\to0$.
At each $N$ some sign vector does no better than the mixture.

**Checked by Claude, 9 Nov (later): (2) and the median corollary. No gap found.** The steps, as verified:

- *Second moment.* Mix over fair signs and a uniform placement of the amplitudes $a_i=r_i^2$ on the $N$ positions. For
  $X\sim N(0,1)$, $E\exp((\epsilon\sqrt a+\epsilon'\sqrt{a'})X-(a+a')/2)=\exp(\epsilon\epsilon'\sqrt{aa'})$, so
  $E_0L_{\rm sym}^2=E_\sigma\prod_i\cosh\sqrt{a_ia_{\sigma(i)}}\le E_\sigma\exp(\tfrac12\sum_ia_ia_{\sigma(i)})$, $\sigma$ a uniform permutation.
- *Negative association.* A uniform permutation of a fixed population is negatively associated (Joag-Dev and Proschan
  1983), so $E\prod_if_i(a_{\sigma(i)})\le\prod_iEf_i(a_{\sigma(i)})$ for nonnegative increasing $f_i$. With
  $f_i(x)=e^{a_ix/2}$: $E_0L_{\rm sym}^2\le\prod_i\frac1N\sum_ke^{a_ia_k/2}$.
- *The stage bound.* If $a_i\le j/\sqrt i$ for $i\le N$, then $e^x\le1+x+e^{j^2/2}x^2/2$ on $[0,j^2/2]$, $\log(1+y)\le y$,
  $\sum_{i\le N}i^{-1/2}\le2\sqrt N$ and $\sum_{i\le N}1/i\le1+\log N$ give
  $\log E_0L_{\rm sym}^2\le2j^2+e^{j^2/2}j^4(1+\log N)^2/(8N)$.
- *The history.* $a_i=j/\sqrt i$ on $(N_{j-1},N_j]$, each $N_j$ taken so large that the remainder above is at most 1,
  $\alpha_{N_j}e^{2j^2+1}\le j^{-4}$ (possible by W) and $N_j\ge(j+1)^4$. At $N_j$ the whole prefix has $a_i\le j/\sqrt i$,
  so by Cauchy–Schwarz the symmetrized mixture gives $A_{N_j}$ probability at most $j^{-2}$. P makes that the
  probability under the fixed-order sign mixture. With $\kappa_i$ the stage of $i$:
  $\Delta_N/\sqrt N\ge(\tfrac34+o(1))\kappa_{\lceil N/4\rceil}\to\infty$, and $a_i\le1/j\to0$.
- *One history.* Under one product measure on sign streams, if C held on every signed version of the history,
  bounded convergence would send the sign mixture's probability of $A_N$ to 1, against $j^{-2}$ along $N_j$. So C
  fails on a set of sign streams of positive measure.
- *The median.* For $\pi$ with a strict unique median $c$, W sends the returned median to $c$; with
  $A_N=\{|\text{median}-c|>|m_0-c|/3\}$ for a true mass $m_0\ne c$ the same argument runs, so the point statement of
  L11 fails too.

Two remarks (Claude's). The history depends on the procedure, through $\alpha_N$: one bad history for each procedure,
not one for all. And a procedure blind to each reading's sign convention (L16) gives every signed version the same
probability, so for it the all-positive history itself fails and the last step is not needed. The finite ingredients

### B21 (10 Nov): the maximal-invariant likelihood recovers the target but breaks Z and T30

**Result (Codex; not checked by Claude).** Moreira's maximal-invariant method
([2009, §2 and §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.0613)) reduces the one-instrument
Gaussian experiment to a noncentral Wishart; proofs and scope are in
`../definition/theorems/T69.md`. No production equation, prior or premise changes.

Stack $d=Nk$ whitened rows: $Z=\alpha v_m^\top+E$, $G=Z^\top Z$,
$H(m)=v_m^\top Gv_m$, $\Delta=\|\alpha\|^2$. The maximal group is $O(d)$;
per reading it is only $O(k)$, leaving that reading's radius incidental.
Measurable invertible maps do not enlarge the group modulo null sets.
Independent diagonal channels group by **equal noise ratios**, not equal absolute covariances. Different
ratios give a product of groups with one $\Delta_g$ per class; no off-slice
estimator was implemented.

The parameter-dependent likelihood is
$e^{-\Delta/2}{}_0F_1(;d/2;\Delta H/4)$, including the singular $d=1$ case.
Its profile has $\widehat\Delta=0$ for $H\le d$ and otherwise a unique
positive optimum. The comparison integral under flat $d\Delta$ is
$2{}_1F_1(1;d/2;H/2)$. Each factor times a proper $\pi$ gives a proper mass
law on every finite measured input. Flat $d\Delta$ is an unforced improper
nuisance reference; its permission under L10 remains unknown. Profile MILE
is positive TLS only when the maximum is interior and above the threshold:
otherwise it can be nonunique or have only a boundary supremum. The law median is a different object.

At an interior maximum the profile/flat curvature ratio is exactly
$\rho=\widehat\Delta/H$. For fixed nonzero signal per reading as $N\to\infty$,
it tends to $\Delta/(\Delta+d)$, so **$M=d=Nk$ is the incidental count**.
At fixed $d$ and growing signal it tends to 1. $\eta_G=1-\lambda_-/\lambda_+$
shares the first limit but differs at finite $d$. Invariance has supplied a
law with the target, not selected G3's body: it retains the count Z forbids.
