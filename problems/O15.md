# O15: The null floor

**Status:** open; half sorted 30 Oct (the uniform part is Gleser–Hwang, pending B13). **Kind:** proof or counterexample. **Last updated:** 2026-10-30.

## The item (lemma list d4, verbatim)

**The null floor.** $\eta$ stops the null tilt growing but leaves it bounded (about 2 nats; the quoted width covers 75–85% against a mass drawn from $\pi$). Under Z and L19 null evidence does not accumulate (a long null series looks no more null than a short one), so this may be the most any law can do, with "we know nothing" living in the resolution.

## What would close it

a proof, or a counterexample law

## Attempts

### 30 Oct: two regimes, and what Gleser–Hwang closes (Claude's sort, not ruled)

Can, 30 Oct: "But full coverage is impossible." True in one regime and false in the other; the project
already split them on 27 Sept (`../docs/ours26/README.md`, "Coverage: two different failures"), and
the split had dropped out of the living layer.

**(a) The corner: signal → 0 (this item).** Standard name: Gleser & Hwang (1987); Dufour (1997) for the
general form. Near parameters where $m$ is not identified, no procedure whose interval is always finite
has a positive guaranteed coverage. Ours is always finite: L2 promises a mass on every measured input
and π is proper (G8), so every quoted interval is bounded. Claude's direct argument for our type, to be
checked (B13): as every true push → 0 the readings' law tends to one that does not depend on $m$, so the
interval's law tends to one law for every $m$; a.s. finite, so for $m$ far enough from $c$ its coverage
→ 0. Coverage at the null is therefore set by where $m$ sits against π (T24: the all-zero law is π), which
is why the floor is quoted against a mass drawn from π. **So the item's "may be the most any law can do"
is half answered:** no law covers uniformly here (a theorem, if B13 confirms the hypotheses hold); the
number 75–85% against π is not fixed by Gleser–Hwang and is still open.

**(b) Fixed nonzero signal, N growing.** Standard name: the Neyman–Scott incidental-parameter problem
(1948), here through the flat measure on the latent pairs (L10's endpoint; T18; Lindley 1966 in
`../docs/papers/jrsssb_28_2_288.pdf`). Gleser–Hwang does not reach it. The flat factor alone gets
**worse** as readings are added; ours30 with η approaches nominal (`tools/ours30_coverage_vs_n.py`,
100 series a cell, so ±2–3 points):

| 3D | N = 5 | 20 | 80 | 200 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| signal 1, ours30 | 0.84 | 0.91 | 0.95 | 0.93 |
| signal 1, without η | 0.84 | 0.79 | 0.69 | 0.72 |
| signal 2, ours30 | 0.90 | 0.92 | 0.91 | 0.95 |
| signal 2, without η | 0.89 | 0.90 | 0.87 | 0.82 |

**What follows.**
- **Property N is not mis-stated.** As ruled (R-2026-09-29-4) it asks for "a true statement", which
  step 11 reads as the Bartlett identity in the long-series limit; it never asked for nominal coverage
  in the corner. It is consistent with Gleser–Hwang. (This corrects Claude's chat suggestion of the same
  afternoon that property N demanded exact coverage and should be weakened.)
- **η has a real target and reaches it in the limit.** The shortfall at small N in (b) is G3's
  finite-N form (O14), not a wall. What is wrong with η is its form (a power on every reading: L24, B12),
  not its job. The standard non-hierarchical cures for Neyman–Scott (conditional likelihood; Cox & Reid
  1987's adjusted profile likelihood; integrated likelihood with a nuisance prior, Berger, Liseo &
  Wolpert 1999 in `../docs/papers/`) are where to look for a latent measure that does η's job in
  principle. The hierarchical cure is a size law, which L10 forbids (ours28).
- **What would close this item now:** B13's check of the hypotheses, then either a proof that some
  function of π alone bounds the null coverage (so the floor is π's, an input), or a counterexample law
  that beats 75–85% against π while meeting L2, Z and L19.

### 30 Oct (later): can a latent measure do η's job without a size law? (Claude's sort, not ruled)

Closes the "where to look" of the section above. The question is regime (b): with the pushes free
(L10) and nothing borrowed (L19), is there a measure on the latent pairs under which the law is a
genuine likelihood, so that no η is needed?

**What the flat law is, in standard terms.** Whitened, each push parameterised by its arc-length $t_i$
along the line $u_m$ (Beale's scaling; T6, T7): $y_i=t_iu_m+\varepsilon_i$. Integrating $t_i$ flat gives
$\exp(-K_i(m)/2)$ up to an $m$-free constant, which is also the **profile** likelihood (the maximum
over $t_i$), because the nuisance information $j_{tt}=\lvert u_m\rvert^2=1$ does not depend on $m$.
So the law is a profile likelihood with $N$ incidental parameters: Neyman–Scott (1948). Its score is
unbiased (the point is consistent: TLS), but the information identity fails, $E[J]>\mathcal H$, by a
per-reading term the noise alone contributes (T26). A likelihood whose identity fails is a
**quasi-likelihood** (Wedderburn 1974; McCullagh 1983), and the standard calibration of a
quasi-likelihood is the sandwich or Bartlett's factor: $\mathcal H/J$, which is η (T31).

**The non-hierarchical cures, one by one.** Every standard repair of a profile likelihood for
incidental parameters is checked against this model:

| cure | what it gives here | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cox & Reid (1987) adjusted profile, $L_p\lvert j_{tt}\rvert^{-1/2}$ | the flat law, unchanged | $j_{tt}=1$, $m$-free |
| integrated likelihood with the reference (flat) prior on a location nuisance (Berger, Liseo & Wolpert 1999) | the flat law, unchanged | same integral |
| conditional likelihood (Kalbfleisch & Sprott 1970; Andersen 1970) | **does not exist** | the sufficient statistic for $t_i$ given $m$ is $y_i\cdot u_m$, which depends on $m$: the case KS name as where conditioning fails |
| marginal likelihood on a $t$-free statistic | **does not exist** | a statistic free of $t_i$ for every $m$ must be orthogonal to the unknown true line |
| Barndorff-Nielsen's modified profile (1983), the Jacobian $\lvert\partial\hat t/\partial\hat t_m\rvert$ | **not checked** | the one candidate with an $m$-dependent term; brief B14 |
| the hierarchical cure: a size law on the pushes (Lindley & El-Sayyad 1968; ours28) | **restores calibration** (below) | forbidden by L10 (a declared population of setups, R-2026-09-30-3) |

So, pending B14: **every non-hierarchical measure coincides with the flat one here**, because the push's
information is $m$-free in whitened arc-length. There is no latent measure that does η's job; the job
can only be done by a size law (forbidden) or by a quasi-likelihood correction. η's *job* is therefore
forced given L10 and L19, and T31's argument for its *form* in the limit stands. Its finite-$N$ form
is G3 (B04). What B12 found is consistent with this and sharper than "η fails L24": η is not a noise
statement at all, so a noise-size posterior was the wrong test for it.

**Check** (`../tests/test_latent_measure.py`, seeded; `MET_SLOW=1` for these numbers). Bayesian coverage,
$m\sim\pi$, pushes $\alpha_i\sim N(0,w^2I)$ whitened, 3D, $N=20$, 300 series: the structural law with the
true $w$ declared is calibrated (0.963, 0.927, 0.943 at $w=0.3,0.7,1.5$); the flat law is not (0.697,
0.847, 0.910); ours30 recovers most of the gap (0.863, 0.917, 0.933). As $w\to\infty$ the structural law
is the flat law times Lindley's pull $(1+m^2)^{-Nk/2}$, which Beale's scaling removes.

**What this does to L24.** As written, L24 bans any $m$-constant tempering not backed by a noise-size
law, which bans η. The sort says L24 is right about the reading channel (the misfit may not be read as
evidence about σ: R1) and over-reaches at the series level, where the same operation has a second,
non-noise source: the quasi-likelihood identity. The two are the same formula (homogeneity, T36) with
different reasons, and under F2 only the body counts; what matters is whether the body is forced, and
η's is, in the limit. **Proposed narrowing (Claude, candidate):** L24's test applies to temperings
justified by a reading's own misfit; a series-level factor forced by property N through the identity
is not in its scope. For Can to check with L24 itself.

re-justified, with G3's finite-$N$ form still the hole, unless B14 finds a measure.
