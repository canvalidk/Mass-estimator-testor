## What is known

**Three readings of $g$.** For an exact anisotropic body, $\mathbf F=g\mathbf a$:

- $m_1=g(\hat{\mathbf a},\hat{\mathbf a})$, the energy reading: linear in $g$, so it passes L5
  (lumping, $g_1+g_2$) exactly.
- $1/m_2=g^{-1}(\hat{\mathbf F},\hat{\mathbf F})$, the compliance reading: $m_2$ passes L14
  (common force, $g^{-1}=g_1^{-1}+g_2^{-1}$) exactly.
- The gain $\lvert\mathbf F\rvert/\lvert\mathbf a\rvert=\sqrt{m_1m_2}$ (T34) passes neither once $g$ is
  anisotropic. Toy: near isotropy ($g=mI+\varepsilon h$) it carries exactly half of $m_2$'s L5
  defect and half of $m_1$'s L14 defect, at $O(\varepsilon^2)$, and its defects have a fixed sign
  (lumped anisotropic bodies are under-counted, never over-counted).
- All three coincide iff $\mathbf a$ is a principal axis of $g$: T1's $P=1$ reads "pushed along a
  principal axis".
- **L5 and L14 together, along every direction, hold iff $g$ is isotropic.** So the unruled
  L14/swap trade (R-2026-10-10-3) is a question about isotropy.

**What isotropy buys.**

| model of mass | parameters | leftover checks over $N$ readings in $d$ dims |
| --- | --- | --- |
| scalar (L22) | 1 | $Nd-1$ |
| symmetric $g$ | $d(d+1)/2$ | $Nd-d(d+1)/2$ |
| general linear $M$ | $d^2$ | $Nd-d^2$ |

- One reading: a positive-definite $g$ with $g\mathbf a=\mathbf F$ exists iff $\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf a>0$
  (the BFGS secant condition). So T1's "no mass" on an unaligned exact pair is "no isotropic
  mass", and positivity of mass reads "pushing along makes kinetic energy grow".
- **Can:** "if mass weren't isotropic there would be no noise; we could fit any data." True
  per reading; across a persistent series anisotropy shrinks the noise budget rather than
  removing it (table).
- Symmetry of $g$ is a second commitment: two readings must satisfy reciprocity,
  $\mathbf a_1\cdot\mathbf F_2=\mathbf a_2\cdot\mathbf F_1$. Newton II read as a bare linear map does not
  give it; a kinetic-energy form does.
- $\nu=k-1$ in T30 is the number of codirectionality conditions, i.e. what L22 imposes per
  reading. That is why T30 is inert in 1D: every 1D linear map is a scalar.

**Three metrics.** Space $\delta$, mass $g$, noise $\Sigma$. The estimator measures in $\Sigma$
(L9), so every isotropy result above is in $\delta$ units and has to be redone whitened.

## Attempts

### 27 Oct pressure check: scalar compatibility and narrow laws in a tensor world

**Codex derivation and counterexamples; L22 remains proposed, not ruled.** This run asks
what the current scalar operation communicates when a **declared** positive-definite
tensor G generates F*=G a* with calibrated instruments. It does not assign a scalar
accuracy target where no common scalar exists, or change the measured-input type to
return "no mass". Exact scalar representability, scalar compatibility under declared
noise, and the equation's law/point are separate quantities.

#### 1. The exact scalar question is about the excited eigenspace

Let S=span{a_i*}. One scalar m fits all the latent pairs iff

$$ (G-mI)S=0. $$

The forward implication follows by linearity on the spanning vectors; the converse
follows by applying it to each reading. Since G is symmetric positive definite, any
nonzero such S lies in **one eigenspace with one positive eigenvalue**. Zero excitation
admits every m. Full-rank excitation would force G=mI, but limited excitation can hide
anisotropy:

- For G=diag(1,2,4), an e_2 line admits scalar 2, identical to the scalar world G=2I on
  that line. Calibrated noisy measurements in the two worlds have identical distributions.
- A line along (e_1+e_2)/sqrt(2) admits no scalar, despite its excitation rank being one.
- For G=diag(2,2,5), even a whole plane spanned by e_1,e_2 admits scalar 2. Rank two
  alone is not a test of isotropic inertia.

`met/tensor_geometry.py::scalar_representability` and `tests/test_tensor_geometry.py`
check these statements with a stated relative tolerance on the latent vectors. They are
investigator checks, not operations the estimator can perform with hidden G or a*.

#### 2. Independent measured-data compatibility, without a fitted chi-square shortcut

For the recipe's independent isotropic channels, put x_i=F_i/sigma_F,
y_i=a_i/sigma_a, t=m/(sigma_F/sigma_a), and
A=sum|x_i|^2, B=sum|y_i|^2, C=sum x_i dot y_i. Direct minimization over each compatible
latent vector, at **one common mass**, gives

$$K(m)=\sum_i r_i(m)^T V_i(m)^{-1}r_i(m)
 =\frac{A+Bt^2-2Ct}{1+t^2},\qquad r_i(m)=\widetilde F_i-m\widetilde a_i.$$

For C>0 the positive-domain infimum is

$$ C_* =\frac{A+B-\sqrt{(A-B)^2+4C^2}}{2}. $$

For C<=0 it is min(A,B), a boundary infimum rather than an assigned finite mass.
This is a **total** Gram problem. Sum Q_i lets every reading choose its own mass;
C_* cannot be less than that sum. Independent Cholesky/QR latent-vector fits check
the cost formula and endpoint cases in `tests/test_tensor_geometry.py`.

There is a conservative statistical diagnostic with an argument that accounts for the
mass fitting. Under any true positive scalar m_0, correct fixed covariance declarations
and independent zero-mean Gaussian errors,

$$ C_*\le K(m_0),\qquad K(m_0)\sim\chi^2_{3N}. $$

Because the survival function decreases, p_*=sf_(chi-square_(3N))(C_*) is at least
sf_(chi-square_(3N))(K(m_0)), which is uniform under that model. Thus
P(p_*<=alpha)<=alpha. No degrees of freedom are subtracted for a fitted mass, and
the fitted minimum is **not** claimed to have that exact chi-square law. The exact
global minimum in this isotropic setting makes the inequality available. This is an
investigator diagnostic, **not a new rejection gate in ours29** or a newly accepted
physical response requirement. Alpha=0.01 in the report is per series, not familywise.

The declared tensor supplies a different, unfitted reference. With
r_G=F-Ga and R_G=[I,-G], its covariance is

$$V_G=\Sigma_{FF}+G\Sigma_{aa}G^T-\Sigma_{Fa}G^T-G\Sigma_{aF}.$$

Sum r_G^T V_G^(-1) r_G is exactly chi-square_(3N) in the generated G world. G is
supplied by the investigator, not estimated from those observations. The projection
identity and fixed-G Gaussian moments are checked independently in the geometry/world
tests. Fitting G would invalidate this unfitted reference.

#### 3. Two exact counterexamples: compatibility cost grows while a scalar law narrows

Set G=diag(1,2,4), common channel SD sigma, prior centre c=1, and

$$q_0=(7-3\sqrt5)/4,\qquad m_*=(1+\sqrt5)/2,\quad\theta_*=\arctan m_*.$$

These are deterministic **permitted measured inputs with positive declared noise**,
not assertions that a typical Gaussian experiment draws exactly zero noise. Repetition
means N distinct declared readings with the stated equal values, not relabelling a
single physical measurement as independent. No common scalar fits the noiseless pair
series in either family.

**Repeated oblique pair.** Every reading is a=(1,1,0)/sqrt(2), F=G a. Its Gram entries
before division by sigma^2 are A_0=5/2, B_0=1, C_0=3/2, with gap
Delta_0=3sqrt(5)/2 and minimum q_0. Once q_0/sigma^2>nu=2,

$$Q_i=q_0/\sigma^2,\quad g_i=2\sigma^2/q_0,\quad\eta=1,\quad
 C_*=Nq_0/\sigma^2.$$

All individual scores vanish at the same optimum, so J=0 and eta=1. The quoted
angular curvature **per reading** is

$$c_\theta=2\Delta_0/q_0=45+21\sqrt5.$$

In angle theta=atan m, the half-Cauchy prior at c=1 is uniform, and the quoted
law is proportional to exp[(N c_theta/4) cos(2(theta-theta_*))]. It freezes with
shrinking sigma after g saturates, yet concentrates at m_* as N increases.

**Repeated principal-axis block.** Alternate (F,a)=(e_1,e_1) and (2e_2,e_2), with
even N. Here each Q_i=0 and g_i=1, but the total Gram sums are the same as in the
first family. The common scalar incompatibility is again C_*=Nq_0/sigma^2.
Opposite per-reading scores at the best angle give

$$\eta=\min(1,(15\sqrt5/2)\sigma^2).$$

Once this cap is inactive, the quoted angular curvature per reading is c_theta=225/4.
Eta **does see disagreement** here, but repeating the whole block leaves eta unchanged
and multiplies the quoted evidence by N. Thus this law concentrates at m_* too.

For both families d(log m)/d theta=sqrt(5) at m_*, so

$$\operatorname{Var}(\log m)\sim\frac{5}{N c_\theta}.$$

The interval width therefore falls as N^(-1/2), and the current cloud readout tends to
m_*. This is a **pseudomass selected by the scalar operation**, not a hidden true scalar
of the anisotropic world. `tests/test_tensor_scalar_limits.py` checks the exact Q/g/eta
identities, frozen kernels, growing absolute costs, and the N=8/32/128 concentration.
Independent compact-angle integration checks the reported interval endpoints.

The scalar incompatibility becomes arbitrarily extreme by the conservative diagnostic,
while the quoted scalar law can be arbitrarily tight. At the same time the **complete**
output is not devoid of mismatch information: the oblique family has large Q and small
g, while the axis family has small eta. Tightness of the law alone is not a compatibility
assessment. These facts do not supply a new premise specifying how the complete output
must communicate or act on incompatibility.

#### 4. A seeded noisy tensor-world preset, with a genuine hidden-direction control

`python -m met run presets/anisotropic_world.yaml --workers 1` runs 48 cells, each with
100 series, seed 20261027: G=2I versus diag(1,2,4); principal e_2 line, mixed principal
axes, repeated oblique line and broad random directions; N=3/24/96; independent channel
SD 0.1/0.03. Signal length is 1 and the declared half-Cauchy centre is 1. The dimensions
and tensors are part of the **world**, not additions to the production estimator input.
The recipe's named principal directions require tensors diagonal in the chosen common
frame; the independent geometry functions also handle rotated SPD tensors and covariances.

Latent directions and instrument errors are paired across the two tensors. In the
principal-line case their entire measured inputs are identical; tests require identical
outputs. The investigator records whether that excited world admits a scalar, its
known-G correction cost and the conservative scalar diagnostic. Where no scalar exists,
the report assigns no scalar error or coverage score. Known-G costs check the stated
world/noise independently rather than allowing a mismatched scalar fit to define truth.

The estimator boundary is `evaluate(readings, prior_center)` and has no tensor or latent
argument. It retains native production point, median, raw and quoted intervals, Q/g/eta,
and unresolved-grid flags. The first series of each cell also exports unweighted,
untempered and quoted kernels, prior density and global cloud-size curve. These curves
use an explicit diagnostic grid with fine windows around each stage's native intervals;
their finite-grid normalization and endpoint ratios are **not** native probability
quantiles or rigorous tail bounds. Measured inputs and g/eta reconstruct the full kernel.
Each representative is an ordinary `met inspect` replay with its exact saved mass grid;
tensor and latent vectors are confined to its separate investigator truth record.

**What this establishes about L22.** Scalar isotropy has an experimentally visible role
when excitation leaves one eigenspace and instruments are calibrated as declared. It can
also be invisible on a restricted eigenspace. In these counterfactual worlds the scalar
premise was deliberately broken, so “wrong scalar mass” is not an accuracy score. On
real data a shared sensor map can imitate anisotropic inertia unless calibration supplies
the missing information (O16). This run does not decide whether real inertia is a tensor,
adopt L22, change T1's exact branch, or add a noisy no-mass output. O17 remains open.
