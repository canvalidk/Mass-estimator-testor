# O13: Readings that contradict each other

**Status:** open. **Kind:** ruling. **Last updated:** 2026-10-26.

## The item (lemma list d4, verbatim)

**Contradiction between readings.** Two readings declared separate that contradict each other about $m$ collapse the law onto the stronger with unbounded confidence; the law cannot see the contradiction, the data-only resolution (property N) can. Same family as the $\sqrt N$ tilt. **Partly answered by T31:** contradiction about $m$ is exactly what $\eta$ reads, and it widens the law; the resolution still reports it.

ruling on whether anything more is owed

### 26 Oct follow-up: positivity, normalization and two boundary limits

**Codex derivations and diagnostic checks, not a ruling or a replacement equation.**
Reproduce with `python -m met run presets/misalignment_positive_boundaries.yaml --workers 1`:
20 fixed-reading cells, seed 0 (unused), four cases and five common noise scales.
`tests/test_misalignment_information.py` checks the following using latent-vector least
squares, direct angle integrals and reciprocal symmetry.

1. **Two different costs.** T32 now writes out the exact isotropic decomposition
   $Q_+=Q+(Q_+-Q)$: $Q$ measures distance to the whole projective line; $Q_+$ is the
   infimum correction cost over positive mass. The additional term is zero for acute
   angles and positive for obtuse angles with both channels nonzero. Opposite scalar
   signs already exhibit it although $\nu=k-1=0$. T31's whole-line reason supplies a
   stationary reference for the series curvature; it does not separately show that
   physical compatibility must ignore this sign restriction.
2. **Where the baseline cancels.** For one unrestricted isotropic reading, $H=E-K$,
   so the same normalized law can be written

   $$p(m)\propto\pi(m)\exp[-\eta g\{K(m)-Q_+\}/2].$$

   The constant baseline $Q_+$ cancels. Its only possible influence through this law
   is indirect, through the supplied weighting construction. The remaining log-evidence
   span is $\eta g\operatorname{osc}_{m>0}K/2$. The diagnostic now exports the baseline,
   extra positivity cost, excess curve and this span separately. Above the misfit
   threshold, common noise scaling cancels exactly in $gH$ when $Q>0$ and $\nu>0$.
   Thus the frozen acute/perpendicular law follows from accepted T30/O12; the plot is
   not an independent failure criterion. Existing outputs also retain $Q$, $g$,
   curvature and a whole-line best angle. A changing absolute cost must not be called
   absent from the entire result merely because it cancels from normalized density.
3. **A stronger diagnostic example.** Take $F=(1,0)$, $a=(-1,0)$, equal channel SD
   $\sigma>0$, and $c=1$. Here $Q=0$, $g=\eta=1$, $Q_+=1/\sigma^2$, and

   $$p_\sigma(m)=Z_\sigma^{-1}(1+m^2)^{-1}
       \exp[-m/\{\sigma^2(1+m^2)\}],\quad m>0.$$

   The exact identity $p_\sigma(1/m)/m^2=p_\sigma(m)$ fixes the mathematical median
   at 1. The conditional cloud has mean $(m-1)e_1/(1+m^2)$ and covariance
   $\sigma^2I/(1+m^2)$, hence $A(1/m)=mA(m)$; substitution in the two readout
   integrals fixes the cloud readout at 1 too.

   Nevertheless, probability leaves **every** fixed compact interval $[L,U]$ inside
   $(0,\infty)$ as $\sigma\to0$. Proof: let
   $\delta=\min_{[L,U]}m/(1+m^2)>0$. Its unnormalized probability is at most
   a fixed constant times $e^{-\delta/\sigma^2}$; integration over
   $0<m<\sigma^2$ gives $Z_\sigma\ge e^{-1}\sigma^2/2$ for $\sigma\le1$.
   Their ratio tends to zero. Reciprocal symmetry leaves half the probability
   approaching each endpoint. This is not a null reading: $K(m)\ge1/\sigma^2$
   everywhere. The aligned equal-energy control instead concentrates at 1.

   Thus a stable central readout does not establish that a law is stable or that
   evidence is accumulating at that readout. The intervals and full law contain
   essential information. This is not yet a premise-level contradiction: every
   finite-noise output remains positive and defined, and no accepted property
   currently requires a tight positive-mass limiting law for incompatible fixed data.
4. **Numerical finding, kept as a strict xfail.** In that symmetric example at
   $\sigma=0.1$, the mathematical median is exactly **1**, while `met.full.ours29`
   reports **2.3416363971870715** in the current environment; the point remains 1.
   The CDF's near-flat region around probability one-half cannot resolve its unique
   centre at floating-point precision. The strict xfail
   `test_equal_energy_antiparallel_mathematical_median_is_one[0.1]` retains both facts.
   The equation and estimator were left unchanged. This is a numerical discrepancy,
   not physical evidence of an asymmetric law. Angle integrals independently verify
   the interval endpoints and show that the median's usual CDF tolerance misses it.

**What this rules out as an immediate repair.** Simply substituting $Q_+$ in
$\min(1,\nu/Q)$ would give zero on opposite scalar signs if that expression were
applied at $\nu=0$, contrary to the no-gate requirement. Keeping the present explicit
$\nu=0\Rightarrow g=1$ branch leaves the sign issue untreated. Positivity is an
inequality/boundary constraint, so its treatment needs an argument of its own.

**Next fork under TASK.** The sign and boundary information is computable from a reading,
so it cannot be supplied as missing experimental input. What role must it have in the
complete mass result? Derive that role from the physical/information properties, or
identify the premise that fails to account for it. Neither the no-gate rule nor finite
totality alone selects a new weighting rule or requires the law to return the prior.
The earlier argument that a faster-decaying positive weight would be "none" also needs
care: positive weight at every finite input does not by itself require a nonzero limiting
tilt. T30/O12 remains accepted; this observation marks the argument's scope and does not
silently reopen the ruling. The separate multi-reading question in O13 remains open.
