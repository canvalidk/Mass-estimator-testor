# O13: Readings that contradict each other

**Status:** open. **Kind:** ruling; since 27 Oct also a wrong-premise sort (L8), not ruled. **Last updated:** 2026-10-27.

## The item (lemma list d4, verbatim)

**Contradiction between readings.** Two readings declared separate that contradict each other about $m$ collapse the law onto the stronger with unbounded confidence; the law cannot see the contradiction, the data-only resolution (property N) can. Same family as the $\sqrt N$ tilt. **Partly answered by T31:** contradiction about $m$ is exactly what $\eta$ reads, and it widens the law; the resolution still reports it.

### 26 Oct follow-up: positivity, normalization and two boundary limits

**Codex derivations and diagnostic checks, not a ruling or a replacement equation.**
Reproduce with `python -m met run presets/misalignment_positive_boundaries.yaml --workers 1`:
20 fixed-reading cells, seed 0 (unused), four cases and five common noise scales.
`tests/test_misalignment_information.py` checks the following using latent-vector least
squares, direct angle integrals and reciprocal symmetry.

1. **Two different costs.** T32 now writes out the exact isotropic decomposition
   $Q_+=Q+(Q_+-Q)$: $Q$ measures distance to the whole projective line; $Q_+$ is the
   infimum correction cost over positive mass. The additional term is zero for acute
   angles and positive for obtuse angles with both channels nonzero. Opposite scalar
   signs already exhibit it although $\nu=k-1=0$. T31's whole-line reason supplies a
   stationary reference for the series curvature; it does not separately show that
   physical compatibility must ignore this sign restriction.
2. **Where the baseline cancels.** For one unrestricted isotropic reading, $H=E-K$,
   so the same normalized law can be written

   $$p(m)\propto\pi(m)\exp[-\eta g\{K(m)-Q_+\}/2].$$

   The constant baseline $Q_+$ cancels. Its only possible influence through this law
   is indirect, through the supplied weighting construction. The remaining log-evidence
   span is $\eta g\operatorname{osc}_{m>0}K/2$. The diagnostic now exports the baseline,
   extra positivity cost, excess curve and this span separately. Above the misfit
   threshold, common noise scaling cancels exactly in $gH$ when $Q>0$ and $\nu>0$.
   Thus the frozen acute/perpendicular law follows from accepted T30/O12; the plot is
   not an independent failure criterion. Existing outputs also retain $Q$, $g$,
   curvature and a whole-line best angle. A changing absolute cost must not be called
   absent from the entire result merely because it cancels from normalized density.
3. **A stronger diagnostic example.** Take $F=(1,0)$, $a=(-1,0)$, equal channel SD
   $\sigma>0$, and $c=1$. Here $Q=0$, $g=\eta=1$, $Q_+=1/\sigma^2$, and

   $$p_\sigma(m)=Z_\sigma^{-1}(1+m^2)^{-1}
       \exp[-m/\{\sigma^2(1+m^2)\}],\quad m>0.$$

   The exact identity $p_\sigma(1/m)/m^2=p_\sigma(m)$ fixes the mathematical median
   at 1. The conditional cloud has mean $(m-1)e_1/(1+m^2)$ and covariance
   $\sigma^2I/(1+m^2)$, hence $A(1/m)=mA(m)$; substitution in the two readout
   integrals fixes the cloud readout at 1 too.

   Nevertheless, probability leaves **every** fixed compact interval $[L,U]$ inside
   $(0,\infty)$ as $\sigma\to0$. Proof: let
   $\delta=\min_{[L,U]}m/(1+m^2)>0$. Its unnormalized probability is at most
   a fixed constant times $e^{-\delta/\sigma^2}$; integration over
   $0<m<\sigma^2$ gives $Z_\sigma\ge e^{-1}\sigma^2/2$ for $\sigma\le1$.
   Their ratio tends to zero. Reciprocal symmetry leaves half the probability
   approaching each endpoint. This is not a null reading: $K(m)\ge1/\sigma^2$
   everywhere. The aligned equal-energy control instead concentrates at 1.

   Thus a stable central readout does not establish that a law is stable or that
   evidence is accumulating at that readout. The intervals and full law contain
   essential information. This is not yet a premise-level contradiction: every
   finite-noise output remains positive and defined, and no accepted property
   currently requires a tight positive-mass limiting law for incompatible fixed data.
4. **Numerical finding, kept as a strict xfail.** In that symmetric example at
   $\sigma=0.1$, the mathematical median is exactly **1**, while `met.full.ours29`
   reports **2.3416363971870715** in the current environment; the point remains 1.
   The CDF's near-flat region around probability one-half cannot resolve its unique
   centre at floating-point precision. The strict xfail
   `test_equal_energy_antiparallel_mathematical_median_is_one[0.1]` retains both facts.
   The equation and estimator were left unchanged. This is a numerical discrepancy,
   not physical evidence of an asymmetric law. Angle integrals independently verify
   the interval endpoints and show that the median's usual CDF tolerance misses it.
   **Fixed 27 Oct (Claude):** `met.full._plateau_centre` returns the log-midpoint of a run
   of nodes where the CDF equals the probability to 1e-12; the test now passes, and
   `test_plateau_quantiles_commute_with_the_swap` checks the rule off the symmetric case.

**What this rules out as an immediate repair.** Simply substituting $Q_+$ in
$\min(1,\nu/Q)$ would give zero on opposite scalar signs if that expression were
applied at $\nu=0$, contrary to the no-gate requirement. Keeping the present explicit
$\nu=0\Rightarrow g=1$ branch leaves the sign issue untreated. Positivity is an
inequality/boundary constraint, so its treatment needs an argument of its own.

**Next fork under TASK.** The sign and boundary information is computable from a reading,
so it cannot be supplied as missing experimental input. What role must it have in the
complete mass result? Derive that role from the physical/information properties, or
identify the premise that fails to account for it. Neither the no-gate rule nor finite
totality alone selects a new weighting rule or requires the law to return the prior.
The earlier argument that a faster-decaying positive weight would be "none" also needs
care: positive weight at every finite input does not by itself require a nonzero limiting
tilt. T30/O12 remains accepted; this observation marks the argument's scope and does not
silently reopen the ruling. The separate multi-reading question in O13 remains open.

### 26 Oct path inspection: precision, relative preference and signed disagreement

**Codex pressure experiment and algebraic consequences; no new ruling.**
`python -m met run presets/inspection_noise_paths.yaml --workers 1` inspects 48
cases through the general inspector: eight fixed vector pairs at six common SDs,
with c = 1 held fixed. Source cases and all expanded cases are replayable. The
comparison follows the unweighted, g-weighted and quoted law, Q/g/eta, positive
correction cost, a fixed mass band's probability and the separate cloud readout.
Independent angle integration checks probabilities and interval endpoints.

The questions and what licenses their answers are:

1. **What becomes more precise?** Multiplying the entire covariance by lambda²
   multiplies the quadratic fit objective by lambda^-2. Consequently every fitted
   correction at fixed m is unchanged, its cost scales by lambda^-2, and the
   conditional latent-vector covariance scales by lambda². This holds for general
   covariance and declared subspaces, not only isotropic examples. The independent
   projection test in `tests/test_inspection_paths.py` checks that identity with
   correlated errors and a restricted subspace.
2. **Where does absolute incompatibility disappear?** The mass-independent baseline
   cancels on normalization (previous derivation). For one reading eta is 1: its
   score vanishes at its own projective optimum, so there is no between-reading
   disagreement to temper. Above Q = nu, g = nu/Q cancels common covariance scaling
   in relative evidence. The conditional cloud still changes, so the readout can
   change even when the mass law does not. This locates distinct operations rather
   than attributing all behaviour to the final point.
3. **Can absence of mass discrimination coexist with arbitrarily large cost?**
   For F = (1,0), a = (0,1), equal SD sigma and c = 1, K(m) = sigma^-2 for every
   m. H is constant too. The normalized law therefore equals the prior at every
   sigma, while Q+ = Q = sigma^-2 grows. Its point and mathematical median are 1.
   This is an exact example, not a claim that all incompatible readings must return
   the prior. `test_perpendicular_equal_energy_reference_is_exactly_the_prior`
   supplies the independent CDF identity.
4. **What does unsigned Q fail to distinguish?** The 30- and 150-degree paths have
   the same vector sizes and squared dot product, hence the same Q and g. Their
   signed dot products differ: the positive-mass correction curve, normalized law
   and readout differ. Q alone loses the sign, while H(m) retains it. The complete
   operation must not be described as blind to sign merely because its Q is.

The equal-opposite control retains the earlier median defect and shows why a stable
point cannot stand in for the law. More generally, for exactly equal vector energies,
unit lengths, equal SD and c = 1, set w = cos(theta) and x = ln(m). Then

$$q(x)\propto\operatorname{sech}(x)\exp[\beta\operatorname{sech}(x)],
\qquad \beta=\frac{g w}{2\sigma^2}.$$

This is even in x, so the mathematical median is 1. Reciprocal cloud-size symmetry
also fixes the point at 1. Yet the law can concentrate, stay at the prior, or move
toward both boundaries. For beta < -1 it is bimodal in log mass, with modes
x = +/- arcosh(-beta). These are density modes in the stated coordinate, not an
invariant choice of readout. A central equal-tailed interval can bridge the gap.

**Remaining premise question:** which aspects of compatibility and mass discrimination

### 26 Oct prior-centre paths: fixed evidence, different priors and readouts

**Codex pressure study and derivations; no ruling or equation change.** Reproduce
with `python -m met run presets/inspection_prior_paths.yaml --workers 1` (seed 0,
unused). Four fixed 2D readings at equal independent channel SD 0.1 each use prior
centre c = 0.1, 1 and 10. All twelve cases share the same physical diagnostic grid.

| Reading | Point at c = 0.1 | At c = 1 | At c = 10 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| aligned, sizes 2 and 1 | 1.99007085 | 2 | 2.03980745 |
| 30 degrees, sizes 2 and 1 | 1.84433197 | 2.11296949 | 4.15420347 |
| perpendicular, equal sizes | 0.203533525 | 1 | 4.91319551 |
| opposite, equal sizes | 0.0197625952 | 1 | 50.6006418 |

At fixed physical m, the independent corrections, conditional covariances and cloud
size curves are unchanged. Q and g agree across priors to rounding; eta = 1 for
these single readings. After subtracting each prior's log density, the relative
log-evidence curves agree modulo a constant to 4.42e-13 across all three law stages.
The different answers therefore arise from multiplication by the declared prior and
the subsequent size-weighted readout. Stored projective angles and raw curvature
depend on the c-based coordinate; no invariance of those coordinates is claimed.

**Perpendicular control.** Its law is exactly pi_c, with mathematical median c.
Its cloud-size curve is a constant times (1+m²)^-1/2, so the cloud point is R(c),
given in O2 below. It equals c only at c = 1. This distinguishes exact return of
the supplied mass law from return of its centre by the separate readout. This
nonzero reading has a changing compatibility cost even though its evidence is
constant in m; it is not being declared a Z-null reading by this observation.

**Opposite control.** For F=(1,0), a=(-1,0), write epsilon=sigma² and

$$h_c(m)=\frac{c}{c^2+m^2},\quad f(m)=\frac{m}{1+m^2},\quad
p_c(m)\propto h_c(m)e^{-f(m)/\epsilon}.$$

Since f(1/m)=f(m) and h_c(1/m)/m²=h_(1/c)(m), their normalizers agree and

$$p_c(1/m)/m^2=p_{1/c}(m).$$

The cloud symmetry A(1/m)=m A(m) also gives R(c)R(1/c)=1. Thus the earlier point
fixed at 1 specifically uses c=1 reciprocal symmetry. At SD 0.1, the probabilities
below mass 1 are 0.989917666, 0.5 and 0.010082334 as c increases through the three
values; the prior changes the relative weight of the two boundary branches.

For **fixed c>0** as epsilon tends to zero, the low-branch unnormalized integral
I_c^- over (0,1) satisfies I_c^-/epsilon -> 1/c. Substitute m=epsilon*t and use
f(m)>=m/2 on (0,1), h_c<=1/c, to apply dominated convergence. Reciprocity gives
I_c^+/epsilon -> c. Hence the low/high probabilities tend to 1/(1+c²) and
c²/(1+c²). For the cloud denominator D_c, A_sigma(epsilon*t)->1 and A_sigma is
uniformly bounded for sigma<=1; its low contribution divided by epsilon tends
to 1/c. Inverting the high contribution supplies an extra factor u and makes it
O(epsilon²). The cloud numerator N_c=D_(1/c), so R(c)->c². This convergence is not
uniform when c varies with sigma: c² is not the finite-SD 0.1 answer in the table.

**Independent checks and limits.** The shared residual-angle CDF and Rice/angle
readout in `inspection/references.py` do not use the estimator's native law grid
or its cloud-length integral. Across 36 law stages, interval-endpoint CDF errors
were at most 2.24e-8 and absolute log point errors at most 6.82e-9. There were no
reference quadrature warnings, native support-edge warnings or operational check
errors in this study. Tests use direct scalar integrals and reciprocal identities.

The known numerical median defect remains: with this preset's covariance 0.01 I,
the c=1 opposite case reports 0.4230518228822659 despite mathematical median 1.
The earlier SD 0.1 example, whose variance is computed as 0.1², reported 2.341636397.
The tiny floating-point variance difference changes which side of the flat CDF
receives the numerical crossing. A second strict xfail retains this saved case;
neither value is evidence for a physically asymmetric law. **Fixed 27 Oct** with the SD 0.1 case (item 4 above).

These findings measure prior influence for the supplied cases. They do not select a
preferred prior from the data, require prior independence at finite noise, or settle
the physical response owed to incompatible readings.

### 27 Oct: the sign fork sorted (Claude's sort, not ruled)

**Sorted as outcome 3, a wrong premise** (`../TASK.md` §3); **provisional (from the goal):** L1's
$m>0$ is bedrock, so a misfit measure that cannot see its violation is mis-stated. The
mis-stated premise is **L8's count**.

- **The fork.** T32's Q is the smallest eigenvalue over the whole projective line, so it
  measures the $k-1$ codirectionality conditions L8 names and nothing else. L1 also declares
  $m>0$, which adds one more condition per reading: $\mathbf F^\ast\cdot\mathbf a^\ast>0$, an
  inequality. Q never sees it. `tests/test_sign_fork.py` pins it: the pair (2,0)/(−1,0) gets
  the same Q, g and evidence span as (2,0)/(1,0), at every SD (200 nats at 0.1, 20 000 at 0.01).
- **Not an input.** The sign is in the reading, computable from $\tilde{\mathbf F}_i,\tilde{\mathbf a}_i$.
  Moving it to the type would be the dodge `TASK.md` §5 names.
- **Not forced by a present premise, and one is violated.** T30's own requirement is bounded
  confidence on a fixed pair that no positive mass fits. It holds for the 30° pair (frozen at
  9.0825 nats) and fails for the anti-parallel pair: 49.9994 nats at SD 0.1, 4999.94 at SD
  0.01 (strict xfail `test_t30_bounded_tilt_on_an_anti_parallel_pair`). Codex's proof above,
  that the law escapes every compact set, is the same failure seen from the law's side.
- **So L8 is mis-stated.** A $k$-dimensional reading under L1 imposes $k-1$ equalities **and one
  inequality**. L8 lists the equalities, and T32 measures only them. T31's whole-line choice
  is a different job (a stationary point for the curvature, so $\eta$ is parametrisation-free)
  and is not implicated.

**What T30's requirement then forces.** The positivity violation must enter $g$ with the same
$1/\text{misfit}$ tail, or confidence on an anti-parallel pair stays unbounded. That much is
forced. The numerator is not, and it is an inner fork:

- T30's $\nu=k-1$ is the **strong-signal** expectation of Q ($\chi^2_{k-1}$). At null the
  expectation is lower: 0.43 for $k=2$, 1.00 for $k=3$ (2·10⁶ draws, seed 0).
- The positivity cost $Q_+-Q$ runs the other way. It vanishes at strong aligned signal and
  is largest at null: 0.1817 for $k=1$ (exactly $(1-2/\pi)/2$), 0.286 for $k=2$, 0.363 for
  $k=3$. So taking the positivity numerator at T30's reference (strong signal) gives zero,
  which is a gate and breaks L21.
- **Candidate (Claude's, unproven):** the numerator is the supremum over signal size of
  $E[Q_+-Q]$, presumably its null value. The reason offered is L10: the definition declares no
  size, so the share the noise can account for is the largest share over sizes. If $E[Q]$
  grows with signal size, as the two values above suggest, then T30's $\nu$ is already that
  supremum, and one rule gives both numerators: **the supremum over signal size of the
  expected misfit**. Nothing in T30 would change.
- **Quick check (Claude, 4·10⁵ draws, seed 1; aligned signal of whitened size r in both
  channels, so m = s; one geometry only):**

  | k | r = 0 | 0.5 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | 2: E[Q] / E[Q₊−Q] / E[Q₊] | 0.427 / 0.284 / 0.711 | 0.481 / 0.245 / 0.726 | 0.602 / 0.155 / 0.758 | 0.837 / 0.026 / 0.864 | 0.968 / 0 / 0.968 | 0.988 / 0 / 0.988 |
  | 3: E[Q] / E[Q₊−Q] / E[Q₊] | 1.000 / 0.363 / 1.363 | 1.079 / 0.317 / 1.396 | 1.276 / 0.212 / 1.488 | 1.685 / 0.043 / 1.728 | 1.933 / 0 / 1.933 | 1.987 / 0 / 1.987 |

  E[Q] rises to ν; the positivity cost falls from its null value; **their sum E[Q₊] also
  rises to ν**. So one weight, $g=\min(1,\kappa_k/Q_+)$ with $\kappa_k=\sup_r E[Q_+]$, gives
  $\kappa_k=k-1$ for $k\ge2$: T30 unchanged on every acute pair (where $Q_+=Q$), and the
  anti-parallel pair weighted down. For $k=1$, E[Q] = 0 at every size, so
  $\kappa_1=\sup_r E[Q_+]$ is the null value $(1-2/\pi)/2\approx0.1817$, not 0: no gate.
  Not shown: monotonicity for unequal channel sizes, for $m\ne s$ and in general; whether one
  weight on $Q_+$ or two factors (one per kind of condition) is forced.

The candidate must be checked against (i) T30's bounded requirement on the anti-parallel
pair, (ii) L21, $g>0$ everywhere, including 1D where $\nu=0$, (iii) Z on exact zeros,
$g(0)=1$ (T24, T25), (iv) the swap and units, (v) no change on any acute pair. This is
handed to a Codex run as a sorted question with those five checks as its deliverable.
