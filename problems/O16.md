# O16: errors shared across readings

**Status:** open; found 2026-10-24 on the robot data (Claude). **Kind:** fork, sorted below as
an input (Claude's sort, not ruled).
**Last updated:** 2026-10-27.

## The item

L13's model makes the errors of different readings independent: the series' covariance is
block diagonal, one $\Sigma_i$ per reading. A real instrument has errors that are the same in
every reading: a calibration bias, a gain, a misalignment. The type has no slot for them. If
they are put into each $\Sigma_i$ as a floor, the law treats them as independent and their
contribution to the width falls like $1/\sqrt N$ as the run is cut into more windows.

## Evidence (robot, `tools/robot_run.py`, `tests/test_robot.py`)

Budget: steady-state variance over the window's sample count, plus the calibration residual
as a per-reading floor (`met/robot.py`). Static calibration mass 0.9323 kg.

| Series | Windows | Median | 95% interval | Covers 0.9323 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1-baseline | 125 × 0.05 s | 0.9226 | [0.9187, 0.9264] | no |
| 1-baseline | 31 × 0.2 s | 0.9213 | [0.9137, 0.9290] | no (strict xfail) |
| 1-baseline | 6 × 1 s | 0.9196 | [0.9023, 0.9371] | yes |
| 2-vibrations | 31 × 0.2 s | 0.9248 | [0.9186, 0.9310] | no |
| static held out, fit on rows 1–12 | 11 orientations | 0.9302 | [0.9239, 0.9365] | yes |
| static held out, fit on rows 13–23 | 12 orientations | 0.9320 | [0.9257, 0.9383] | yes |

The median barely moves with the window (0.9226 to 0.9196); the interval narrows as
$1/\sqrt N$ although the floor, which dominates the budget, is one error repeated.

Two further facts from the same runs, not caused by O16:

- The dynamic readings carry error beyond the declared budget: smallest misfit weight 0.15
  with the floor, $10^{-3}$ without it, against 0.3–0.7 for the static readings. Dropping the
  floor moves the baseline median from 0.9213 to 0.9347, so the answer depends on the
  declared $\Sigma_i$ by 1.5%. The static readings agree with the static fit under either
  budget, so the excess comes with motion. Candidates (not checked): the IMU does not sit at
  the load's centre (the arm turns at about 0.72 rad/s), vibration above the IMU's rate. Both
  are a wrong premise of the experiment's pairing (that $\mathbf A$ is the load's
  acceleration), not of the definition.
- 3-vibrations-contact: the FTS force is not the net force while the load touches something.
  The misfit weights fall to $10^{-4}$ but the median still moves to 0.96. Wrong premise of
  the experiment; relevant to O13 (readings that contradict their declaration).

## Sort (Claude's)

**Input.** A shared gain error on the force channel is exactly a change of m: no function of
the data can detect it, so it enters as a declaration or not at all. A shared bias is
partly visible (it bends the readings off a line through the origin) but is not determined
by them. So the series' cross-reading covariance is declared with the readings, as $\Sigma_i$
is (TASK.md §2), and block diagonal is its value when nothing shared is declared. It does
not merge readings under T33: a shared error is not a shared signal.

**Not a wrong premise of L13 as used so far:** with nothing shared declared, L13 is what the
input says. **Not forced:** η sees only disagreement between readings, and a shared error
produces none.

## What would close it

The full type taking a series covariance (block diagonal by default), `met/full.py` extended
to it, and a test that a shared floor no longer averages down; then the robot run repeated.
Whether the law's form survives a non-block-diagonal covariance (the likelihood no longer
factorises into per-reading terms, so $g_i$ and η need restating) is the theorem work.

## Attempts

- 2026-10-26, Claude (generation session; a split, partly tested): **shared linear errors split
  three ways under isotropy of inertia (L22).** The body is isotropic and the sensors read
  $\mathbf a_{\text{obs}}=A\mathbf a$, $\mathbf F_{\text{obs}}=B\mathbf F$. The data see only
  $BA^{-1}=\lambda RS_0$ (overall scale, rotation, symmetric with determinant 1).

  | piece | physical error | against scalar mass | against symmetric $g$ |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | $\lambda$ | relative gain | invisible | invisible |
  | $R$ | relative tilt | detectable: fixed misalignment | detectable: breaks reciprocity |
  | $S_0$ | per-axis scale, shear | detectable: fixed misalignment | invisible, looks like anisotropic mass |

  Isotropy turns $d^2-1$ kinds of shared linear error into testable residuals; only $\lambda$
  escapes, and $\lambda$ is the L3 units freedom, which needs an outside calibration. In 1D every
  shared linear error is $\lambda$. **Finding:** a shared tilt or shear gives $Q_i>\nu_i$ on every
  reading in a consistent direction; T30 discounts each reading separately, which widens the
  interval without removing the bias, and the widening still falls as $1/\sqrt N$, which is this
  item's complaint. A series-level check, whether the misfit directions agree across readings,
  would separate shared error from noise (random directions feed O14's noise channel,
  consistent ones flag shared error). **Owed:** inject a fixed tilt into a 3D preset and watch
  the $Q_i$ directions; check the robot data's effective dimension (if it is effectively 1D, its
  ~1% shortfall is pure $\lambda$ and has no in-series fix); additive offsets are not linear maps
  and need their own split; detecting $R$ or $S_0$ needs excitation along several directions.
