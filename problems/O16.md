# O16: errors shared across readings

**Status:** open; found 2026-10-24 on the robot data (Claude). **Kind:** fork, sorted below as
an input (Claude's sort, not ruled).
**Last updated:** 2026-10-28.

## The item

L13's model makes the errors of different readings independent: the series' covariance is
block diagonal, one $\Sigma_i$ per reading. A real instrument has errors that are the same in
every reading: a calibration bias, a gain, a misalignment. The type has no slot for them. If
they are put into each $\Sigma_i$ as a floor, the law treats them as independent and their
contribution to the width falls like $1/\sqrt N$ as the run is cut into more windows.

## Evidence (robot, `tools/robot_run.py`, `tests/test_robot.py`)

Budget: steady-state variance over the window's sample count, plus the calibration residual
as a per-reading floor (`met/robot.py`). Static calibration mass 0.9323 kg.

| Series | Windows | Median | 95% interval | Covers 0.9323 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1-baseline | 125 × 0.05 s | 0.9226 | [0.9187, 0.9264] | no |
| 1-baseline | 31 × 0.2 s | 0.9213 | [0.9137, 0.9290] | no (strict xfail) |
| 1-baseline | 6 × 1 s | 0.9196 | [0.9023, 0.9371] | yes |
| 2-vibrations | 31 × 0.2 s | 0.9248 | [0.9186, 0.9310] | no |

| static held out, fit on rows 13–23 | 12 orientations | 0.9320 | [0.9257, 0.9383] | yes |

The median barely moves with the window (0.9226 to 0.9196); the interval narrows as
$1/\sqrt N$ although the floor, which dominates the budget, is one error repeated.

Two further facts from the same runs, not caused by O16:

- The dynamic readings carry error beyond the declared budget: smallest misfit weight 0.15
  with the floor, $10^{-3}$ without it, against 0.3–0.7 for the static readings. Dropping the
  floor moves the baseline median from 0.9213 to 0.9347, so the answer depends on the
  declared $\Sigma_i$ by 1.5%. The static readings agree with the static fit under either
  budget, so the excess comes with motion. Candidates (not checked): the IMU does not sit at
  the load's centre (the arm turns at about 0.72 rad/s), vibration above the IMU's rate. Both
  are a wrong premise of the experiment's pairing (that $\mathbf A$ is the load's
  acceleration), not of the definition.
- 3-vibrations-contact: the FTS force is not the net force while the load touches something.
  The misfit weights fall to $10^{-4}$ but the median still moves to 0.96. Wrong premise of
  the experiment; relevant to O13 (readings that contradict their declaration).

## Sort (Claude's)

**Input.** A shared gain error on the force channel is exactly a change of m: no function of
the data can detect it, so it enters as a declaration or not at all. A shared bias is
partly visible (it bends the readings off a line through the origin) but is not determined
by them. So the series' cross-reading covariance is declared with the readings, as $\Sigma_i$
is (TASK.md §2), and block diagonal is its value when nothing shared is declared. It does
not merge readings under T33: a shared error is not a shared signal.

**Not a wrong premise of L13 as used so far:** with nothing shared declared, L13 is what the
input says. **Not forced:** η sees only disagreement between readings, and a shared error
produces none.

## What would close it

The full type taking a series covariance (block diagonal by default), `met/full.py` extended
to it, and a test that a shared floor no longer averages down; then the robot run repeated.
Whether the law's form survives a non-block-diagonal covariance (the likelihood no longer
factorises into per-reading terms, so $g_i$ and η need restating) is the theorem work.

## Attempts

- 2026-10-26, Claude (generation session; a split, partly tested): **shared linear errors split
  three ways under isotropy of inertia (L22).** The body is isotropic and the sensors read
  $\mathbf a_{\text{obs}}=A\mathbf a$, $\mathbf F_{\text{obs}}=B\mathbf F$. The data see only
  $BA^{-1}=\lambda RS_0$ (overall scale, rotation, symmetric with determinant 1).

  | piece | physical error | against scalar mass | against symmetric $g$ |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | $\lambda$ | relative gain | invisible | invisible |
  | $R$ | relative tilt | detectable: fixed misalignment | detectable: breaks reciprocity |
  | $S_0$ | per-axis scale, shear | detectable: fixed misalignment | invisible, looks like anisotropic mass |

  Isotropy turns $d^2-1$ kinds of shared linear error into testable residuals; only $\lambda$
  escapes, and $\lambda$ is the L3 units freedom, which needs an outside calibration. In 1D every
  shared linear error is $\lambda$. **Finding:** a shared tilt or shear gives $Q_i>\nu_i$ on every
  reading in a consistent direction; T30 discounts each reading separately, which widens the
  interval without removing the bias, and the widening still falls as $1/\sqrt N$, which is this
  item's complaint. A series-level check, whether the misfit directions agree across readings,
  would separate shared error from noise (random directions feed O14's noise channel,
  consistent ones flag shared error). **Owed:** inject a fixed tilt into a 3D preset and watch
  the $Q_i$ directions; check the robot data's effective dimension (if it is effectively 1D, its
  ~1% shortfall is pure $\lambda$ and has no in-series fix); additive offsets are not linear maps
  and need their own split; detecting $R$ or $S_0$ needs excitation along several directions.

### 27 Oct pressure check: shared rotation fields and the robot's resolved directions

**Codex derivation, counterexamples and reproducible experiment; no ruling or instrument
correction.** This checks the detection/identifiability part of O16. It does not supply
the missing shared-error slot in the type. The earlier statements in Attempts are kept
as their historical hypotheses; several need the qualifications below.

#### 1. A fixed tilt is a field, not a common residual direction

Suppose the body obeys scalar mass m and the force sensor is rotated relative to the
acceleration sensor by R=exp([omega]_cross). In one common physical sensor frame,

$$r_i=\widetilde F_i-m\widetilde a_i
 =m(R-I)a_i^*+\epsilon_{F,i}-m\epsilon_{a,i},\qquad
 m(R-I)a_i^*=-m[a_i^*]_{\times}\omega+O(\|\omega\|^2).$$

The ideal design H_i=-m[a_i^*]_cross maps **one common rotation parameter** into different
residual vectors for different excitations. The fitted diagnostic substitutes the
measured acceleration; that is an errors-in-variables approximation, not this exact
noise identity. For the following noiseless R_z(theta) example, a_1=e_1, a_2=e_2,

$$r_1=m(\cos\theta-1,\sin\theta,0),\quad
  r_2=m(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta-1,0),\quad r_1\cdot r_2=0.$$

Thus orthogonal residuals can come from the same fixed tilt. Also excitation along e_3
is unchanged by this rotation and has Q=0. A fixed tilt does **not** imply Q_i>nu_i on
every reading, and small tilts/signals need not cross that threshold either.
`tests/test_shared_error_geometry.py` checks both counterexamples.

Q has no direction: it is the scalar cost after profiling each reading on the whole
projective line. This check instead names r_i at an **external reference mass**, propagates
its covariance V_i=[I,-mI] Sigma_i [I,-mI]^T, and fits the local shared field. Neither
these fitted residuals nor compatible corrections are recovered instrument errors.

#### 2. Which rotations can the excitation identify?

With isotropic residual covariance V_i=tau_i^2 I, local rotational information is

$$J_\omega=m^2\sum_i\tau_i^{-2}
 (\|a_i\|^2I-a_i a_i^T).$$

Here a_i denotes exact/latent excitation in the identifiability argument. The observed
design replaces it with measured acceleration and need not have the same numerical rank.

If S=sum tau_i^(-2) a_i a_i^T has eigenvalues s_1>=s_2>=s_3, then J has eigenvalues

$$m^2(s_2+s_3),\quad m^2(s_1+s_3),\quad m^2(s_1+s_2).$$

Therefore a nonzero **rank-one** excitation identifies two off-axis rotation components
and leaves rotation about its own direction invisible. **Rank two already identifies all
three local rotation components.** Rank three is useful for conditioning but not necessary
for this local identifiability. This is a property of the latent design; measurement noise
can make the observed numerical design full rank even on a true line. Tests check the
rank-one and rank-two cases and the information eigenvalue formula independently.

A free local mass change adds the column a_i to H_i. Under isotropic noise it is
orthogonal to the rotation columns. Under general positive-definite covariance the
implementation whitens both residual and design, then projects the rotation columns
off that scalar column before reporting their singular values. It does not compare
directions whitened differently as though they remained in one Euclidean frame.

The relative scalar gain remains exactly confounded with mass: m lambda R, not m and
lambda separately, is what the data see. But **one-dimensional excitation does not prove
that a discrepancy is pure gain**; two tilt components may still be detectable. More
general shared maps can also cause mass disagreement: with true m=1 and force-channel
map diag(1,2,3), readings along e_1 and e_2 have Q_1=Q_2=0 but prefer masses 1 and 2.
Bartlett eta then reacts as noise shrinks. The old claim that shared error creates no
between-reading disagreement holds for scalar gain and special geometries, not every
shared error. `test_shared_axis_gain_can_create_mass_disagreement_and_tempering`
preserves this counterexample.

#### 3. An independent controlled test, with no fitted-data significance claim

`presets/shared_sensor_tilt.yaml` declares 24 synthetic cells: broad, planar, transverse
line and rotation-axis line excitation, 0/5-degree rotations, and N=12/48/192. Each uses
200 series, seed 20261027, true mass 1.5, acceleration length 2, and independent channel
SDs 0.02. A fixed 5-degree hypothesis is declared before the observations. The inspector
gets only measured vectors, covariance and the supplied reference mass; latent excitation
is retained only by the investigator's independent check.

For latent a_i^*, define the predeclared template delta_i=m(R_hyp-I)a_i^*. Then

$$Z=\frac{\sum_i\delta_i^T V_i^{-1}r_i}
           {\sqrt\Lambda},\qquad
 \Lambda=\sum_i\delta_i^T V_i^{-1}\delta_i.$$

Under the independent Gaussian generation declared in this preset, conditional on the
latent excitation, Z is exactly normal with variance 1. Its mean is 0 in the no-tilt
control and sqrt(Lambda) when the injected and hypothesized rotations agree. More
generally the mean is sum delta_i^T V_i^(-1) delta_(injected,i)/sqrt(Lambda).
This follows directly from the covariance of a linear functional of the channel errors;
no fitted degree-of-freedom correction is needed. The rotation-axis line has Lambda=0:
the check is explicitly **inapplicable**, not a failed detection.

`tests/test_shared_tilt_study.py` checks this conditional Gaussian reference, replay,
replicate prefixes and resource constraints. Each series also retains its local observed-
data fit, odd-row fit/even-row predictive cost, Q/g/eta, and full rotation vector. On true
lines the displayed fitted norm is projected onto the **investigator-known observable
components**; the unobservable component is not claimed recovered. Fit and predictive
costs have no assigned p-values: their regressors are noisy and the local model is only
first order. The matched score, not an in-sample cost reduction, supplies the independent
expectation for this experiment. The production mass equation is unchanged.

#### 4. Robot effective dimension is conditional on a stated noise budget

The maintained loader `met/robot.py` keeps the support-sign pairing, recorded lag and
common FTS frame. Gravity is part of a, so report **uncentered excitation** separately
from **centered variation**. Ordinary full numerical rank is insufficient: noise can
create tiny extra singular values. Independently whitening each row and then taking a
physical SVD would change its direction geometry.

Here the physical N-by-3 acceleration matrix is X=X^*+E. With correct zero-mean marginal
covariances C_i, E||E||_F^2=sum trace C_i, even with dependence between readings. Markov
and the singular-value perturbation inequality give, with probability at least 1-alpha,

$$|s_j(X)-s_j(X^*)|\le b,\qquad
 b=\sqrt{\sum_i\operatorname{tr}(C_i)/\alpha}.$$

Thus the count of measured singular values exceeding b is a **latent rank lower bound**,
not an exact rank estimate. Centering is a contraction, so the same conservative radius
works for centered variation without assuming independent errors. Alpha=0.05 is per
case, not a simultaneous 95% guarantee for the full collection. Signed eigenvalues of
X^T X-sum C_i are also retained as moment diagnostics, not recovered signal energies.
If the stated budget or zero-mean assumption is wrong, these conditional bounds do not
validate it. `tests/test_shared_error_geometry.py` checks the geometric perturbation
bound; `tests/test_robot_shared_error.py` checks physical-frame spectra and floor sensitivity.

**Baseline result.** At 0.2 s windows (31 readings), physical singular values are
approximately **(45.817, 29.734, 0.13586)**; centered values are
**(45.459, 19.767, 0.10452)**, in acceleration units. The conservative radius is
**3.3073 with the calibration floor**, and **0.43331 without it**. Both raw and centered
rank lower bounds are **2** under both declarations. The 0.05 s and 1 s baseline windows
give the same lower bounds. The experiment resolves at least two excitation directions
under these budgets; the third is weak, and its small singular value does not prove an
exact zero. In this sense the baseline is **not effectively one-dimensional**, and its
two resolved directions suffice for local rotational observability in the scalar model.

The fitted baseline rotation norm at 0.2 s is about 0.935 degrees with the floor and
1.270 degrees without it. The simultaneous local mass adjustments are about -0.01083 kg
and +0.002021 kg respectively. These are descriptive, budget-dependent parameters, not
a calibrated correction for the mass shortfall. Even-row predictive improvement does
not identify the cause: calibration error, additive offsets, noisy regressors, temporal
dependence, off-centre IMU acceleration or other pairing errors may imitate a shared
field. Contact data are explicitly labelled as a known net-force pairing violation.

The static control resolves all three directions, but it uses the **same stored rows
that fit calibration**, and is not held-out validation. A provenance discrepancy is
retained: the existing loader description says 23 orientations, while the CSV contains
**24 data rows**, consistent with the source metadata. A strict xfail names both counts
(`test_calibration_count_matches_the_existing_23_orientation_description`); neither the
old description nor the raw data has been rewritten to agree.

Reproduce the synthetic and real-data collection with
`python -m met run presets/shared_sensor_tilt.yaml --workers 1`. Its report includes all
three dynamic runs at 0.05/0.2/1 s with floor on/off, plus the in-calibration control.
It retains the source hashes and attribution to Aleksander Skrede's dataset,
DOI 10.5281/zenodo.11096791, CC-BY-4.0, and full spectra/residual arrays. Data and
calibration/window/budget code are unchanged. **O16 remains open:** detecting or fitting
a shared pattern does not supply the missing shared-error declaration or prove a cause.

### 28 Oct: what the robot's shared error is, and is not (Claude, pressure; provisional)

Checked before briefing the slot, since a slot is only as good as what is declared into it.
All four are in `../tests/test_robot_budget_checks.py`.

1. **The floor is not a shared offset.** It is the static fit's residual, and that fit has a
   per-axis intercept, so the floor is zero-mean over the 24 orientations: model error that
   changes with orientation. `met/robot.py`'s "the same error in every reading" is wrong as
   physics, and declaring the floor as one constant shared error would be wrong too.
2. **A real misalignment, which is not the cause.** On the static rows alone,
   $\mathbf F_{\rm fts}\approx sR\mathbf A+\mathbf b$ gives a force–IMU rotation of **1.153°**
   (bootstrap SD under 0.2° per component; $s=0.93126$ kg): the dataset's $R_{FA}$ is nominal.
   Codex's dynamic fit above agrees on the y and z components, not on x (−0.09° static, 0.64°
   dynamic). Rotating the IMU channel by the static rotation moves the baseline 0.2 s medians to
   0.9191 (floor) and 0.9335 (no floor), against 0.9213 and 0.9347: the 1.4% gap between the
   two budgets stays.
3. **Not a lever arm.** The trajectory is a steady rotation about the FTS y-axis (median
   $\lvert\omega\rvert$ 0.724 rad/s, mean $\omega$ along y), under which an IMU away from the
   load's centre reads a constant offset $-\omega^2\mathbf r_\perp$, perpendicular to y. A
   constant offset fitted on one run is 0.22–0.27 m/s² (a 42–53 cm arm), has a y part
   (0.05–0.08 m/s²), and applied to the other run does not bring the budgets together (run 1:
   0.9335 with the floor, 0.9406 without, the latter missing 0.9323).
4. **The white part is understated.** "Rest variance over the window's sample count" assumes
   independent samples. The rest recordings are autocorrelated: integrated autocorrelation
   time 3.9, 2.7, 18.8 samples on the force axes and 1.5, 1.6, 5.1 on the acceleration axes
   (one second of rest data per sensor, so rough). Every dynamic window's variance is
   understated 2–19 times, axis by axis, and its $Q$ inflated by as much. That is part of the
   roughly hundredfold excess without the floor (median $g$ 0.012), not all of it. The static
   readings use one sample each, no averaging, and agree under either budget, so "the excess
   comes with motion" (Evidence, above) may partly be "comes with averaging".

**Sort, provisional.** The robot's miss is not yet evidence about the definition, nor about
this item's slot: the per-reading budget it rests on is mis-declared (4), and what would be
declared into the slot is not known (1–3). The budget is an input (`../TASK.md` §2), so the
move is a declaration from calibration-side data only (rest files, static rows), by a method
fixed before the dynamic runs are read, then the rerun. Briefed to Codex, 28 Oct.

**The slot itself, for after that (Claude, provisional).** A declared series covariance, block
diagonal by default. Under it the raw law ($g=\eta=1$) is forced: the flat-candidate likelihood
of the declared Gaussian model on the projective line, the series' explained energy
$B^\top G^{-1}B$ with $C$ block diagonal, which is ours29's raw law when nothing is shared.
ours29's tempering is the raw law with each reading's covariance divided by $\eta g_i$ ($H$ is
homogeneous of degree −1 in $\Sigma$). Under a non-block-diagonal declaration, whether the shared
part is inflated too, and what $J$ is when the readings' scores are correlated: **unknown yet**.
Not briefed.
