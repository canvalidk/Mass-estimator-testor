## The problem in one paragraph

The starting law $\pi$, including its shape and centre $c>0$, is an input
(R-2026-10-24-3; `../TASK.md` §6). This file separates the family allowed by
units, reciprocal symmetry and properness from the question of a principal
member. The latter remains open here; B08 does not select a shape or change
the half-Cauchy default. The default centre $c=s$ for one noise pair has the
separate, conditional status in L3′ and O11.

### The family derivation (B08)

The following characterizes **L3, L4 and properness**, at a fixed declared
shape. It does not prove that every member satisfies every other premise,
such as L11's consistency requirement. It does not choose the centre or
derive the table's historical claim about using one shape in every dimension.

First work with a density $\pi_c(m)$ with respect to physical $dm$ on
$(0,\infty)$. A force-unit change by $\lambda>0$ and acceleration-unit change
by $\mu>0$ give $r=\lambda/\mu$, $m'=rm$, $c'=rc$. L3 is the probability
change-of-variables identity
$$
 \pi_{rc}(rm)\,r\,dm=\pi_c(m)\,dm,
 \qquad\text{or}\qquad \pi_{rc}(rm)=r^{-1}\pi_c(m).
$$
Thus $\pi_c(m)=c^{-1}f(m/c)$ for a dimensionless shape $f$. This says that
the **same declared shape** transforms covariantly; it does not force all
callers or instruments to declare the same shape or dimensionless spread.

L4 is equality of probability laws under the decreasing map
$T_c(m)=c^2/m$, not equality of densities without a Jacobian. It requires
$$
 \pi_c(m)=\frac{c^2}{m^2}\,\pi_c(c^2/m)
 \quad\text{for almost every }m>0.
$$
Introduce the dimensionless log coordinate $x=\ln(m/c)$, so $dm=m\,dx$.
Its density is
$$
 g(x)=ce^x\pi_c(ce^x)=e^x f(e^x).
$$
The unit identity makes $g$ independent of the numeric scale $c$ at fixed
shape. The reciprocal identity gives $g(-x)=g(x)$ almost everywhere.
Properness is exactly $g\ge0$ and $\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)\,dx=1$. Therefore
$$
 \boxed{\pi_c(m)=\frac1m\,g\!\left(\ln\frac mc\right),\qquad
 g\ge0,\quad g(-x)=g(x),\quad\int_{\mathbb R}g=1.}
$$
An unnormalized even shape is sufficient only when it is nonnegative and
$0<\int g<\infty$; divide it by that integral. Even integrability by itself
would also allow signed functions or the zero function, neither a law.

Conversely, any such $g$ gives a nonnegative density of integral one by
$dm=m\,dx$, satisfies the reciprocal identity because $g$ is even, and
satisfies L3 because $\ln(rm/(rc))=\ln(m/c)$. This proves necessity and
sufficiency for the three conditions, **within the density class**.

There is no absolute-continuity clause in `../definition/type.md`: it calls
$\pi$ a law. The complete statement for general probability laws is
$$
 M=c\,e^X,\qquad X\text{ has a symmetric probability law on }\mathbb R.
$$
Indeed the reciprocal map is exactly $X\mapsto-X$, and a unit change sends
$M$ to $rM$ while leaving $X$ alone. The displayed density family is its
absolutely continuous case. For example, $\delta_c$, or equal probability
at $ce^{-a}$ and $ce^a$ for $a>0$, obey all three conditions without a
density $g$. B08 does not add a premise excluding them.

Symmetry always makes $c$ **a median**: $P(X\le0)$ and $P(X\ge0)$ are both
at least $1/2$. It need not be the unique median. For instance the density
$g(x)=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{[-2,-1]\cup[1,2]}(x)$ has every mass in
$[ce^{-1},ce]$ as a median; its lower 0.5 quantile is $ce^{-1}$. The three
positive-density examples below have the unique median $c$. Choosing a point
from a general median interval is a readout convention, not a consequence of
L3/L4/properness proved here.

### What counted once adds

T7 and T20 put one declared starting law on the **whole series**:
$$
 P_D(dm)=\frac{L_D(m)\,\Pi_c(dm)}{\int L_D(u)\,\Pi_c(du)}.
$$
Here $\Pi_c$ denotes the starting probability measure and $L_D$ the data
factor in `equation.md`. In the density case this is $\pi_c(m)L_D(m)$,
not one copy of $\pi_c$ per reading. Counted once fixes its place in the
equation; it fixes no shape, spread, tails or preferred coordinate.

It also does not by itself imply washout as the **number** of readings grows:
any number of all-zero readings still returns $\Pi_c$. Washout requires a
concentrating data factor and prior support near the limiting mass (a
positive continuous local density is a common sufficient local condition).
Symmetric compact or gapped shapes can exclude a true mass, so the converse
above is not a proof of L11 for every proper symmetric prior.

### Three members and the null experiment

The following normalized log densities illustrate the family, without
selecting a member. Here $s_\ell>0$ and $b>0$ are declared dimensionless
shape parameters, not the instrument ratio $s$:

| Member | $g(x)$ | CDF at $m>0$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Half-Cauchy | $1/(\pi\cosh x)$ | $(2/\pi)\arctan(m/c)$ |
| Log-normal | $e^{-x^2/(2s_\ell^2)}/(s_\ell\sqrt{2\pi})$ | $\Phi(\ln(m/c)/s_\ell)$ |
| Log-logistic | $b/[4\cosh^2(bx/2)]$ | $[1+e^{-b\ln(m/c)}]^{-1}$ |

Each CDF is strictly increasing and equals $1/2$ at $m=c$. For all-zero
readings, every $H_i(m)=0$, hence the current equation's factor
$L_D(m)=\exp(\eta\sum_i g_iH_i(m)/2)$ is identically 1. Normalization gives
$P_D=\Pi_c$ exactly, including its median(s); the empty series does the same.

**Implementation scope:** `met/full.py::ours30` takes a centre $c$ but no
general starting-law argument. Its `_log_prior` hard-codes the half-Cauchy
density in log mass. `tests/test_starting_law_family.py` therefore checks
the three-member density/CDF algebra and null identity, including an
independent Gaussian zero-reading integral. The existing
`tests/test_full.py::test_the_all_zero_experiment_returns_the_input_centre_as_its_median`
and `tests/test_theorems.py::test_t28_fisher_orthogonal_and_uniform_in_theta`
cover the production default's null result and starting-law form. The new file
does not claim to run log-normal or log-logistic priors through an API that
does not accept them; no API is changed.

## The current choice and its support

Half-Cauchy, $\pi(m)\propto c/(c^2+m^2)$: uniform in the angle $\theta$ that the noise defines
($m=s\tan\theta$). Median c; about 95% between $0.039c$ and $25.45c$; spread of $\ln m$ equal
to $\pi/2$. It is also the law at zero readings.

## What rides on it

The shape matters most at weak signal. Shape-independent concentration needs
the data factor to concentrate within the prior's support, with suitable
local regularity (T17's strong-signal setting); neither a large reading
count nor the three family conditions alone guarantees it. The all-zero
law is exactly the declared starting law. For the three positive-density
members above its unique median is $c$; generally $c$ is a median and a
separate convention may be needed to choose among medians (R-c′ in O2).

## Routes not yet tried

- A property of mass stated at the level of the law, not the data, that selects $g$. The
  obvious candidates (maximum entropy in $\ln m$ under a fixed spread; invariance under some
  group larger than the swap) have not been written down and tested against F2.
- Showing that two admissible shapes give different answers on some reachable measured input
  that practice already adjudicates. That would turn O6 from a choice into an empirical
  question, which is what F1 wants.
- The ruling route: record the shape as a declared parameter of the definition, state what an
  alternative shape would be an alternative *of*, and accept that under F2 this is a family
  of mechanics that differ at weak signal. The full-covariance record §5 argued that a
  declared shape is "the experiment's description", not a rival definition; the lemma list
  calls that the "nobody cares" move unless it is argued.

### 4 Nov: a conditional geometric principal branch and the restriction obstruction (Codex)

Standard mathematics: canonical Grassmann geometry, invariant manifold
volume and conditioning on lower-dimensional sets. T49 treats a matrix
relation through its graph in the declared noise metric and then pulls
that metric back to the physical scalar family M=mI. With a common
uncorrelated noise ratio s, its normalized intrinsic curve volume is
2s/[pi(s^2+m^2)] dm in every spatial dimension. The angle is supplied by
that geometric construction. The additional choice is to use intrinsic
curve volume as a principal prior, not an arbitrary choice of coordinate.
That extra reference rule has not been forced by the VD commitments.

Full covariance matters. For one scalar pair with channel correlation rho,
the intrinsic curve is a shifted Cauchy restricted to positive mass,
normalized by its arc length pi/2+arcsin(rho). Its median is still s for
every |rho|<1. Generic vector covariance need not give that shape; choosing
a reference geometry across heterogeneous instruments remains a further
question. The declared input prior is not replaced by these constructions.

The bigger matrix space does not make scalar restriction automatic.
Canonical ambient graph volume has density det(I+M^T M)^(-d) for square
d-by-d matrices. Evaluating it on M=mI gives (1+m^2)^(-d^2), whereas induced
curve volume gives (1+m^2)^(-1). For d>1 the curve has ambient probability
zero. Fixed Euclidean transverse tubes give the former limiting weight;
canonical Grassmann-normal tubes give the latter. Simple density evaluation
is therefore not ordinary conditioning or an invariant way of importing a
matrix prior into the scalar model. The naive normalized substitution also
fails scalar reciprocal symmetry for d>1.

The active O(2d) group behind ambient Haar uniqueness is larger than
physical frame bookkeeping. A rotation diag(R,I) generally sends graph(mI)
to graph(mR), outside scalar mass. Physical common rotations diag(R,R)
instead fix every scalar hypothesis individually and cannot choose a
prior over m. T49 distinguishes this from passive changes of whitening,
which transform the data, covariance and complete family together.

Nor does graph volume resolve T48's latent-fiber measure: these live on
different spaces, and scalar noise-scale multiplication leaves graph
geometry unchanged. Finally, internally recomputing a prior from a product
of all reading geometries can change it when a zero reading is appended.
That is not a Z-neutral derivation at fixed supplied pi; declaring a
different caller prior changes the input. The existing O6 family remains
unchanged and the geometric member remains conditional.

`tests/test_graph_geometry.py` independently checks projector Jacobians,
full-covariance metric determinants, scalar/correlated curve volumes,
finite-radius tube integrals and the zero-padding/default counterexample.

### 9 Nov: the null-limit fixed-point test (E10, exploration)

The user-directed follow-up to T42 asks whether the average of its normalized
pure-noise limiting laws can select a principal prior. The result on
`explore/e10-null-limit-prior` is negative: **no full-support probability law
on positive mass is fixed** by a nontrivial isotropic angular exponential
tilt with the stated exponential-moment bound. Uniform angle is the unique
full-support fixed point on the whole circle, but restricting each random
law to positive mass before averaging does not preserve that identity.
At T42's strength the half-Cauchy's average moves about 0.0955 in total
variation. This is a conditional obstruction to the proposed *additional*
average-neutrality test, not a new restriction on the current prior family.

If null tilt instead vanishes, every prior is preserved, so that limit alone
does not select a member either. Derivation, scope and checks are in
`../explore/e10-null-limit-prior.md` and `../tests/test_null_limit.py`.
No prior is adopted; O6 remains open. This follows the E9 investigation
on its separate branch without importing E9's geometry into the argument.
