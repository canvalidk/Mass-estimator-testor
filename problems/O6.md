# O6: the shape of the starting law

**Status:** open; the second item that needs a real idea.
**Owner of the next move:** anyone; a ruling can also close it (see below).
**Last updated:** 2026-10-24.

## The problem in one paragraph

The starting law $\pi$ is the one declared shape left in the definition. Its centre is forced
(to c, with c = s under L3′) and several of its properties are forced, but its shape is not.
Under F2 any free choice is an alternative Newtonian mechanics, so either some property of
mass forces the shape, or a ruling records it as a parameter and says what that means.

## What is forced

| Property | From |
| --- | --- |
| Symmetric under $m/c \leftrightarrow c/m$ | L4 (swap, law half) |
| Counted once, so it washes out as readings accumulate | T7 |
| Declared before data, part of the specification | T20 |
| Unit-covariant: force units ×λ, acceleration units ×μ scale it by λ/μ | L3 |
| Centre c = s for one noise pair | T14, given L3′ (unruled) and the units argument of O11 |
| The same in every dimension | d2 step 7 (axis splitting applied to the law) |

So $\pi(m)=m^{-1}g(\ln(m/c))$ with $g$ even and integrable. Everything else is free.

## The current choice and its support

Half-Cauchy, $\pi(m)=c/(c^2+m^2)$: uniform in the angle $\theta$ that the noise defines
($m=s\tan\theta$). Median c; about 95% between $0.039c$ and $25.45c$; spread of $\ln m$ equal
to $\pi/2$. It is also the law at zero readings.

Arguments offered so far, none a proof:

| Argument | Where | Why it does not force |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uniform in the angle the noise defines | d2 step 8 | depends on choosing that angle as the coordinate |
| Measured from it, a tilt adds no fake data (T16) | d2 step 10 | measured from the choice itself |
| In 1D it makes the law Liseo's reference-prior posterior restricted to m > 0 | d2 §5 | a known default, not a property of mass |
| T28: under ours28's model mass and push scale are Fisher-orthogonal and the reference prior on mass is uniform in $\theta$ for any size law | Jeffreys-check record, 28 Sept | the record itself says the agreement "was not evidence" for the principle; under ours29 (no size law) T28 says only that $\pi$'s reference form does not depend on one; gives the half-Cauchy only at c = s |

## What rides on it

Only answers at weak signal. Where many readings converge does not depend on the shape
(T17). The all-zero answer is c under every admissible shape (R-c′ in O2).

## Routes not yet tried

- A property of mass stated at the level of the law, not the data, that selects $g$. The
  obvious candidates (maximum entropy in $\ln m$ under a fixed spread; invariance under some
  group larger than the swap) have not been written down and tested against F2.
- Showing that two admissible shapes give different answers on some reachable measured input
  that practice already adjudicates. That would turn O6 from a choice into an empirical
  question, which is what F1 wants.
- The ruling route: record the shape as a declared parameter of the definition, state what an
  alternative shape would be an alternative *of*, and accept that under F2 this is a family
  of mechanics that differ at weak signal. The full-covariance record §5 argued that a
  declared shape is "the experiment's description", not a rival definition; the lemma list
  calls that the "nobody cares" move unless it is argued.

## Questions for Can

1. Is there a property of mass, in the sense of the L list, that you think the shape should
   answer to? If none comes to mind, the ruling route is the honest close.
2. If the ruling route: does the parameter belong to the definition of mass or to the
   description of the experiment (the full-covariance record's position)?
