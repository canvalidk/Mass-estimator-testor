**Status:** open. **Kind:** proof, then the tester. **Last updated:** 2026-10-27.

## The item (lemma list d4, verbatim)

**$\eta$ at finite $N$** (T31). The power form is asymptotically forced; with few readings the raw $J$ is biased low (weak signal at $N=20$ covers 88–89%). A Z-safe small-sample correction would count effective readings, $(\sum_ic_i)^2/\sum_ic_i^2$ with $c_i$ the curvature contributions, never $N$. With several instruments, one $\eta_g$ per noise pair may be what property N asks for (the ours28b calibration theorem: sharing is exact only within classes of equal sensitivity).

### 27 Oct pressure check: the pooled scale's bias and its null-point obstruction

**29 Oct (R-2026-10-29-2):** this section's second objection, that feeding the pooled scale back
moves the point when exact-zero readings are appended, was judged against point-level Z, which
was never ruled (his 27 Sept position was the opposite). It no longer counts. The first objection,
that the pooled ratio is biased low at every finite signal strength, stands. The second noise
channel is therefore **open again** with that one objection against it, not dead.

**Codex derivation and counterexample; no ruling or replacement for eta.** This checks the
candidate sum Q / sum nu on O7's proposed covariance-up-to-scale branch. The derivation
concerns independent isotropic Gaussian channel errors, Q on the **whole projective line**,
and full subspaces of dimension k=2 or 3. It covers unequal channel strengths and arbitrary
positive mass/noise ratios. It does not concern the positivity cost Q_+ (O13).

**Outcome.** The ratio is biased low at every finite signal strength; more readings remove
spread but not this bias. Feeding its reciprocal back as a common evidence power can move
the mass point when exact-zero readings are appended, even with the original cloud-size
function held fixed. Thus this raw pooling-and-powering construction does not meet the
proposed branch's point neutrality. The input branch itself remains open.

#### 1. The exact bias curve, including unequal channels

In declared noise units write x=alpha u+sqrt(v) xi and y=beta u+sqrt(v) zeta, with u a
unit vector, xi,zeta independent N(0,I_k), alpha,beta>0, and v the unknown common
**variance** multiplier. The physical mass/noise ratio is alpha/beta. Put

$$\lambda=(\alpha^2+\beta^2)/v,\qquad \nu=k-1.$$

A right-orthogonal rotation of the two data columns preserves their Gram eigenvalues and
Gaussian noise law. It puts their means at (sqrt(lambda) u,0) after dividing by sqrt(v).
This is a distributional calculation for Q, not a proposed physical transformation of the
readings or of positive mass. Conditional on the first column's length, decompose the
second column along and perpendicular to it. Then the following variables are independent:

$$A\sim\chi'^2_k(\lambda),\quad W\sim\chi^2_1,\quad V\sim\chi^2_\nu,$$

and

$$Q/v\ \overset d=\ q(A,W,V)
 =\frac{2AV}{A+W+V+\sqrt{(A-W-V)^2+4AW}}.$$

Consequently the first two moments are exact positive triple integrals against those three
densities. `met/pooled_reference.py::moments` evaluates them by independent quantile
quadrature, using two resolutions. Their difference is an error estimate, not a rigorous
bound. The null uses exact moments instead. `tests/test_pooled_reference.py` checks the
null against the integral and the approach to the strong-signal moments.

Let mu_k(lambda)=E q and s_k^2(lambda)=Var(q). For W,V>0, the eigenvalue equation rearranges
to A=q+qW/(V-q), with 0<q<V. Its right side strictly increases in q, so q increases in A.
The noncentral chi-square's Poisson mixture A|J~chi-square_(k+2J), J~Poisson(lambda/2),
couples A stochastically increasingly with lambda. Therefore mu_k strictly increases.
Couple A_lambda=|sqrt(lambda)u+xi|^2 independently of W,V: it tends to infinity, q tends
to V, and 0<=q<=V supplies domination for both moments. Hence

$$0<\mu_k(\lambda)<\nu\quad\hbox{at finite }\lambda,\qquad
  \mu_k(\lambda)\to\nu,\quad s_k^2(\lambda)\to2\nu.$$

This proves the general Q bias curve's dependence on **total** squared SNR, not its
allocation between channels. The rotation argument does not hold for Q_+, whose
half-line domain is changed by that rotation; O13's unequal-channel counterexample stands.

At null, O13's eigenvalue coordinates Q=T(1-rho)/2 give
E T^2=4k(k+1), E rho^2=2/(k+1), and
E Q^2=k(k+3)-2k(k+1)E rho. Thus:

| k | mu_k(0) | E q^2 at null | s_k^2(0) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 2-pi/2 | 10-3pi | 6-pi-pi^2/4 |
| 3 | 1 | 2 | 1 |

#### 2. Finite N: persistent bias, falling spread, and reciprocal tails

For N independent readings at the same k and lambda,

$$\widehat v=\frac{\sum_i Q_i}{N\nu},\quad
 E\widehat v=v\frac{\mu_k(\lambda)}{\nu},\quad
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat v)=
 \frac{v^2s_k^2(\lambda)}{N\nu^2}.$$

In particular at null the mean remains **0.429203673 v in 2D and 0.5 v in 3D**, regardless
of N. There is no dimension-only denominator making it unbiased both at null and strong
signal. At strong signal its SD approaches v sqrt(2/(N nu)). With heterogeneous strengths
or dimensions and one common v, replace the mean numerator by v sum mu_(k_i)(lambda_i)
and the variance numerator by v^2 sum s_(k_i)^2(lambda_i), with denominator sum nu_i or
its square respectively. The 1D proposal is undefined: Q=nu=0.

There is an exact small-N distribution in 3D at null. The ordered central Gram eigenvalue
density has factor exp[-(lambda_++lambda_-)/2](lambda_+-lambda_-). With q=lambda_-
and delta=lambda_+-lambda_-, it separates into exp(-q) delta exp(-delta/2).
Thus Q/v is Exp(1), and G=sum Q_i/v is Gamma(N,1). For the **uncapped reciprocal**,

$$E[\widehat v^{-1}]=\frac{2N}{v(N-1)}\ (N>1),\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat v^{-1})=
 \frac{4N^2}{v^2(N-1)^2(N-2)}\ (N>2).$$

Its mean diverges at N=1, and its second moment at N<=2. A cap removes those divergences
but defines a different estimator. The Gamma-integral test in `test_pooled_reference.py`
checks the finite moments. This is a small-sample obstruction, not evidence that its
spread is uniformly better or worse than eta's.

#### 3. Comparison with eta is not a comparison of estimators of the same quantity

For the **unweighted** flat working law, rotate to the true mass line. The parallel vector
has squared signal lambda and the perpendicular vector is noise. At v=1 the expected
per-reading curvature is lambda; the expected squared score is lambda+k. Hence the
long-series Bartlett power is lambda/(lambda+k), capped at 1. At general v it is
min(1,lambda/[v(lambda+k)]). Gaussian polynomial integration checks these score and
curvature moments in `tests/test_pooled_reference.py`.

In contrast, the raw pooled precision tends to nu/[v mu_k(lambda)]. It overstates the
instrument precision 1/v at finite lambda. At v=1 its capped version tends to 1, whereas
the unweighted Bartlett power is strictly below 1 at finite nonzero lambda and tends
to zero at null. The current g-weighted eta has a different score distribution again.
Therefore neither a lower pooled SD nor the inversion of its mean establishes a valid
replacement for eta. The unknown instrument scale and the flat law's calibration are
different questions.

The reproducible paired check is
`python -m met run presets/pooled_noise_scale.yaml --workers 1`: 90 cells, 1,000 series
each, seed 20261027, dimensions 2/3, masses 0.25/1/4, acceleration SNR 0/0.5/1/2/5 and
N=5/20/400. The investigator uses correct unit covariance to supply the references;
`residual_statistics` receives only observations and supplied SDs. It records pooled
variance, raw/capped precision and both unweighted and current ours29 eta on the same
draws. These are pressure diagnostics, not new readouts or a scale declaration in the
production type. CSV, exact effective preset, replicate arrays and code hashes are saved.
`tests/test_pooled_scale.py` checks SVD geometry, column-rotation/scaling identities,
sampling moments, replay and the single-worker constraint.

#### 4. A finite null-padding counterexample isolates the scale channel

Take independent unit SDs and two 2D **observed** readings:

$$r_1:\ F=(2,0),\ a=(0,0),\qquad
  r_2:\ F=(2,0),\ a=(0,2).$$

Here Q_1=0, Q_2=4 and nu_1=nu_2=1. The pooled variance is 2, precision t=1/2.
Appending an exact-zero 2D reading changes it to 4/3, precision t'=3/4. Both are below
the precision cap 1. More generally, when S=sum Q>0, padding d degrees of freedom gives
vhat'=vhat D/(D+d) and t'=t(D+d)/D, where D=sum nu.

H_1(m)=4m^2/(1+m^2), while H_2=4 is constant. The current fixed g_2=1/4 changes only
that constant; eta itself is 1 and stays 1 on padding. Up to a constant the proposed
powered evidence is t L(m), with **L(m)=2m^2/(1+m^2) strictly increasing**.

Freeze the original cloud-size function A(m), explicitly excluding O2's extra-cloud
mechanism. For p_t proportional to pi exp(tL), let

$$R_t=\frac{\int mA(m)\pi(m)e^{tL(m)}dm}
                {\int A(m)\pi(m)e^{tL(m)}dm}.
\quad \frac{dR_t}{dt}=\operatorname{Cov}_{w_t}(m,L(m))>0,
\quad w_t\propto A\pi e^{tL}.$$

Strictness follows by taking independent M,M' from w_t:
2 Cov(M,L(M))=E[(M-M')(L(M)-L(M'))]>0. Thus **R_(3/4)>R_(1/2)**. The law's median
also increases by strict monotone likelihood-ratio ordering. Tests freeze the actual
original stacked cloud and independently check the identity with A=1/sqrt(1+m^2), c=1,
whose point has the compact reference

$$R_t=e^{2t}\frac{\int_0^1 e^{-2tu^2}du}{\int_0^1 e^{2tu^2}du}.$$

These are deterministic permitted inputs, not random zero-signal samples. On exactly
all-zero observations S=0, the raw reciprocal is singular; a total new operation would
also need to define that case. The finite counterexample above does not rely on it.

**Scope of the obstruction.** This defeats using the pooled precision merely as a common
power and assuming width changes leave the existing point fixed. It does not disprove
the input branch or every possible point rule: positive powering leaves a likelihood
mode alone, for example. The theorem and tests do not silently add the proposed input
to the production type, or infer a signal population to correct the bias. A correction
must explain how permitted inputs supply the needed information; investigator-known
lambda in this study is not an estimator input. O14 and O7 remain open.

### 30 Oct: what $g_i$ and $\eta$ are (Claude's sort, provisional; pressure mode)

The fork left by T36: once a noise scale can be in the model, what are $g_i$ and $\eta$ for?
Standard names first (GOAL rule 6): $g_i=\min(1,\kappa/Q_+)$ is a per-reading **Birge ratio**,
one-sided, the PDG scale-factor convention at reading level (T30's status line already says so);
its smooth form $\nu/(\nu+Q)$ is the reweighting of a per-reading Student-$t$ model (Lange, Little &
Taylor 1989). $\eta$ is the series' Birge ratio read through the Bartlett/sandwich comparison of
score variance with curvature (T31). L21's "misfit at the best mass is evidence about the reading;
misfit at a mass is evidence about the mass" is, in standard terms, a **cut** (modular inference:
Plummer 2015; Jacob, Murray, Holmes & Robert 2017): the reading's reliability is read without
feedback from the mass hypothesis.

**1. Both are noise scales (forced, by algebra).** $H_i$ is homogeneous of degree $-1$ in
$\Sigma_i$, so tempering a reading's evidence by $w$ *is* declaring its covariance $\Sigma_i/w$.
$1/g_i$ is reading $i$'s noise scale, $1/\eta$ the series'. Both are capped at 1: neither ever
declares less noise than $\Sigma_i$.
`tests/test_scale_reading.py::test_tempering_a_reading_is_declaring_its_noise_larger`.

**2. The find: ours29/30 has been running on an unwritten declaration.** The two caps mean the
declared $\Sigma_i$ is read as a **floor**: each reading's noise is at least $\Sigma_i$, with an
unknown inflation per reading ($1/g_i$) and an unknown common inflation for the series ($1/\eta$).
That is neither row of O7's table ("exact", "up to a scale"): it is a third declaration, and the
one the equation actually implements. On the exact row, $Q\gg\nu$ is only a flag that the
declaration is wrong (O7's own table) and $g$ would have no reason to exist. Bin: **M**
(measurement; Birge 1932, the PDG's practice). Guard (`TASK.md` §5): it is something practice
supplies, and practice's default.

**3. G13, both halves, provisional.** *The power form:* $g_i$ is a scale read at the reading's
best mass (L21's cut), so it is constant in $m$, and a constant rescaling of $\Sigma_i$ is a power
on $E_i$ (1). The power is not an extra choice; it is what "a scale read once" looks like.
*Bounded confidence (T30):* once $g_i<1$, $g_iK_i(m)=\kappa K_i(m)/Q_{+,i}$ is unchanged when the
declared $\Sigma_i$ is scaled, so $\sigma\to0$ cannot sharpen a misaligned reading. T30 is then a
theorem of the floor declaration with the scale read from the misfit, not "forced by meaning";
the meaning was the floor, unwritten.
`::test_t30_bounded_confidence_follows_from_reading_the_scale_from_the_misfit`.

**4. They read different components of T36's one residual.** $g_i$ reads the within-reading part
($Q_{+,i}$: $k_i-1$ dimensions plus the sign); $\eta$ reads the between-reading part (the scores at
the series' best line), after $g$. So on the floor branch they do not count the same thing twice.
They do not compose cleanly, though, when the world's error is not what each level assumes.
Seeded, strong signal, $N=40$, 150 series a cell (`::test_g_and_eta_on_worlds_where_the_declaration_is_off`,
`MET_SLOW=1`):

| world | 95% coverage | median $g\cdot\eta$ | correct scale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| every reading 4× noisier, 1D ($g$ inert) | 0.93 | 0.260 | 0.25 |
| every reading 4× noisier, 3D | 0.92 | **0.165** | 0.25 |
| one reading 25× noisier, 3D | 0.96 | 1.000 | 1 |

$\eta$ alone finds a common inflation. $g$ finds one bad reading (in 3D) and leaves $\eta$ at 1.
On a common inflation in 3D, $g$ reads it as per-reading noise from two dimensions each, reweights
the readings at random, and $\eta$ over-corrects on top: the effective scale is about 6 where it is
4. So the per-reading level is the wrong level for a common error, as the series level would be
for one bad reading.

**5. The answer to "are $g$ and $\eta$ still needed?": an input, level by level.** Each level, the
reading and the series, has its noise scale declared **exact**, a **floor**, or **unknown**
(B11's common scale). Given the declaration, what each level does is fixed:

| level | exact | floor | unknown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| reading | $g_i=1$ | $g_i$, read at the best mass (L21's cut) | per-reading scales integrated (not briefed; see 6) |
| series | $\eta=1$ | $\eta$ (T31) | integrated: B11's law; $\eta$'s job is absorbed |

ours30 is floor/floor. B11's law is exact/unknown, so on its branch $g_i=1$ and $\eta=1$ by the
declaration, not by choice; keeping $g$ there would do what row 2 of the table shows. What a
caller who says nothing declares, the principal branch of this input, is **unknown yet**:
practice (PDG) says floor, and ours30 already behaves so.

**6. One tension, flagged, not resolved.** On a per-reading "unknown" level, integrating each
reading's scale is the standard route, and it would read the scale from $K_i(m)$ at every $m$:
the misfit at a mass feeding the reading's reliability, which L21's cut (ruled 29 Sept) forbids.
So L21 is what keeps the reading level on the floor-and-cut form. That is consistent; it is also
the one place where the ruled form, not the declaration, decides.

**Not shown.** That floor/floor is the default; whether a combined floor/unknown declaration is
needed by any instrument; $\eta$'s finite-$N$ form (B04 still stands); anything at weak signal
(the table is at strong signal only).

### 30 Oct (afternoon): G20, where α comes from (Claude; T37)

The GUM has it: a stated uncertainty carries effective degrees of freedom $\nu_{\rm eff}$ (Annex G),
and a σ estimated from $\nu_{\rm eff}$ Gaussian degrees of freedom gives exactly T37's inverse-gamma
noise size, $\alpha=\nu_{\rm eff}/2$. So α is an input practice supplies, and T30 is the GUM's own "no
uncertainty is exact". Correction to T37: practice normalises the precision ($E[1/v]=1$), not the
covariance. Restated: ours30's κ is a σ declared with $\nu_{\rm eff}=\tfrac12$ (uncertain by ~100%); the cap
holds exactly in 1D and fails in $k\ge2$ by $\nu/\nu_{\rm eff}$. B12 now reports both normalisations.
