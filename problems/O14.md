### 27 Oct pressure check: the pooled scale's bias and its null-point obstruction

**29 Oct (R-2026-10-29-2):** this section's second objection, that feeding the pooled scale back
moves the point when exact-zero readings are appended, was judged against point-level Z, which
was never ruled (his 27 Sept position was the opposite). It no longer counts. The first objection,
that the pooled ratio is biased low at every finite signal strength, stands. The second noise
channel is therefore **open again** with that one objection against it, not dead.

**Codex derivation and counterexample; no ruling or replacement for eta.** This checks the
candidate sum Q / sum nu on O7's proposed covariance-up-to-scale branch. The derivation
concerns independent isotropic Gaussian channel errors, Q on the **whole projective line**,
and full subspaces of dimension k=2 or 3. It covers unequal channel strengths and arbitrary
positive mass/noise ratios. It does not concern the positivity cost Q_+ (O13).

**Outcome.** The ratio is biased low at every finite signal strength; more readings remove
spread but not this bias. Feeding its reciprocal back as a common evidence power can move
the mass point when exact-zero readings are appended, even with the original cloud-size
function held fixed. Thus this raw pooling-and-powering construction does not meet the
proposed branch's point neutrality. The input branch itself remains open.

#### 1. The exact bias curve, including unequal channels

In declared noise units write x=alpha u+sqrt(v) xi and y=beta u+sqrt(v) zeta, with u a
unit vector, xi,zeta independent N(0,I_k), alpha,beta>0, and v the unknown common
**variance** multiplier. The physical mass/noise ratio is alpha/beta. Put

$$\lambda=(\alpha^2+\beta^2)/v,\qquad \nu=k-1.$$

A right-orthogonal rotation of the two data columns preserves their Gram eigenvalues and
Gaussian noise law. It puts their means at (sqrt(lambda) u,0) after dividing by sqrt(v).
This is a distributional calculation for Q, not a proposed physical transformation of the
readings or of positive mass. Conditional on the first column's length, decompose the
second column along and perpendicular to it. Then the following variables are independent:

$$A\sim\chi'^2_k(\lambda),\quad W\sim\chi^2_1,\quad V\sim\chi^2_\nu,$$

and

$$Q/v\ \overset d=\ q(A,W,V)
 =\frac{2AV}{A+W+V+\sqrt{(A-W-V)^2+4AW}}.$$

Consequently the first two moments are exact positive triple integrals against those three
densities. `met/pooled_reference.py::moments` evaluates them by independent quantile
quadrature, using two resolutions. Their difference is an error estimate, not a rigorous
bound. The null uses exact moments instead. `tests/test_pooled_reference.py` checks the
null against the integral and the approach to the strong-signal moments.

Let mu_k(lambda)=E q and s_k^2(lambda)=Var(q). For W,V>0, the eigenvalue equation rearranges
to A=q+qW/(V-q), with 0<q<V. Its right side strictly increases in q, so q increases in A.
The noncentral chi-square's Poisson mixture A|J~chi-square_(k+2J), J~Poisson(lambda/2),
couples A stochastically increasingly with lambda. Therefore mu_k strictly increases.
Couple A_lambda=|sqrt(lambda)u+xi|^2 independently of W,V: it tends to infinity, q tends
to V, and 0<=q<=V supplies domination for both moments. Hence

$$0<\mu_k(\lambda)<\nu\quad\hbox{at finite }\lambda,\qquad
  \mu_k(\lambda)\to\nu,\quad s_k^2(\lambda)\to2\nu.$$

This proves the general Q bias curve's dependence on **total** squared SNR, not its
allocation between channels. The rotation argument does not hold for Q_+, whose
half-line domain is changed by that rotation; O13's unequal-channel counterexample stands.

At null, O13's eigenvalue coordinates Q=T(1-rho)/2 give
E T^2=4k(k+1), E rho^2=2/(k+1), and
E Q^2=k(k+3)-2k(k+1)E rho. Thus:

| k | mu_k(0) | E q^2 at null | s_k^2(0) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 2-pi/2 | 10-3pi | 6-pi-pi^2/4 |
| 3 | 1 | 2 | 1 |

#### 2. Finite N: persistent bias, falling spread, and reciprocal tails

For N independent readings at the same k and lambda,

$$\widehat v=\frac{\sum_i Q_i}{N\nu},\quad
 E\widehat v=v\frac{\mu_k(\lambda)}{\nu},\quad
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat v)=
 \frac{v^2s_k^2(\lambda)}{N\nu^2}.$$

In particular at null the mean remains **0.429203673 v in 2D and 0.5 v in 3D**, regardless
of N. There is no dimension-only denominator making it unbiased both at null and strong
signal. At strong signal its SD approaches v sqrt(2/(N nu)). With heterogeneous strengths
or dimensions and one common v, replace the mean numerator by v sum mu_(k_i)(lambda_i)
and the variance numerator by v^2 sum s_(k_i)^2(lambda_i), with denominator sum nu_i or
its square respectively. The 1D proposal is undefined: Q=nu=0.

There is an exact small-N distribution in 3D at null. The ordered central Gram eigenvalue
density has factor exp[-(lambda_++lambda_-)/2](lambda_+-lambda_-). With q=lambda_-
and delta=lambda_+-lambda_-, it separates into exp(-q) delta exp(-delta/2).
Thus Q/v is Exp(1), and G=sum Q_i/v is Gamma(N,1). For the **uncapped reciprocal**,

$$E[\widehat v^{-1}]=\frac{2N}{v(N-1)}\ (N>1),\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(\widehat v^{-1})=
 \frac{4N^2}{v^2(N-1)^2(N-2)}\ (N>2).$$

Its mean diverges at N=1, and its second moment at N<=2. A cap removes those divergences
but defines a different estimator. The Gamma-integral test in `test_pooled_reference.py`
checks the finite moments. This is a small-sample obstruction, not evidence that its
spread is uniformly better or worse than eta's.

#### 3. Comparison with eta is not a comparison of estimators of the same quantity

For the **unweighted** flat working law, rotate to the true mass line. The parallel vector
has squared signal lambda and the perpendicular vector is noise. At v=1 the expected
per-reading curvature is lambda; the expected squared score is lambda+k. Hence the
long-series Bartlett power is lambda/(lambda+k), capped at 1. At general v it is
min(1,lambda/[v(lambda+k)]). Gaussian polynomial integration checks these score and
curvature moments in `tests/test_pooled_reference.py`.

In contrast, the raw pooled precision tends to nu/[v mu_k(lambda)]. It overstates the
instrument precision 1/v at finite lambda. At v=1 its capped version tends to 1, whereas
the unweighted Bartlett power is strictly below 1 at finite nonzero lambda and tends
to zero at null. The current g-weighted eta has a different score distribution again.
Therefore neither a lower pooled SD nor the inversion of its mean establishes a valid
replacement for eta. The unknown instrument scale and the flat law's calibration are
different questions.

#### 4. A finite null-padding counterexample isolates the scale channel

Take independent unit SDs and two 2D **observed** readings:

$$r_1:\ F=(2,0),\ a=(0,0),\qquad
  r_2:\ F=(2,0),\ a=(0,2).$$

Here Q_1=0, Q_2=4 and nu_1=nu_2=1. The pooled variance is 2, precision t=1/2.
Appending an exact-zero 2D reading changes it to 4/3, precision t'=3/4. Both are below
the precision cap 1. More generally, when S=sum Q>0, padding d degrees of freedom gives
vhat'=vhat D/(D+d) and t'=t(D+d)/D, where D=sum nu.

H_1(m)=4m^2/(1+m^2), while H_2=4 is constant. The current fixed g_2=1/4 changes only
that constant; eta itself is 1 and stays 1 on padding. Up to a constant the proposed
powered evidence is t L(m), with **L(m)=2m^2/(1+m^2) strictly increasing**.

Freeze the original cloud-size function A(m), explicitly excluding O2's extra-cloud
mechanism. For p_t proportional to pi exp(tL), let

$$R_t=\frac{\int mA(m)\pi(m)e^{tL(m)}dm}
                {\int A(m)\pi(m)e^{tL(m)}dm}.
\quad \frac{dR_t}{dt}=\operatorname{Cov}_{w_t}(m,L(m))>0,
\quad w_t\propto A\pi e^{tL}.$$

Strictness follows by taking independent M,M' from w_t:
2 Cov(M,L(M))=E[(M-M')(L(M)-L(M'))]>0. Thus **R_(3/4)>R_(1/2)**. The law's median
also increases by strict monotone likelihood-ratio ordering. Tests freeze the actual
original stacked cloud and independently check the identity with A=1/sqrt(1+m^2), c=1,
whose point has the compact reference

$$R_t=e^{2t}\frac{\int_0^1 e^{-2tu^2}du}{\int_0^1 e^{2tu^2}du}.$$

These are deterministic permitted inputs, not random zero-signal samples. On exactly
all-zero observations S=0, the raw reciprocal is singular; a total new operation would
also need to define that case. The finite counterexample above does not rely on it.

**Scope of the obstruction.** This defeats using the pooled precision merely as a common
power and assuming width changes leave the existing point fixed. It does not disprove
the input branch or every possible point rule: positive powering leaves a likelihood
mode alone, for example. The theorem and tests do not silently add the proposed input
to the production type, or infer a signal population to correct the bias. A correction
must explain how permitted inputs supply the needed information; investigator-known
lambda in this study is not an estimator input. O14 and O7 remain open.

### 30 Oct: what $g_i$ and $\eta$ are (Claude's sort, provisional; pressure mode)

The fork left by T36: once a noise scale can be in the model, what are $g_i$ and $\eta$ for?
Standard names first (GOAL rule 6): $g_i=\min(1,\kappa/Q_+)$ is a per-reading **Birge ratio**,
one-sided, the PDG scale-factor convention at reading level (T30's status line already says so);
its smooth form $\nu/(\nu+Q)$ is the reweighting of a per-reading Student-$t$ model (Lange, Little &
Taylor 1989). $\eta$ is the series' Birge ratio read through the Bartlett/sandwich comparison of
score variance with curvature (T31). L21's "misfit at the best mass is evidence about the reading;
misfit at a mass is evidence about the mass" is, in standard terms, a **cut** (modular inference:
Plummer 2015; Jacob, Murray, Holmes & Robert 2017): the reading's reliability is read without
feedback from the mass hypothesis.

**1. Both are noise scales (forced, by algebra).** $H_i$ is homogeneous of degree $-1$ in
$\Sigma_i$, so tempering a reading's evidence by $w$ *is* declaring its covariance $\Sigma_i/w$.
$1/g_i$ is reading $i$'s noise scale, $1/\eta$ the series'. Both are capped at 1: neither ever
declares less noise than $\Sigma_i$.
`tests/test_scale_reading.py::test_tempering_a_reading_is_declaring_its_noise_larger`.

**2. The find: ours29/30 has been running on an unwritten declaration.** The two caps mean the
declared $\Sigma_i$ is read as a **floor**: each reading's noise is at least $\Sigma_i$, with an
unknown inflation per reading ($1/g_i$) and an unknown common inflation for the series ($1/\eta$).
That is neither row of O7's table ("exact", "up to a scale"): it is a third declaration, and the
one the equation actually implements. On the exact row, $Q\gg\nu$ is only a flag that the
declaration is wrong (O7's own table) and $g$ would have no reason to exist. Bin: **M**
(measurement; Birge 1932, the PDG's practice). Guard (`TASK.md` §5): it is something practice
supplies, and practice's default.

**3. G13, both halves, provisional.** *The power form:* $g_i$ is a scale read at the reading's
best mass (L21's cut), so it is constant in $m$, and a constant rescaling of $\Sigma_i$ is a power
on $E_i$ (1). The power is not an extra choice; it is what "a scale read once" looks like.
*Bounded confidence (T30):* once $g_i<1$, $g_iK_i(m)=\kappa K_i(m)/Q_{+,i}$ is unchanged when the
declared $\Sigma_i$ is scaled, so $\sigma\to0$ cannot sharpen a misaligned reading. T30 is then a
theorem of the floor declaration with the scale read from the misfit, not "forced by meaning";
the meaning was the floor, unwritten.
`::test_t30_bounded_confidence_follows_from_reading_the_scale_from_the_misfit`.

**4. They read different components of T36's one residual.** $g_i$ reads the within-reading part
($Q_{+,i}$: $k_i-1$ dimensions plus the sign); $\eta$ reads the between-reading part (the scores at

They do not compose cleanly, though, when the world's error is not what each level assumes.

### 30 Oct: B12 admissible cut precisions (Codex; derivation and checks, no ruling)

**Standard mathematics:** Bernstein's theorem for Laplace transforms, exponential tilting,
and recovery of a mixing probability law. The reference is Schilling, Song and Vondraček,
*Bernstein Functions*, 2nd ed., Theorem 1.4 and Proposition 1.2
([publisher preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783110269338_A19166479/preview-9783110269338_A19166479.pdf),
printed pp. 2–3). The calculations below are Codex's application to the strong-signal,
whole-line model in B12. L24 remains a candidate; no equation or premise row changes.

#### 1. Corrected criterion: a Laplace measure is not yet a noise-size law

Put $u=1/v$, $r=\nu/2$, and write $P$ for the law of $u$. For $\nu>0$, the likelihood of
the misfit, after removing factors independent of $u$, is $u^r e^{-Qu/2}$. Define
$d\mu=u^r dP$ and $L(Q)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-Qu/2}d\mu(u)$. Then

$$g(Q)=E[u\mid Q]=-2L'(Q)/L(Q),\qquad
L(Q)=L(Q_*)\exp\left[-\tfrac12\int_{Q_*}^Q g(q)dq\right],\quad Q_*>0.$$

This anchored form is safe even when $L(0+)=E_P[u^r]=\infty$. The brief's formula with
$L(0)$ additionally needs that moment finite. Bernstein's theorem says that $L$ is a
Laplace transform of a positive measure iff it is completely monotone:
$L\in C^\infty(0,\infty)$ and $(-1)^jL^{(j)}\ge0$ for every $j\ge0$.
**The sketch's "iff a law $P$ exists" omits a gate.** The unique representing measure
must also satisfy

$$\mu(\{0\})=0,\qquad 0<Z=\int_{[0,\infty)}u^{-r}d\mu(u)<\infty.$$

Only then is $dP=Z^{-1}u^{-r}d\mu$ a probability on finite positive noise sizes. For
$r>0$ the integrand is extended to $+\infty$ at zero, so finite $Z$ already excludes
that atom. This is a correction to a step
explicitly marked "to be derived and checked" in B12, not a choice of a new premise.

Let $M_s=\int u^{-s}d\mu$. For $s>0$, Tonelli and the gamma integral give exactly the
brief's Mellin identity, allowing infinity:

$$M_s=\frac{2^{-s}}{\Gamma(s)}\int_0^\infty Q^{s-1}L(Q)dQ.$$

If $P$ exists, $E[v]=M_{r+1}/M_r$ and $E[u]=M_{r-1}/M_r$. The second index need not be
positive; for $r>0$ a formula avoiding a negative-index Mellin integral is

$$E[v]=\frac{\int_0^\infty Q^rL(Q)dQ}
{2r\int_0^\infty Q^{r-1}L(Q)dQ},\qquad
E[u]=\frac{\int_0^\infty Q^{r-1}g(Q)L(Q)dQ}
{\int_0^\infty Q^{r-1}L(Q)dQ}.$$

Thus the covariance convention tests $E[v]=1$; the precision convention tests $E[u]=1$.
Undefined moments of a nonexistent $P$ are distinguished from infinite moments of a
proper $P$.

**At $\nu=0$.** For the formal exponential-tilt continuation, $\mu=P$ up to scale.
Recovery requires $0<L(0+)<\infty$ and $L(\infty)=0$ (no $u=0$ atom). Then

$$E[v]=\frac{\int_0^\infty L(Q)dQ}{2L(0+)},\qquad
E[u]=-2L'(0+)/L(0+).$$

Physically $Q\mid v\sim v\chi^2_0$ means **$Q=0$ almost surely**, so only
$g(0)=E[u]$ is identified. A functional verdict on $Q>0$ at $k=1$ below concerns that
formal continuation, not an observable whole-line misfit experiment. The supplied
`misfit_weight` guard makes ours29 and the requested literal-$\nu$ smooth form

#### 2. Requested forms: analytic verdicts

For every positive threshold $b$, the capped weight $g=\min(1,b/Q)$ reconstructs

$$L(Q)=\begin{cases}e^{-Q/2},&Q\le b,\\
e^{-b/2}(Q/b)^{-b/2},&Q>b.\end{cases}$$

Its second derivative jumps by $e^{-b/2}/(2b)$ at $b$, so it is not completely
monotone. This rejects the entire capped function, independently of its tail coefficient
or either mean normalisation. Equivalently, $g'=-\operatorname{Var}(u\mid Q)/2$;
a plateau on a positive interval forces a point-mass mixing law and hence a constant
weight everywhere.

**Floor, exact cut.** Under $p(v)\propto v^{-a}1_{v\ge1}$, a proper prior requires
$a>1$. Its precision density is $(a-1)u^{a-2}$ on $(0,1]$. Put
$h=a-1+r$ and $I_h(t)=\int_0^1u^{h-1}e^{-tu}du$. Then

$$L(Q)=hI_h(Q/2),\qquad g(Q)=I_{h+1}(Q/2)/I_h(Q/2),\qquad
g(0)=h/(h+1).$$

The integral proves complete monotonicity. For $a>1$ the proper prior has
$E[v]=(a-1)/(a-2)$ if $a>2$, infinity if $1<a\le2$, and
$E[u]=(a-1)/a$. Every finite nondegenerate floor prior therefore fails both mean-one
normalisations. At $a=1$, $\nu>0$, the tilted $L$ is still finite and completely monotone,
but $P$ is improper; at $a=1$, $\nu=0$, even the cut integral is undefined.

"Plug-in" needs a convention. The floor **MLE** gives $\widehat v=\max(1,Q/\nu)$ for
$\nu>0$, while the **mode of the posterior density kernel in the $v$ coordinate** gives

and fail Bernstein. The latter is a posterior MAP when that posterior is proper;
its formal kernel mode is also reported for $a=1,\nu=0$, where the posterior is improper.
At $\nu=0,Q=0$, every $v\ge1$ is an MLE: the floor-MLE result is
**unknown/not uniquely defined**, without inventing an endpoint rule.

#### 3. Series-level cut and Bartlett's η

In B04's linear-Gaussian limit let $x_i\mid\theta,v\sim N(\theta,v/c_i)$,
$C=\sum c_i$, $\widehat\theta=\sum c_ix_i/C$, $r_i=x_i-\widehat\theta$, and
$S=\sum c_ir_i^2$. Completing the square gives
$\sum c_i(x_i-\theta)^2=S+C(\theta-\widehat\theta)^2$. Gaussian residual contrasts
(or integrating flat $\theta$) therefore give $S\mid v\sim v\chi^2_{N-1}$.
A declared shared-size law has

$$L_s(S)=\int u^{(N-1)/2}e^{-Su/2}dP(u),\qquad
\eta_P(S)=-2L_s'(S)/L_s(S),\quad
\eta_P'(S)=-\tfrac12\operatorname{Var}(u\mid S).$$

For equal $c_i=c$, Bartlett's $\mathcal H=Nc$, $J=cS$, so
$\eta=\min(1,N/S)$. For every $N\ge2$ this has a positive plateau followed by a
nonconstant tail, which no proper shared-size posterior precision can have. It fails
the same analytic test under **both** normalisations, not just a proposed prior family.
For $N=1$, $S=J=0$ and $\eta=1$ is compatible with $\delta_1$.

Heterogeneous $c_i$ create another obstruction: the full Gaussian residual cut depends
only on $S$, whereas $J=\sum c_i^2r_i^2$ also depends on residual direction. With
$c=(1,2,3)$, both $x_A=(2\sqrt2,-\sqrt2,0)$ and $x_B=(3,0,-1)$ have
$\widehat\theta=0$, $S=12$ and $\mathcal H=6$, but $(J,\eta)$ are $(16,3/8)$ and
$(18,1/3)$. No cut using the full shared-Gaussian residual likelihood reproduces both.
Discarding residual direction would not remove the equal-precision impossibility.

A nondegenerate shared $v$ is a new joint noise declaration. It induces **marginal
dependence**, even though the errors' pairwise covariance is zero when finite:
$\epsilon_i=\sqrt{v/c_i}Z_i$ gives
$\operatorname{Cov}(\epsilon_i^2,\epsilon_j^2)=\operatorname{Var}(v)/(c_ic_j)>0$
when those moments exist. The current type has no shared noise-size law; L13 explicitly
states independence across readings. T31's algebraic shared-*signal*-size correspondence
does not establish this shared-*noise*-posterior representation. These are findings
under L24's candidate test, not a ruling against η or a proposal to replace it.

### 30 Oct: B11 common-scale kernel fails totality (Codex; counterexample, no ruling)

**Standard mathematics:** Gaussian integrated likelihood with an improper variance-scale
prior, and posterior propriety. **Result per requirement:** the stated residual dimension

measured input fails, already at the principal input $a=1$. The coverage comparison is not

#### The scale integral and its conditions

For arbitrary positive-definite $\Sigma_i$ and declared $d_i\times k_i$ subspace $N_i$, put
$C_i(m)=(mN_i;N_i)$, $G_i=C_i^\top\Sigma_i^{-1}C_i$ and
$\widehat\alpha_i=G_i^{-1}C_i^\top\Sigma_i^{-1}y_i$. Completing the square gives

$$\|y_i-C_i\alpha_i\|_{\Sigma_i^{-1}}^2
=K_i(m)+(\alpha_i-\widehat\alpha_i)^\top G_i(\alpha_i-\widehat\alpha_i),$$
$$K_i(m)=y_i^\top\Sigma_i^{-1}y_i-H_i(m).$$

Here $H_i$ is exactly `met.full._h_terms`, since rescaling the columns of $C_i$ to its
angular parameterisation leaves the projection unchanged. The energy is the **whole**
reading's energy, not `Reading.pre()["energy"]`, which projects into the declared span.
Whitening makes the residual an orthogonal projection of rank $2d_i-k_i$. Thus it has
$k_i+2(d_i-k_i)$ dimensions; correlations change the projection, not this count.

Keeping B11's stated convention of a $v$-independent flat candidate weight, the Gaussian
data density supplies $v^{-d_i}$ and its $k_i$ latent Gaussian integrals supply
$v^{k_i/2}$. Hence the scale-dependent factor is
$v^{-n/2}\exp[-K(m)/(2v)]$, with $n=\sum_i(2d_i-k_i)$ and $K=\sum_iK_i$.
This checks the scale count, conditional on the brief's convention; it does not settle
T36's parked measure question. Grant the proposed $m$-kernel for the following check.

Multiplying by $v^{-a}$ and putting $\beta=n/2+a-1$, $t=K/(2v)$ gives

$$\int_0^\infty v^{-\beta-1}e^{-K/(2v)}\,dv
=2^\beta\Gamma(\beta)K^{-\beta},\qquad K>0,\quad\beta>0.$$

These conditions are necessary. For $K>0$, $\beta\le0$ diverges at large $v$. For $K=0$,
the integral is $\int_0^\infty v^{-\beta-1}dv$, divergent for **every** $\beta$ (one
endpoint or both). Even when it is finite at every positive $m$, normalisation over $m$
is a separate condition. The scale identity alone does not establish a mass law.

### 30 Oct: B04 effective readings and finite-N score bias (Codex; no ruling)

**Standard mathematics:** weighted least-squares leverage, Gaussian quadratic-form
moments, and a sandwich information comparison. **Result per requirement:** the brief's
fixed-curvature Gaussian identity passes by exact matrix moments. Its proposed
repeated-world extension to the signed, fitted curvatures of ours30 fails as a
finite-expectation identity: $E[1/n_{\rm eff}]=\infty$, even though every nondegenerate
finite sample has a computable ratio. The study below retains the requested η′ and

#### Exact linear-Gaussian identity and its scope

Let $D=\operatorname{diag}(c_i)$, $C=\sum c_i>0$, and
$\widehat\theta=\sum c_ix_i/C$ for independent
$x_i\sim N(\theta,1/c_i)$ on the positive-$c_i$ rows. The fitted score vector is
$s=D(x-\widehat\theta\mathbf1)$. Its covariance is

$$B=\operatorname{Cov}(s)=D-cc^\top/C.$$

Consequently, for $J=s^\top s$,

$$E[J]=\operatorname{tr}B=C-\frac{\sum c_i^2}{C}
=C(1-1/n_{\rm eff}),\qquad
n_{\rm eff}=\frac{C^2}{\sum c_i^2},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(J)=2\operatorname{tr}(B^2).$$

These are exact Gaussian matrix moments, not sampling claims. A zero-curvature row
means an identically zero score contribution, omitted from the random model; no
infinite-variance Gaussian observation is introduced. Appending it leaves every
displayed quantity unchanged. This proves the stated linear identity for **fixed,
nonnegative** curvatures with the specified observation variances.

For ours30, $s_i=\ell_i'(\widehat\phi)$ and $c_i=-\ell_i''(\widehat\phi)$ are instead
data-dependent derivatives. Individual $c_i$ can be negative even though
$C=\sum c_i\ge0$ at the whole-line best angle. Thus $C^2/\sum c_i^2$ can fall below 1;
it is a signed-curvature diagnostic here, not necessarily a count of observations.
Nothing in the linear calculation establishes its score-covariance formula for these
working terms. This distinction persists alongside the noise-declaration questions
already in O14; the present check keeps ours30 as specified by B04.

#### A cancellation singularity: the target's mean does not exist finitely

In standardized isotropic channels with common ratio $c=s$, define the complex
reading coefficient $z_i=(Q_i-P_i)+2\mathrm iD_i$, where here $P_i=\|F_i/\sigma_F\|^2$,
$Q_i=\|a_i/\sigma_a\|^2$, and $D_i=F_i\cdot a_i/(\sigma_F\sigma_a)$ are batch reading
statistics, **not** the profiled misfit. Put $Z=\sum g_i z_i$ and $\theta=\arg Z$.
The exact quadratic law gives

$$C=|Z|,\qquad c_i=g_i\operatorname{Re}(\overline z_i e^{\mathrm i\theta}),\qquad
s_i=-\tfrac12g_i\operatorname{Im}(\overline z_i e^{\mathrm i\theta}).$$

There is an open region where all $g_i=1$. In 1D choose two observed pairs
$(F,a)=(t,t),(t,-t)$, with $0<t^2<1/2$, and make any other observations sufficiently
small. At the central two-reading configuration, $z_1=2\mathrm it^2$ and
$z_2=-2\mathrm it^2$, so $Z=0$. The map from the second reading to
$z_2=(a+\mathrm iF)^2$ has an invertible two-dimensional Jacobian there. It can
therefore vary the total $Z$ freely in a neighbourhood of zero.

On the central two-reading slice, along $Z=\epsilon e^{\mathrm i\theta}$ with
$\theta\in[\pi/6,\pi/3]$ and $\epsilon\downarrow0$, the first two readings give

$$\sum c_i^2\longrightarrow8t^4\sin^2\theta>0,\qquad
J\longrightarrow2t^4\cos^2\theta>0,\qquad C=\epsilon.$$

Independent Gaussian errors have positive smooth density near this configuration at
**every finite signal strength**. Restricting the other coordinates to small
positive-volume neighbourhoods gives a positive lower bound on the density of $Z$
near zero. Polar area is $\epsilon\,d\epsilon\,d\theta$, so both
$\sum c_i^2/C^2$ and $(J/C)^2$ have a divergent lower-bound integral
$\int_0^\delta d\epsilon/\epsilon$. By continuity the positive lower bounds persist
on small open neighbourhoods of the other coordinates. The construction extends to
any finite $N\ge2$ and $k=1,2,3$ by using one axis and keeping the remaining
observations/axes small.
The weights remain 1 on this open region. Hence, in the study's Gaussian worlds,

$$E[1/n_{\rm eff}]=\infty,\qquad
E[1-1/n_{\rm eff}]=-\infty,\qquad E[(J/C)^2]=\infty.$$

Also $J/C-(1-1/n_{\rm eff})\ge1/n_{\rm eff}-1$, so its expectation is $+\infty$.
This proves the second-moment obstruction without needing a claim about existence
of the first moment of $J/C$. Multiplying the target by $C$ reduces the local
singularity to $1/C$, but its second moment still diverges; no global first-moment
claim for that comparator follows just from the local argument. Strong signal makes
these events rare, not impossible. Agreement in typical strong-signal samples does
not reinstate the population identity.

### 3 Nov, continuation: repairing the tails requires more than curvature (Codex)

**The named question:** within pointwise total-deviance calibration, can the
regular repeated-reading Gaussian benchmark coexist with strong-amplitude
consistency? T40 (`../definition/theorems/T40.md`) gives a conditional yes,
and identifies an additional scale that the local sandwich argument does
not select. No production estimator is changed.

Let $D=\widehat L-L\ge0$. A fixed $F_\eta(D)$ with $F'(0)=\eta$ has the right
curvature at the mode, but on the root-N scale D is O(1). Its nonlinear terms
remain in the limiting law. Exact equality to the reference η-powered
Gaussian forces $F_\eta(d)=\eta d$ at every finite d, conflicting with raw
tail preservation when η < 1. This uses an explicit full-Gaussian benchmark,
stronger than property N's verbatim meaning; it is not a new ruling.

**A compatibility witness, not an equation selection.** For a > 0,
$F^{\rm L}_{\eta,a}(d)=a\log[1+\eta(e^{d/a}-1)]$ and
$F^{\rm W}_{\eta,a}(d)=aW_0[\eta(d/a)e^{d/a}]$ have the same local slope η,
tail rate 1 and tail offset $a\log\eta$. Each is a semigroup in η at fixed a;
they are nevertheless different functions. Thus even these extra algebraic
requirements leave nonuniqueness.

The comparison bounds $\eta d\le F(d)\le d$ and
$0\le d-F(d)\le a|\log\eta|$ give two conditional results:

- If the raw law concentrates, a stays bounded and η stays away from zero,
  the transformed law also concentrates. In particular both repair T39's
  ordinary ν = 10, opposite-error family. The raw ν < 1 failure is untouched.
- If the reference powered posterior is localized with O(1) posterior
  deviance and a grows without bound, the transformed law is TV-close to
  that reference. Its Gaussian local limit transfers. It is the full
  sandwich target when the cap leaves C/J unchanged, including C/J = 1.

Under the explicit null scalings $\sup D_N=O_p(\sqrt N)$,
$\eta_N=O_p(N^{-1/2})$ and $a_N\asymp_p N$, the same families are uniformly
close to the reference global power; its bounded-null-tilt behavior survives.
This neither proves uniform null coverage nor sets its optimal floor.

**Where the additional state could come from.** For each reading's own data
factor, $o_i=\sup\ell_i-\inf\ell_i$ is coordinate- and constant-invariant.
Then $a=\gamma\sum_iw(o_i/u)$, with bounded continuous w, w(0)=0, can be
Z-exact, bounded for fixed N, and grow linearly in an iid repeated-reading
world. Rational $w(x)=x/(1+x)$ and exponential $w(x)=1-e^{-x}$ are two
examples, not selected members. Counting nonconstant factors alone is
Z-exact but discontinuous when a small factor becomes flat.

This aggregate does not estimate or share latent signal sizes. That is not
enough to call it admissible under L19: the recorded permission concerns
residual disagreement, whereas oscillation measures potential discrimination
about m. That interpretation remains **unknown**. The constants γ and u,
the saturation function and the operator family are research settings, not
newly licensed physical inputs. They cannot be moved to the type merely
because selection is hard (TASK §5). G24 names the missing justification.

### 3 Nov, calibration continuation: consistency does not calibrate the quoted interval (Codex)

T43 (`../definition/theorems/T43.md`) tests the **actual current Bartlett
power**, rather than a fixed-curvature approximation, in the flat Gaussian
submodel of T42. It supplies a correctly specified, identifying experiment
where the quoted central 95% interval has limiting coverage below 95%.
This is not merely a failure to match a preferred Gaussian shape, or an
example of harmless overcoverage from the cap.

Use common-isotropic 1D Gaussian noise, a fixed interior positive mass and
the stated regular fixed prior. Among N independent readings, one has true
squared standardized amplitude $\Delta_N=4N$ and the other $N-1$ have zero
signal. T42's information criterion diverges:
$\Delta_N^2/(N+\Delta_N)=16N/5\to\infty$. The fitted point and quoted law
are consistent, but the dominant reading has non-vanishing leverage.

T43 also embeds strict undercoverage in prefixes of one fixed signal history:
geometrically spaced spikes have identifying information growth at every
prefix and a limiting leverage profile arbitrarily close to the one-reading
example. The counterexample does not require choosing a new history at each N.

Let $\tau=N/\Delta_N=1/4$, let $W,G$ be independent standard normals and
write $h=\sqrt{\Delta_N}(\widehat\theta_N-\theta_0)$. T43 derives

$$h\Rightarrow W+\sqrt\tau G,\qquad
J_N/\Delta_N\Rightarrow\tau(1+G^2),\qquad
\mathcal H_N/\Delta_N\to1.$$

The same G moves the point and the fitted uncertainty. Consequently the
limiting coverage of the current central interval is, with
$z=\Phi^{-1}(0.975)$,

$$\int_{\mathbb R}\varphi(g)
\left[\Phi\!\left(z\sqrt{\max(1,\tau(1+g^2))}-\sqrt\tau g\right)
-\Phi\!\left(-z\sqrt{\max(1,\tau(1+g^2))}-\sqrt\tau g\right)\right]dg
=0.9272811852285586\ldots.$$

This analytic one-dimensional quadrature is an identified Gaussian
counterexample, not a Monte Carlo ranking. It fails both an exact 95%
coverage target and an at-least-95% guarantee. T42 did not assert either
target: concentration, local shape and coverage are different statements.
The failure is also outside O15's non-identifying null corner. Adding many
readings did not make the observed $J$ a stable estimate of its sampling
target when one reading retains leverage.

**A Z-safe spectral comparison, not an adopted repair.** In this same flat
Gaussian geometry let $\lambda_1$ be the largest eigenvalue of the observed
scatter matrix and $\mathcal H=\lambda_1-\lambda_2$. T43 studies

$$\eta_{\rm sp}=\mathcal H/\lambda_1.$$

An exact-zero reading adds the zero matrix, so this state is Z-safe and
requires no count N as a caller input. Under T42's identifying condition,
$\lambda_1/(N+\Delta_N)\to1$ and the fitted point has the stated Gaussian
limit. The resulting local width gives nominal asymptotic Gaussian
coverage for arbitrary deterministic signal arrays in that submodel,
including the dominant-reading example. This does not derive a unique
finite-N correction, license its use under the full input type, or select
it by a better score. The spectral comparison and its scope are the
mathematical deliverable; no production power changes.

### 3 Nov: exact conditional Gaussian calibration without a signal population (Codex)

T45 connects T43's spectral local width to a standard conditional score
test. For each candidate mass, whiten each reading by its declared
covariance, and split its observation into candidate-mean projection U_i
and independent Gaussian residual V_i. The profile score S is a sum of
linear functions of those residuals conditional on each reading's own U_i;
its conditional variance C is computed from those same projections. In a
nonempty declared Gaussian experiment S/sqrt(C) is exactly standard normal
at truth, including arbitrary fixed latent signals, covariances and
subspaces. No signal-size population or reading count is inferred.

Both sums are unchanged by exact-zero padding and transform together under
units, channel swap and frame changes. This avoids the operation L19
explicitly forbids: no signal inferred in one reading is used in another.
It does not force a specific calibration body or replace the quoted-law
interface. Candidate-dependent conditioning is valid for test inversion,
without providing a single conditional likelihood over all masses.

The inverted set has exact coverage but can be disconnected or unbounded.
It is therefore not a proper mass law's central interval. At an identifying
common-angle Gaussian fit its local derivative is -H/sqrt(lambda_max),
so its endpoints have T43's spectral width. This explains attainability
without selecting a global law or satisfying T30. T45 also proves that
central intervals of any proper positive law have infimum coverage zero
over arbitrarily weak Gaussian signals at every fixed finite N (O15).
The two results concern different output objects; no stronger property N
or permission to use arbitrary aggregate signal statistics is invented.

### 3 Nov: recalibrating probabilities avoids the global-power mechanism (Codex)

T47 examines a different class from T40's scalar-deviance transforms. Let P

$$F_Q(m)=\Phi[\sqrt\eta\,\Phi^{-1}(F_P(m))],\qquad 0<\eta\le1.$$

#### What variance was computed

The standard issue is model-based versus fitted-residual score covariance
in a sandwich (T43's leverage discussion). T45's B15 section now derives
the variance for the *specified T48 working residual model*. With
U=P y, t=R P' U and derivatives in phi=atan(m/c), it is

$$C_{33}(\phi)=\sum_i\frac{\nu_i+k_i}{\nu_i+k_i+2}\|t_i\|^2.$$

The normalized Student residual makes K/(nu+K) a

shared latent V, conditioning on the observed parallel coordinate p

sampling conditional variance depends on unknown mu; T45 gives its

an exact nuisance-free sampling extension of T45 to that world.

**The covariance distinction matters.** Under HHN's regularity assumptions,
including Assumption 2's signal-allocation restriction $\sum_t r_t^4/\Delta^2\to0$ in this normalization (pp. 13–14),
translate the linear Gaussian scalar model to a local angle chart with orthogonal
unit-variance errors, $\tau=K/n<1$, and energy
$\Delta$. Taking their scalar normalizer $S_n=\sqrt\Delta$ gives
$H_n=1-\tau$ and
$\Sigma_n=(1-\tau)[(1-\tau)+K/\Delta]$. Their original unknown-covariance
experiment therefore gives

$$V_{\theta,\mathrm{HHN}}\sim
\frac1\Delta+\frac{K}{(1-\tau)\Delta^2}.$$

This translation is not exactly $V_\theta$ at nonzero $\tau$. In our known
covariance canonical experiment the orthogonal regression complement is
parameter-free and discarded; T43's calculation gives $K/\Delta^2$ instead.
The two expressions agree as $\tau\to0$; replacing $n$ by the reading count
would instead give $\tau=1$ and invalidate the source assumptions.
T43 does not require HHN's signal-allocation restriction.

There is also no blanket claim that known-noise Bekker formulas fail Z.
For a supplied regression design, put the known structural error variance
in $\widehat\Sigma_B$ and omit the Gaussian nonnormality terms. HHN's displayed
formula then has no explicit $n$: appending a zero regression row and extending
$P$ by a zero block leaves it unchanged. This algebraic padding property does
not reconstruct $P$ or its complementary observations from canonical pairs;
with $P=I$, $\tilde\alpha=1$ and $\widehat H=0$.

**MS estimates a different variance.** The checked [arXiv v3 (4 Oct 2021)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.12445v3)
has Lemma 1 on printed p. 12 and §4.1, equation (4), Theorems 2–3 on pp. 13–14.
Write $R=I-P$, $e=e(\beta_0)$, and $\sigma_i^2=\operatorname{Var}(e_i)$:

$$\Phi=\frac2K\sum_{i\ne j}P_{ij}^2\sigma_i^2\sigma_j^2,\qquad
\widehat\Phi=\frac2K\sum_{i\ne j}
\frac{P_{ij}^2(e_iR_i e)(e_jR_j e)}{R_{ii}R_{jj}+R_{ij}^2}.$$

Equation (4) is empirical; Theorem 2 proves ratio consistency under the null,
with Theorem 3 covering specified local alternatives. Known covariance makes
$\Phi$ computable directly; under unit residual variance it is
$2(K-\sum_iP_{ii}^2)/K$. In the standardized AR statistic the explicit $K$
cancels, leaving denominator $\sqrt{2\sum_{i\ne j}P_{ij}^2}$, but $P$ remains.
Setting $P=I$ gives zero numerator and variance and violates Assumption 1's
$\max_i P_{ii}\le\delta<1$. Hence this cannot be imported as a readings-only
variance correction merely by writing $K=N$.

**What survives for G3/G26.** At a candidate angle T45's known-noise conditional
score variance is $C(\theta)=\sum_i X_i(\theta)^2$. At the principal fit it
equals $\lambda_1$, so the local sandwich is $C(\hat\theta)/H^2$.
It estimates the target without a count, unlike substituting an estimated
signal energy into $\Delta/(\Delta+K)$ with a declared $K$. Exact-zero readings
add zero scatter. This explains the existing spectral control as an empirical
variance counterpart; it selects neither its finite-$N$ law nor a full-type
rule. Source identification, asymptotic equivalence and premise permission
remain separate. No estimator, quoted width, premise or ruling is changed.
