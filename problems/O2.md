## The problem in one paragraph

The law of mass (T8) is settled up to O6. The point reported from it is not. The current
readout, the ratio of mean sizes over each reading's candidate cloud (T10), satisfies the
composition laws on one supplied joint law but changes when a reading that carries no
information is appended, and it
returns the starting law's centre at the all-zero experiment only when that centre is the
instrument ratio. What is wanted is a readout that composes, ignores null readings, and
gives the declared centre at the all-zero experiment. If these requirements conflict,
TASK §4 calls for finding the mis-stated premise. The finite candidate checks below do
not prove a universal impossibility theorem.

## Facts that constrain the search

- The all-zero answer, whatever readout, depends neither on N nor on the $\Sigma_i$ (Z plus
  order-free combination, T12).
- Under the best-guess repair the law tends to $\pi$ as the data shrink while the weight is
  homogeneous of degree one in the data; that is why its limit depends on direction. Any
  nontrivial direction dependence of such a weight survives the shrinking limit. This
  does not settle whether a discontinuous extension at zero is allowed.
- The law itself is Z-exact (T24) and gives median c at all-zero. So any readout that is a
  functional of the law alone passes R-b and R-c but, by the uniqueness theorem, fails R-a
  unless it is a ratio of means of two sizes.
- The identity $\widehat m=E[\alpha m]/E[\alpha]$ (d2 step 5): the cloud readout is the mass
  averaged with weight proportional to how hard each candidate accelerates. The weight is
  what cancels the $1/\alpha$ that makes $E[m]$ infinite (T19). A replacement must keep
  $E[m]$ finite, which rules out plain posterior means.
- 24 Oct, Claude's observation, not checked beyond the one run: at c = s the cloud readout
  and the median agree to $10^{-13}$ on the *original* series and differ only after null
  padding. If that holds in general, the Z failure of the cloud readout is entirely the null
  readings' noise-sized clouds entering the stacked length.

## Prior-return law and the separate cloud readout (Codex, 26 Oct)

`presets/inspection_prior_paths.yaml` supplies a nonzero control: F=(1,0), a=(0,1),
equal independent SD sigma, s=1, and an explicitly varied half-Cauchy centre c.
Its correction cost is 1/sigma² at every mass, so its normalized law is exactly
pi_c. Its conditional mean is (m,1)/(1+m²), with covariance sigma² I/(1+m²).
Consequently A(m)=kappa_sigma/sqrt(1+m²). The factor kappa_sigma cancels in the
readout. This has the same mass dependence as the all-zero isotropic cloud, whose
A(m) is a different constant times 1/sqrt(1+m²).

The readout in both cases is therefore

$$R(c)=\frac{\int_0^\infty m\,h_c(m)/\sqrt{1+m^2}\,dm}
                  {\int_0^\infty h_c(m)/\sqrt{1+m^2}\,dm}
       =\frac{B(1/c)}{B(c)},\qquad
B(c)=\int_0^1\frac{c\,du}{c^2+(1-c^2)u^2},$$

where h_c=c/(c²+m²). Equivalently,

$$R(c)=\begin{cases}
c\,\operatorname{acosh}(1/c)/\arccos(c),&0<c<1,\\
1,&c=1,\\
c\,\arccos(1/c)/\operatorname{acosh}(c),&c>1.
\end{cases}$$

At c = 0.1, 1, 10 the computed points are 0.203533525, 1, 4.91319551, while the
law's mathematical median is respectively 0.1, 1, 10. Independent direct mass
integrals, a Rice/angle readout and reciprocal pairing check the closed form in
`tests/test_inspection_prior_reference.py`; `tests/test_inspection_prior_paths.py`
checks the implemented operation. The point is independent of common SD here.

This isolates the already known O2 mechanism: returning the supplied law does not
make the cloud readout return its centre. The data can carry candidate-size
information beyond the normalized mass law. Whether this nonzero constant-evidence
reading counts as null under Z's intended scope is not decided here; no new premise
or repair is adopted.

## 27 Oct (evening): L12 checked; where the Z failure lives (Claude, pressure; provisional)

Per candidate $X=mY$, so T10's readout is the law tilted by $A(m)=E[Y\mid m]$:
$\widehat m=\int mA\,p\,dm/\int A\,p\,dm$. $A$'s scale cancels; only its $m$-dependence acts.
`../tests/test_readout_weight.py` has both results below.

**1. The series join (T9, L12) is second order, and no rung fixes it.**
- *Composition does not fix it.* In both composition experiments the per-reading sizes being
  joined are proportional: lumping is at common acceleration ($X_{1,i}=m_1Y_i$,
  $X_{2,i}=m_2Y_i$), common force at common force. So every symmetric join homogeneous of
  degree 1 passes L5 and L14: $\ell_1$, $\ell_2$, $\ell_\infty$ alike.
- *No rung bears on it* (`../definition/ladder.md` §3). R4's force sum joins forces on one
  target at one time, which is sources, not readings.
- *L12's "one joining rule" is false inside a reading once the window is in the type.* T33
  makes a reading's force and acceleration time averages over $\Delta t_i$, so sub-intervals
  join by a duration-weighted vector sum, while components join by the Euclidean norm. Two
  joins inside one reading; T9's support picks the component one without saying why.
- *It barely matters.* On the STATE.md series scaled to weak signal (scale 0.05, s = 2), the
  readouts are 2.4075 ($\ell_1$), 2.4082 ($\ell_2$ = T9 = ours29's 2.4083), 2.4090 ($\ell_4$),
  2.4099 ($\ell_\infty$). Ten null readings move each by −0.0098, −0.0098, −0.0093, −0.0080.
- **Call: unknown yet, low stakes.** Not smuggling (it decides no mechanical fact), not an
  input (no instrument sheet supplies a join), not forced. It is not where O2 is blocked.
  Parked until the weight question below is settled, since it only shapes $A$.

**2. Null readings replace the weight by a function of the instrument alone.**
- A null reading's candidate cloud given $m$ is $N(0,v(m)I)$ with
  $v(m)=1/(1/\sigma_a^2+m^2/\sigma_F^2)$, so its size goes as $(1+m^2/s^2)^{-1/2}$. That shape
  is fixed by $\Sigma$, centred at $s$, and the same for every join.
- Appending null readings leaves the law unchanged (T24) and drags $A$ toward that shape.
  With 1000 of them the readout *is* the law tilted by it: 2.3931 against 2.3930 (scale 0.05),
  2.0827 against 2.0826 (scale 0.02). Every move is toward $s$.
- This is one mechanism behind three failures already in this file: Z (T29), the all-zero
  value $R(c)\ne c$ for $c\ne s$, and Codex's perpendicular reading. In each, the weight
  carries a second centre, $s$, from the noise model, besides $\pi$'s $c$.

**3. Provisional sort: outcome 3, and the mis-stated premise is the domain of T21's "law of
the two joined sizes".** L17 (ruled 28 Sept) says the readout's score cannot read the declared
instrument facts. The cloud readout satisfies L17's letter, since it depends on the candidate
law only, but on a null reading the candidate law *is* the instrument: $\Sigma$ alone sets its
$m$-dependence. So the letter (same candidate law, same mass) and the reason (it cannot read
the instrument) come apart exactly where O2 fails. Under `../TASK.md` §4 the reason is kept,
and what is mis-stated is what T21 and T4 average over: the full posterior cloud, noise part
included, instead of what the readings support about the true sizes.

**What any repair must meet, and why it is not yet in hand.**
- *The weight does essential work.* The law has no mean (T19); $A$'s decay, about $1/m$ in the
  tail, is what makes the readout finite. A readout that is a functional of the law of $m$
  alone loses that, and by the uniqueness theorem fails L5 or L14 (the median row above).
- *The obvious signal-only weight is wrong too.* The best-guess size $\lvert E[\mathbf a^\ast\mid m]\rvert$
  is Z-exact (a null reading contributes 0) and decays as $1/m$, but it is homogeneous of
  degree 0 in the data: scaling every reading down leaves its shape unchanged, so near the
  all-zero experiment it reads the noise's *ratio* at full weight. That is the
  direction-dependent limit, 0.546 to 1.830, in the table above.
- So the object sought is a weight that is (i) Z-exact, (ii) decays fast enough for
  finiteness, (iii) loses its shape continuously as the signal goes to zero, so the all-zero
  readout returns $\pi$'s centre. **Unknown yet** whether one exists. If none does, the
  contradiction is between L17's reason and T4's composition, and by TASK §4 one of them is
  mis-stated; on the ladder, L14 is the one resting on a provisional rung (R5).

## 28 Oct: the no-go, and which premise gives (Claude, pressure; provisional)

**1. Under L19 the point's only cross-reading content is the law.** L19 (ruled 28 Sept,
"ironclad" 30 Sept): readings are joined only through the mass they share; nothing inferred
from one reading about signals (size, direction, spread) is used on another. T9's series size
$Y=\oplus Y_i$ joins readings through their signal sizes, and the cloud weight $A(m)=E[Y\mid m]$
reweights every reading's evidence by the others' sizes. `tests/test_readout_weight.py`
shows it directly: null readings leave the law unchanged and move the point by their sizes
alone. That is borrowing. So, under L19, the point is a functional of the law of $m$ (and the
declared inputs).

**2. No law functional meets point-level L5 and L14 on spread laws.** L5 at the point needs
additivity under convolution (the mean), L14 additivity of the inverse (the harmonic mean),
and by Jensen they agree only on point masses; the half-Cauchy has no mean at all (T19). This is
the median row of the table above. So L19, Z and point-level composition are jointly
inconsistent, and by `../TASK.md` §4 one of them is mis-stated.

**3. The one that gives is point-level composition at finite noise.**
- On the ladder, L5 and L14 come from R3 and R5, which are **exact-value** commitments:
  exact masses add; exact inverse masses add under a common force.
- On data, no point meets L5 at finite noise. Bodies 1 and 2 sharing each acceleration
  reading, the lump measured with force $F_1+F_2$, priors that compose ($1+2=3$), 30 series,
  seed 7: the cloud readout's defect is 0.273% (strong), 2.44% (mid), 20.96% (weak); the law's
  median's is 0.273%, 2.62%, 23.25%. Both shrink to zero as the signal grows.
- The ratio of means passes L5 only on laws of sizes from *one* posterior, where the lump's
  force is $X_1+X_2$. A lump is a separate measurement, so data never supply that.
- So what the rungs commit, exact composition, is delivered by T1 on exact inputs and by L11
  in the limit, for any consistent point. As a constraint on the point at finite noise it
  cannot be met by anything, so it cannot be a premise. T3, T4 and T21 stay true of laws of
  sizes and stop constraining the point. `tests/test_point_composition.py`.

**4. What is left, and what it forces.** The point is a functional of the law that is Z-exact
(every law functional is: T24), returns $c$ at the all-zero experiment, respects the swap and
units, and is finite on a law with no mean (T19). Units and the swap leave a family: every
location functional of $\ln m$ that commutes with negation (median, geometric mean, …). That
is a fork. **Sorted as forced, provisionally:** a definition of mass should not depend on the
coordinate its law is read in (L23, proposed: the point commutes with every increasing
reparametrisation, as T31 already requires of $\eta$). Then the point is a quantile, and the
swap makes it the median (T35, tested).

**Candidate: the point is the law's median.** It meets every column of the table above:
Z (exactly), the all-zero value $c$ (for every $c$, not only $c=s$), and composition on data
as well as the cloud does. ours29 already reports it; STATE.md's robot numbers are medians.

## Candidate pressure check (Codex, 28 Oct): derivations and counterexamples

**Sorted fork and deliverable.** Test what follows from replacing the series size,
without changing the current mass law, prior, covariance declaration or g/eta. The
deliverable is this candidate table with tests and counterexamples, not a selected
readout. `met/readout_candidates.py` implements six explicit diagnostics;
`presets/readout_candidates.yaml` visits the perpendicular case first, then all-zero,
weak aligned, two near-zero directions, strong aligned and outside-subspace cases,
with three declared prior centres and four exact-zero padding declarations. It is
deterministic (seed 0 recorded but unused), serial, and leaves production unchanged.

### What each size means

Let U be the stacked conditional candidate acceleration vector, M the candidate
mass, and p the unchanged mass law. The force candidate is M U. The four size rules
use $R_A=E_p[M A(M)]/E_p[A(M)]$:

| Identifier | Conditional size A(m) |
| --- | --- |
| `cloud_mean` | $E[\|U\|\mid M=m]$ (current T10) |
| `mean_vector` | $\|E[U\mid M=m]\|$ |
| `conditional_rms` | $\sqrt{E[\|U\|^2\mid M=m]}$ |
| `plain_sum_cloud` | $\sum_i E[\|\alpha_i\|\mid M=m]$ (different join) |

Write $S_2(m)=E[\|U\|^2\mid m]$. The **global** RMS quotient is instead
$\sqrt{E_p[M^2 S_2(M)]/E_p[S_2(M)]}$. Moving the square root outside the mass
integral changes the operation. The median has no size weight. No candidate
shrinks the conditional covariance by g or eta. Native probability entries saved
by the recipe are normalized quadrature weights, not densities.

### Perpendicular first: a control shared by the size rules

For F=(1,0), a=(0,1), independent equal SD σ, s=1,

$$\mu_m=(m,1)/(1+m^2),\qquad C_m=\sigma^2 I_2/(1+m^2).$$

Therefore the mean-vector size is $1/\sqrt{1+m^2}$, the conditional RMS is
$\sqrt{1+2\sigma^2}/\sqrt{1+m^2}$, and the cloud mean is
$\kappa_\sigma/\sqrt{1+m^2}$. Plain sum equals cloud mean for one reading.
All four ratio weights give exactly R(c) derived above: 0.203533525, 1, 4.91319551
at c=0.1,1,10. Global RMS gives √c, and the median gives c. For general noise
ratio s, the scaled perpendicular control F=s e₁, a=e₂, σ_F/σ_a=s again has law
π_c and global RMS √(cs). The all-zero common-ratio control has that result too.

Thus the perpendicular example does **not** distinguish the main size repairs.
It also does not by itself prove they violate Z: equality of the marginal mass law
with π is not equality of the full candidate-size law. The all-zero case does
test R-c′: its cloud/RMS shapes are the same, while mean-vector size is zero.
`tests/test_readout_candidates.py::test_perpendicular_size_rules_share_a_shape_but_global_rms_is_distinct`
checks the closed form against a separate compact-angle integral; the conditional
moments and all-zero values have their own tests in the same file.

### An exact Gaussian point-Z counterexample

Take that perpendicular reading with σ=1, c=1. Append a whole reading whose
measured force and acceleration are exactly zero, with correctly declared
σ_F=3, σ_a=1, full 2D subspace and independent errors. Its H and score are zero;
the mass law stays π₁ and eta stays 1. This invokes exact-zero neutrality without
declaring the perpendicular base itself null.

The appended conditional covariance is $9I_2/(9+m^2)$, so

$$S_{2,\mathrm{new}}(m)=\frac3{1+m^2}+\frac{18}{9+m^2}.$$

The conditional-RMS new/base weight ratio is
$\sqrt{1+6(1+m^2)/(9+m^2)}$, strictly increasing with m. For cloud mean, put
V∼N(e,I₂), |e|=1, and independent Z∼N(0,I₂). The corresponding weight ratio is

$$\frac{E\sqrt{\|V\|^2+v(m)\|Z\|^2}}{E\|V\|},\qquad
v(m)=\frac{9(1+m^2)}{9+m^2}.$$

This is strictly increasing too. Plain sum has ratio
$1+(E\|Z\|/E\|V\|)\sqrt{v(m)}$. Under the base probability proportional to
$\pi_1(m)/\sqrt{1+m^2}$, each increasing multiplier has strictly positive
covariance with m, so each point increases from 1. No numerical comparison is
needed to establish the sign.

For normalized π₁,
$E[1/(s^2+M^2)]=1/[s(1+s)]$ and
$E[M^2/(s^2+M^2)]=1/(1+s)$. Global RMS therefore has denominator 3 and numerator
6 after padding: its point moves exactly **1 → √2**. Mean-vector size and median
stay 1. `test_perpendicular_different_ratio_null_padding_has_exact_global_rms_shift`
checks these moments, unchanged law and the predicted movements. Same-ratio
padding is a blind control here: it changes only a constant prefactor for the
cloud, conditional-RMS and plain-sum size ratios, hence cannot substitute for this test.

### Composition: one propagated law, no fresh fit

The composition theorem's stated domain includes **all** strictly positive
finite-first-moment magnitude laws and arbitrary common-channel couplings
(6 Sept source, §1), not just laws reachable through the Gaussian adapter.
The following counterexamples use that domain. Their reachability as current
Gaussian measurement posteriors has **not** been shown. This distinction matters
if the theorem's domain is later revised.

For each row use equiprobable atoms, common acceleration Y, and force channels
X₁, X₂. Compare R(X₁+X₂,Y) with R(X₁,Y)+R(X₂,Y):

| Candidate | Y | X₁ | X₂ | Combined | Sum of separate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mean vector (signed 1D vectors) | (1,−1,1) | (1,−1,2) | (1,−2,1) | 2 | 10/3 |
| Conditional RMS (positive magnitudes) | (1,3,1,3) | (1,3,2,6) | (1,6,1,6) | 1+√5 | 13/4 |
| Global RMS | (1,1) | (1,3) | (3,1) | 4 | 2√5 |
| Unique median | (1,1,1) | (1,1,4) | (1,4,1) | 5 | 2 |

For the mean-vector row every force is a positive mass times the same atom's
acceleration. In the first channel, the mass-1 conditional mean cancels, leaving
readout 2; in the second the readout is 4/3. After adding forces, the mass-3
conditional mean cancels, leaving readout 2. Every whole readout denominator is
positive. A fallback at all-zero cannot change this contradiction. Swapping
channels and using reciprocity gives the same inverse-L14 failures. Cloud mean
composes on each supplied law by expectation linearity.

`met/readout_law_checks.py` retains the atoms, exact expressions and scope in
JSON-ready records. `tests/test_readout_composition.py::test_exact_finite_law_composition_values`
checks both composition identities. These are readout-level tests, not an assertion
that propagating a law equals re-fitting combined measured inputs.

Two additional finite-law checks locate the mean-vector failure. With equal
mass-1/mass-2 probabilities and unit acceleration magnitudes, directions all +e
give point 3/2; splitting the mass-1 directions equally between +e and −e gives
point 2. The original radial law is identical, so the rule uses information beyond
it (C3). Redefining the size law would require a new argument; the direct
composition counterexample does not depend on this terminology. At fixed mass 2,
U=±e equally gives sure magnitudes (2,1), yet the mean-vector readout is undefined.
There is also a reachable measured example with no zero observations:
F=a=(0,1), Σ=I₄, declared candidate line span(e₁). Its conditional mean is zero
for every mass. The Gaussian tests check this case explicitly.

RMS constructions additionally exceed L7's domain: take positive Y with finite
mean but infinite second moment and independent M∈{1,2}. X=MY has finite mean,
but conditional RMS sizes are infinite. Global RMS already fails on Y=1 and
positive X with finite mean and infinite second moment. The finite counterexamples
above suffice even on a domain where all second moments exist.

### What this sorts, and what remains

**Wrong premise within a proposed repair:** keeping the full composition domain
while replacing cloud mean by conditional mean or either RMS operation fails the
explicit identities above. Plain sum changes T9/L12 and still fails point Z.
The median fails composition. No member of this finite catalog closes O2.

There is a conditional obstruction: fix the current construction of the joint candidate-size law,
T9's join and the full-domain composition theorem's C1–C5 assumptions. T4 then
forces cloud mean, which the Gaussian zero-padding example shows fails point Z.
Changing only its summary cannot repair that conjunction. This locates the
tension among law construction/propagation, join and readout-domain premises;
it does not prove that every admissible mass function is impossible, identify
which premise is mis-stated, or license an arbitrary new input. No premise,
continuity requirement, production equation or ruling is changed here.

## Physical composition and measurement coarsening (Codex, 28 Oct)

**Sorted fork and deliverable.** Does physical lumping require that propagating a
joint candidate law agree with fitting the summed measurements? The derivation
below and `tests/test_composition_bridge.py` show why generic equality is an extra,
false requirement. The lawful composition identity survives. This develops the
unpromoted 28 Sept scratch derivation in
`../legacy/vd-newton-tools/ours27_bridge_2026_09_28/composition/composition_bridge.md`;
the archive is unchanged. The tests use strictly positive instrument errors and
check shared-observation covariance, a direct current-cloud calculation, the
Gaussian contrast factor, finite-law counterexamples and weighted aggregation.
They are deterministic proof checks, not a new sampled study or estimator.

### One joint experiment and its physical operation

For two bodies with one shared acceleration candidate a, write

$$\theta=(m_1,m_2,\mathbf a),\quad m_j>0,\qquad
h(\theta)=(m_1\mathbf a,m_2\mathbf a,\mathbf a).$$

The source observation is y=(F₁,F₂,A), with a declared positive-definite joint
Gaussian error covariance Σ. A normalized joint reference measure Π gives
$P_y(d\theta)\propto\phi_\Sigma(y-h(\theta))\Pi(d\theta)$. This is an explicit
diagnostic model for deriving the bridge; arbitrary Π is **not** an adopted L19
size law or a primitive supplied by the current ours29 type.

Lumping maps the candidate state to $q(\theta)=(M,\mathbf a)$, M=m₁+m₂.
With $\alpha=\|\mathbf a\|$, on this **same** law, assuming
$0<E[\alpha]<\infty$ and $E[m_j\alpha]<\infty$,

$$r_1=\frac{E[m_1\alpha]}{E[\alpha]},\quad
r_2=\frac{E[m_2\alpha]}{E[\alpha]},\quad
r_{\rm total}=\frac{E[(m_1+m_2)\alpha]}{E[\alpha]}=r_1+r_2.$$

Thus composition concerns the pushforward $q_\#P_y$. No independent part fits are
needed. The common-force construction replaces mⱼ by μⱼ=1/mⱼ and the shared
acceleration by a shared force candidate, giving inverse-mass addition with the
analogous positive finite common-force denominator and finite numerators. With
several trials, the body masses must retain their identities across
trials; merely giving all readings one common mass addresses a different problem.
`test_shared_force_dual_composes_inverse_mass_on_the_same_joint_law` checks the
common-force dual with positive instrument errors and a shared force observation.

### Shared measurement identity forces covariance

The two displayed pairs (F₁,A), (F₂,A) reuse the **same observed A**. If D copies
the source vector into (F₁,A,F₂,A), its covariance is $D\Sigma D^\top$; the
covariance of the difference between the two A copies is zero. For a 3d-dimensional
source with positive-definite Σ, the copied 4d covariance has rank 3d. Both pair
marginal covariances are positive definite, but their independent block-diagonal
product assigns nonzero variance to that difference and repeats the A likelihood.
This is an exact consequence of the observation's identity, not a tunable weight.

A calculation with current `Reading` conditional clouds makes the mismatch
explicit. In 1D, take independent source errors Σ=I₃, observed (F₁,F₂,A)=(1,2,1),
and candidate masses m₁=1, m₂=2. The standalone conditional acceleration clouds
both have mean 1 but variances 1/2 and 1/5. No coupling can make these two
acceleration variables identical while preserving both marginals. The genuine
shared-acceleration likelihood has precision 1²+2²+1=6, hence variance 1/6.
Multiplying standalone clouds counts A twice and gives variance 1/7.
`test_current_separate_clouds_cannot_preserve_one_shared_acceleration` checks this
using the actual `Reading.pre()` blocks.

This establishes a concrete connection to O16: cross-reading dependence is needed
to retain shared evidence. It does not show that O16 causes the point-Z failure.
The current one-body type has neither multiple body-mass identities nor a joint
covariance slot across readings; packing two bodies as independent trials or axes
would silently change the declared experiment. No type extension is adopted here.

### Summing the data loses a contrast

Let z=Ly=(S,A), S=F₁+F₂. Its covariance is forced to be $L\Sigma L^\top$.
The compatible coarse reference measure is $\Pi_q=q_\#\Pi$; resetting a fresh
default measure is a further change. Even with both declarations correct, fitting
only z generally differs from pushing forward the fine-data law: z omits evidence.

For independent isotropic force errors with variances v₁,v₂, independent of the
acceleration error, put

$$w=\frac{v_1}{v_1+v_2},\quad C=F_1-wS,\quad
v_C=\frac{v_1v_2}{v_1+v_2},\quad \delta=m_1-wM.$$

The coordinates (S,A,C) retain all the original data. Their S and C errors are
independent, C has covariance v_C I, and its candidate mean is δa. Positivity of
the part masses gives $-wM<\delta<(1-w)M$. Disintegrate the reference measure as
$\Pi_q(dM,d\mathbf a)K(d\delta\mid M,\mathbf a)$. Then

$$q_\#P_{\rm fine}(dM,d\mathbf a\mid S,A,C)\ \propto
L_{\rm coarse}(S,A\mid M,\mathbf a)\Pi_q(dM,d\mathbf a)
\underbrace{\int\phi_{v_C I}(C-\delta\mathbf a)
K(d\delta\mid M,\mathbf a)}_{\overline K(C\mid M,\mathbf a)}.$$

The coarse fit omits precisely $\overline K$. Their full candidate laws coincide
at given data iff that factor is constant over the coarse posterior support.
`test_omitted_contrast_factor_is_exact_with_propagated_joint_prior` checks the
formula against a direct joint Gaussian fit with nontrivial conditional splits.

For general correlated Σ, use any invertible coordinates (z,t)=Ty with z=Ly and
covariance blocks Γ. Set

$$B=\Gamma_{tz}\Gamma_{zz}^{-1},\quad
V=\Gamma_{tt}-B\Gamma_{zt},\quad u=t-Bz.$$

The z and u errors are independent, and the candidate innovation mean is
$d(\theta)=h_t(\theta)-Bh_z(q\theta)$. Replace the contrast factor above by
$\int\phi_V(u-d(\theta))K(d\theta\mid q)$. The correlated-Gaussian test checks
this Schur-complement factorization against the full density in 1D, 2D and 3D.
An arbitrary invertible coordinate transform contributes a parameter-independent
Jacobian to the original-data density; it cancels from these posterior formulas.
Thus the loss-of-evidence argument does not depend on neglecting correlations.

### Exact equality for all data would impose a special mass split

In the independent-force model, suppose equality of the **full (M,a) laws** is
required for all observed contrasts and all M>0 at fixed nonzero a. Assume proper
conditional split kernels and coarse support reaching M↓0. Equality requires
$\overline K(C\mid M,\mathbf a)$ to be parameter-independent. A Gaussian's
characteristic function never vanishes, so equality of these convolutions forces
the conditional distribution of δa to be independent of M. But its support shrinks
to {0} as M↓0. Therefore δa=0; with a≠0, δ=0:

$$m_1=wM,\qquad m_2=(1-w)M.$$

Conversely this split makes C pure measurement noise, independent of the coarse
candidate. The equality test checks this case at three force-variance ratios and
shows a nearby different split fails. Requiring universal equality would therefore
tie a physical mass fraction to instrument error variances. Newton II does not
supply that relation. At a=0 every split has zero contrast mean, and a restricted
mass support can avoid the shrinking-support argument. Equality of only a mass
marginal or a single point is weaker and is not ruled out by this theorem.

### Counterexample with every instrument SD positive

Use 1D source covariance I₃ and equal reference weights on states
(m₁,m₂,a)=(1,1,1) and (3,1,1). The acceleration instrument has SD 1; fixing a in
this diagnostic reference measure does not make its measurement exact. Observe
either (F₁,F₂,A)=(1.5,1.5,1) or (2.5,0.5,1). Both yield the identical coarse
input (S,A)=(3,1), covariance diag(2,1), and the same pushed-forward reference
weights on M=2,4. The coarse point is 3.

Their contrasts are C=0 and C=1. The fine posterior odds of the second state are
exp(2C−1), giving

$$r_{\rm fine}=2+\frac{2}{1+\exp(1-2C)}
=\begin{cases}2.53788284274,&C=0,\\3.46211715726,&C=1.\end{cases}$$

Each fine law satisfies r_total=r₁+r₂ exactly. No function of only the identical
coarse inputs can reproduce both fine points.
`test_positive_noise_counterexample_preserves_composition_but_not_refitting` and
`test_same_coarse_measurement_and_prior_can_have_different_fine_points` check the
Gaussian posterior weights and these values. This is a counterexample
to generic fine/coarse point equality, with compatible priors and correct error
propagation. It is not an ours29 joint-posterior reachability claim or a proposed
signal prior.

### The coherent averaging identity and its limits

For one normalized joint probability model, forgetting the contrast obeys the
posterior tower identity, using that model's predictive law:

$$P_{\rm coarse}(dq\mid z)=\int P_{\rm fine}(dq\mid z,C)\,p(dC\mid z).$$

If $A_C=E[\alpha\mid z,C]>0$ almost surely, r_C is the fine total ratio readout,
and the required coarse numerator and denominator are finite and positive, this gives

$$r_{\rm coarse}=\frac{\int A_C r_C\,p(dC\mid z)}
                           {\int A_C\,p(dC\mid z)}.$$

It is not generally the ordinary average of the fine points. The tower test uses
two compatible states with a=1/2,3/2 and M=2,4, all instrument SDs 1. At coarse
S=3.5,A=1 the state weights are equal, the coarse point is 3.5, and independent
contrast integration returns numerator 3.5 and denominator 1. Ordinary point
averaging differs. A mass marginal alone need not retain the size weights required
by this identity.

This identity is conditional on one probability model, compatible reference
measures and its own predictive contrast law. It is **not automatically proved
for ours29's data-dependent g and eta**. Nor does the bridge determine the joint
reference measure: identical part-mass marginal priors can give different sum
priors. Equal weights on (m₁,m₂)=(1,1),(2,2) yield M=2,4, whereas equal weights
on (1,2),(2,1) yield M=3 surely. The final test checks that elementary ambiguity.
The physical dependence and joint prior must come from the declared experiment
and prior knowledge; no readout formula can recover them from marginals alone.
